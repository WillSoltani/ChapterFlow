#voice.md
Write like a sharp human explainer with taste.

Priorities:
- Specific over generic
- Concrete over abstract
- Insight over padding
- Strong openings
- Distinct paragraph jobs
- Vivid examples
- Honest limits
- Memorable sentences that still sound human

Voice:
- Clear, confident, readable
- Feels authored, not assembled
- Sounds like a smart person explaining something they actually understand
- Uses "you" naturally, but does not spray it everywhere
- Reads like a person with taste, not a helper model trying to sound polished

Avoid:
- generic self-help language
- corporate tone
- textbook tone
- fake profundity
- repetitive sentence skeletons
- filler mechanism language

Bad patterns:
- repeating the same idea in new words
- "X is not Y, it is Z" over and over
- broad claims unsupported by the brief
- conclusions that sound polished but say nothing
- longer prose that does not become deeper prose
- paragraphs that all sound equally "important"

10/10 chapter signals:
- the opening could not belong to another chapter
- the paragraph jobs are visibly different
- the anchor stories are load-bearing, not decorative
- the chapter adds at least one real distinction, tension, or limit
- the last paragraph opens a live question rather than ending in mush

Read-aloud test:
- if a sentence sounds pre-manufactured, cut it
- if a sentence sounds like a generic podcast summary, cut it
- if a sentence sounds smarter than the brief, cut it

The chapter should feel unmistakably about this chapter, not the book in general.


## v15 sealed additions

Teach the idea, not the chapter.

- In reader-facing content, do not write:
  - "the chapter says"
  - "Chapter N teaches"
  - "the chapter's lesson is"
  - "the chapter closes by"
- Use the author name sparingly for attribution inside breakdowns only.
- In takeaways, moreDetails, prompts, quiz explanations, review cards, implementation plans, and examples, meta-references to "the chapter" should be zero.

Grade-band intent:
- easy = comfortable for grade 8-9
- medium = comfortable for grade 10-11
- hard = comfortable for grade 12

The chapter should feel:
- written, not assembled
- specific, not templated
- sourced, not stitched
- distinct across tones, not cosmetically varied

Do not let internal planning language leak into reader-facing prose.
If a phrase sounds like an outline note, rewrite it.
