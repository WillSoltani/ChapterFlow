# The Golden Circle

## Hook
3 labels can still blur the stake: if no behavior answers them, the slogan wins.

## Fast read
3 labels sit on founder Sylvia's pitch: WHY, HOW, WHAT. The first says why the effort matters. The second says how the reason shows up. The third says what people can see. Simple enough.

But the pitch still feels thin. Apple Inc., the company founded in 1976 and based in Cupertino, gives the claim a visible setting. Macintosh, one product in the Apple account, cannot carry meaning by itself. If Sylvia starts with product facts, the facts have to invent a reason. If she moves WHY ahead of HOW, the visible thing can prove a reason. Who asks, and where can they see the answer?

## Deep read
3 labels work only when each one has a job. WHY names the reason. HOW names the way that reason shows up. WHAT names the visible offer or result. The order matters because the first term frames the second; reverse it and a tactic has to carry a price it cannot pay alone.

Use restraint. If the reason is already shared and the person only needs the action, give the action first. If a true detail belongs to one source, keep it there. Cupertino is a hard fact inside the Apple account, not spare weight for any weak paragraph.

Price the default before you copy it. The default says, "Lead with the thing; people will infer the belief." That costs trust. The thing has to explain itself while the reason stays vague. Sylvia moves one reason above the product line and keeps the product as the part someone can inspect. The sentence gets less grand. It gets more useful.

At Kitty Hawk on December 17, 1903, Orville Wright and Wilbur Wright flew. That story is not the Apple story with another place attached. It shows aim and staying power before credentials. One vivid story can show a claim, but comparison shows what travels and what does not.

You can use the same move without title power. Pick one live message. Ask which reason should lead, which way makes it visible, and what thing should bear the price. If the line has no answer, do not warm it up. Move the reason up.

## Full read
A failed paragraph shows the other side. Cupertino had been moved into a Wright brothers note because the place sounded exact. The sentence stayed factual in one sense and became false in use. A hard detail is a promise tied to one source. Move it and you may make the whole claim feel made up.

The Golden Circle is useful because the parts do not do the same job. WHY gives the reason. HOW gives the way. WHAT gives the thing someone can see. Blend them and you lose the question that matters most: what is carrying the price?

Apple is the first visible setting here, not a mascot. Its 1976 start and Cupertino base let you inspect the claim instead of nodding at a slogan. Macintosh gives the WHAT a body. The product does not lead; the reason gives the product a job. That job is narrow: show that the challenger belief was more than a line. That is why the visible setting matters. You are not being asked to admire Apple; you are being asked to inspect the order.

The Wright brothers give the comparison a new surface. Orville Wright is a factual token Apple does not have, and Wilbur Wright keeps the story from sounding like one lone inventor. December 17, 1903 and Kitty Hawk do not make them more Apple-like. They make the difference visible. If every source gets stamped into the same mold, the price is trust.

Martin Luther King Jr., the civil-rights speaker at the Lincoln Memorial in Washington, DC on August 28, 1963, marks a limit. The claim is not that a public cause, a company, and a flight story all operate in the same way. It is that belief can attract people before a policy list when the setting supports that read. The Lincoln Memorial tells you where not to stretch.

Your own moment can be smaller. When a project update opens with the thing you shipped, ask what reason that thing is meant to show. When a method line sounds neat, ask what belief gives it a job. When a detail feels useful only because it is sharp, ask whose story it belongs to.

The cost of skipping this step is plain. A product has to act like a cause. A place becomes a borrowed badge. A hard fact turns into a stamp. Then the message may sound firm while every unowned promise costs a week of pretending.

Use the order as a question, not a chant. What belief comes first? What action makes it visible? What visible thing can pay the price? If you cannot answer, do less. Move the reason, keep the detail home, and let the visible act earn its place.

## Key takeaway
Use the Golden Circle as an order you can inspect: put WHY before HOW, let WHAT show the reason, and keep every hard detail with its own source.

## Try this now
When a message starts with what you made, move 1 reason above it in 5 minutes and cut 1 line no behavior can prove.

## Examples
### Example 1: Sylvia Prices The Apple Ledger
Why does a pitch with company facts still feel weak? Because founder Sylvia has four true pieces and no priced reason while the dashboard light blinks. Before: Apple Inc., 1976, Cupertino, Macintosh. After: the same four pieces sit under one challenger belief. The line is clearer, but the open risk remains: behavior still has to prove it.

What to do: Sylvia moves the reason above the four company details, then deletes any product claim that cannot point to visible behavior.

Why it matters: Sylvia leaves with a measured delta, not a finished win: four loose facts become one inspectable claim, and the unpaid price is now in view.

### Example 2: Yann Refuses The Stamp
Coach Yann states the standard: "One visible fact earns one reason." Sylvia wants the flight story to mirror Apple with a neat year-and-place shell. Yann keeps Orville and Wilbur Wright tied to Kitty Hawk on December 17, 1903. "Less tidy," Sylvia says. "More honest," Yann answers. The move works, and the cost is fewer easy parallels.

What to do: Yann keeps the Wright facts in their own lane and says what changed between the two stories.

Why it matters: Yann trades neatness for trust. The second source now compares instead of copying Apple Inc.

### Example 3: Jerome Catches The Famous Close
Community coach Jerome is already cutting the policy list when the microphone hums. The demand had passed: belief before visible list. He is about to miss it by treating Martin Luther King Jr., who spoke at the Lincoln Memorial in Washington, DC on August 28, 1963, as a famous close. Jerome catches it late and moves the belief line above the items.

What to do: Jerome tells you: move belief before the list, keep the public-cause setting intact, and cut any line that makes fame do the work.

Why it matters: Jerome warns you: skip the order and a cause shrinks into bullets; keep belief first and the visible list has a job.

### Example 4: Brock Repairs The Cupertino Drift
At the follow-up call, message coach Brock names the miss after it has already played out. A true Apple detail, Cupertino, had been moved into another paragraph because a real place felt strong. The after was worse than the before: one true detail made the other story look fake. Brock moves the place detail back, then leaves the open question of which behavior will prove the reason.

What to do: Brock names the skipped cost first: move Cupertino back to Apple Inc., then use only facts the current source can prove.

Why it matters: Brock keeps the cost plain. A true fact in the wrong story weakens both stories and makes the reader doubt the claim.

## Review cards
Card 1 — Front: What is the first move when a message opens with WHAT?
          Back: Move WHY above the thing made. HOW and WHAT can help only after the reason gives them a job.

Card 2 — Front: What job does HOW do in the Golden Circle?
          Back: HOW names the way a reason becomes visible. It is not decoration; it is the bridge between belief and the thing people can inspect.

Card 3 — Front: How should you use a sharp detail like Cupertino?
          Back: Keep it inside the source that can prove it. A true detail moved for force becomes false grounding.

Card 4 — Front: When is the full WHY-first order too much?
          Back: When the reason is already shared and the person only needs the visible action. Then give the action plainly and save the order for real confusion.

Card 5 — Front: What does the Wright brothers source add after Apple?
          Back: It lets you compare. Apple can show the idea, but Kitty Hawk on December 17, 1903 helps reveal what repeats in a new setting.

Card 6 — Front: What keeps purpose advice from floating?
          Back: Named sources and visible behavior. Without Apple Inc., the Wright brothers, and Martin Luther King Jr., the lesson becomes broad purpose talk.

## Implementation plan
Title: Move The Reason Up
Core skill: Use the Golden Circle as an order for one live message. Put the reason first, name the way it shows up, and let the visible thing carry proof. Keep hard details with the source that can prove them.
If-then 1: [Feature opener] If your first line names the visible thing, then move one reason above it.
If-then 2: [Hard detail drift] If a true detail sounds useful outside its source, then move it back.
If-then 3: [Shared reason] If the reason is already shared, then give the needed WHAT first.
24-hour challenge: Teach one person for 10 minutes: move WHY ahead of HOW and WHAT in your own words, then ask for one behavior that would prove the promise.
Weekly practice: Each week, measure one number: how many live messages began with a reason before method or visible proof. Move the weakest miss and count again.
