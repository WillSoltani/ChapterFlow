# Carrots and Sticks

## Hook
A discount can move the sale and still fail to win the buyer.

## Fast read
A discount can move the sale and still fail to win the buyer. General Motors makes that claim visible through the 2005 employee discount. The discount could move volume, but volume does not prove preference. Read Price before Promotion: first name the cut, then ask what the public offer made easy. If you reverse the order, the offer gets asked to prove a reason it cannot prove alone. What comes back after the cut is gone? If nothing does, the carrot bought motion, not loyalty.

## Deep read
The mechanism is simple: the push and the reason owe you different things. Price is the cost or cut the buyer sees. Promotion is the wrapper that makes that cut hard to miss. Put Price first and you can ask whether the buyer wanted the thing, wanted the deal, feared missing out, followed people they knew, chased a better self, or liked the newness. Put Promotion first and all those causes blur into one happy line. The sale happened. So what?

General Motors keeps the lesson visible. In 2005, the Detroit employee discount could explain a lift without proving preference. The loop that closes is plain: after the discount ends, what action should still show up, and what reason would carry it? The concrete price of a borrowed spike is a stale reason and another push next week.

MCI (the long-distance calling company behind the 1991 Friends & Family program) changes the pressure. Friends & Family worked through Peer pressure: the pull of people you know. That pull can help. It can lower the risk of saying yes. But it does not prove the same thing as belief. Put it beside General Motors and the flatter read gets harder to keep: action moved before durable preference was clear.

TiVo (the 1999 digital video recorder service built around time shifting) changes the error. Newness can make people look, and a digital video recorder can sound smart, but Novelty is not yet a buying reason. You are not hunting for a noble story behind every response. You are sorting the push from the reason, then asking whether the action comes back without being dragged.

## Full read
TiVo is where the idea stops being a complaint about discounts. Its service made time shifting visible, so people could see a new way to watch. New can be real value. It can also steal the whole conversation. If the buying reason stays unclear, Novelty has done its job too well: it has gathered attention and hidden the missing why.

That is why the list matters. Price changes the cost. Promotion makes the offer visible. Fear says the cost of not acting is high. Aspiration promises a better version of the buyer. Peer pressure borrows force from other people. Novelty wins attention because it feels fresh. These are not one word with six costumes. Once their jobs are separate, the diagnosis gets simple enough to use at a desk.

General Motors makes the first claim inspectable. Do not say, "people bought, so they preferred us." Say what the employee discount did, then ask what stayed when that move no longer carried the sale. MCI then asks a sharper social question. Do not say, "people joined because the brand was stronger." Say Friends & Family used the pull of known people in long-distance calling, then ask what remained after that pull had done its job. TiVo changes the risk again. Do not say, "people noticed it, so they were ready." Say the digital video recorder made time shifting novel, then ask whether the reason to buy had become plain.

You can run the same loop without a title. If you write an offer, request, or small launch, cross out the carrot before you trust the response. What would still move if the offer were gone? A clear answer means the push may help. A vague answer means the push is carrying too much. Cut the perk before it writes the reason for you.

The order is the quiet guardrail. Price before Promotion means the premise has to stand before the tactic gets credit. Fear before the urgent ask means the real risk must be named before pressure is used. Peer pressure before referral copy means the social pull must be marked as social pull, not confused with belief. This does not make carrots and sticks useless. It makes them honest.

Limits matter. Sometimes a push is the right tool when the task is openly short term. The move fails when you treat every prompt as shallow or every buyer as manipulated. Do the opposite when the task is only to move a near-term action and nobody is pretending it proves loyalty. Just keep the receipt: name the push, name the behavior it bought, and do not call it preference until the behavior returns without the push.

## Key takeaway
When a spike comes from Price, Promotion, Fear, Aspiration, Peer pressure, or Novelty, treat it as borrowed until people act again without that push.

## Try this now
Cross out one perk in a live offer for 10 minutes; write the behavior that must come back when the perk is gone.

## Examples
### Example 1: The Late Catch
General Motors reaches the return point in 2005, and the sales lead's easy read is preference: the Detroit employee discount has moved volume. The miss is almost caught too late. The count can show a sales lift, but it cannot show what buyers do once the cut stops carrying the offer. The late catch is to name Price before Promotion gets credit.

What to do: When skipping this would let borrowed volume stand in for loyalty, first state what the employee discount bought and what buyer action still has to return.

Why it matters: If you skip it, another push has to make demand look healthy. The sale rises, but the reason stays unproved.

### Example 2: The Unmet Mark
After the offer is over, imagine analyst William tracing a referral drive against MCI's 1991 Friends & Family pattern in long-distance calling. One blue pencil line marks the gap. Known people helped the yes come sooner, but William still cannot say who would choose the service after that social pull was gone.

What to do: After the response slows, name why the pull worked: known people lowered the risk of yes. Then mark the behavior that has not come back yet.

Why it matters: While that mark stays open, the response is useful but borrowed. Calling it belief would hide the Peer pressure that did the early job.

### Example 3: The Downstream Yes
Why would a customer feel a long-distance calling offer before weighing it? Imagine partner Noah downstream of an MCI-style Friends & Family ask in 1991. The pressure reaches him through people tied to the choice. He records the response as social pull, not belief. The move works, but he pays the cost of making a smaller claim.

What to do: When the first yes arrives through someone familiar, count the social pull before you count preference; the order keeps the result honest.

Why it matters: Because the nudge can be useful and still borrowed, you keep the gain without pretending the deeper reason has been earned.

### Example 4: The Novelty Miss
TiVo's product lead signal appears before anyone can celebrate adoption: in 1999, the digital video recorder makes time shifting sound new. That one device is the detail. If newness carries the story, the miss lands when attention gets treated as a buying reason. Novelty worked, but the need stayed unclear.

What to do: When newness gets the first yes, write one line that names what people noticed and one line that names why they would buy after the surprise fades.

Why it matters: Before attention counts, ask what it settles. TiVo shows the miss: a product can seem fresh while the buying reason stays unspoken.

### Example 5: The Perk Standard
The sales manager's standard is stated inside the General Motors story: do not call the 2005 employee discount preference until action returns without the cut. That demand changes the read. The promotion may have moved volume in Detroit, but the open item is still plain: show the buyer's reason after Price no longer explains the sale.

What to do: When the standard is stated, write the action that must return without the perk. The offer can help, but it does not get to be the reason.

Why it matters: If the perk starts to explain the whole ask, cross it out. Demand proof from the offer before you trust the response.

## Implementation plan
Title: Name the Push First
Core skill: Name the push before you trust the response. Price, Promotion, Fear, Aspiration, Peer pressure, and Novelty can help move action. They cannot write the reason for you. The loop closes only when the action returns without the push.
If-then 1: [A price cut starts to explain the whole offer.] If the price cut starts carrying the sale, then say aloud: "This is the push, not the reason; the reason has to stand after it ends."
If-then 2: [A new feature gets more attention than the buying reason.] If newness is doing most of the carrying, then say aloud: "Attention is the signal; the reason still has to earn the repeat."
If-then 3: [An outside fact makes your offer sound stronger.] If an outside fact starts carrying your offer, then say aloud: "My offer needs its own returning action before I trust the response."
24-hour challenge: Audit one existing artifact for 10 minutes: cross out every perk, then write the behavior that must return without it.
Weekly practice: When a response jumps after a push, observe the first return window; note the pull used, the behavior it bought, and what came back without it.
