# Start with WHY, But Know HOW

## Hook
1975 ties Microsoft to Bill Gates and Paul Allen: WHY needs HOW that can carry it.

## Fast read
1975 is the first hard mark. Bill Gates and Paul Allen founded Microsoft in 1975, and that pair gives the idea a place you can inspect. The story is not here so you can admire founders. It is here so you can ask whether belief has a working partner.

The local order is plain. WHY-type names the reason. HOW-type shows how the reason moves. The WHAT output is what you can see once the method has run. Put method first and it may serve the wrong reason. Keep reason alone and the promise feels good before it can stand.

What happens when you say the reason but cannot name the path? You get heat, not weight. The sequence uses Microsoft first, then Walt Disney with Roy O. Disney (Disney Brothers Studio, 1923), then Steve Jobs with Steve Wozniak (Apple I, 1976), to show the same strain without turning the idea into a loose slogan.

## Deep read
The sequence matters because the first job frames the second one. A reason tells the method what it is for, and the method gives the reason a way to act. The order matters. If a team names the method first, the visible activity can carry a premise no one has checked.

The Microsoft founders are not background color here. Their Microsoft founding year is 1975. That hard mark keeps the lesson close to behavior: vision-plus-implementation is not a mood, it is a pair doing separate jobs in the same promise.

Ask what the promise must pass through. That question keeps WHY from floating above the actual route. If a bicycle shop says it exists to keep riders moving, the next useful ask is not for warmer words. It is for the method that will carry the promise and the output a rider could see.

Walt Disney and Roy O. Disney, who began Disney Brothers Studio in 1923, add pressure to the reading. Walt gives the creative pull; Roy gives the financial HOW. Now the Microsoft pair is not alone. A second pair with a new year and a new venue lets you compare the mechanism instead of treating one founding story as enough.

That comparison costs comfort. A single story can feel clean. A second one asks what truly repeats: a clear reason, a capable method, and an output that shows the fit was more than talk. It also asks what does not repeat. The year changes. The venue changes. The roles are not interchangeable.

HOW does not shrink WHY; it keeps WHY from floating. The method is not the dull afterword to the noble part. It is the route by which the noble part reaches anyone else. Without that route, the sentence may sound high-minded while the promise remains underbuilt.

You can use this without rank. When a peer makes a strong claim, ask who owes what by when, and what result would show the method held. If no one can answer, lower the claim. That is not cynicism. It is respect for the promise.

## Full read
Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak, founders in 1976 around the Apple I, give the outer edge of the reading. Their pair shows belief needing engineering expression. It does not let you paste one founder pattern over every group with a dreamer and a maker.

That matters because the easiest misread is generous. You hear a strong WHY, see someone who can build, and decide the partnership is proven. It is not. The third pair only helps if it makes the scope clearer. 1976 marks a place where the reading stops as well as starts.

Keep the three jobs apart. WHY-type names what the choice is for. HOW-type names the way it can be done. WHAT output names the visible result after the method has carried the reason. Blend them and the early alarm goes quiet.

A short promise can hide the blend. Suppose a team says, "We help riders move faster," and then lists tune-ups, scheduling, and follow-up as if the list itself settled the reason. That is method without a framed WHY. Suppose the same team says the reason beautifully but never says who will do the tune-ups. That is WHY without a carrier.

The founder pairs help because they keep the roles concrete. The Microsoft pair makes 1975 inspectable. The Disney Brothers Studio pair makes 1923 inspectable. Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak make Apple I expression inspectable in 1976. Each pair gives the reader a hard mark, not a cloud.

Partnership is belief tied to a method that can carry it. That definition is stricter than a warm story about two people who fit. It asks the WHY side to name the reason, the HOW side to name the route, and both sides to accept the output that follows.

The same move works at desk scale. Before you rely on a promise, ask who carries the method and what visible result should follow. You do not need the title to ask. You only need enough care to keep a stirring sentence from outrunning the path beneath it.

There is a cost. You may have to cut a claim that sounded strong. You may have to say that one famous pair illustrates, while comparison shows whether the mechanism repeats. You may have to stop at the Apple I instead of stretching the pair across every attractive organization.

The payoff is a smaller promise with a cleaner path. A reason starts the work, a method carries it, and an output lets the reader see whether the fit held. That is why the second pair matters. It lowers the chance that the first pair was just a story you liked.

This move does not rescue a false reason. It does not make a weak method strong because the WHY sounds good. It does not turn three founder pairs into a license for generic advice. When the reason is false, reject it. When the method is missing, build it before trust is asked for. When the output cannot be seen, narrow the promise until it can be judged.

## Key takeaway
Begin with the reason, then ask which method can carry it and what visible result should follow. The founder pairs matter because each makes that fit inspectable.

## Try this now
Ask one live promise for 10 minutes: what reason starts it, what method carries it, and what visible output would make it real.

## Examples
### Example 1: The Year Moves Back
Which year is carrying the claim? Imagine Anais at the final approval check for a bicycle-shop promise. The draft has 1975 under Walt Disney and Roy O. Disney to make the comparison feel firm. The miss is caught just before send. Before: a true year props up the wrong pair. After: 1975 stays with Bill Gates and Paul Allen, who founded Microsoft in 1975, while Walt and Roy keep 1923.

What to do: When a hard year slides toward a smoother sentence, Anais moves it back to the pair that earned it before the promise leaves the shop.

Why it matters: If the year stays in the wrong place, the claim feels grounded while its support is false. The late catch saves the comparison from borrowed weight.

### Example 2: The Partner Fades
Suppose software lead Brandon, at a software vendor after the Monday buyer call, traces a paused order. The pitch highlighted Gates and Microsoft, but Paul Allen had become a courtesy mention. The consequence is not fixed yet: the buyer still waits, because the reason sounds vivid while the method side of the pair has not been named.

What to do: After Paul Allen fades, restore the method side before the new note goes out. Name the output that would show Microsoft is evidence, not decor.

Why it matters: Once one partner becomes a face and the other a footnote, trust has nowhere to land. The stalled order is the cost.

### Example 3: The Second Pair Costs
Outside the room, Beau first feels the cut: the sponsor will fund less after the Microsoft-only pitch fails. He adds Walt Disney and Roy O. Disney, who began Disney Brothers Studio in 1923, as a strict comparison. The red budget line is the cost. Creative pull now has to meet financial HOW, and one feature is removed.

What to do: When one pair feels enough, compare it with Walt and Roy. Ask what changed, what stayed, and what method carried the reason.

Why it matters: When the cut hurts, keep it visible. You are not collecting a warmer founder tale; you are testing whether the fit survives cost.

### Example 4: The Edge Stops Stretching
Before anyone flags it, Charles spots the early signal: Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak, founders in 1976 around the Apple I, are being used to bless every belief-plus-maker pair. The miss is barely caught. One broad claim is cut, and the paragraph now says only what this pair can support: belief needs engineering expression.

What to do: Skipping the edge would make Apple I bless too much; Charles asks where 1976 makes the reading stop before the claim gets wider.

Why it matters: Letting the stretch pass costs the reader a limit. A useful pair becomes a stamp for promises whose facts never earned it.

### Example 5: The Promise Leaves Empty
"Name the method before we ask riders to trust this," service lead Anais says at 8 p.m. in the shop's long text thread. Imagine the thread using Bill Gates and Paul Allen as its model: they founded Microsoft in 1975, so belief has to meet method. The phone buzzes. Charles sends only the WHY, a promise to keep riders moving. A rider asks when the result will be visible, and no one can answer.

What to do: When the standard works, it works because the ask ties belief to method. Ask for HOW and WHAT output before the promise leaves.

Why it matters: When the ask is skipped, the miss lands with the rider. A strong reason went out without the method that would make it true.

## Implementation plan
Title: Ask How It Carries
Core skill: Use one small audit before you trust a promise. Name the reason, ask which method can carry it, and name the visible output that should follow. If the method is missing or the output cannot be seen, narrow the promise until it can be judged.
If-then 1: [When a method arrives before the reason] If HOW shows up first, then say aloud: "What reason is this method meant to carry?"
If-then 2: [When a promise has no visible output] If the result is not named, then say aloud: "What output would let us judge this promise?"
If-then 3: [When a famous pair starts to stretch] If the story is doing too much, then say aloud: "Which hard mark shows where this stops?"
24-hour challenge: Write one exact sentence to ask today: "Can I ask what method will carry this promise, and what visible output will show it held?"
Weekly practice: Every Friday, spend 15 minutes observing one promise you rely on; then note the reason, the method, the visible output, and any hard mark that kept the claim honest.
