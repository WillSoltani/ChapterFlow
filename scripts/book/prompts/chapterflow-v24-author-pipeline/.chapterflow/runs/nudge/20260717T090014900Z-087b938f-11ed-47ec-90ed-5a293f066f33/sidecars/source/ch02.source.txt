Chapter 2 focus: Preferences change between calm planning and immediate temptation. Commitment devices, mental accounts, and environmental controls let a farsighted Planner protect decisions from a myopic Doer.

Core claim: Self-control improves when people design constraints while calm instead of relying on willpower while aroused.

Central concept (Planner–Doer conflict):
  One set of preferences favors future welfare while another responds strongly to immediate rewards and arousal.
  Why it matters: A decision that seems inconsistent can be understood and improved by moving control to the earlier planning moment.

Key claims:
- Dynamic inconsistency lets a person prefer restraint in advance but indulgence at the moment of choice.
- Cold states underestimate how strongly hot states alter desire.
- Commitment devices intentionally narrow future options.
- Mindless consumption responds to portion and container cues.
- Mental accounting assigns money to purposes even though dollars are interchangeable.
- External controls can support the Planner without eliminating all choice.

Named examples:
- George Loewenstein / hot–cold empathy gap: Loewenstein formalized how people in a calm state underpredict the force of hunger, arousal, craving, or pain. The 1996 analysis explains why advance plans fail precisely when the tempting state arrives. (teaches: Forecasting one's future preferences is state-dependent.)
- Richard Thaler / dinner-party cashews: Before dinner, guests kept eating a bowl of cashews and thanked Thaler when he removed it. They preferred having the option beforehand yet felt better when the immediate temptation disappeared. (teaches: More available options can reduce welfare when present desire conflicts with a prior plan.)
- Christmas Club savings accounts: Depositors historically accepted low-interest accounts that restricted withdrawals until the holiday season. The restriction made the money harder to spend early and converted a future intention into a commitment. (teaches: People willingly pay for constraints that protect earmarked savings.)

Hard edge / typical misreading:
  Commitment is not a license for someone else to impose arbitrary restrictions. Its value comes from a person's own advance judgment about a predictable future conflict, with the constraint calibrated to that conflict.

Paraphrase notes:
A dinner-party bowl of cashews exposes a flaw in the claim that more choice can never hurt. Guests kept eating even though dinner was coming, then thanked Richard Thaler for removing the bowl. Their calm preference for preserving appetite lost to the food directly in front of them. George Loewenstein's 1996 hot–cold empathy-gap account explains the forecasting failure: a person can know that hunger, craving, pain, or arousal will matter while still underestimating its intensity from a calm state. The Planner–Doer model turns that temporal conflict into a design problem. The Planner values future welfare; the Doer reacts to what is vivid now. A good commitment device lets the Planner act first. Ulysses asks to be tied to the mast before hearing the Sirens, while Christmas Club depositors accept lower interest and restricted withdrawals so holiday money survives earlier spending opportunities. Those arrangements are useful precisely because unrestricted choice would not be neutral between the two selves. Mental accounting offers a softer boundary by labeling dollars for particular purposes. A Cochrane review of 72 randomized studies found moderate-quality evidence that larger portions, packages, and tableware increase food consumption. The best-known Brian Wansink cases nevertheless suffered serious credibility and replication failures, so they should not be used as the proof of that broader effect. Predict the hot state, choose the smallest effective barrier while calm, and distinguish self-chosen protection from control imposed for someone else's ends. Grounding anchors: George Loewenstein's 1996 analysis; cold-state forecasts underweight hot-state visceral forces; bowl of cashews removed before dinner; guests expressed relief after removal; withdrawals restricted until the holiday period; lower interest than ordinary savings accounts.
