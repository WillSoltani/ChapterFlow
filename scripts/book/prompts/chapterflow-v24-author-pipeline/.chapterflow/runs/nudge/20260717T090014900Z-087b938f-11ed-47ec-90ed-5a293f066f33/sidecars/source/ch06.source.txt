Chapter 6 focus: Curation and enjoyment can make a beneficial action attractive rather than merely easy. Smaller choice environments compete through selection and serendipity, while playful rewards can enlist voluntary participation.

Core claim: A well-designed experience can motivate action by making the path enjoyable and meaningfully curated.

Central concept (fun and curation):
  Curation reduces a large universe to a purposeful selection, while fun supplies an intrinsic or symbolic reward for participation.
  Why it matters: People are more likely to engage when design offers discovery, pleasure, or a memorable reward instead of another obligation.

Key claims:
- Small retailers can compete with scale by curating what appears and how it is encountered.
- Navigation solves search while curation can create surprise.
- Play can reframe a chore as a scarce privilege.
- Receipt lotteries make customers want the document needed for tax enforcement.
- Symbolic or guilt-free rewards may motivate differently from cash.
- Fun must serve the target behavior rather than distract from it.

Named examples:
- Taiwan Uniform Invoice Lottery: Taiwan attaches lottery numbers to standardized sales invoices and draws winning numbers on a regular schedule. Customers gain a reason to request receipts, which makes retail transactions harder to conceal from the tax authority. (teaches: A playful reward can align the customer's action with tax reporting.)
- Beijing tax authority / restaurant receipt lottery: Beijing's tax authority tested scratch-off prizes printed on restaurant invoices. Diners who wanted a chance to win asked for the official receipt, increasing the likelihood that the sale entered the tax system. (teaches: The target behavior becomes self-reinforcing when compliance carries immediate fun.)
- Hyde Park bookstores and Concord Bookshop versus Amazon: The Final Edition contrasts Amazon's searchable catalog with bookstores in the University of Chicago's Hyde Park neighborhood and the Concord Bookshop in Massachusetts. Staff judgment, tables, and shelf placement can create discovery even with a smaller inventory. (teaches: Curation and navigation solve different problems in a large choice universe.)

Hard edge / typical misreading:
  Fun is not a substitute for truth or a justification for gambling-like manipulation. The reward should make a beneficial action inviting without hiding odds, costs, or the underlying purpose.

Paraphrase notes:
Ease is only part of good design. A large online catalog and an independent bookstore illustrate two different services. Amazon-scale navigation helps a shopper find a known title among millions, while a small shop uses staff judgment, table placement, and a constrained inventory to create surprise and trust. Curation makes selection itself informative. Fun supplies a second lever. In Mark Twain's fence scene, work becomes desirable when Tom Sawyer performs it as a scarce privilege rather than an assigned chore. Taiwan's Uniform Invoice Lottery turns that insight into tax administration: lottery numbers on official receipts give customers a reason to request and retain the document, making hidden sales harder. Chinese local authorities similarly placed scratch-off prizes on restaurant invoices so diners would pull transactions into the reporting system. The prize does not improve the tax form or increase a fine; it changes the experience of asking for the receipt. Loyalty programs use related mechanisms through stamps, miles, progress, and guilt-free treats. These tools demand ethical boundaries. Curation must express credible judgment rather than arbitrary exclusion, and play must not conceal odds or costs. The design question is whether enjoyment, discovery, or symbolic progress supports an action people have reason to value. When it does, the beneficial path can feel like an opportunity rather than another instruction. Grounding anchors: Taiwan Ministry of Finance program introduced on January 1, 1951; lottery numbers printed on uniform sales invoices; customers must retain receipts to claim prizes; Beijing trial launched August 1, 2002 in 11 districts and counties; official receipt request exposes the transaction to tax reporting; Amazon catalog relies on navigation tools; bookstores in the University of Chicago's Hyde Park neighborhood; Concord Bookshop in Massachusetts uses curated selection.
