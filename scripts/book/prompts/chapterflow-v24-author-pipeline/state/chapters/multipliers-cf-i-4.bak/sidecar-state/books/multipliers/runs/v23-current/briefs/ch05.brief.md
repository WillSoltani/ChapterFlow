## VARIETY (dealt — do NOT default to the house pattern)
- CHAPTER ARCHITECTURE (historical-narrative): Tell ONE case as a chronological story with real dates, actors, and stakes. No invented stand-in characters and no second/third mirror case — the single narrative carries it. This is the chapter's overall SHAPE — the sections below dress it; they do not override it.
- THIS CHAPTER'S JOB: Because the 1986 date fixes the cautionary decision case in a specific public event — serve it through a DIFFERENT facet per example. NOT THIS CHAPTER: prev ch owns "Because the public date and venue make the Moon goal a verifiab…"; next ch owns "Because a 2009 written artifact makes the case checkable rather…" — mention only in passing, never re-teach.
- OPENER: Open the hook with a concrete NUMBER or measured result, then the stakes. Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as replace-one-Y — have the reader SWAP one existing habit or artifact for the taught move — 'Replace one <thing> with …' Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow around one measurement: capture a single concrete number.
- EXAMPLE SCENES: your 5 examples must cover all three dealt lenses below; at most 2 examples may be a person-handling-a-document scene.
    * dialogue-beat: a short spoken exchange carries the turn. Quoted lines ONLY from your invented cast; real source people are paraphrased, never given invented quotes.
    * before-after-ledger: show the same numbers or facts BEFORE and AFTER the move — the delta is the story.
    * postmortem: a past-tense autopsy: the decision already played out; trace why it went the way it went.
- EXAMPLE PLAN: write EXACTLY 5 examples. Each slot's dealt arc below (entry → resolution → field register). Dramatize ONE beat of the framework per example — the other beats get at most one clause; at most 2 examples may walk the full loop explicitly. An example that scenes one of YOUR owned cases must enter it at a DIFFERENT point than the breakdown's telling. Slots marked +anchor get exactly ONE concrete physical/sensory detail; the others get none (props on every scene read as scaffold).
    * ex01: open on the early signal nobody has flagged yet → the move works — but earn it, show the cost paid → open with the question the reader would ask, answer it
    * ex02: open at the moment the demand/standard is stated → headed for a miss, caught late — barely → make both fields the tersest in the chapter — no preamble → +anchor
    * ex03: open mid-action, demand already in the past — no setup → the move half-works; name what stayed broken → whatToDo/whyItMatters as direct commands to the reader → +anchor
    * ex04: open AT the check-in/return moment itself → the move works — but earn it, show the cost paid → lead whatToDo/whyItMatters with what skipping this costs
    * ex05: open after it's over, tracing back what happened → headed for a miss, caught late — barely → lead with WHY it works, then the move → +anchor
- LEAD THREAD: Janelle carries this chapter — the fastRead and at least 2 examples follow Janelle's situation; other cast support. Introduce invented people role-BEFORE-name in varied wording (never one fixed "call her X" phrase).
- QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — ordering-priority (several valid actions; ask which comes FIRST and why); transfer-new-domain (the chapter's move in a non-business setting; ask what maps); failure-postmortem (it already failed; ask which earlier step was the cause); cold-diagnosis (present symptoms; ask what is actually wrong). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — over-correction (overshoots into the opposite failure); borrowed-authority (outsources the judgment to a rank, brand, or precedent); wrong-target (the right move aimed at the wrong person/thing); wrong-timing (the right move too early or too late). Different modes within one question; never a generic bad answer.
- QUESTION ORDER: do not quiz facts in packet order — cover them in this dealt order: 9, 6, 3, 4, 5, 2, 7, 8, 1.
- PRACTICE SLOT SHAPES: the four practice surfaces must NOT share one skeleton — tryThisNow: dealt above; weekly practice: "if-then-trigger" structure; if-then contexts: "two-step-sequence" structure. Never repeat one prompt style (read-aloud, touch-the-object) across surfaces, and keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: your 3 lines use these dealt shapes, one each — redefinition (redefine a familiar word on the chapter's terms); cost-statement (name the concrete price of the default behavior); pointed-question (a question sharp enough to reread).
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: put the limits/when-NOT-to paragraph EARLY in the deep read, as an aside — do not save it for the ending
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: ground anchors with a short appositive — 'Session C, GE's talent review, …'. Every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences.
- PRACTICE VERB: build the physical actions in tryThisNow and the 24-hour challenge around "move" — not "touch" or "open" (other chapters own other verbs; a shared verb becomes a book-wide tic).
- FRAMEWORK IDIOM: every chapter of this book teaches the same framework, and a book-level reader FAILED the last draft because all chapters verbalized it identically. When the framework recurs beyond your noun budget, speak it through YOUR dealt idiom families — question-speak (carry it as the question someone asks — 'who checks, and when?'); contrast-speak (set it against its counterfeit with a chapter-native antithesis) — other chapters own other registers. Never invent a formal synonym; re-ground in your cases instead.
- EXAMPLE SHELL REGISTER: open your examples' whatToDo/whyItMatters fields in the "actor-first" register — open on the named person ('Priya starts by…'). No two of your examples may open BOTH fields the same way, and never reuse another chapter's fixed formula (book readers see all chapters side by side).
- FRAMEWORK VOCABULARY BUDGET: the book's framework nouns are: leader, rather, assuming, thinking, team, concrete. Every chapter leans on them, so they saturate book-wide. Your budget: the single noun you need most ≤15 uses in this chapter; every other listed noun ≤10. The same budget applies to any generic role-noun you find yourself repeating (owner, leader, manager) even if unlisted — name people by their case names and titles instead. When the budget is spent, use YOUR cases' concrete referents — the person's name, the artifact, the number — never an invented formal synonym ("the accountable party" is worse than the budget).
- SCENE-FURNITURE BUDGET (same rule, fixed list): review, meeting, room, plan, work, check, test, date, news, next — each ≤10 uses in this chapter. Every chapter of every business book reaches for these; at 20+ uses each they make nine chapters read as one. When you feel the next "the review meeting" or "the room goes quiet" coming, write the specific thing instead: the named meeting, the artifact on the table, the person who speaks.
