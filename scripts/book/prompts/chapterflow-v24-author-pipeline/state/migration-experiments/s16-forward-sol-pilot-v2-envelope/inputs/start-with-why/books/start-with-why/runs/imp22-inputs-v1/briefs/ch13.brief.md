- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as attach-to-existing-routine — BOLT the action onto a routine already in the reader's day — name the routine. Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow around one measurement: capture a single concrete number.
- EXAMPLES: write EXACTLY 6 examples. - LEAD THREAD: this chapter runs on YOUR case "Simon Sinek career background" — the fastRead and at least 2 examples live inside that case's real story (its real actors, numbers, dates; packet-attested actions only, never invented quotes). - QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — failure-postmortem (it already failed; ask which earlier step was the cause); cold-diagnosis (present symptoms; ask what is actually wrong); choose-next-move (a live scenario; ask for the single best next action); predict-consequence (a choice was just made; ask what happens downstream). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — wrong-target (the right move aimed at the wrong person/thing); wrong-timing (the right move too early or too late); wrong-proof (accepts the wrong evidence as settling it); wrong-scope (applies the move too broadly or too narrowly). - PRACTICE SURFACES: the four practice surfaces (tryThisNow, 24h challenge, weekly practice, if-then contexts) must NOT share one skeleton or repeat one prompt style. Keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: write 3; no two share one grammatical mold — vary the sentence shape, not just the words.
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: let a failing/partial example CARRY the limits — show where the move breaks instead of appending a warning paragraph
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences. Vary the grounding form — one repeated rhythm chapter-wide reads as a stamp.
- PRACTICE & FIELD VARIETY: no two of your examples may open BOTH the whatToDo and whyItMatters fields the same way, and the physical actions in your practice items must not lean on one verb (a shared verb becomes a book-wide tic).
- FRAMEWORK VOCABULARY BUDGET: the book's framework nouns are: case, different, third, language, test, boundary. Every chapter leans on them, so they saturate book-wide.
