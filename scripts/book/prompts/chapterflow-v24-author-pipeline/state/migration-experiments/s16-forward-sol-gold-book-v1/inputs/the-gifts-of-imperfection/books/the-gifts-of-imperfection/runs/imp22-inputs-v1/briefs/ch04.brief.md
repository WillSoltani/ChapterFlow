## VARIETY (dealt — do NOT default to the house pattern)
- CHAPTER ARCHITECTURE (single-deep-case): Build the WHOLE chapter on ONE case, developed end to end — one real situation, followed all the way through, carries everything. This is the chapter's overall SHAPE — the sections below dress it; they do not override it.
- THIS CHAPTER'S JOB: Because Authenticity is a repeated choice, not a trait Isabelle either owns or lacks. The practice… — serve it through a DIFFERENT facet per example. NOT THIS CHAPTER: prev ch owns "Because Shame turns exposure into a verdict on belonging. Embar…"; next ch owns "Because Neff's framework has three parts: warmth toward the sel…" — mention only in passing, never re-teach.
- OPENER: Open the hook with a concrete NUMBER or measured result, then the stakes. Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as replace-one-Y — have the reader SWAP one existing habit or artifact for the taught move — 'Replace one <thing> with …' Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow as observe-then-note: watch one specific thing, then write down what you saw.
- EXAMPLES: write EXACTLY 6 examples. Vary where each enters the material and how it resolves — no two examples share both their entry point and their outcome; at most 2 walk the framework loop end to end; at most 2 may be a person-handling-a-document scene. An example that scenes one of YOUR owned cases must enter it at a DIFFERENT point than the breakdown's telling. At least ONE example must show the move failing or only partially working — real friction, not another frictionless win.
- ANCHORS: give 2-3 examples exactly ONE concrete physical/sensory detail each; the rest get none (props on every scene read as scaffold).
- LEAD THREAD: Blair carries this chapter — the fastRead and at least 2 examples follow Blair's situation; other cast support. This thread is a CONSTRUCTED application: make its non-factual status clear at first entry in natural wording (varied per scene; no single fixed phrase), and never let a later paragraph report the invented events as history. Introduce invented people role-BEFORE-name in varied wording (never one fixed "call her X" phrase).
- QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — failure-postmortem (it already failed; ask which earlier step was the cause); cold-diagnosis (present symptoms; ask what is actually wrong); choose-next-move (a live scenario; ask for the single best next action); predict-consequence (a choice was just made; ask what happens downstream). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — over-correction (overshoots into the opposite failure); borrowed-authority (outsources the judgment to a rank, brand, or precedent); wrong-target (the right move aimed at the wrong person/thing); wrong-timing (the right move too early or too late). Different modes within one question; never a generic bad answer.
- QUESTION ORDER: do not quiz facts in packet order — cover them in this dealt order: 9, 3, 4, 6, 5, 8, 7, 2, 1.
- PRACTICE SURFACES: the four practice surfaces (tryThisNow, 24h challenge, weekly practice, if-then contexts) must NOT share one skeleton or repeat one prompt style. Keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: write 3; no two share one grammatical mold — vary the sentence shape, not just the words.
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: put the limits/when-NOT-to paragraph EARLY in the deep read, as an aside — do not save it for the ending
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences. Vary the grounding form — one repeated rhythm chapter-wide reads as a stamp.
- PRACTICE & FIELD VARIETY: no two of your examples may open BOTH the whatToDo and whyItMatters fields the same way, and the physical actions in your practice items must not lean on one verb (a shared verb becomes a book-wide tic).
- FRAMEWORK VOCABULARY BUDGET: the book's framework nouns are: connection, care, control, move, approval, safer. Every chapter leans on them, so they saturate book-wide. Your budget: the single noun you need most ≤15 uses in this chapter; every other listed noun ≤10. The same budget applies to any generic role-noun you find yourself repeating (owner, leader, manager) even if unlisted — name people by their case names and titles instead. When the budget is spent, use YOUR cases' concrete referents — the person's name, the artifact, the number — never an invented formal synonym ("the accountable party" is worse than the budget).
- SCENE-FURNITURE BUDGET (same rule, fixed list): review, meeting, room, plan, work, check, test, date, news, next — each ≤10 uses in this chapter. Every chapter of every business book reaches for these; at 20+ uses each they make nine chapters read as one. When you feel the next "the review meeting" or "the room goes quiet" coming, write the specific thing instead: the named meeting, the artifact on the table, the person who speaks.
