- OPENER: Open the hook with a concrete NUMBER or measured result, then the stakes. Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as pre-write-the-exact-line — have the reader DRAFT the precise line they will use, before the moment arrives. Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow as a two-step sequence: do A, then do B.
- EXAMPLES: write EXACTLY 4 examples. Vary where each enters the material and how it resolves — no two examples share both their entry point and their outcome; at most 2 walk the framework loop end to end; at most 2 may be a person-handling-a-document scene. An example that scenes one of YOUR owned cases must enter it at a DIFFERENT point than the breakdown's telling.
- ANCHORS: give 2-3 examples exactly ONE concrete physical/sensory detail each; the rest get none (props on every scene read as scaffold).
- LEAD THREAD: this chapter runs on YOUR case "Recipe Card Under Thread" — the fastRead and at least 2 examples live inside that case's real story (its real actors, numbers, dates; packet-attested actions only, never invented quotes). - QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — transfer-new-domain (the chapter's move in a non-business setting; ask what maps); failure-postmortem (it already failed; ask which earlier step was the cause); cold-diagnosis (present symptoms; ask what is actually wrong); choose-next-move (a live scenario; ask for the single best next action). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — right-move-wrong-trigger (correct action fired by the wrong signal); over-correction (overshoots into the opposite failure); borrowed-authority (outsources the judgment to a rank, brand, or precedent); wrong-target (the right move aimed at the wrong person/thing). Different modes within one question; never a generic bad answer.
- QUESTION ORDER: do not quiz facts in packet order — cover them in this dealt order: 2, 6, 1, 7, 9, 5, 8, 3, 4.
- PRACTICE SURFACES: the four practice surfaces (tryThisNow, 24h challenge, weekly practice, if-then contexts) must NOT share one skeleton or repeat one prompt style. Keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: write 3; no two share one grammatical mold — vary the sentence shape, not just the words.
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: close the full read with the limits paragraph (the classic slot — fine here, other chapters own other slots)
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences. Vary the grounding form — one repeated rhythm chapter-wide reads as a stamp.
- PRACTICE & FIELD VARIETY: no two of your examples may open BOTH the whatToDo and whyItMatters fields the same way, and the physical actions in your practice items must not lean on one verb (a shared verb becomes a book-wide tic).
- FRAMEWORK VOCABULARY BUDGET: the book's framework nouns are: connection, care, control, move, approval, safer. Every chapter leans on them, so they saturate book-wide. Your budget: the single noun you need most ≤15 uses in this chapter; every other listed noun ≤10. When the budget is spent, use YOUR cases' concrete referents — the person's name, the artifact, the number — never an invented formal synonym ("the accountable party" is worse than the budget).
- SCENE-FURNITURE BUDGET (same rule, fixed list): review, meeting, room, plan, work, check, test, date, news, next — each ≤10 uses in this chapter. Every chapter of every business book reaches for these; at 20+ uses each they make nine chapters read as one. When you feel the next "the review meeting" or "the room goes quiet" coming, write the specific thing instead: the named meeting, the artifact on the table, the person who speaks.
