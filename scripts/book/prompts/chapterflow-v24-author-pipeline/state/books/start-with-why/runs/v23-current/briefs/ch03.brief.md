- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as teach-it-to-someone — have the reader TEACH the move to one other person in their own words. Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow as an if-then trigger: 'When X happens, do Y.'
- EXAMPLE SCENES: your 4 examples must cover all three dealt lenses below; at most 2 examples may be a person-handling-a-document scene.

    * before-after-ledger: show the same numbers or facts BEFORE and AFTER the move — the delta is the story.
    * postmortem: a past-tense autopsy: the decision already played out; trace why it went the way it went.

- QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — choose-next-move (a live scenario; ask for the single best next action); predict-consequence (a choice was just made; ask what happens downstream); spot-the-violation (a plausible-looking plan; ask which principle it breaks); best-explanation-why (an outcome happened; ask WHY (mechanism, not recall)). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — wrong-proof (accepts the wrong evidence as settling it); wrong-scope (applies the move too broadly or too narrowly); half-measure (starts the move but stops before the part that matters); right-move-wrong-trigger (correct action fired by the wrong signal). - PRACTICE SLOT SHAPES: the four practice surfaces must NOT share one skeleton — tryThisNow: dealt above; weekly practice: "measure-one-number" structure; if-then contexts: "single-imperative" structure. Never repeat one prompt style (read-aloud, touch-the-object) across surfaces, and keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: your 3 lines use these dealt shapes, one each — imperative (a command short enough to say from memory); reversal (a line that flips the expected direction); redefinition (redefine a familiar word on the chapter's terms).
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: put the limits/when-NOT-to paragraph EARLY in the deep read, as an aside — do not save it for the ending
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: ground anchors with a short appositive — 'Session C, GE's talent review, …'. Every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences.
