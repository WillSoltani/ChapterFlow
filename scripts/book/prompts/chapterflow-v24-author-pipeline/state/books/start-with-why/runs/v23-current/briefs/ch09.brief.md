## VARIETY (dealt — do NOT default to the house pattern)
- OPENER: Open the hook with the chapter's core tension stated flat in two short sentences — no named person, no scene furniture; the second sentence carries the friction ('Everyone agreed. No one knew who would bring back proof.'). Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as timebox-N-minutes — give a named MINUTE budget — 'Spend <N> minutes …' — not a 24-hour window. Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow as an if-then trigger: 'When X happens, do Y.'
- EXAMPLE SCENES: your 4 examples must cover all three dealt lenses below; at most 2 examples may be a person-handling-a-document scene.
    * postmortem: a past-tense autopsy: the decision already played out; trace why it went the way it went.
    * walkthrough: a step-by-step trace of someone applying the move, decision by decision.
    * counterfactual: reason out loud what would have followed WITHOUT the move — framed explicitly as reasoning ('had they not…'), never narrated as events that happened.
- EXAMPLE PLAN: write EXACTLY 4 examples. Each slot's dealt arc below (entry → resolution → field register). Dramatize ONE beat of the framework per example — the other beats get at most one clause; at most 2 examples may walk the full loop explicitly. An example that scenes one of YOUR owned cases must enter it at a DIFFERENT point than the breakdown's telling. Slots marked +anchor get exactly ONE concrete physical/sensory detail; the others get none (props on every scene read as scaffold).
    * ex01: open on the early signal nobody has flagged yet → ends unresolved; the return point is set but not yet met → whatToDo/whyItMatters as direct commands to the reader → +anchor
    * ex02: open at the moment the demand/standard is stated → the move works — but earn it, show the cost paid → lead whatToDo/whyItMatters with what skipping this costs
    * ex03: open mid-action, demand already in the past — no setup → headed for a miss, caught late — barely → lead with WHY it works, then the move → +anchor
    * ex04: open AT the check-in/return moment itself → the move half-works; name what stayed broken → open with the question the reader would ask, answer it
- LEAD THREAD: Vincent carries this chapter — the fastRead and at least 2 examples follow Vincent's situation; other cast support. Introduce invented people role-BEFORE-name in varied wording (never one fixed "call her X" phrase).
- QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — spot-the-violation (a plausible-looking plan; ask which principle it breaks); best-explanation-why (an outcome happened; ask WHY (mechanism, not recall)); ordering-priority (several valid actions; ask which comes FIRST and why); transfer-new-domain (the chapter's move in a non-business setting; ask what maps). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — half-measure (starts the move but stops before the part that matters); right-move-wrong-trigger (correct action fired by the wrong signal); over-correction (overshoots into the opposite failure); borrowed-authority (outsources the judgment to a rank, brand, or precedent). Different modes within one question; never a generic bad answer.
- QUESTION ORDER: do not quiz facts in packet order — cover them in this dealt order: 1, 5, 3, 6, 7, 8, 4, 9, 2.
- PRACTICE SLOT SHAPES: the four practice surfaces must NOT share one skeleton — tryThisNow: dealt above; weekly practice: "measure-one-number" structure; if-then contexts: "single-imperative" structure. Never repeat one prompt style (read-aloud, touch-the-object) across surfaces, and keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: your 3 lines use these dealt shapes, one each — redefinition (redefine a familiar word on the chapter's terms); cost-statement (name the concrete price of the default behavior); pointed-question (a question sharp enough to reread).
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: put the limits/when-NOT-to paragraph EARLY in the deep read, as an aside — do not save it for the ending
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: ground anchors with a short appositive — 'Session C, GE's talent review, …'. Every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences.
- PRACTICE VERB: build the physical actions in tryThisNow and the 24-hour challenge around "circle" — not "touch" or "open" (other chapters own other verbs; a shared verb becomes a book-wide tic).
- FRAMEWORK IDIOM: every chapter of this book teaches the same framework, and a book-level reader FAILED the last draft because all chapters verbalized it identically. When the framework recurs beyond your noun budget, speak it through YOUR dealt idiom families — cost-speak (price the default — 'every unowned promise costs a week of pretending'); question-speak (carry it as the question someone asks — 'who checks, and when?') — other chapters own other registers. Never invent a formal synonym; re-ground in your cases instead.
- EXAMPLE SHELL REGISTER: open your examples' whatToDo/whyItMatters fields in the "actor-first" register — open on the named person ('Priya starts by…'). No two of your examples may open BOTH fields the same way, and never reuse another chapter's fixed formula (book readers see all chapters side by side).
- FRAMEWORK VOCABULARY BUDGET: the book's framework nouns are: case, different, third, language, test, boundary. Every chapter leans on them, so they saturate book-wide. Your budget: the single noun you need most ≤15 uses in this chapter; every other listed noun ≤10. When the budget is spent, use YOUR cases' concrete referents — the person's name, the artifact, the number — never an invented formal synonym ("the accountable party" is worse than the budget).
