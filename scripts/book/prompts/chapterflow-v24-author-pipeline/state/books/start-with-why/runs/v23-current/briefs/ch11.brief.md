## VARIETY (dealt — do NOT default to the house pattern)
- OPENER: Open the hook with a SCENE — a specific person mid-moment, no thesis first. Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as teach-it-to-someone — have the reader TEACH the move to one other person in their own words. Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow as observe-then-note: watch one specific thing, then write down what you saw.
- EXAMPLE SCENES: your 5 examples must cover all three dealt lenses below; at most 2 examples may be a person-handling-a-document scene.
    * dialogue-beat: a short spoken exchange carries the turn. Quoted lines ONLY from your invented cast; real source people are paraphrased, never given invented quotes.
    * before-after-ledger: show the same numbers or facts BEFORE and AFTER the move — the delta is the story.
    * postmortem: a past-tense autopsy: the decision already played out; trace why it went the way it went.
- EXAMPLE PLAN: write EXACTLY 5 examples. Each slot's dealt arc below (entry → resolution → field register). Dramatize ONE beat of the framework per example — the other beats get at most one clause; at most 2 examples may walk the full loop explicitly. An example that scenes one of YOUR owned cases must enter it at a DIFFERENT point than the breakdown's telling. Slots marked +anchor get exactly ONE concrete physical/sensory detail; the others get none (props on every scene read as scaffold).
    * ex01: open on the early signal nobody has flagged yet → headed for a miss, caught late — barely → make both fields the tersest in the chapter — no preamble → +anchor
    * ex02: open at the moment the demand/standard is stated → ends unresolved; the return point is set but not yet met → whatToDo/whyItMatters as direct commands to the reader

- LEAD THREAD: Heath carries this chapter — the fastRead and at least 2 examples follow Heath's situation; other cast support. Introduce invented people role-BEFORE-name in varied wording (never one fixed "call her X" phrase).
- QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — choose-next-move (a live scenario; ask for the single best next action); predict-consequence (a choice was just made; ask what happens downstream); spot-the-violation (a plausible-looking plan; ask which principle it breaks); best-explanation-why (an outcome happened; ask WHY (mechanism, not recall)). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — wrong-proof (accepts the wrong evidence as settling it); wrong-scope (applies the move too broadly or too narrowly); half-measure (starts the move but stops before the part that matters); right-move-wrong-trigger (correct action fired by the wrong signal). Different modes within one question; never a generic bad answer.
- QUESTION ORDER: do not quiz facts in packet order — cover them in this dealt order: 3, 4, 7, 6, 1, 8, 9, 5, 2.
- PRACTICE SLOT SHAPES: the four practice surfaces must NOT share one skeleton — tryThisNow: dealt above; weekly practice: "say-aloud-script" structure; if-then contexts: "measure-one-number" structure. Never repeat one prompt style (read-aloud, touch-the-object) across surfaces, and keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: your 3 lines use these dealt shapes, one each — reversal (a line that flips the expected direction); redefinition (redefine a familiar word on the chapter's terms); cost-statement (name the concrete price of the default behavior).
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: close the full read with the limits paragraph (the classic slot — fine here, other chapters own other slots)
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: ground anchors with a parenthetical era/role — '(GE's 1990s operating review)'. Every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences.
- PRACTICE VERB: build the physical actions in tryThisNow and the 24-hour challenge around "read-aloud" — not "touch" or "open" (other chapters own other verbs; a shared verb becomes a book-wide tic).
- FRAMEWORK IDIOM: every chapter of this book teaches the same framework, and a book-level reader FAILED the last draft because all chapters verbalized it identically. When the framework recurs beyond your noun budget, speak it through YOUR dealt idiom families — mechanism-speak (name what the move DOES — 'the loop that closes', 'the check that fires before the miss'); people-speak (name who owes whom what by when — 'Elena owes Marcus the count on Friday') — other chapters own other registers. Never invent a formal synonym; re-ground in your cases instead.
- EXAMPLE SHELL REGISTER: open your examples' whatToDo/whyItMatters fields in the "cost-first" register — open on what skipping costs ('Skip this and…'). No two of your examples may open BOTH fields the same way, and never reuse another chapter's fixed formula (book readers see all chapters side by side).
- FRAMEWORK VOCABULARY BUDGET: the book's framework nouns are: case, different, third, language, test, boundary. Every chapter leans on them, so they saturate book-wide. Your budget: the single noun you need most ≤15 uses in this chapter; every other listed noun ≤10. The same budget applies to any generic role-noun you find yourself repeating (owner, leader, manager) even if unlisted — name people by their case names and titles instead. When the budget is spent, use YOUR cases' concrete referents — the person's name, the artifact, the number — never an invented formal synonym ("the accountable party" is worse than the budget).
