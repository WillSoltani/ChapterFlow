## VARIETY (dealt — do NOT default to the house pattern)
- OPENER: Open the hook with a QUESTION the reader can't answer yet. Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as audit-one-artifact — have the reader AUDIT one existing artifact for the chapter's pattern. Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow as a two-step sequence: do A, then do B.
- EXAMPLE SCENES: your 6 examples must cover all three dealt lenses below; at most 2 examples may be a person-handling-a-document scene.
    * outsider-witness: the scene through the eyes of someone downstream — a customer, a junior, a partner — who feels the consequences without attending the meeting.
    * numbers-detective: start from one number or fact that does not add up, and trace it back to the cause.
    * prop-tableau: a person mid-moment with one physical artifact (the classic scene — use it, but in AT MOST 2 of your 6 examples).
- EXAMPLE PLAN: write EXACTLY 6 examples. Each slot's dealt arc below (entry → resolution → field register). Dramatize ONE beat of the framework per example — the other beats get at most one clause; at most 2 examples may walk the full loop explicitly. An example that scenes one of YOUR owned cases must enter it at a DIFFERENT point than the breakdown's telling. Slots marked +anchor get exactly ONE concrete physical/sensory detail; the others get none (props on every scene read as scaffold).
    * ex01: open AT the check-in/return moment itself → the move is skipped or botched and the miss lands → lead with WHY it works, then the move
    * ex02: open after it's over, tracing back what happened → headed for a miss, caught late — barely → open with the question the reader would ask, answer it → +anchor
    * ex03: open when someone outside the room first feels the effect → ends unresolved; the return point is set but not yet met → make both fields the tersest in the chapter — no preamble
    * ex04: open on the early signal nobody has flagged yet → the move works — but earn it, show the cost paid → whatToDo/whyItMatters as direct commands to the reader
    * ex05: open at the moment the demand/standard is stated → headed for a miss, caught late — barely → lead whatToDo/whyItMatters with what skipping this costs

- LEAD THREAD: this chapter runs on YOUR case "Celery test" — the fastRead and at least 2 examples live inside that case's real story (its real actors, numbers, dates; packet-attested actions only, never invented quotes). Invented cast appears only in supporting scenes.
- QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — transfer-new-domain (the chapter's move in a non-business setting; ask what maps); failure-postmortem (it already failed; ask which earlier step was the cause); cold-diagnosis (present symptoms; ask what is actually wrong); choose-next-move (a live scenario; ask for the single best next action). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — borrowed-authority (outsources the judgment to a rank, brand, or precedent); wrong-target (the right move aimed at the wrong person/thing); wrong-timing (the right move too early or too late); wrong-proof (accepts the wrong evidence as settling it). Different modes within one question; never a generic bad answer.
- QUESTION ORDER: do not quiz facts in packet order — cover them in this dealt order: 1, 5, 7, 6, 3, 4, 9, 8, 2.
- PRACTICE SLOT SHAPES: the four practice surfaces must NOT share one skeleton — tryThisNow: dealt above; weekly practice: "if-then-trigger" structure; if-then contexts: "observe-then-note" structure. Never repeat one prompt style (read-aloud, touch-the-object) across surfaces, and keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: your 3 lines use these dealt shapes, one each — pointed-question (a question sharp enough to reread); imperative (a command short enough to say from memory); reversal (a line that flips the expected direction).
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: let a failing/partial example CARRY the limits — show where the move breaks instead of appending a warning paragraph
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: spend the SENTENCE BEFORE the anchor setting it up in plain words. Every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences.
- PRACTICE VERB: build the physical actions in tryThisNow and the 24-hour challenge around "schedule" — not "touch" or "open" (other chapters own other verbs; a shared verb becomes a book-wide tic).
- FRAMEWORK IDIOM: every chapter of this book teaches the same framework, and a book-level reader FAILED the last draft because all chapters verbalized it identically. When the framework recurs beyond your noun budget, speak it through YOUR dealt idiom families — motion-speak (verbs of travel — 'the promise comes back on a date, or it drifts'); ledger-speak (counts and balances — 'three promises out, one return date set') — other chapters own other registers. Never invent a formal synonym; re-ground in your cases instead.
- EXAMPLE SHELL REGISTER: open your examples' whatToDo/whyItMatters fields in the "artifact-first" register — open on the object ('The board shows…'). No two of your examples may open BOTH fields the same way, and never reuse another chapter's fixed formula (book readers see all chapters side by side).
- FRAMEWORK VOCABULARY BUDGET: the book's framework nouns are: case, different, third, language, test, boundary. Every chapter leans on them, so they saturate book-wide. Your budget: the single noun you need most ≤15 uses in this chapter; every other listed noun ≤10. The same budget applies to any generic role-noun you find yourself repeating (owner, leader, manager) even if unlisted — name people by their case names and titles instead. When the budget is spent, use YOUR cases' concrete referents — the person's name, the artifact, the number — never an invented formal synonym ("the accountable party" is worse than the budget).
- SCENE-FURNITURE BUDGET (same rule, fixed list): review, meeting, room, plan, work, check, test, date, news, next — each ≤10 uses in this chapter. Every chapter of every business book reaches for these; at 20+ uses each they make nine chapters read as one. When you feel the next "the review meeting" or "the room goes quiet" coming, write the specific thing instead: the named meeting, the artifact on the table, the person who speaks.
