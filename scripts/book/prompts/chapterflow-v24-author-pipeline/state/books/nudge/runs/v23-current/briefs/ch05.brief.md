## VARIETY (dealt — do NOT default to the house pattern)
- CHAPTER ARCHITECTURE (two-way-contrast): Structure the chapter as exactly TWO cases held in tension — the contrast itself is the lesson; let the two do all the work. This is the chapter's overall SHAPE — the sections below dress it; they do not override it.
- THIS CHAPTER'S JOB: This mechanism operates because the visible stimulus activates an incompatible response before refl… — serve it through a DIFFERENT facet per example. NOT THIS CHAPTER: prev ch owns "This mechanism operates because forming an implementation plan…"; next ch owns "This mechanism operates because an immediate chance to win made…" — mention only in passing, never re-teach.
- CONNECT: in ONE sentence, name how this chapter's move builds on the prior chapter's anchor — "This mechanism operates because forming an implementation plan…" — composing the two; that is the ONLY re-teach exception (name it, do not re-explain it).
- OPENER: Open the hook with a QUESTION the reader can't answer yet. Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as script-one-sentence — have the reader pre-write ONE exact sentence to say or send. Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow around a say-aloud script: give the exact sentence to say out loud.
- EXAMPLES: write EXACTLY 4 examples. Vary where each enters the material and how it resolves — no two examples share both their entry point and their outcome; at most 2 walk the framework loop end to end; at most 2 may be a person-handling-a-document scene. An example that scenes one of YOUR owned cases must enter it at a DIFFERENT point than the breakdown's telling. At least ONE example must show the move failing or only partially working — real friction, not another frictionless win.
- ANCHORS: give 2-3 examples exactly ONE concrete physical/sensory detail each; the rest get none (props on every scene read as scaffold).
- LEAD THREAD: this chapter runs on YOUR case "Richard Thaler / confusing classroom doors" — the fastRead and at least 2 examples live inside that case's real story (its real actors, numbers, dates; packet-attested actions only, never invented quotes). Invented cast appears only in supporting scenes.
- QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — predict-consequence (a choice was just made; ask what happens downstream); spot-the-violation (a plausible-looking plan; ask which principle it breaks); best-explanation-why (an outcome happened; ask WHY (mechanism, not recall)); ordering-priority (several valid actions; ask which comes FIRST and why). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — wrong-timing (the right move too early or too late); wrong-proof (accepts the wrong evidence as settling it); wrong-scope (applies the move too broadly or too narrowly); half-measure (starts the move but stops before the part that matters). Different modes within one question; never a generic bad answer.
- QUESTION ORDER: do not quiz facts in packet order — cover them in this dealt order: 4, 9, 6, 3, 2, 5, 1, 7, 8.
- PRACTICE SURFACES: the four practice surfaces (tryThisNow, 24h challenge, weekly practice, if-then contexts) must NOT share one skeleton or repeat one prompt style. Keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: write 3; no two share one grammatical mold — vary the sentence shape, not just the words.
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: put the limits/when-NOT-to paragraph EARLY in the deep read, as an aside — do not save it for the ending
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences. Vary the grounding form — one repeated rhythm chapter-wide reads as a stamp.
- PRACTICE & FIELD VARIETY: no two of your examples may open BOTH the whatToDo and whyItMatters fields the same way, and the physical actions in your practice items must not lean on one verb (a shared verb becomes a book-wide tic).
- FRAMEWORK VOCABULARY BUDGET: the book's framework nouns are: mechanism, operates, rather, choice, attention, cost. Every chapter leans on them, so they saturate book-wide. Your budget: the single noun you need most ≤15 uses in this chapter; every other listed noun ≤10. The same budget applies to any generic role-noun you find yourself repeating (owner, leader, manager) even if unlisted — name people by their case names and titles instead. When the budget is spent, use YOUR cases' concrete referents — the person's name, the artifact, the number — never an invented formal synonym ("the accountable party" is worse than the budget).
- SCENE-FURNITURE BUDGET (same rule, fixed list): review, meeting, room, plan, work, check, test, date, news, next — each ≤10 uses in this chapter. Every chapter of every business book reaches for these; at 20+ uses each they make nine chapters read as one. When you feel the next "the review meeting" or "the room goes quiet" coming, write the specific thing instead: the named meeting, the artifact on the table, the person who speaks.
