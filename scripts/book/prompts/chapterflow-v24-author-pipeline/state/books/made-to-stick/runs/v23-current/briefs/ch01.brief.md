Thesis: Iyengar and Lepper's 2000 experiments compared limited sets of 6 choices with extensive sets of 24 or 30 choices.

- THIS CHAPTER'S JOB: Because evaluating many attractive alternatives raises comparison and commitment costs, the extensi… — serve it through a DIFFERENT facet per example. NOT THIS CHAPTER: next ch owns "Because the pocket-sized target forced simultaneous miniaturiza…" — mention only in passing, never re-teach.
- DEFINE THE MODEL: this is chapter 1 — name the book's central framework once, memorably (a short repeatable handle a reader carries into every later chapter), then teach THIS chapter's move as its first application.
- OPENER: Open the hook with the chapter's core tension stated flat in two short sentences — no named person, no scene furniture; the second sentence carries the friction ('Everyone agreed. No one knew who would bring back proof.'). Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as teach-it-to-someone — have the reader TEACH the move to one other person in their own words. Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow as observe-then-note: watch one specific thing, then write down what you saw.
- EXAMPLES: write EXACTLY 4 examples. Vary where each enters the material and how it resolves — no two examples share both their entry point and their outcome; at most 2 walk the framework loop end to end; at most 2 may be a person-handling-a-document scene. An example that scenes one of YOUR owned cases must enter it at a DIFFERENT point than the breakdown's telling. At least ONE example must show the move failing or only partially working — real friction, not another frictionless win.
- ANCHORS: give 2-3 examples exactly ONE concrete physical/sensory detail each; the rest get none (props on every scene read as scaffold).
- LEAD THREAD: this chapter runs on YOUR case "U.S. Army Commander's Intent" — the fastRead and at least 2 examples live inside that case's real story (its real actors, numbers, dates; packet-attested actions only, never invented quotes). NO invented stand-in characters in this chapter — every scene runs on the case's real, attested actors (this chapter's CONTENT DEVICES ban the invented proxy cast).
- QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — choose-next-move (a live scenario; ask for the single best next action); predict-consequence (a choice was just made; ask what happens downstream); spot-the-violation (a plausible-looking plan; ask which principle it breaks); best-explanation-why (an outcome happened; ask WHY (mechanism, not recall)). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — wrong-proof (accepts the wrong evidence as settling it); wrong-scope (applies the move too broadly or too narrowly); half-measure (starts the move but stops before the part that matters); right-move-wrong-trigger (correct action fired by the wrong signal). Different modes within one question; never a generic bad answer.
- QUESTION ORDER: do not quiz facts in packet order — cover them in this dealt order: 7, 5, 6, 8, 3, 2, 9, 4, 1.
- PRACTICE SURFACES: the four practice surfaces (tryThisNow, 24h challenge, weekly practice, if-then contexts) must NOT share one skeleton or repeat one prompt style. Keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: write 3; no two share one grammatical mold — vary the sentence shape, not just the words.
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: put the limits/when-NOT-to paragraph EARLY in the deep read, as an aside — do not save it for the ending
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences. Vary the grounding form — one repeated rhythm chapter-wide reads as a stamp.
- PRACTICE & FIELD VARIETY: no two of your examples may open BOTH the whatToDo and whyItMatters fields the same way, and the physical actions in your practice items must not lean on one verb (a shared verb becomes a book-wide tic).
- FRAMEWORK VOCABULARY BUDGET: the book's framework nouns are: rather, later, audience, first, story, concrete. Every chapter leans on them, so they saturate book-wide. Your budget: the single noun you need most ≤15 uses in this chapter; every other listed noun ≤10. The same budget applies to any generic role-noun you find yourself repeating (owner, leader, manager) even if unlisted — name people by their case names and titles instead. When the budget is spent, use YOUR cases' concrete referents — the person's name, the artifact, the number — never an invented formal synonym ("the accountable party" is worse than the budget).
- SCENE-FURNITURE BUDGET (same rule, fixed list): review, meeting, room, plan, work, check, test, date, news, next — each ≤10 uses in this chapter. Every chapter of every business book reaches for these; at 20+ uses each they make nine chapters read as one. When you feel the next "the review meeting" or "the room goes quiet" coming, write the specific thing instead: the named meeting, the artifact on the table, the person who speaks.
