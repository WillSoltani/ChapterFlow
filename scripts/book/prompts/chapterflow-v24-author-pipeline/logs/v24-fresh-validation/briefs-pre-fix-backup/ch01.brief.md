No invented stand-in characters and no second/third mirror case — the single narrative carries it. This is the chapter's overall SHAPE — the sections below dress it; they do not override it.
- OPENER: Open the hook with the chapter's core tension stated flat in two short sentences — no named person, no scene furniture; the second sentence carries the friction ('Everyone agreed. No one knew who would bring back proof.'). Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as replace-one-Y — have the reader SWAP one existing habit or artifact for the taught move — 'Replace one <thing> with …' Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow as an if-then trigger: 'When X happens, do Y.'

    * postmortem: a past-tense autopsy: the decision already played out; trace why it went the way it went.

- QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — ordering-priority (several valid actions; ask which comes FIRST and why); transfer-new-domain (the chapter's move in a non-business setting; ask what maps); failure-postmortem (it already failed; ask which earlier step was the cause); cold-diagnosis (present symptoms; ask what is actually wrong). - MEMORABLE LINES: your 3 lines use these dealt shapes, one each — pointed-question (a question sharp enough to reread); imperative (a command short enough to say from memory); reversal (a line that flips the expected direction).

- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: spend the SENTENCE BEFORE the anchor setting it up in plain words.
