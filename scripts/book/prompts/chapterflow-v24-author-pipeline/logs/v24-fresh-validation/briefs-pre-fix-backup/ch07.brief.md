## VARIETY (dealt — do NOT default to the house pattern)
- CHAPTER ARCHITECTURE (everyday-first-person): Ground the chapter in the READER's own moment — write to 'you', a decision they actually face. No famous brand/founder anchor and no invented proxy cast; the reader is the protagonist. This is the chapter's overall SHAPE — the sections below dress it; they do not override it.
- OPENER: Open the hook with a QUESTION the reader can't answer yet. Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as attach-to-existing-routine — BOLT the action onto a routine already in the reader's day — name the routine. Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow as an if-then trigger: 'When X happens, do Y.'
- EXAMPLE SCENES: your 5 examples must cover all three dealt lenses below; at most 2 examples may be a person-handling-a-document scene.
    * postmortem: a past-tense autopsy: the decision already played out; trace why it went the way it went.
    * walkthrough: a step-by-step trace of someone applying the move, decision by decision.
    * counterfactual: reason out loud what would have followed WITHOUT the move — framed explicitly as reasoning ('had they not…'), never narrated as events that happened.
- EXAMPLE PLAN: write EXACTLY 5 examples. Each slot's dealt arc below (entry → resolution → field register). Dramatize ONE beat of the framework per example — the other beats get at most one clause; at most 2 examples may walk the full loop explicitly. An example that scenes one of YOUR owned cases must enter it at a DIFFERENT point than the breakdown's telling. Slots marked +anchor get exactly ONE concrete physical/sensory detail; the others get none (props on every scene read as scaffold).
    * ex01: open mid-action, demand already in the past — no setup → ends unresolved; the return point is set but not yet met → lead with WHY it works, then the move → +anchor
    * ex02: open AT the check-in/return moment itself → the move works — but earn it, show the cost paid → open with the question the reader would ask, answer it
    * ex03: open after it's over, tracing back what happened → headed for a miss, caught late — barely → make both fields the tersest in the chapter — no preamble → +anchor
    * ex04: open when someone outside the room first feels the effect → ends unresolved; the return point is set but not yet met → whatToDo/whyItMatters as direct commands to the reader → +anchor
    * ex05: open on the early signal nobody has flagged yet → the move half-works; name what stayed broken → lead whatToDo/whyItMatters with what skipping this costs
- LEAD THREAD: Olivia carries this chapter — the fastRead and at least 2 examples follow Olivia's situation; other cast support. Introduce invented people role-BEFORE-name in varied wording (never one fixed "call her X" phrase).
- QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — failure-postmortem (it already failed; ask which earlier step was the cause); cold-diagnosis (present symptoms; ask what is actually wrong); choose-next-move (a live scenario; ask for the single best next action); predict-consequence (a choice was just made; ask what happens downstream). Shapes may repeat; WORDING may not: no stem's first four words may repeat another stem's.
- DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — wrong-target (the right move aimed at the wrong person/thing); wrong-timing (the right move too early or too late); wrong-proof (accepts the wrong evidence as settling it); wrong-scope (applies the move too broadly or too narrowly). Different modes within one question; never a generic bad answer.
- QUESTION ORDER: do not quiz facts in packet order — cover them in this dealt order: 5, 8, 1, 2, 9, 7, 4, 6, 3.
- PRACTICE SLOT SHAPES: the four practice surfaces must NOT share one skeleton — tryThisNow: dealt above; weekly practice: "observe-then-note" structure; if-then contexts: "say-aloud-script" structure. Never repeat one prompt style (read-aloud, touch-the-object) across surfaces, and keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: your 3 lines use these dealt shapes, one each — reversal (a line that flips the expected direction); redefinition (redefine a familiar word on the chapter's terms); cost-statement (name the concrete price of the default behavior).
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: put the limits/when-NOT-to paragraph EARLY in the deep read, as an aside — do not save it for the ending
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: spend the SENTENCE BEFORE the anchor setting it up in plain words. Every real company/event/date gets one plain-words grounding at first mention, drawn from the packet — if the packet gives no context, soften or drop the anchor, never invent. Rephrase any term of art in plain words in the same paragraph. The hook may not hang on a date or name that is not anchored within the next two sentences.
- PRACTICE VERB: build the physical actions in tryThisNow and the 24-hour challenge around "say" — not "touch" or "open" (other chapters own other verbs; a shared verb becomes a book-wide tic).
