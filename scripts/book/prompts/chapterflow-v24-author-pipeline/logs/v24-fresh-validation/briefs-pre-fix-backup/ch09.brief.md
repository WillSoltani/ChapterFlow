## VARIETY (dealt — do NOT default to the house pattern)
- CHAPTER ARCHITECTURE (historical-narrative): Tell ONE case as a chronological story with real dates, actors, and stakes. No invented stand-in characters and no second/third mirror case — the single narrative carries it. This is the chapter's overall SHAPE — the sections below dress it; they do not override it.
- OPENER: Open the hook with a flat CLAIM that sounds wrong until the chapter proves it. Carry the SAME mode into the fastRead opening sentence.
- 24-HOUR CHALLENGE: Frame it as replace-one-Y — have the reader SWAP one existing habit or artifact for the taught move — 'Replace one <thing> with …' Do NOT use the "In the next 24 hours," stem.
- PRACTICE: Shape tryThisNow as observe-then-note: watch one specific thing, then write down what you saw.
- EXAMPLE SCENES: your 6 examples must cover all three dealt lenses below; at most 2 examples may be a person-handling-a-document scene.

    * before-after-ledger: show the same numbers or facts BEFORE and AFTER the move — the delta is the story.
    * postmortem: a past-tense autopsy: the decision already played out; trace why it went the way it went.
- EXAMPLE PLAN: write EXACTLY 6 examples. Each slot's dealt arc below (entry → resolution → field register). Dramatize ONE beat of the framework per example — the other beats get at most one clause; at most 2 examples may walk the full loop explicitly. - QUIZ STEMS: draw your 9 stems from these dealt shapes — ordering-priority (several valid actions; ask which comes FIRST and why); transfer-new-domain (the chapter's move in a non-business setting; ask what maps); failure-postmortem (it already failed; ask which earlier step was the cause); cold-diagnosis (present symptoms; ask what is actually wrong). - DISTRACTOR MODES: derive every wrong answer FROM your key via one of — over-correction (overshoots into the opposite failure); borrowed-authority (outsources the judgment to a rank, brand, or precedent); wrong-target (the right move aimed at the wrong person/thing); wrong-timing (the right move too early or too late). Different modes within one question; never a generic bad answer.

- PRACTICE SLOT SHAPES: the four practice surfaces must NOT share one skeleton — tryThisNow: dealt above; weekly practice: "if-then-trigger" structure; if-then contexts: "two-step-sequence" structure. Never repeat one prompt style (read-aloud, touch-the-object) across surfaces, and keep any timers round (5/10/15/20/25/30/45/60) and consistent wherever the same action is restated.
- MEMORABLE LINES: your 3 lines use these dealt shapes, one each — imperative (a command short enough to say from memory); reversal (a line that flips the expected direction); redefinition (redefine a familiar word on the chapter's terms).
- LIMITS PLACEMENT: close the full read with the limits paragraph (the classic slot — fine here, other chapters own other slots)
- FIRST-MENTION GROUNDING: ground anchors with a short appositive — 'Session C, GE's talent review, …'. - EXAMPLE SHELL REGISTER: open your examples' whatToDo/whyItMatters fields in the "verb-first" register — open on the imperative verb ('Trace the promise…'). No two of your examples may open BOTH fields the same way, and never reuse another chapter's fixed formula (book readers see all chapters side by side).
- FRAMEWORK VOCABULARY BUDGET: the book's framework nouns are: output, automatically, creates, enough, manager, value. Every chapter leans on them, so they saturate book-wide. - SCENE-FURNITURE BUDGET (same rule, fixed list): review, meeting, room, plan, work, check, test, date, news, next — each ≤10 uses in this chapter. When you feel the next "the review meeting" or "the room goes quiet" coming, write the specific thing instead: the named meeting, the artifact on the table, the person who speaks.
