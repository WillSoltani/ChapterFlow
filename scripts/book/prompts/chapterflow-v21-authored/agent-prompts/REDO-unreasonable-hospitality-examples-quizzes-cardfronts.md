## Composition rules

### Examples
- Open on a concrete person doing something, in a real situation grounded in this
  chapter's source — Guidara's actual EMP material (the hot-dog gesture, the $2
  street-cart course, the "Make it nice" ethos, Danny Meyer mentorship, the
  cocktail-napkin notes, the four-star NYT review, the 95/5 rule). Reference a real
  proper noun, but as a *fact understood and applied*, never a noun the character
  "studies"/"acts on". A concept ("hospitality", "the hospitality economy") is
  NEVER an object, place, or thing a person studies.
- Vary scene structure across the 6 examples and across chapters (time-first,
  dialogue, data, role-action). 80–140 words.

### Quiz (9 per chapter)
- Each `prompt` is a real question (a scenario or a concept question), not a pasted
  source sentence with a trailing stub.
- Each of the 3 `choices` is a complete, grammatical, standalone proposition. Banned:
  any choice containing `"hospitality economy <noun> <noun>"`, `"Source case N"`,
  `"Source Moment"`, or the bare concept label used as a subject/verb.
- `correctIndex` points at the genuinely correct choice; `explanation` justifies
  THAT choice in plain prose (never `"Source case N: ..."`). Distractors plausible-
  but-wrong. Keep a balanced 3-3-3 / 4-3-2 per chapter; sequences distinct across chapters.

### Review-card fronts
- A question about a behavior, distinction, or skill the reader recalls. No proper
  noun or concept label as the subject. `back` already answers it — make the front
  match the back.
