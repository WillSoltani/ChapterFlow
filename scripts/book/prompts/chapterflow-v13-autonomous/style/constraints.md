Hard rules:
- Do not introduce facts, quotes, studies, mechanisms, or anecdotes not present in the chapter brief.
- If the brief is thin, stay narrow instead of inventing depth.
- No em dashes.
- No made-up quotes.
- No filler neuroscience.
- No pseudo-precision.
- No generic recap language.
- No duplicate sentence inside the same reader-facing surface.
- No repeated concluding beat once the point has landed.
- No thesis-first opener in chapter breakdowns unless the brief explicitly requires framing before pressure.
- No slogan-first competitive opener.
- If morally gray, frame as strategic awareness, not endorsement.
- No invented authority signals like:
  - mirror neurons
  - oxytocin
  - vagal tone
  - cortisol
  - dopamine
  - evolutionary psychology
  - millisecond timing claims
  unless the brief explicitly supports them.
- Do not copy raw source text into breakdowns unless the quote ledger explicitly allows a short quote.
- Do not leak brief, outline, seed, calibration, or validator language into reader-facing content.

Quality checks:
- every paragraph must do a distinct job
- no repeated argument loops
- no repeated paragraph landing
- no fake-deep abstractions
- no unsupported mechanism language
- no chapter-generic implementation advice
- no generic consistency, mindset, or accountability slogans in implementation plans
- no story-shaped filler pretending to be specificity
- no source-splice contamination
- no exact reuse of a tone line across all three tones
- no review-card echo of the key takeaway card
- no recap that functions as a second breakdown
- no identity-language filler detached from chapter pressure

Auto-fail moves:
- opening with a thesis instead of a live scene or tension
- hiding weak thinking behind smooth phrasing
- using abstraction to avoid choosing a concrete claim
- producing canonical drafts from structured depth blocks
- extending a weak section by appending restatements
