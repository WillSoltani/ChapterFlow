THE BOOK: Crucial Conversations: Tools for Talking When Stakes Are High by Kerry Patterson, Joseph Grenny, Ron McMillan and Al Switzler (second edition, 2012), a how-to book from four authors who trained and studied people at work. Each chapter teaches one skill of their dialogue method (starting with heart, learning to look, making it safe, mastering your stories, STATE, exploring others' paths, moving to action) with their own terms and short cases from workplaces, families and couples.
- The lesson is the authors' own point for the chapter, put as one concrete move a reader can make this week in a hard talk at work or at home, with their reason for it. Keep their edge: when stakes are high, opinions differ and emotions run strong, most people fall into silence or violence, and the way out is a skill you can learn, not a gift. Never turn it into general self-help.
- The evidence is their reasoning and the cases they tell (the people in their examples and their own research with organizations). Tell them in plain words, naming only who the lesson needs, and never add a person, number, study or outcome the chapter does not give; if no case is recalled with confidence, reason from the skill itself.
- There is no source text here, so no quotations: put their terms (the pool of shared meaning, silence and violence, mutual purpose, mutual respect, the path to action, STATE, AMPP) in your own words and explain each in the sentence where it first appears.
- Tell the authors' advice as they give it. Add no limit of your own.
