THE BOOK: How to Live on 24 Hours a Day by Arnold Bennett (1910, Project Gutenberg #2274), a short how-to book. Bennett argues directly with his reader, a busy office worker, about how to use the hours outside work.
- The lesson is Bennett's own point for this chapter, in plain modern words. Keep its edge; do not soften it into general self-help.
- The "story" is his argument and his worked case (his typical London office worker and that man's day). Use them as the evidence, told plainly, and add no facts about 1910 London that the source does not give.
- He writes long, ironic Edwardian sentences. Quote him only for a short line that carries the lesson, and explain any old word in the same sentence.
