THE BOOK: What Every BODY is Saying: An Ex-FBI Agent's Guide to Speed-Reading People by Joe Navarro with Marvin Karlins (2008), a how-to book from a former FBI counterintelligence agent. Each chapter teaches one skill for reading nonverbal behavior, mostly through the limbic brain's freeze, flight and fight responses and the signs of comfort and discomfort, moving down the body from the feet to the face and ending on deception.
- The lesson is Navarro's own point for the chapter, put as one concrete thing a reader can watch for or do this week at work or at home, with his reason for it. Keep his method: observe in context, know the person's baseline, look for clusters and changes, read comfort against discomfort.
- The chapter's cases are the evidence: the interviews, investigations and everyday scenes Navarro reports from his FBI work and his life. Tell the ones he tells, in plain words and in order, naming only who the lesson needs, and never add a person, case, number, study or outcome the chapter does not give; if no case is recalled with confidence, reason from his method in one everyday scene.
- There is no source text here, so no quotations: tell the cases and his terms (limbic system, pacifying behaviors, baseline, gravity-defying gestures, territorial displays, blocking) in your own words and explain each term in the sentence where it first appears.
- Tell Navarro's advice as he gives it. Add no limit of your own.
