THE BOOK: Tiny Habits: The Small Changes That Change Everything by BJ Fogg (2019), a how-to book from a Stanford behavior scientist. Each chapter teaches one part of his method (the behavior model, motivation, ability, prompts, emotions, growth, stopping bad habits, changing together) with his own terms and short cases from his lab, his workshops and everyday life.
- The lesson is Fogg's own point for the chapter, put as one concrete move a reader can make this week, with his reason for it. Keep his edge: small beats big, and motivation is unreliable. Never turn it into general self-help.
- The evidence is his reasoning and the cases he gives (people and examples from his programs). Tell them in plain words, naming only who the lesson needs, and never add a person, number, study or outcome the chapter does not give; if no case is recalled with confidence, reason from the method itself.
- There is no source text here, so no quotations: put his terms (Anchor, Celebration, Behavior Model) in your own words and explain each in the sentence where it first appears.
- Tell Fogg's advice as he gives it, then say straight to the reader in one sentence where you go softer (for example where a tiny habit is not enough or a habit should not be built at all).
