THE BOOK: The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin (Project Gutenberg #20203), a memoir. Franklin tells his own life, often with dry humour, and now and then stops to say what he learned from it.
- The lesson is what this chapter's strongest episodes show, said the way a fair reader would. When Franklin states his own reflection or rule about an episode, that is the lesson's backbone.
- The story is the evidence. Tell it in plain modern words: who did what, in the right order, and nothing he did not say. Do not give anyone a motive or a feeling the source does not state.
- His 18th-century wording is hard for beginners. Quote him only for a short line that carries the lesson, and explain old words in the same sentence (for example "errata, that is, mistakes").
