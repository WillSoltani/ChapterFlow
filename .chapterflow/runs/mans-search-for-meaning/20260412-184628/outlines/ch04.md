Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that logotherapy is a meaning-centered framework aimed at forms of suffering that cannot be explained by pleasure-loss alone and cannot be solved by generic positivity.

Opening Move:
Open on emptiness or frustration that pleasure and success fail to solve, then turn to the will to meaning.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for will to meaning versus rival motivational frames
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for existential vacuum and noogenic distress
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for routes to meaning and paradoxical intention

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: introduce the will to meaning as the chapter's governing move
- P2: distinguish it from pleasure-first or power-first explanations
- P3: define existential vacuum and its modern symptoms
- P4: explain noogenic distress as meaning-frustration
- P5: routes to meaning through work, love, and attitude toward unavoidable suffering
- P6: therapeutic tools such as paradoxical intention / dereflection
- P7 if needed: bridge to tragic optimism and the postscript

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes meaning-frustration from instinct frustration and meaning-centered therapy from generic encouragement.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Preserve the danger that meaning-talk can become coercive if it stops respecting the difference between inviting responsibility and demanding redemptive purpose from suffering.

Threshold Question:
How can a meaning-centered therapy ask for responsibility without turning pain into a moral obligation to produce purpose on command?
