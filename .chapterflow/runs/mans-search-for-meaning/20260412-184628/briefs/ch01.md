Core Claim:
Frankl uses the preface to make a narrow but decisive claim: the book matters only if meaning can survive conditions that appear to cancel human dignity altogether. The preface links his decision to remain with his parents in Vienna, his experience of the camps, and the speed with which he later wrote the book into one pressure line. Theory is not offered first. Testimony comes first so the later explanation of logotherapy has earned weight rather than sounding like abstract uplift.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is not yet the camp memoir and not yet the theory chapter. Its distinct job is to frame why testimony must precede explanation and why Frankl thinks the question of meaning has to be tested under extremity. It should feel impossible to confuse with later chapters because it is rooted in the Vienna decision, the proof-demand of suffering, and the refusal to let meaning become a comfortable slogan.

Author Logic Chain:
First Frankl frames the book as a response to the question of whether life keeps meaning under the worst imaginable conditions.
Then he connects that question to his own biography, especially the decision not to leave Vienna and the camp experience that followed.
Then he concludes that the memoir is not background decoration for logotherapy; it is the evidence that gives the later theory moral and psychological force.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The preface does not solve the argument for meaning; it only frames the test. It should not overclaim that one survivor's testimony settles every philosophical dispute. Keep the chapter honest by preserving the difference between opening proof pressure and later theoretical explanation.
