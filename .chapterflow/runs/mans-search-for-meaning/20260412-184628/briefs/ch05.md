Core Claim:
The postscript asks whether optimism can remain honest after tragedy rather than before it. Frankl's answer is tragic optimism: not a sunny denial of pain, but a disciplined insistence that life may still be met meaningfully despite pain, guilt, and death. The distinct move is to preserve responsibility and dignity without turning suffering into hidden blessing or moral requirement.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the tragic triad chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with the broader theory chapter because it directly names pain, guilt, and death and asks whether meaning can survive them without sentimentality or coercion.

Author Logic Chain:
First Frankl identifies the tragic triad of pain, guilt, and death.
Then he argues that each can still be met in a way that preserves meaning.
Then he concludes that tragic optimism is not cheerfulness but a disciplined stance toward unavoidable tragedy.

Counterarguments or Limits:
This chapter is especially vulnerable to sentimentality. Keep it narrow. Do not claim tragedy is good, necessary, or secretly rewarding. Preserve the full weight of pain, guilt, and death while still allowing Frankl's response claim to stand.
