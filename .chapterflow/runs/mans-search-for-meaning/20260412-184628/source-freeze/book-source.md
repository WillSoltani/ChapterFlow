# Frozen Source Bundle: Man's Search for Meaning

## Edition basis

- Working title: `Man's Search for Meaning`
- Author: `Viktor E. Frankl`
- Working edition family: modern Beacon Press English trade line
- Translation support found in metadata: `Ilse Lasch`; later print metadata also credits `Helen Pisano`

## Authorized / publisher-backed observations

- Beacon Press metadata and support material present the book as Frankl's camp memoir plus his explanation of logotherapy.
- The 2006 Beacon metadata emphasizes four camps, the memoir of life in Nazi camps, and the argument that suffering cannot always be avoided but response remains open.
- The 1992 revised-edition preview metadata exposes section-level structure including `Preface to the 1992 Edition`, `Logotherapy in a Nutshell`, and `The Case for a Tragic Optimism`.
- The Beacon teacher guide describes the book as autobiographical concentration-camp testimony followed by a logotherapy overview, with modern classroom framing in later adaptation material.

## Secondary structure support frozen for chapter planning

- SparkNotes confirms that Part One is commonly read as three psychological phases of camp life: shock, apathy, and reactions after liberation.
- SparkNotes also confirms the second major unit as `Logotherapy in a Nutshell` and the concluding added unit as `The Case for a Tragic Optimism`.
- Wikipedia is frozen only as a low-weight publication-history cross-check for title and translation lineage.

## Usable content boundaries

- Safe to use:
  - Frankl's camp experience as the basis for meaning-under-suffering claims
  - the distinction between pleasure, power, and meaning
  - the existential vacuum
  - the three broad routes to meaning in the modern English presentation
  - paradoxical intention and attitude toward unavoidable suffering
  - the tragic triad of pain, guilt, and death in the postscript
- Not safe to overstate:
  - exact camp chronology beyond what the frozen bundle clearly supports
  - detailed direct quotations unless directly visible in the frozen authorized material
  - disputed historical or critical claims not present in the frozen bundle
