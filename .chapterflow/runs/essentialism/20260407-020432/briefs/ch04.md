Book: Essentialism
Author: Greg McKeown
Chapter: 4
Title: Trade-Off: Which Problem Do I Want?
Section Heading: Essence: What is the core mind-set of an Essentialist?

Objective
Write a chapter package that explains why trade-offs are real, and the essentialist stops asking how to do both and starts choosing which problem is worth carrying.

Strict Intent
The chapter must show how people waste time trying to escape the cost built into every meaningful choice. It must then guide the reader to name the trade-off openly and choose the burden attached to what matters most. Keep the stakes visible: until trade-offs are accepted, priorities stay theoretical and overcommitment keeps winning.

Source Notes
- Nonessentialists ask how to do both, while essentialists ask which problem they want.
- The better question is not what must be sacrificed, but what deserves disproportionate investment.
- A small change in the trade-off question reshapes the whole decision.

Required Emphasis
- Skill: deliberate trade-off selection
- Bridge: the chapter converts discernment into commitment by forcing the cost of choice into the open
- Live tension: the reader wants expansion without loss, but essentialism insists that every yes already contains a no
