Core Claim:
Chapter 2 argues that choice is not only something we possess in theory but something we actively do. The chapter warns that when people forget this, they learn helplessness a little at a time and begin living as functions of other people's expectations or of their own past decisions. Essentialism begins by recovering the habit of choosing.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Strongest at separating options from agency and showing how surrendering choice becomes a slow habit of helplessness.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author reframes choice as an action, not merely a menu of options.
Then the author shows how people drip into helplessness by treating obligations and past commitments as total determinants.
Then the author argues that the Essentialist protects the act of choosing even when options are constrained.

Required Anchors:
