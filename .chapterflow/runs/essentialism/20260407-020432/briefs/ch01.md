Core Claim:
Chapter 1 contrasts the Essentialist with the Nonessentialist and introduces the paradox of success. Early clarity produces success, but success creates new options, expectations, and noise that can pull a person away from the few things that produced that success in the first place. The chapter argues that essentialism is not a productivity trick. It is a disciplined commitment to less but better.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Strongest at naming the paradox of success and showing why "less but better" is a hard operating philosophy rather than a decorative slogan.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author contrasts the Essentialist and the Nonessentialist.
Then the author shows how success itself can create overcommitment and dilution.
Then the author replaces false assumptions with three core truths about choice, importance, and limits.
