Sleep: Protect the Asset is built around a simple claim: sleep is not a luxury reward after important work; it is what protects the human asset required for important work. McKeown uses the chapter to expose how ambitious people often underinvest in themselves and damage the very instrument through which they contribute. People often tolerate that pattern because it comes wrapped in good intentions, ambition, duty, or convenience.

The answer is to treat sleep as strategic protection for judgment, connection, and contribution. This is the operative skill of the chapter. The best asset people have for contribution is themselves. Lack of sleep damages that asset, especially in successful people who keep pushing. The reader is being pushed away from vague preference and toward a clear operating rule.

The stakes are practical, not cosmetic. when the asset is depleted, discernment, creativity, and execution all decline together. That is why the chapter matters in the larger sequence. the chapter widens exploration from information and space to the condition of the person doing the choosing.
