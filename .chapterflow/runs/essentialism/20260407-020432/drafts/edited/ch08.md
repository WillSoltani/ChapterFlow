Sleep: Protect the Asset sharpens the book by attacking a pattern most people keep excusing. ambitious people often underinvest in themselves and damage the very instrument through which they contribute. McKeown's counterpunch is to treat sleep as strategic protection for judgment, connection, and contribution. The chapter insists that sleep is not a luxury reward after important work; it is what protects the human asset required for important work.

That claim lands because the chapter keeps moving from diagnosis into rule. The best asset people have for contribution is themselves. Lack of sleep damages that asset, especially in successful people who keep pushing. Instead of giving the reader one more slogan to admire, it gives them a harder standard to obey.

The stakes are obvious once the chapter is taken seriously: when the asset is depleted, discernment, creativity, and execution all decline together. It also advances the architecture of the book because the chapter widens exploration from information and space to the condition of the person doing the choosing. The chapter keeps pressure on the reader by refusing to hide the cost: high-achievement cultures admire sacrifice even when the sacrifice quietly ruins performance.
