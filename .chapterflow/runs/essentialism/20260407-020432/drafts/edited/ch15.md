Buffer: The Unfair Advantage sharpens the book by attacking a pattern most people keep excusing. people build plans with no margin and then act shocked when reality interrupts the schedule. McKeown's counterpunch is to prepare buffers through scenario planning, larger time estimates, and resilience against the foreseeable surprise. The chapter insists that the unexpected is predictable enough that essentialists plan for it instead of pretending best-case timing will hold.

That claim lands because the chapter keeps moving from diagnosis into rule. The essentialist expects the unexpected and prepares for contingencies. The planning fallacy explains why people underestimate duration even when they have done the work before. Instead of giving the reader one more slogan to admire, it gives them a harder standard to obey.

The stakes are obvious once the chapter is taken seriously: without margin, every disruption becomes an emergency that can wreck essential work.
