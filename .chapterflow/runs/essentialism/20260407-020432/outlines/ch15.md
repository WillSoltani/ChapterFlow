# Outline: Buffer: The Unfair Advantage

1. Open with the chapter's central claim:
- the unexpected is predictable enough that essentialists plan for it instead of pretending best-case timing will hold

2. Diagnose the weak pattern:
- people build plans with no margin and then act shocked when reality interrupts the schedule

3. Introduce the chapter's move:
- prepare buffers through scenario planning, larger time estimates, and resilience against the foreseeable surprise

4. Explain why it matters:
- without margin, every disruption becomes an emergency that can wreck essential work

5. Keep the book-level bridge visible:
- the execute section begins by building the slack that keeps the essential plan from collapsing at first contact with reality

6. End with the live tension:
- buffers can feel inefficient to optimizers even though no-buffer planning is usually fake efficiency
