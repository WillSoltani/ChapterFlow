# Outline: Trade-Off: Which Problem Do I Want?

1. Open with the chapter's central claim:
- trade-offs are real, and the essentialist stops asking how to do both and starts choosing which problem is worth carrying

2. Diagnose the weak pattern:
- people waste time trying to escape the cost built into every meaningful choice

3. Introduce the chapter's move:
- name the trade-off openly and choose the burden attached to what matters most

4. Explain why it matters:
- until trade-offs are accepted, priorities stay theoretical and overcommitment keeps winning

5. Keep the book-level bridge visible:
- the chapter converts discernment into commitment by forcing the cost of choice into the open

6. End with the live tension:
- the reader wants expansion without loss, but essentialism insists that every yes already contains a no
