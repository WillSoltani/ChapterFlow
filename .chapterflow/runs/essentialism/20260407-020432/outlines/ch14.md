# Outline: Limit: The Freedom of Setting Boundaries

1. Open with the chapter's central claim:
- boundaries look restrictive only to people who ignore the cost of having no line at all

2. Diagnose the weak pattern:
- nonessentialists experience limits as threats, even though the absence of limits lets everyone else capture their time

3. Introduce the chapter's move:
- treat boundaries as structures that protect attention, time, and intention

4. Explain why it matters:
- without boundaries, the essential is endlessly available for interruption and takeover

5. Keep the book-level bridge visible:
- the chapter turns graceful no into durable structure so the reader is not renegotiating everything from scratch

6. End with the live tension:
- boundaries feel impolite in the short run even when boundary failure is destructive in the long run
