# Outline: Focus: What's Important Now?

1. Open with the chapter's central claim:
- essential focus is not fake multifocus; it is the discipline of naming what matters now and giving it undivided attention

2. Diagnose the weak pattern:
- people confuse multitasking with real concentration and keep fragmenting attention across incompatible demands

3. Introduce the chapter's move:
- replace broad scattered attention with a present-tense question about what is important now

4. Explain why it matters:
- without present focus, even a strong plan breaks down in the moment of execution

5. Keep the book-level bridge visible:
- the chapter narrows execution from systems and routines to the immediate moment where attention is either given or lost

6. End with the live tension:
- presence sounds simple, but every modern environment is built to compete for the same slice of attention
