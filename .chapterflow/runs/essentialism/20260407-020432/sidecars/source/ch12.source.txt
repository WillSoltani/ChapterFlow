Chapter 12: Uncommit: Win Big by Cutting Your Losses
Section heading: Eliminate: How can we cut out the trivial many?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: past investment is not a reason to keep funding the nonessential, and essentialists learn to cut sunk costs cleanly
- Problem: people keep feeding dead commitments because walking away feels like admitting waste or failure
- Move: use zero-based thinking, reverse pilots, and slower commitments to stop inertia from governing the future
- Stakes: until old commitments are reviewed honestly, the calendar remains owned by yesterday's bad decisions
- Bridge: the chapter sharpens elimination by showing how to withdraw from work that no longer fits
- Tension: cutting losses hurts because it turns previous investment into a visible mistake

Supporting notes
- Sunk-cost bias keeps people investing in losing propositions because previous cost cannot be recovered.
- Zero-based budgeting asks which commitments would be chosen again from scratch.
- Reverse pilots and a deliberate pause before speaking make future uncommitment easier.
