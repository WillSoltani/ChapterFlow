Chapter 3: Discern: The Unimportance of Practically Everything
Section heading: Essence: What is the core mind-set of an Essentialist?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: almost everything is noise and only a vital few options deserve concentrated effort
- Problem: capable people keep treating many decent options as if they were equally important
- Move: use the law of the vital few to rank sharply instead of spreading effort evenly
- Stakes: without discernment, energy gets diluted before any real trade-off is named
- Bridge: this chapter makes Chapter 2's recovered agency useful by showing what to do with it
- Tension: discernment feels harsh because it rejects many good options before they become commitments

Supporting notes
- Joseph Moses Juran's law of the vital few explains why a small fraction of issues often drives most of the result.
- Many capable people stall their next level of contribution because they cannot release the idea that everything matters.
- Discernment starts with everyday choices, then expands to bigger areas of work and life.
