Chapter 17: Progress: The Power of Small Wins
Section heading: Execute: How can we make doing the vital few things almost effortless?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: small wins create momentum, and essentialists use minimum viable progress instead of waiting for dramatic breakthroughs
- Problem: people aim so big, so fast, that they burn out before meaningful movement ever begins
- Move: shrink the step, reward progress, and build confidence through repeated movement on meaningful work
- Stakes: without visible progress, motivation collapses and the essential task starts to feel heavier than it is
- Bridge: the chapter gives execution a sustainable pace that compounds instead of flaring out
- Tension: small steps can feel unserious to ambitious people even though they are often the only steps that keep going

Supporting notes
- Research is cited showing that progress is one of the strongest forms of motivation.
- Minimum viable progress asks for the smallest useful next step.
- Minimum viable preparation keeps people moving before perfectionism stalls the work.
