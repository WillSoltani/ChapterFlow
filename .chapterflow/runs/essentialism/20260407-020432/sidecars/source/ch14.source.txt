Chapter 14: Limit: The Freedom of Setting Boundaries
Section heading: Eliminate: How can we cut out the trivial many?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: boundaries look restrictive only to people who ignore the cost of having no line at all
- Problem: nonessentialists experience limits as threats, even though the absence of limits lets everyone else capture their time
- Move: treat boundaries as structures that protect attention, time, and intention
- Stakes: without boundaries, the essential is endlessly available for interruption and takeover
- Bridge: the chapter turns graceful no into durable structure so the reader is not renegotiating everything from scratch
- Tension: boundaries feel impolite in the short run even when boundary failure is destructive in the long run

Supporting notes
- Nonessentialists tend to see boundaries as constraints that get in the way.
- Essentialists see boundaries as empowering because they protect time from being hijacked.
- Boundaries reduce the number of moments that require a fresh no.
