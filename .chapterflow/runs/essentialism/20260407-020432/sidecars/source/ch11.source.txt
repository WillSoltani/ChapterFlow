Chapter 11: Dare: The Power of a Graceful "No"
Section heading: Eliminate: How can we cut out the trivial many?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: essentialism becomes real only when the reader can say no with courage, clarity, and relational grace
- Problem: people keep vague yeses in circulation because they fear awkwardness more than dilution
- Move: separate the decision from the relationship and build a repertoire of graceful refusals
- Stakes: without a practiced no, every earlier insight collapses under social pressure
- Bridge: the chapter translates essential intent into a boundary the world can actually hear
- Tension: refusal can cost popularity, but half-hearted acceptance costs respect and clarity

Supporting notes
- Without courage, the disciplined pursuit of less is just lip service.
- A graceful no separates the decision from the relationship and can use scripts other than the bare word no.
- The chapter emphasizes trade-offs, clarity, humor, and specific refusal patterns.
