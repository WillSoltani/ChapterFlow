Chapter 5: Escape: The Perks of Being Unavailable
Section heading: Explore: How can we discern the trivial many from the vital few?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: serious discernment needs protected space, and availability to everything destroys the conditions for thinking
- Problem: people stay so reachable and reactive that they never create the silence needed to see what matters
- Move: carve solitude, reading time, and reflective distance into ordinary life
- Stakes: without space, the loudest input wins by default and the essential remains hidden
- Bridge: the chapter opens the explore section by defending unavailability as a tool for better selection
- Tension: space looks indulgent in busy systems even though it is what protects serious judgment

Supporting notes
- Pablo Picasso is cited for the claim that no serious work is possible without great solitude.
- Busy people can still carve out time and space to think.
- Reading serious material can create distance from the noise of the day.
