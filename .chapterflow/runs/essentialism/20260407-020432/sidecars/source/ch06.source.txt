Chapter 6: Look: See What Really Matters
Section heading: Explore: How can we discern the trivial many from the vital few?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: essentialists do not inspect everything equally; they learn to scan the field and filter for what is actually significant
- Problem: too much information makes people feel informed while leaving them unable to distinguish signal from noise
- Move: filter for the fascinating, journal selectively, and look for patterns instead of drowning in inputs
- Stakes: if attention is spent on every fact and opinion, the reader never sees the few clues that matter most
- Bridge: the chapter turns protected space into a method for seeing what is important inside a cluttered field
- Tension: looking well requires saying no to most available information even when collecting it feels productive

Supporting notes
- Discernment requires disciplined scanning and filtering of facts, options, and opinions.
- The journal practice matters because it captures patterns without turning the person into a stenographer of life.
- Writing less than you feel like writing sharpens attention to what is worth keeping.
