Chapter 13: Edit: The Invisible Art
Section heading: Eliminate: How can we cut out the trivial many?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: editing is not loss for loss's sake; it is a creative act that makes the important clearer, cleaner, and stronger
- Problem: people keep treating subtraction as damage when it is often the reason meaning becomes visible
- Move: cut, condense, and correct until the essential has room to stand out
- Stakes: without disciplined editing, life fills with clutter and the best work never gets enough space to breathe
- Bridge: the chapter reframes elimination as an art rather than a punishment
- Tension: editing looks invisible precisely because success is often experienced as what remains, not what was removed

Supporting notes
- Disciplined editing increases contribution by creating more room for what matters.
- Editors ask whether the point is clear and whether it is being said as concisely as possible.
- The life version of editing includes cutting options, condensing effort, and correcting course.
