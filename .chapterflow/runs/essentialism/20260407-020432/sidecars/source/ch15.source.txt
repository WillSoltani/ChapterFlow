Chapter 15: Buffer: The Unfair Advantage
Section heading: Execute: How can we make doing the vital few things almost effortless?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: the unexpected is predictable enough that essentialists plan for it instead of pretending best-case timing will hold
- Problem: people build plans with no margin and then act shocked when reality interrupts the schedule
- Move: prepare buffers through scenario planning, larger time estimates, and resilience against the foreseeable surprise
- Stakes: without margin, every disruption becomes an emergency that can wreck essential work
- Bridge: the execute section begins by building the slack that keeps the essential plan from collapsing at first contact with reality
- Tension: buffers can feel inefficient to optimizers even though no-buffer planning is usually fake efficiency

Supporting notes
- The essentialist expects the unexpected and prepares for contingencies.
- The planning fallacy explains why people underestimate duration even when they have done the work before.
- Scenario planning helps build social, financial, and operational resilience.
