Chapter 19: Focus: What's Important Now?
Section heading: Execute: How can we make doing the vital few things almost effortless?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: essential focus is not fake multifocus; it is the discipline of naming what matters now and giving it undivided attention
- Problem: people confuse multitasking with real concentration and keep fragmenting attention across incompatible demands
- Move: replace broad scattered attention with a present-tense question about what is important now
- Stakes: without present focus, even a strong plan breaks down in the moment of execution
- Bridge: the chapter narrows execution from systems and routines to the immediate moment where attention is either given or lost
- Tension: presence sounds simple, but every modern environment is built to compete for the same slice of attention

Supporting notes
- The chapter distinguishes multitasking from the fantasy of multifocusing.
- The pause that refreshes helps people notice kairos moments rather than rushing past them.
- The central question is what is important now.
