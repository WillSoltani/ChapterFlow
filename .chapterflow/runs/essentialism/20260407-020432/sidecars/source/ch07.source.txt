Chapter 7: Play: Embrace the Wisdom of Your Inner Child
Section heading: Explore: How can we discern the trivial many from the vital few?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: play is not a childish extra; it expands options, lowers stress, and strengthens the mind used for discernment
- Problem: adults often strip play out of life first, then wonder why creativity and resilience begin to collapse
- Move: treat joyful exploration as essential fuel for better thinking rather than as wasted time
- Stakes: without play, exploration narrows and the mind becomes too rigid to notice better possibilities
- Bridge: the chapter argues that better discernment depends on a mind that is not permanently clenched
- Tension: play sounds nonessential in achievement cultures, which is exactly why the chapter has to defend it so directly

Supporting notes
- Play broadens the range of options people can see.
- Play acts as an antidote to stress and helps executive function.
- The chapter suggests reconnecting with childhood sources of joyful energy.
