Chapter 4: Trade-Off: Which Problem Do I Want?
Section heading: Essence: What is the core mind-set of an Essentialist?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: trade-offs are real, and the essentialist stops asking how to do both and starts choosing which problem is worth carrying
- Problem: people waste time trying to escape the cost built into every meaningful choice
- Move: name the trade-off openly and choose the burden attached to what matters most
- Stakes: until trade-offs are accepted, priorities stay theoretical and overcommitment keeps winning
- Bridge: the chapter converts discernment into commitment by forcing the cost of choice into the open
- Tension: the reader wants expansion without loss, but essentialism insists that every yes already contains a no

Supporting notes
- Nonessentialists ask how to do both, while essentialists ask which problem they want.
- The better question is not what must be sacrificed, but what deserves disproportionate investment.
- A small change in the trade-off question reshapes the whole decision.
