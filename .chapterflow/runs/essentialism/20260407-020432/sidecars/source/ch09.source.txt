Chapter 9: Select: The Power of Extreme Criteria
Section heading: Explore: How can we discern the trivial many from the vital few?

Rights-safe chapter framing
- Thesis: weak criteria produce too many yeses, while extreme criteria protect the few options that truly fit
- Problem: people call options good enough too quickly and then wonder why their calendars fill with near-matches
- Move: use minimum criteria and extreme criteria, including the 90 percent rule, to reject merely decent options
- Stakes: if standards stay soft, every attractive possibility gets mistaken for the essential one
- Bridge: the chapter closes the explore section by turning discernment into a repeatable selection rule
- Tension: extreme criteria feel risky because they require saying no before certainty arrives

Supporting notes
- The 90 percent rule converts vague liking into a harsh decision filter.
- Writing minimum and extreme criteria makes selection concrete.
- Selective criteria force the chooser, rather than the world, to decide what to wait for.
