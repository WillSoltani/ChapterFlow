Core book thesis:
Essentialism argues that the disciplined pursuit of less but better creates higher contribution than trying to do everything. The book contrasts the Essentialist, who chooses deliberately and filters aggressively, with the Nonessentialist, who is spread thin by default yeses, noise, and competing demands.

Chapter-level framing notes:
- The early chapters define three false assumptions to replace: "I have to," "It's all important," and "I can do both."
- The book repeatedly returns to the paradox of success: early success creates options and noise that can erode clarity.
- Chapter 2 stresses that choice is an action and that people can drift into learned helplessness by forgetting this.
- Later chapters turn the philosophy into filters, boundaries, buffers, routines, and progress loops rather than heroic willpower.
