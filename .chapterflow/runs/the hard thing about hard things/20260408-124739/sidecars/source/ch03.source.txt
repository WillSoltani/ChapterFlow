        Core claim:
        - In a collapse, the CEO's first job is not optimism but disciplined survival work: face reality, keep moving, and protect the possibility of another day.

        Distinct mechanism:
        - Horowitz uses Loudcloud's post-crash danger to show that survival comes from repeated hard calls on financing, communication, and endurance rather than from one dramatic save.

        Required anchors:
        - Dot-com crash pressure: assumptions about funding and growth stop being reliable.
- Cash and existential risk: survival becomes a sequence of decisions under fear and limited options.
- Relentless continuation: the title chapter treats persistence as operational discipline, not mood.

        Common misreadings:
        - Mistaking survival for denial or cheerleading rather than a sober sequence of corrective actions.
- Treating persistence as passive endurance instead of active crisis management.

        Specific applications:
        - Separate useful persistence from wishful thinking in a downturn.
- Use cash pressure and runway reality to force sharper prioritization.
- Teach that crisis stamina is a decision discipline, not a personality trait alone.

        Hard-edge requirement:
        - Hard mode must show why survival leadership feels psychologically brutal because the CEO has to keep choosing under conditions that rarely reward certainty.

        Threshold question:
        - What does disciplined leadership look like when the primary objective is no longer growth but survival?
