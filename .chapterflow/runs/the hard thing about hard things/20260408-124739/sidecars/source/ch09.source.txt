        Core claim:
        - Entrepreneurship resists universal rules because leaders constantly face tradeoffs that only make sense in context, not in abstract best-practice slogans.

        Distinct mechanism:
        - Horowitz uses acquisition pressure, accountability-versus-creativity tension, and people-management paradoxes to argue that judgment beats rigid adherence to generic rules.

        Required anchors:
        - Deals and operating outcomes remain contingent deep into the process, so certainty is fragile.
- Accountability versus creativity is a live paradox, not a rule with a clean permanent answer.
- The chapter broadens the book's anti-formula stance by making context-sensitive judgment central.

        Common misreadings:
        - Treating the chapter as anti-discipline instead of anti-rigidity.
- Reading 'no rules' as an excuse to avoid standards or accountability.

        Specific applications:
        - Identify where a best practice is solving the wrong problem for the current company stage.
- Hold accountability and creativity together without pretending the tension disappears.
- Use principles to guide judgment instead of replacing it.

        Hard-edge requirement:
        - Hard mode must show why leaders fail when they cling to a rule that solves the wrong context more neatly than the real one.

        Threshold question:
        - When does following a clean rule become a way to avoid harder judgment?
