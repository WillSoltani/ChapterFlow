        Core claim:
        - A leader does not get to spend prior suffering as a coupon against future hard decisions; the job keeps demanding clear action even when the emotional reserves are thin.

        Distinct mechanism:
        - Horowitz uses repeated operational and financing shocks to show that leadership toughness is not one breakthrough moment but the ability to keep deciding under recurring strain.

        Required anchors:
        - Turnaround pressure continues after the first survival phase rather than ending cleanly.
- Strategic shifts carry emotional cost because the leader knows exactly how painful the next round may be.
- The chapter deepens the book's realism by showing that repetition itself is one of the hard things.

        Common misreadings:
        - Reading the chapter as merely another crisis recap instead of a lesson about cumulative leadership burden.
- Assuming experience makes later hard calls emotionally easy.

        Specific applications:
        - Audit how cumulative stress changes a leader's decision quality.
- Use repeated shocks to teach endurance without glamorizing exhaustion.
- Show why leaders need methods, not just adrenaline, for recurring crisis.

        Hard-edge requirement:
        - Hard mode must show how repeated pressure can distort judgment if the leader mistakes numbness for resilience.

        Threshold question:
        - How do you keep making clean decisions when the cost of the last round is still in your system?
