        Part: Main chapters
        Richness: high

        Canonical edition: 2014 first edition, HarperBusiness, ISBN 9780062273208
        Quote posture: paraphrase_first

        Core claim:
        - A company becomes durable when leaders invest in people development and product quality first, because profits depend on that operating order rather than replacing it.

        Distinct mechanism:
        - Horowitz links culture, training, hiring, management debt, and management quality assurance into one argument: if you neglect the people system, both product and profit decay later.

        Required anchors:
        - A good place to work: culture has to support performance, not just comfort.
- Training and executive hiring: startups cannot outsource managerial development forever.
- Management debt: neglected people systems eventually create compounding operating problems.

        Common misreadings:
        - Reading the chapter as anti-profit rather than as an argument about the order that makes profit durable.
- Reducing people care to perks while ignoring training, management quality, and role clarity.

        Specific applications:
        - Use training as a force multiplier instead of treating it as big-company overhead.
- Recognize management debt before it compounds into culture and execution failures.
- Tie people systems directly to product and business outcomes.

        Hard-edge requirement:
        - Hard mode must show how founders create expensive problems when they postpone management discipline because the startup still feels small.

        Threshold question:
        - What breaks first when a company grows faster than its management system?
