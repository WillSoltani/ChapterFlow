        Quote posture: paraphrase_first

        Core claim:
        - The hard thing in leadership is not ambition or planning but staying effective when the company is hurting and no painless option exists.

        Distinct mechanism:
        - Horowitz reframes the book as an operator's survival manual by contrasting polished business advice with the lonely, sleep-losing reality of running a company in crisis.

        Required anchors:
        - Opening contrast: management books often skip the truly painful parts of the job.
- The struggle frame: CEOs face fear, doubt, and brutal decisions without a formula that removes the burden.
- Lived-advice promise: the book will rely on lived startup experience rather than abstract leadership theory.

        Common misreadings:
        - Treating the introduction as generic startup inspiration instead of a contract about the kind of hard situations the book will face directly.
- Assuming the struggle is only emotional rather than operational, strategic, and managerial at the same time.

        Specific applications:
        - Recognize that leadership problems often begin where templates stop helping.
- Frame crisis management as a repeated judgment burden rather than a one-time trick.
- Use the struggle lens to evaluate later advice for practical honesty.

        Hard-edge requirement:
        - Hard mode must show why no framework can remove the CEO's responsibility once the company is in danger and every option hurts someone.

        Threshold question:
        - What do you do when the advice sounds clean but the real situation gives you only bad choices?
