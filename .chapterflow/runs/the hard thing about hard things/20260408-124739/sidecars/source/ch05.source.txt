        Core claim:
        - When the company is breaking, the CEO earns the role by facing ugly reality directly and making painful people decisions without false comfort or performative softness.

        Distinct mechanism:
        - Horowitz bundles specific crisis-management situations into one operating doctrine: tell it like it is, execute layoffs cleanly, fire or demote decisively when needed, and stop expecting anyone to rescue you.

        Required anchors:
        - The struggle: the private psychological burden of knowing the company can fail on your watch.
- Tell it like it is: truthful communication matters more in fear than polished reassurance.
- Layoffs and executive decisions: hard people calls must be made clearly, quickly, and without self-protective delay.

        Common misreadings:
        - Reducing the chapter to macho suffering instead of disciplined truth and responsibility.
- Treating tough people decisions as cruelty rather than clarity about the company's needs.

        Specific applications:
        - Use direct communication to stabilize a frightened team.
- Design layoffs and executive transitions around clarity, speed, and respect.
- Treat the struggle as a leadership burden to metabolize, not a reason to freeze.

        Hard-edge requirement:
        - Hard mode must show why delaying painful decisions to feel kinder often increases the damage for everyone involved.

        Threshold question:
        - What obligations remain when every available option harms someone and the leader still has to choose?
