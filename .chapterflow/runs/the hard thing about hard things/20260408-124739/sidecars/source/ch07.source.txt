        Core claim:
        - A growing company survives only if its leaders intentionally design the organization instead of assuming early-stage habits will scale on their own.

        Distinct mechanism:
        - Horowitz treats the going concern as an organizational-design challenge: minimize politics, formalize the right systems, and match executives to the company's actual stage and needs.

        Required anchors:
        - Politics and process: growth makes informal signals and incentives more dangerous.
- Executive fit is contextual: there is no universally great executive independent of company stage and need.
- Culture and operating cadence must be programmed deliberately as the company scales.

        Common misreadings:
        - Treating the chapter as a call for bureaucracy rather than for intentional design.
- Assuming culture programs itself if the founder's values are merely sincere.

        Specific applications:
        - Identify where informal startup habits are now creating political drift.
- Evaluate executives for fit against stage, not prestige alone.
- Use process to reduce ambiguity where growth has outpaced direct oversight.

        Hard-edge requirement:
        - Hard mode must show why refusing to formalize the right systems is not preserving startup agility but exporting confusion into a larger company.

        Threshold question:
        - Which small-company habit becomes dangerous once the company is too large for informal coordination alone?
