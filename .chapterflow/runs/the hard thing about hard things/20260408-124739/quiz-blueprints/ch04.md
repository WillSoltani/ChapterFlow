# Quiz Blueprint: ch04 "This Time with Feeling"

## Assessment Target
Test whether the learner can apply the chapter mechanism: making clean decisions during repeated pressure when emotional reserves are thin.

## What Must Be Measured
1. Learner can identify cumulative burden as distinct from one-time crisis response.
2. Learner can diagnose numbness being mistaken for resilience.
3. Learner can choose decision actions that preserve clarity under recurring strain.
4. Learner can separate surface compliance from real ownership.
5. Learner can anticipate consequences of repeated hard calls that are handled poorly.

## Core Quiz Items
### Item 1
- Type: multiple choice (mechanism).
- Prompt: A leader says, "We survived the last round, so this next decision should be easier." Which chapter claim does this violate?
- Correct idea: prior suffering does not reduce the burden of future hard decisions.
- Distractor pattern: experience automatically removes emotional cost.
- Explanation focus: repeated pressure changes conditions; it does not erase strain.

### Item 2
- Type: multiple choice (application).
- Prompt: In a second turnaround cycle, which action best protects decision quality?
- Correct idea: explicit tradeoff review plus accountability check before committing.
- Distractor pattern: rely on instinct because there is no time.
- Explanation focus: method beats fatigue-driven urgency.

### Item 3
- Type: short answer (diagnostic).
- Prompt: Give one sign that numbness is being mistaken for resilience.
- Expected elements: emotional detachment from consequence, rushed closure, or refusal to revisit assumptions.
- Explanation focus: apparent calm can hide degraded judgment.

### Item 4
- Type: scenario multiple choice (work).
- Prompt: Aria and Naveen face another painful strategic shift. Which choice fits chapter logic?
- Correct idea: acknowledge emotional residue and still run a structured decision process.
- Distractor pattern: suppress discussion of fatigue and force immediate alignment.
- Explanation focus: naming burden is compatible with decisive action.

### Item 5
- Type: scenario short answer (school).
- Prompt: In the capstone retro, what separates real ownership from surface compliance after a repeated setback?
- Expected elements: explicit responsibility, corrected process, and consequence-aware next step.
- Explanation focus: agreement language alone is not ownership.

### Item 6
- Type: scenario multiple choice (personal transfer).
- Prompt: After one hard family decision, another arrives quickly. What is the chapter-consistent response?
- Correct idea: pause to re-clarify stakes and tradeoffs before acting.
- Distractor pattern: avoid the decision to recover emotionally first with no timeline.
- Explanation focus: chapter calls for usable clarity under recurring strain, not avoidance.

## Medium Activation Prompt
- Prompt: Name one live decision you are delaying because you feel depleted from a previous round. What is the next concrete step you will take in the next 24 hours to restore decision clarity?
- Check rule: must require immediate use, not chapter summary.

## Medium Self-Check Prompts
- Question 1: Where in your current workload are you treating repeated pressure as proof that you should already have the answer?
- Question 2: Which tradeoff are you most likely to hide when you feel "done" with hard calls?

## Hard Self-Check Prompts (Two Tone-Object Slots)
- Slot A (`whatToDo`, direct pressure): Which decision process will you run this week to prevent fatigue from choosing for you?
- Slot B (`whyItMatters`, competitive pressure): If you skip that process, what specific downstream consequence is most likely?

## Hard Prediction Prompt
- Prompt: If a leader keeps confusing numbness with resilience across the next two hard decisions, what failure pattern is most likely to appear first, and what second-order consequence follows?
- Check rule: must ask for next move/consequence anticipation.

## Structured Recap Blueprint
- Retrieve: What is the core claim about prior suffering and future decisions?
- Connect: How do repeated shocks alter decision quality if no method is used?
- Preview: What additional crisis operating behaviors will be needed next as pressure escalates in Chapter 5?

## Review Card Set
- Card 1 (applied recall): Identify the chapter's threshold question and answer it with one concrete process step.
- Card 2 (transfer): Map the numbness-versus-resilience distinction onto a school or personal context.
- Card 3 (failure analysis): Describe a case where surface compliance looked like progress but increased later risk.
