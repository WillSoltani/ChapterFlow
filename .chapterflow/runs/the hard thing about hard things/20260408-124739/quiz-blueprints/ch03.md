# Chapter 03 Quiz Blueprint: "I Will Survive"

## Blueprint Objective
Assess whether the learner can apply survival leadership under collapse pressure, not merely restate chapter language.

## Competencies To Measure
- Distinguish disciplined survival work from denial or morale theater.
- Apply the financing-communication-endurance loop to constrained decisions.
- Detect when persistence is active management vs passive waiting.
- Predict likely consequences of avoiding truth under cash pressure.

## Question Set Architecture
- Total scored items: 12
- Mix:
  - 4 mechanism recognition items
  - 4 applied decision items
  - 2 misreading-correction items
  - 2 prediction items

## Item Blueprints

### 1) Mechanism Recognition (4 items)
- Prompt shape: choose which leader action best matches Chapter 3 survival discipline.
- Correct pattern: reality-first framing, constrained tradeoff, explicit next action.
- Distractor pattern:
  - optimism message without operational choice
  - delay framed as "waiting for clarity"
  - generic hustle language detached from cash/time pressure

### 2) Applied Decision (4 items)
- Prompt shape: short scenario with shrinking options; select best next move.
- Required signals in stem: limited runway, team uncertainty, imperfect information.
- Correct pattern: makes a hard call, communicates truth, preserves next decision window.
- Explanation requirement: name why the chosen action protects continuation better than alternatives.

### 3) Misreading Correction (2 items)
- Prompt shape: identify what is wrong with a flawed interpretation.
- Misread to test:
  - "survival means staying confident and refusing bad news"
  - "persistence means holding course until conditions improve"
- Correct pattern: survival requires active correction cycles and honest signal handling.

### 4) Prediction (2 items)
- Prompt shape: "If leader chooses X now, what is the most likely next consequence?"
- X options should include one truth-avoidant and one disciplined-survival action.
- Correct pattern: forecasts second-order effect on optionality, execution quality, or team trust.
- Rule: predictions must target next move/consequence, not vague future chapters.

## Difficulty Layering
- Easy: concrete scenario, one dominant correct action, low abstraction.
- Medium: competing plausible options; learner must identify mechanism.
- Hard: contradictory constraints where every option has cost; learner selects least damaging survival path and justifies tradeoff.

## Explanation Blueprint (All Scored Items)
- Include:
  - why correct answer fits chapter mechanism
  - why top distractor fails under survival conditions
  - one transfer line to work, school, or personal domain
- Avoid:
  - quote-memory explanations
  - generic leadership slogans

## Medium Activation Prompts (Not Scored)
- Prompt 1: Name one current obligation where you are treating hope as a plan. What decision loop would Chapter 3 require this week?
- Prompt 2: Where are you preserving comfort over truth in a constrained situation, and what explicit next decision would restore control?

## Self-Check Prompts
- Medium self-check question: Which recurring decision are you postponing that is already reducing your options?
- Hard self-check question (direct tone object): What hard fact are you refusing to communicate because you fear short-term fallout?
- Hard self-check question (competitive tone object): If conditions worsen tomorrow, which decision would expose that your current "persistence" has been passive?

## Structured Recap Blueprint
- retrieve: What three elements make Chapter 3 persistence operational rather than emotional?
- connect: How does truth-telling quality change survival odds when cash pressure is rising?
- preview: If one survival cycle succeeds, what new leadership strain tends to appear next?

## Review Card Blueprint (Applied Recall + Transfer)
- Card 1: A team says, "We just need belief." Ask for the missing survival action.
- Card 2: A leader delays bad-news communication for morale. Ask for likely two-step consequence.
- Card 3: A short runway forces a tradeoff between appearance and control. Ask for better decision criterion.
- Card 4: Transfer prompt: map Chapter 3 survival loop to a school governance crisis choice.
