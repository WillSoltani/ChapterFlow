# Quiz Blueprint

Chapter: Introduction

Core concepts to test:
- the struggle as the book's central leadership condition
- the gap between polished advice and real crisis leadership
- judgment under pressure versus formula-following
- realism without startup melodrama
- lived operator experience as the book's frame

Bloom ceiling:
Apply and analyze

Most natural harder question:
Ask the reader to distinguish between honest leadership under uncertainty and fake certainty that only sounds strong.

Most tempting misconception:
That the introduction is mostly motivational rather than a contract about what kind of leadership problems the book is actually addressing.

Previous chapter connection if supported:
None. This is the opening chapter.

Real-world scenario types to use:
- founder or manager under pressure after bad news
- school team facing a messy project setback
- personal conversation about why advice stopped helping

Question map:
- q01: Identify what the chapter says is actually hard about leadership.
- q02: Distinguish clean advice from the struggle the chapter centers.
- q03: Choose the response that best reflects judgment under pressure.
- q04: Reject the answer that treats confidence language as a substitute for a next move.
- q05: Apply the struggle idea to a school-team failure.
- q06: Apply the chapter to a workplace crisis with only bad options.
- q07: Distinguish realism from startup melodrama.
- q08: Explain why lived operator experience matters in this introduction.
- q09: Identify the most useful question after elegant advice stops fitting reality.
- q10: Select the transition that best prepares the reader for the next chapter's grounding story.
