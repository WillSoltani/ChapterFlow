# Quiz Blueprint

Chapter: From Communist to Venture Capitalist

Core concepts to test:
- operator formation through family, ideology, and environment
- technical apprenticeship before executive judgment
- biography as context for management advice
- transition from employee perspective to founder responsibility
- the difference between founder myth and operator formation

Bloom ceiling:
Apply and analyze

Most natural harder question:
Ask the reader to explain how a leader's background can sharpen judgment without making them automatically right in every later context.

Most tempting misconception:
That the chapter is mostly autobiographical color and does not matter to the later management lessons.

Previous chapter connection if supported:
The introduction defined the struggle. This chapter explains who is speaking and how that operator perspective was formed.

Real-world scenario types to use:
- work situation where a leader's prior training shapes a decision
- school-team leadership transition
- personal conversation about how background affects judgment

Question map:
- q01: Identify the chapter's main purpose in the book.
- q02: Distinguish operator formation from founder mythology.
- q03: Choose the best example of technical apprenticeship shaping later management judgment.
- q04: Apply the chapter to a workplace transition from specialist to leader.
- q05: Reject the answer that treats biography as automatic proof of good leadership.
- q06: Explain why ideological or family background matters here.
- q07: Connect the chapter to the later crisis chapters.
- q08: Detect the misconception that the chapter is just résumé material.
- q09: Choose the strongest explanation for why responsibility changes ambition.
- q10: Identify the best bridge from this chapter into the survival chapter that follows.
