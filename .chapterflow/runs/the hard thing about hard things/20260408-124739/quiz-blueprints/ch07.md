# Quiz Blueprint: ch07 - Concerning the Going Concern

## Blueprint Intent
- Test mechanism and application of Chapter 7, not trivia.
- Ensure every explanation teaches why the correct answer fits the chapter principle.
- Keep prompts source-bound to politics/process, stage-fit executives, and programmed culture/cadence.

## Learning Targets
- Distinguish intentional operating design from bureaucracy.
- Diagnose when informal coordination has become a risk.
- Apply stage-specific executive-fit logic to concrete decisions.
- Identify how culture drifts without explicit cadence and accountability.

## Easy Tier (Recall + Basic Application)
- Q1: Identify the chapter core claim in a short scenario where a team outgrows founder-side coordination.
- Q2: Choose why unmanaged informality increases politics at scale.
- Q3: Select the best reason executive fit cannot be judged as universally fixed.
- Q4: Pick the strongest signal that culture is being assumed rather than programmed.
- Q5: Separate "mission-serving process" from "administrative vanity process."
- Q6: Apply the threshold question to one small-company habit that has become dangerous.

## Medium Tier (Mechanism + Immediate Use)
- Q1 (scenario MC): work setting where two teams conflict on priorities; select the Chapter 7 move that reduces ambiguity and political drift.
- Q2 (scenario MC): school hackathon war room misalignment; choose the operating-cadence fix that creates real ownership.
- Activation prompt 1: what one operating decision would you implement this week to replace a risky informal habit?
- Activation prompt 2: where are incentives currently signaling politics instead of performance?
- Self-check prompt 1: which current process in your environment serves mission, and which one only signals control?
- Self-check prompt 2: where are you overvaluing executive prestige over stage fit?

## Hard Tier (Transfer + Prediction + Constraint Handling)
- Case question 1: evaluate a scaling company that avoids formalization in the name of agility; identify the hidden failure path and first corrective design move.
- Case question 2: choose between two executive candidates with different stage strengths; justify fit based on present constraints.
- Prediction prompt 1: if role clarity is delayed for one more quarter, what consequence appears first and why?
- Prediction prompt 2: if leadership keeps culture implicit, what next-order cross-team behavior emerges?
- Self-check tone slot A (`whatToDo`): what is the next concrete operating design decision you are avoiding?
- Self-check tone slot B (`whyItMatters`): what consequence do you expect if that decision is deferred again?

## Explanation Requirements
- Each answer explanation must tie back to one of the three anchors.
- Each explanation must include mechanism language (cause -> operating design choice -> consequence).
- Wrong-option rationale should expose a common misreading:
- confusing design with bureaucracy.
- assuming culture self-programs.
- assuming executive quality is context-free.

## Structured Recap Items
- Retrieve: what Chapter 7 principle explains why growth changes management rules?
- Connect: how does executive stage-fit logic interact with process design and culture cadence?
- Preview: what leadership challenge remains when systems exist but future direction is still uncertain?

## Review Cards (Applied Recall)
- Card 1: signal of political drift caused by informal scaling + corrective design move.
- Card 2: stage-fit executive mismatch example + better fit criteria.
- Card 3: culture drift symptom + cadence mechanism that corrects it.
- Card 4: "agility" claim stress test + boundary between flexibility and unmanaged ambiguity.
