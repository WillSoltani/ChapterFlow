## Chapter Identity
- Core claim: a growing company survives only if leaders intentionally design the organization rather than assuming early-stage habits will scale.
- Distinct mechanism: the going concern is an organizational-design problem, centered on reducing politics, formalizing the right systems, and matching executives to stage-specific needs.
- Hard-edge tension: refusing to formalize critical systems is not agility; it exports confusion into a larger company.
- Threshold question: which small-company habit becomes dangerous once informal coordination no longer scales?

## Required Anchors
- Politics and process: growth makes informal signals and incentives dangerous.
- Executive fit is contextual: no universally great executive independent of stage and need.
- Culture and operating cadence must be programmed deliberately during scale.

## Specific Applications To Carry Forward
- Detect where informal habits are creating political drift.
- Evaluate executives for stage fit, not prestige.
- Introduce process where growth has outpaced direct oversight.
