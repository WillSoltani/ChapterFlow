# Chapter Brief - ch06

## Chapter Identity
- Chapter: 6
- Title: Take Care of the People, the Products, and the Profits, In That Order
- Core claim: A company becomes durable when leaders invest in people development and product quality first, because profits depend on that operating order rather than replacing it.
- Distinct mechanism: Culture, training, hiring, management debt, and management quality assurance operate as one system. Neglecting the people system causes delayed product and profit decay.
- Threshold question: What breaks first when a company grows faster than its management system?

## Hard-Edge Tension (Locked)
- Primary tension: Care vs accountability.
- Required contradiction: Founders delay management discipline because the company still feels small, then pay for that delay through expensive downstream failures.
- Guardrail: Do not frame the chapter as anti-profit. Frame it as operating order for durable profit.

## Required Anchors (Locked)
- A good place to work: Culture must support performance, not just comfort.
- Training and executive hiring: Startup leaders cannot outsource managerial development forever.
- Management debt: Neglected people systems compound into operating, culture, and execution problems.

## Specific Applications (Locked)
- Training as force multiplier, not big-company overhead.
- Early recognition of management debt before compounding damage.
- Explicit linkage between people systems and product/business outcomes.

## Common Misreadings To Prevent
- Misreading 1: "People first" means profit does not matter.
- Correction: Profit remains required; the chapter argues for sequencing that sustains it.
- Misreading 2: "Care" means perks and comfort.
- Correction: The chapter defines care as training, management quality, role clarity, and performance culture.
