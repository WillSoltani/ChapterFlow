## Principle Lock
- Core claim: Entrepreneurship resists universal rules because leaders constantly face tradeoffs that only make sense in context, not in abstract best-practice slogans.
- Distinct mechanism: Horowitz uses acquisition pressure, accountability-versus-creativity tension, and people-management paradoxes to argue that judgment beats rigid adherence to generic rules.
- Threshold question: When does following a clean rule become a way to avoid harder judgment?
- Limit boundary: Discipline stays required even while rigid formula-thinking is rejected.
- Hard-edge tension: Leaders fail when they cling to a rule that fits the wrong context more neatly than the real one.

## Required Anchors
1. Deals and operating outcomes remain contingent deep into the process, so certainty is fragile.
2. Accountability versus creativity is a live paradox, not a rule with a clean permanent answer.
3. The chapter broadens the book's anti-formula stance by making context-sensitive judgment central.

## Application Targets
- Distinguish principles from rigid rules when the current problem is context-specific.
- Hold accountability and creativity together without pretending the tension disappears.
- Use judgment to choose the right tradeoff instead of outsourcing thought to a slogan.

## Misreading Guards
- Reject treating the chapter as anti-discipline instead of anti-rigidity.
- Reject reading "no rules" as permission to avoid standards or accountability.
- Reject chapter-generic startup advice that ignores contingent deal flow and people-management paradoxes.

## Chapter Positioning

- Bridge to Chapter 10: After the no-rules logic is established, the book closes by looking back on what remains after the operating story is complete.
