        ## Chapter Order And Intent

        1. `Introduction`
   - intent: Open the book by defining the struggle of leadership and rejecting management advice that only covers clean, easy situations.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: moderate
   - premium-routing risk: high
2. `From Communist to Venture Capitalist`
   - intent: Ground Horowitz's management voice in the personal and professional path that led him from family ideology and engineering into startup leadership.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: moderate
   - premium-routing risk: low
3. `"I Will Survive"`
   - intent: Show how startup survival looks when the market breaks, the cash situation turns ugly, and the leader must keep the company alive anyway.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: high
   - premium-routing risk: low
4. `This Time with Feeling`
   - intent: Show how leadership pressure compounds when one crisis leads directly into another and hard choices have to be made again with less emotional margin.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: high
   - premium-routing risk: low
5. `When Things Fall Apart`
   - intent: Make the CEO's crisis job concrete through the book's most operational chapter on layoffs, executive decisions, truth-telling, and the struggle.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: high
   - premium-routing risk: high
6. `Take Care of the People, the Products, and the Profits, In That Order`
   - intent: Explain how company building works after survival by prioritizing people quality, product integrity, and the managerial systems that support both.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: moderate
   - premium-routing risk: low
7. `Concerning the Going Concern`
   - intent: Show how scaling a company changes the rules by introducing politics, process, executive fit, and cultural programming problems that founders cannot ignore.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: moderate
   - premium-routing risk: high
8. `How to Lead Even When You Don't Know Where You Are Going`
   - intent: Explain how CEOs lead under uncertainty by managing their own psychology, setting direction, and remaining credible even when the path is not fully visible.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: high
   - premium-routing risk: high
9. `First Rule of Entrepreneurship: There Are No Rules`
   - intent: Reject formula worship by showing that startup leadership often means solving paradoxes and making case-specific judgments that no tidy rulebook can settle for you.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: high
   - premium-routing risk: low
10. `The End of the Beginning`
   - intent: Close the book by turning the end of Horowitz's operating story into a reflection on transition, acquisition, and what hard-won lessons remain after the crisis era ends.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: moderate
   - premium-routing risk: low
