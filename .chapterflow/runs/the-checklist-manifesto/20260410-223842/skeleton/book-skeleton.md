# Book Skeleton — The Checklist Manifesto

## Metadata
- title: `The Checklist Manifesto: How to Get Things Right`
- author: `Atul Gawande`
- canonical edition: `2010 English first-edition family`
- package chapter count: `9`
- wave size: `2`
- source posture: `official metadata + authorized preview chapter map + secondary chapter summaries`

## Governing voice guardrails for the whole book
- Keep the book procedural, humane, and concrete rather than preachy.
- Preserve the difference between expertise and coordinated reliability.
- Keep aviation, medicine, construction, emergency response, and investing distinct as examples.
- Name the cost of failure without turning the prose melodramatic.

## Chapter order with one-line intents

| # | Title | One-line intent | Source richness | Concept density | Moral flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Problem of Extreme Complexity | Show why modern professional work outgrew lone mastery and became vulnerable to avoidable failure. | high | high | medium |

| 9 | The Save | Extend the checklist logic beyond surgery and close on adoption, judgment, and the practical value of routine discipline. | medium | medium | medium |

## Motif / callback opportunities
- avoidable failure versus complexity should recur as a diagnostic, not a slogan
- all-or-none processes and communication failures should echo across chapters 1 through 7
- the lone expert versus coordinated team line should build from chapters 1, 3, 7, and 8
- checklist quality, brevity, and timing should connect chapters 2, 4, 6, and 7
- the broader-domain adoption question should land in chapters 8 and 9 without feeling like a business-book appendix

## School-setting plan
- ch01: medical-systems seminar mapping why complexity overwhelms memory
- ch02: aviation safety class debating why trained experts still miss simple steps
- ch03: engineering management workshop on who coordinates when no master builder remains
- ch04: public-policy class comparing Katrina response with decentralized execution
- ch05: surgical-improvement lab reviewing an early checklist pilot
- ch06: design course critiquing good versus bad checklist construction
- ch07: global-health seminar comparing rollout conditions across hospitals
- ch08: professional ethics class debating pride, autonomy, and team discipline
- ch09: business-and-medicine crossover seminar on checklist transfer beyond surgery

## Vocabulary watchlist
- keep precise: checklist, complexity, coordination, communication, omission, discipline, verification, pause, team, failure mode
- use carefully: expertise, autonomy, hero, routine, culture, compliance
- avoid overusing: optimization, innovation, disruption, leverage, excellence

## Thin-chapter risk notes
- `ch01`: can drift into generic complexity talk if the concrete medical stakes disappear.
- `ch04`: can become a vague decentralization sermon if the checklist role is not named clearly.
- `ch05`: source support is thinner; stay narrow and procedural.
- `ch08`: can become a culture essay unless it stays tied to checklist adoption and resistance.
- `ch09`: closing applications can sprawl if the book's practical restraint is lost.

## Premium-routing candidates
- `ch01` because it sets the full complexity frame
- `ch03` because the master-builder shift carries a lot of structural logic
- `ch06` because checklist-design quality must stay specific
- `ch07` because the empirical test anchors the whole case
- `ch08` because the hero-versus-team tension is easy to genericize

## Cross-chapter tensions to preserve
- mastery versus coordination
- memory versus routine safeguards
- autonomy versus disciplined pause points
- local judgment versus centralized control
- heroics versus reliable teamwork
