Chapter Promise:
Show why a validated checklist still meets resistance and how the chapter reframes professionalism around disciplined teamwork rather than lone-hero identity.

Opening Move:
Open on the discomfort professionals feel when a checklist seems to question status, judgment, or autonomy.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: frame the move from proven effectiveness to adoption resistance
- P2: explain why checklist pushback is tied to ego and professional identity
- P3: show how the hero story makes disciplined pauses feel insulting
- P4: argue that teamwork is the chapter's answer to that resistance
- P5: clarify what teamwork does and does not mean for judgment and expertise
- P6: bridge toward broader-domain adoption in the final chapter

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish hostility to checklists as a workflow complaint from hostility rooted in professional identity. Its mechanism is the clash between lone-hero self-image and team-based reliability.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Keep alive the pressure between honoring excellence and rejecting the fantasy that excellence means operating above shared discipline.

Threshold Question:
Why does a proven checklist still feel threatening to experts who already see themselves as highly competent?
