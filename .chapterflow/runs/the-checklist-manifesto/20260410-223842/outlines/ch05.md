Chapter Promise:
Show that the first real checklist attempt matters precisely because it is imperfect and has to be refined in contact with real practice.

Opening Move:
Open with the shift from clean design logic to messy first implementation so the reader feels the difference between a good idea and a usable tool.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: move from Chapter 4's abstract idea into the first practical pilot
- P2: describe the first version as promising but awkward in real use
- P3: show why professional resistance can reveal design friction rather than simple stubbornness
- P4: explain the need for revision, trimming, and better timing
- P5: argue that credibility grows when the tool adapts to the room without losing its core purpose
- P6: bridge to the next chapter's focus on checklist design discipline

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish first-version failure from idea failure. Its mechanism is iterative refinement under real working conditions.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Keep alive the danger that revision can either sharpen a checklist or weaken it if the room strips away the disciplined pause it needs.

Threshold Question:
How do you make a checklist workable enough for serious professionals to use without sanding away the exact discipline that makes it useful?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: first implementation reveals friction that abstract design cannot see
- Takeaway 2: resistance can reflect real usability problems rather than mere ego
- Takeaway 3: iteration is part of responsible checklist building
- Takeaway 4: credibility grows when the tool respects local workflow without surrendering its purpose
- Takeaway 5: the next design question is how to build shorter, sharper lists that survive real use

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (contrast, work): teaches the difference between a clean pilot idea and a clumsy first rollout
- Scenario 2 (dilemma, school): teaches why student testers push back on a procedure that interrupts flow
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): teaches why a family planning tool has to be revised after first use
- Scenario 4 (vignette, work): teaches how a team trims a draft process after seeing where people stall
- Scenario 5 (inner_monologue, school): teaches why skepticism can reveal friction rather than laziness
- Scenario 6 (decision_point, personal): teaches how to keep the key pause while simplifying the routine

Scenario Vividness Targets:
- Scenario 1: pre-op room, paper draft, and impatient glances

Preview Constraint:
The preview must point toward design discipline and better checklist construction, not later trial results.

Genericity Risks:
- turning the chapter into generic change-management talk
- treating all resistance as vanity
- skipping the revision logic and calling the first try a proof either way
