Chapter Promise:
Show why modern expert work no longer fits the old master-builder model and now depends on disciplined coordination across distributed specialists.

Opening Move:
Open on the simple / complicated / complex distinction so the reader can feel why some work exceeds what one expert can safely command.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define simple, complicated, and complex problems and explain why the distinction matters
- P2: show why modern professional work moved from difficult-but-commandable toward dense interdependence
- P3: introduce Russia Wharf as a live example of a project no single expert can fully hold
- P4: explain how specialists, handoffs, and communication replace old centralized mastery
- P5: connect that organizational reality back to medicine and the need for visible coordination tools
- P6: bridge to the next chapter by asking what kind of checklist idea can support distributed authority without collapsing into control theater

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes work that one expert can command from work that must be coordinated across many expert slices. Its mechanism is not less expertise but more explicit communication, alignment, and distributed execution.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Keep alive the tension between decentralization and accountability. The hard mode should preserve the difficulty of sharing authority without creating drift, confusion, or leaderless ritual.

Threshold Question:
If no modern master builder can carry the whole field, what replaces that figure without making the system sloppy or overcontrolled?
