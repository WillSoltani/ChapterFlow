# Style Memory — compact

Compiled from the v13 style benchmark files and adapted to the frozen Checklist Manifesto source bundle.

## Voice
- Write like a sharp human explainer, not a hospital policy memo.
- Keep the prose concrete, procedural, and alive to stakes.
- Let medicine, aviation, construction, and investing stay distinct instead of collapsing into one generic complexity sermon.
- Use `you` only when it sharpens application.

## Grade bands
- Easy: clear doorway into the chapter's live problem.
- Medium: mechanism, friction, and practical discipline.
- Hard: limit, boundary, cost, or contradiction that keeps the chapter from turning into checklist evangelism.

## Hard constraints
- No invented cases, statistics, or named professionals beyond the brief and source sidecar.
- No long quotations.
- No thesis-first or slogan-first openings.
- No em dashes.
- No management-consulting filler.

## Book-specific guardrails
- Keep the book's respect for checklists tied to complexity, coordination, and discipline, not blind rule worship.
- Preserve Gawande's recurring contrast between lone expertise and organized teamwork.
- When support is thin, choose narrower claims over broader claims.
