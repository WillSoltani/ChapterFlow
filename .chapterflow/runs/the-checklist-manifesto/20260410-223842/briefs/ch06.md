Core Claim:
The chapter argues that good checklists are engineered, not merely brainstormed. Gawande uses aviation, Dan Boorman, Boeing manuals, and simulator learning to show that strong lists are short, timed well, and built around points where human memory or coordination is most likely to fail. The deeper point is that bad checklists can be bloated, vague, or mistimed, while good ones are disciplined tools shaped by repeated use and design judgment.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Unlike Chapter 5, which showed the pain of the first pilot, Chapter 6 explains what expert checklist builders actually learn from that pain. Its pressure is craft: the difference between a checklist that helps a room and a checklist that adds dead weight.
