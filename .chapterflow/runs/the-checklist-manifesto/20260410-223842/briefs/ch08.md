Core Claim:
The chapter argues that checklist adoption is resisted not only for practical reasons but also because it challenges professional identity. Gawande uses resistance to checklist use, ego, and the lone-hero myth to argue that modern excellence depends on disciplined teamwork rather than solitary brilliance alone. The deeper point is that the checklist threatens a story professionals tell about themselves, even when the tool improves reliability.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Unlike Chapter 7, which asked whether the checklist works across hospitals, Chapter 8 asks why people still resist it. Its pressure is moral and professional: the checklist exposes how pride, autonomy, and teamwork collide.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author turns from proof of effectiveness to the problem of why adoption is still hard.
Then the author ties resistance to ego, professional identity, and the appeal of the hero story.
Then the author argues that serious professionalism in complex work now means disciplined teamwork rather than lone-hero performance.
