Core Claim:
The chapter argues that a carefully built checklist has to survive field testing across very different hospitals. Gawande uses the Do-Confirm versus Read-Do distinction, the WHO 19-item checklist, and the eight-hospital test to show that disciplined checklist design can travel across variation without becoming empty theater. The chapter's deeper point is that a checklist matters most when it still works under differing habits, teams, and local constraints.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Unlike Chapter 6, which explained how expert builders design a good checklist, Chapter 7 asks whether that design survives contact with many real sites. Its pressure is evidence: the book's argument has to face variation, not just craft theory.
