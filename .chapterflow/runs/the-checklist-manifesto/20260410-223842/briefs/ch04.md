Core Claim:
The chapter argues that good checklists in complex environments do not centralize every decision. They decentralize action while tightening communication. Gawande uses Katrina versus Wal-Mart to show that large systems fail when authority is trapped at the center and information does not move clearly across the field. The chapter's idea is that a checklist can create disciplined pause points, shared language, and clear coordination without trying to replace local judgment.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 4 is not mainly about whether checklists work. It is about what kind of checklist works in a distributed system. Its pressure is design logic: local action plus tighter communication, not bureaucratic overcontrol.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author contrasts centralized failure with decentralized response.
Then the author uses Katrina and Wal-Mart to show how execution improves when people can act locally while staying connected through shared checks and communication.
Then the author clarifies that a strong checklist is a coordination device, not a script that tries to think for the whole system.
