Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why pleasure and enjoyment are different qualities of experience, what elements tend to make enjoyment possible, and why autotelic activity matters.

Opening Move:
Open on a familiar mismatch: some easy experiences feel pleasant in the moment but thin in memory, while demanding experiences can feel fuller and more worth having.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): pleasure versus enjoyment in P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): elements of enjoyment in P3-P5
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): autotelic experience in P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: separate pleasure from enjoyment without shaming pleasure
- P2: show why challenge changes the felt quality of life
- P3: introduce goals and feedback as structure for enjoyment
- P4: explain concentration and control as lived features of absorption
- P5: show altered time and reduced self-consciousness as consequences rather than a mystical trick
- P6: define the autotelic experience and why intrinsic worth matters
- P7 if needed: bridge into the conditions that make flow more likely

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The core distinction is pleasure versus enjoyment, with enjoyment tied to structured challenge rather than passive relief.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep enjoyment from becoming a rigid checklist while still making the recurring elements concrete enough to use.

Threshold Question:
What makes one experience briefly pleasant and another experience enlarging enough to remember and seek again?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: pleasure is not the same as enjoyment
- Takeaway 2: enjoyment tends to involve challenge-skill fit
- Takeaway 3: goals and feedback help attention stay ordered
- Takeaway 4: concentration and control change the quality of action
- Takeaway 5: time and self-consciousness can shift in absorption
- Takeaway 6: autotelic experience is worth doing for its own sake

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (comparison, work): comparing a pleasant break with a demanding task that felt fuller
- Scenario 2 (decision_point, work): choosing between easy reward and meaningful challenge
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, school): explaining why one rehearsal or studio session felt more alive than passive downtime
- Scenario 4 (before_after, school): showing how clear goals and feedback changed a practice block
- Scenario 5 (predict_reveal, personal): predicting what happens when a hobby becomes all reward-tracking and no absorption
- Scenario 6 (dilemma, personal): deciding whether to keep optimizing for comfort or for richer involvement

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, specify one sensory or situational detail to include.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must point toward the conditions and activity-types that make flow more likely in Chapter 4.

Genericity Risks:
- treating enjoyment as vague enthusiasm
- turning the elements into a sterile checklist
- making autotelic experience sound like generic passion rhetoric
