Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why better circumstances do not automatically produce happiness and why ordered experience matters more than passive comfort.

Opening Move:
Open on the old promise that more progress should have made life feel settled, then show the friction between abundance and actual dissatisfaction.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): Aristotle in P1 as the framing question
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): modern abundance paradox in P1-P2
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): rising expectations and cultural shields in P3-P4

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: anchor the paradox of happiness with Aristotle and modern abundance
- P2: mechanism showing why external gains fail to settle inner life
- P3: explain rising expectations as a treadmill rather than a finish line
- P4: explain cultural shields and why inherited meaning systems no longer automatically hold
- P5: implication that control of consciousness matters more than passive luck
- P6: bridge into flow as optimal experience and into Chapter 2's anatomy of consciousness

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
External conditions can improve without producing inner order; the governing distinction is passive pleasure versus actively organized experience.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The hard layer must preserve the tension between structural limits outside the self and the author's insistence that consciousness still remains the usable lever.

Threshold Question:
How much of a life can actually be reordered from within when the world keeps sending disorder from without?
