Chapter 3: Enjoyment and the Quality of Life

Frozen support:
- WorldCat headings: Pleasure and enjoyment; The elements of enjoyment; The autotelic experience
- Secondary freeze: the chapter distinguishes pleasure from enjoyment; enjoyment is tied to challenge-skill balance, clear goals, feedback, concentration, control, loss of self-consciousness, and altered sense of time; the autotelic experience is worth doing for its own sake rather than for an external payoff

Safe chapter-level claims:
- Pleasure and enjoyment are not the same thing.
- Enjoyment tends to involve ordered challenge rather than passive satisfaction.
- The chapter identifies recurring elements of enjoyable experience, including goals, feedback, concentration, and a felt sense of control.
- Flow becomes more concrete here as an autotelic experience: an activity can be worth doing for its own sake.
- Time and self-consciousness can feel altered when attention is fully absorbed.

Support limits:
- Do not turn the elements into a rigid numbered formula unless the frozen support clearly justifies it.
- Do not overclaim that every enjoyable activity contains every element at the same intensity.
- Do not treat pleasure as worthless; the distinction is narrower than moral condemnation.
