# Book Skeleton

## Metadata

- Book: `Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience`
- Author: `Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi`
- Book ID: `flow`
- Run ID: `20260412-184712`
- Locked edition: `Harper Perennial Modern Classics (2008 working edition; chapter structure cross-checked to 1990 first edition)`
- Chapter count: `10`

## Chapter Order

1. `Happiness Revisited`
   Intent: establish why happiness is not secured by external conditions and why control of experience becomes the real problem.
   Source richness: medium
   Concept density: medium
   Moral complexity: low
   Thin-chapter risk: low
2. `The Anatomy of Consciousness`
   Intent: define attention, psychic energy, entropy, flow, and self-growth as the architecture underneath the rest of the book.
   Source richness: medium-high
   Concept density: high
   Moral complexity: low
   Thin-chapter risk: low
3. `Enjoyment and the Quality of Life`
   Intent: distinguish pleasure from enjoyment and clarify the elements that make flow possible.
   Source richness: high
   Concept density: high
   Moral complexity: medium because flow can serve harmful pursuits
   Thin-chapter risk: low
4. `The Conditions of Flow`
   Intent: show which activities, environments, and personal traits make flow easier or harder to generate.
   Source richness: medium-high
   Concept density: medium-high
   Moral complexity: medium
   Thin-chapter risk: low
5. `The Body in Flow`
   Intent: move flow from abstraction into movement, sensation, sexuality, discipline, and trained bodily attention.
   Source richness: medium
   Concept density: medium
   Moral complexity: medium
   Thin-chapter risk: medium
6. `The Flow of Thought`
   Intent: show how memory, language, history, science, and philosophy can be organized into mentally absorbing practices.
   Source richness: medium
   Concept density: medium-high
   Moral complexity: low
   Thin-chapter risk: medium
7. `Work as Flow`
   Intent: argue that work can become one of the strongest sites of flow even though people often expect leisure to do that job.
   Source richness: medium
   Concept density: medium
   Moral complexity: medium because work conditions differ sharply across people
   Thin-chapter risk: low
8. `Enjoying Solitude and Other People`
   Intent: show that both being alone and being with others require skill if they are going to become ordered, enjoyable experiences.
   Source richness: medium
   Concept density: medium
   Moral complexity: medium
   Thin-chapter risk: low
9. `Cheating Chaos`
   Intent: explain how some people transform adversity into order rather than letting hardship dictate the structure of consciousness.
   Source richness: medium
   Concept density: high
   Moral complexity: high because suffering cannot be romanticized
   Thin-chapter risk: medium
10. `The Making of Meaning`
   Intent: unify the earlier chapters by arguing that isolated flow episodes are insufficient without a coherent life theme or purpose.
   Source richness: medium
   Concept density: high
   Moral complexity: high
   Thin-chapter risk: low

## Rotation Plan For Examples

- Canonical format rotation per chapter: `decision_point`, `postmortem`, `dialogue`, `predict_reveal`, `dilemma`, `before_after`
- Category split per chapter: `2 work / 2 school / 2 personal`
- Early chapters should favor attention, boredom, anxiety, and skill-matching pressure.
- Middle chapters should move into bodily practice, study, craft, and workplace redesign.
- Late chapters should carry adversity, meaning, solitude, family, and life-theme integration.

## School-Setting Plan

- Rotate settings across seminar room, lab, library, rehearsal room, athletic practice, group project, office-hours conversation, and student apartment.
- Avoid repeating the same school environment in adjacent chapters.

Guardrail: do not let these become reusable filler stems. Each chapter needs a distinct mechanism, not just repeated terminology.

## Motif / Callback Opportunities

- attention as scarce psychic energy
- boredom vs anxiety as twin failure states
- control without rigidity
- complexity as growth through differentiation and integration
- leisure paradox versus work paradox
- shifting from isolated episodes of order to a whole-life pattern of meaning

## Thin-Chapter Risk Notes

- Ch. 5 can drift into list-like examples if the bodily disciplines are not organized around one clear distinction.
- Ch. 6 can become a catalog of mental activities unless the chapter keeps returning to self-directed order.
- Ch. 9 must not romanticize suffering or imply that tragedy is good because it can be transformed.

## Premium-Routing Candidates

- Ch. 2 because the conceptual load is high and later chapters depend on its precision.
- Ch. 3 because the pleasure-versus-enjoyment distinction and the flow conditions are core to the book.
- Ch. 9 because the moral and tonal discipline needs to stay tight.
- Ch. 10 because the final synthesis can easily flatten into generic purpose talk.
