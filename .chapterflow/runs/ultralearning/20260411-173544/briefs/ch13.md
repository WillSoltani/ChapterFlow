Core Claim:
The first ultralearning project is where the book's principles move from abstraction into committed practice. Scott Young uses this chapter to push the learner from admiration into execution with a project that matters, has concrete direction, and competes seriously for time.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the start-now chapter. If it loses the demand for a real project with commitment and concrete direction, it collapses into generic motivation.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author turns the prior principles into a practical threshold for action.
Then the author frames a first project as serious, time-protected, and specific enough to guide effort.
Then the author keeps the chapter realistic by acknowledging that ultralearning projects are demanding and should matter.

Required Anchors:
- ultralearning projects are deep self-education for learning hard things in less time
- ultralearning is the opposite of dabbling because it has commitment, protected time, and concrete direction
