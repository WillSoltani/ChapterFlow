Core Claim:
Retention means avoiding fast forgetting so learning does not leak away after it is gained. Scott Young uses this principle to challenge the idea that learning ends once something is understood once. The chapter matters because gains can decay quickly if the learner never returns to hold them in place.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the anti-leakage chapter. If it loses the problem of forgetting over time, it collapses into generic repetition advice.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that learning can fade quickly when it is not revisited.
Then the author shows why one-time understanding is weaker than knowledge that can still be retained later.
Then the author keeps the principle practical by focusing on actions that keep learning from leaking away.
