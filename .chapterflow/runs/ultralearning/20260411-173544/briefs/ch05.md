Core Claim:
Focus is the principle that protects concentration long enough for ultralearning to work. Scott Young treats it less as inspiration and more as a practical problem of starting, sustaining, and protecting attention. The chapter matters because even a well-mapped project can stall if the learner cannot repeatedly bring full attention to the work.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the attention-protection chapter. If it loses the start-sustain-protect concentration frame, it collapses into generic advice about avoiding distraction.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author identifies concentration as a necessary condition for hard self-education.
Then the author separates focus into starting, sustaining, and protecting attention.
Then the author keeps the principle practical by treating focus as trainable structure rather than mood.
