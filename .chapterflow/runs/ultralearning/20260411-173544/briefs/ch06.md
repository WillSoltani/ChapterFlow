Core Claim:
Directness means practicing in the real context of the target skill instead of staying inside safer proxies. Scott Young uses this principle to argue that transfer improves when learning looks more like the performance it is meant to support. The chapter matters because learners often choose comfortable preparation that keeps them away from the actual arena they want to succeed in.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the transfer-and-target-context chapter. If it loses the real-context anchor, it collapses into generic advice to practice more.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that practice should move closer to the real situation the learner cares about.
Then the author shows how indirect preparation can feel productive while weakening transfer.
Then the author keeps the principle practical by treating directness as something to increase, not as an all-or-nothing purity test.
