Core Claim:
Retrieval means testing to learn rather than rereading passively. Scott Young uses this principle to push learning out of recognition and into recall. The chapter matters because passive review can feel smooth while hiding whether the learner can actually bring knowledge back when needed.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the active recall chapter. If it loses the contrast between testing and passive rereading, it collapses into generic study advice.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that learning deepens when the learner tries to pull knowledge back out rather than only looking at it again.
Then the author contrasts retrieval with passive rereading that can feel familiar without proving usable recall.
Then the author keeps the principle practical by treating tests as learning tools instead of only as judgment devices.

Required Anchors:

Counterarguments or Limits:
