Core Claim:
Feedback means exposing work to correction rather than dodging it. Scott Young uses this principle to keep learning from staying comfortable but blind. The chapter matters because a learner can keep practicing while avoiding the responses that would show what is still weak, off, or missing.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the correction-exposure chapter. If it loses the idea of facing corrective information instead of hiding from it, it collapses into vague advice about getting opinions.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that learning improves when the learner receives information that answers back against the work.
Then the author shows that people often dodge feedback because it is uncomfortable or ego-threatening.
Then the author keeps the principle practical by treating feedback as something to seek and use rather than something to fear and avoid.

Required Anchors:

- correction sharpens learning when the work is actually put in front of it

Counterarguments or Limits:

- the chapter is about not dodging correction, not about becoming passive before every opinion
