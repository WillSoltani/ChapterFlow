Core Claim:
Intuition means understanding deeply before stacking more knowledge on top. Scott Young uses this principle to challenge shallow accumulation without real grasp. The chapter matters because a learner can keep adding facts or procedures while still missing the underlying understanding that would make the skill more flexible and usable.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the depth-before-accumulation chapter. If it loses the demand for deeper understanding, it collapses into generic “understand better” advice.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that piling on more knowledge can be weak if the existing understanding is shallow.
Then the author shows why digging into the underlying meaning of a skill can make later learning stronger.
Then the author keeps the principle practical by focusing on depth before more accumulation.
