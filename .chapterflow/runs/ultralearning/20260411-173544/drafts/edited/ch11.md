Scott Young turns to intuition once the learner has protected gains from leakage. The next problem is different: a learner can keep adding more material while understanding remains thin underneath it. His principle is simple. Intuition means understanding deeply before stacking more knowledge. The chapter matters because shallow accumulation can make later learning look larger without making it stronger.

That is the build-up problem. New layers rest on whatever is already there. If the underlying understanding is weak, the added material can become brittle, confusing, or harder to use flexibly. More information is not always the next right move.

Young uses intuition to slow the learner down at the right moment. If a procedure is being repeated without real grasp, or a concept is being carried forward without understanding why it works, then piling on more may only multiply the weakness. Digging deeper changes the foundation that later learning will stand on.

This is why shallow fluency can mislead. A learner can execute a step, repeat a phrase, or pass through familiar material and still break down when variation appears or a deeper question is asked. The chapter pushes against that surface competence. What matters is not only whether the learner can continue. It is whether the learner understands enough for later learning to rest on something solid.

The principle still needs a boundary. Intuition does not mean endless abstraction or wandering away from the real skill. Depth matters because it changes performance, transfer, and later learning. If understanding never reconnects to the work itself, the digging has drifted.

That balance keeps the chapter practical. Young is not rejecting more knowledge. He is rejecting more knowledge built on a base the learner has not really understood. Dig deep before building up means strengthen the structure first, then add the next layer on something worthy of supporting it.

Once the learner has a deeper grasp instead of only more layers, another question becomes possible. How does the learner move beyond defaults and try alternatives that might work even better? That is where experimentation enters.
