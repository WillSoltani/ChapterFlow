Scott Young turns to feedback once the learner is testing more honestly. Retrieval can show whether an answer comes back, but another question still remains: what happens when the work is exposed to correction? Young's principle is blunt. Feedback means exposing work to correction rather than dodging it. The chapter matters because learners often keep practicing in ways that protect them from the very responses that would show what is still wrong.

That protection can look respectable. A learner keeps polishing privately, keeps extending preparation, keeps telling the story that one more round alone will make outside judgment easier later. Sometimes that is partly true. Often it also keeps recurring problems hidden. If the work never meets correction, the learner may keep strengthening errors alongside strengths.

Young uses feedback to break that shelter. The point is not generic encouragement or random opinion collecting. The point is to put real work where it can answer back against the learner's blind spots. Correction hurts for a reason: it removes the private version of the work and replaces it with a public test of what actually survives outside comfort.

This is why people dodge feedback. It threatens confidence, exposes unfinished parts, and can make effort feel suddenly questionable. Yet that sting is exactly why the chapter matters. If the learner keeps organizing practice to avoid correction, the same weaknesses can stay protected for far too long.

The principle still needs a boundary. Not every comment is equally useful, and not every harsh response deserves obedience. Feedback helps when it connects to the target performance and reveals something the learner can use. Noise is real. But noise does not justify hiding from correction altogether. The learner still has to expose the work and sort signal from distraction.

That balance keeps the chapter demanding without making it passive. Young is not asking the learner to become obedient to every reaction. He is asking the learner to stop building routines that dodge the punches entirely. Expose the work, absorb the correction that actually sharpens it, and let response change practice before the error hardens further.

Once correction is being faced more directly, another challenge emerges. Even useful learning can leak away if it is not held over time. That is where retention enters.
