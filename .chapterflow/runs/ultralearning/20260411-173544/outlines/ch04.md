Chapter Promise:
Show why an ultralearning project needs a map of why, what, and how before intensity starts burning effort in the wrong places.

Opening Move:
Open on the danger of starting hard without orientation rather than on a generic praise of research.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 why-what-how map
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 map before sprint and during the project
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 research as support rather than delay

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: tension
- P2: mechanism
- P3: anchor
- P4: implication
- P5: limit
- P6: boundary
- P7 if needed: bridge

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Metalearning does not replace learning. It orients learning by clarifying purpose, content, and method.

Hard-Depth Tension:
A map helps the learner move intelligently, but mapping can turn into delay if it never yields contact with the subject.

Threshold Question:
How much map is enough to guide the project before map-making becomes another way to postpone difficulty?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: why matters because stakes shape commitment
- Takeaway 2: what matters because a skill has parts
- Takeaway 3: how matters because methods should fit the target
- Takeaway 4: maps are revised during the project
- Takeaway 5: research becomes weak when it delays contact

- Scenario 4 (reset_moment, work): project restarts after method mismatch
