Chapter Promise:
Show that ultralearning becomes credible when a learner turns a vague ambition into a specific, benchmarked mission under real constraints.

Opening Move:
Open on the MIT Challenge as a loaded question rather than a victory lap: can one person seriously approximate MIT's computer science curriculum without admission, tuition, or the standard four-year pace?

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 challenge setup, one-year clock, MIT curriculum pressure
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 33 classes, final exams, programming projects, self-imposed rules
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 why this matters beyond stunt value, learning versus credentials, low-cost self-direction

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Ultralearning is not simply trying hard. It is self-directed learning made serious by mission design, real benchmarks, and the willingness to be judged against an external standard.

Hard-Depth Tension:
A self-directed project can be rigorous enough to teach deeply while still failing to replace the social and credential value of institutions. Hard depth must keep that asymmetry visible.

Threshold Question:
What makes a self-directed learning project count as serious education rather than impressive dabbling?

Takeaway Count Lock:

- Takeaway 1: ultralearning starts as a mission, not a mood
- Takeaway 2: external benchmarks keep self-education honest
- Takeaway 3: speed matters only when tied to a concrete standard
- Takeaway 4: learning value and credential value are different
- Takeaway 5: rigor without institutional permission still has limits
