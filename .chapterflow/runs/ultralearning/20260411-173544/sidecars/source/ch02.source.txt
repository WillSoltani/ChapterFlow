Title: Why ultralearning matters

Frozen support:
- Scott H. Young defines ultralearning as deep self-education for learning hard things in less time.
- He contrasts ultralearning with dabbling: no commitment, no protected time, and no firm direction.
- He argues that intense focus, weakness-first work, and concentrated time can compress learning that might otherwise take years into months.
- He also argues that ultralearning can make learning more engaging because the work is results-driven and stripped of more passive busywork.
- The broader stakes include adaptation and the growing value of quick learners in changing conditions.
- He also notes that dabbling still has a place for exploration before deciding whether a serious project is worth it.

Allowed exact lines:
- none; paraphrase only

Practical support boundary:
- safe to contrast ultralearning with dabbling and to claim concentrated intensity can accelerate progress
- safe to frame adaptability and changing conditions broadly
