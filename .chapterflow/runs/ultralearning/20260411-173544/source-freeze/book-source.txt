Chapter 1 support frozen from Scott H. Young primary materials:
- The MIT Challenge was Scott Young's self-imposed attempt to learn MIT's four-year computer science curriculum in roughly twelve months using MIT OpenCourseWare and similar resources.
- He set rules around passing final exams and completing programming projects instead of relying on institutional enrollment.
- He publicly framed the challenge as a test of whether a serious, low-cost, self-directed alternative could approximate the academic content of an MIT education.

Secondary principle support frozen narrowly for later skeleton work:
- Why ultralearning matters support frozen from Scott H. Young's two-part project-design series:
  - ultralearning is deep self-education for learning hard things in less time
  - ultralearning is the opposite of dabbling because it has commitment, protected time, and a concrete direction
  - intense focus, weakness-first work, and concentrated time can compress multi-year learning into months
  - the rewards include speed, deeper engagement, and the rising value of rapid adaptation
  - ultralearning projects are hard enough that they should matter and compete seriously for time
- Metalearning centers on research into why, what, and how to learn before and during a project.
- Focus concerns starting, sustaining, and protecting concentration.
- Directness means practicing in the real context of the target skill.
- Drill means isolating a rate-determining weakness and attacking it.
- Retrieval means testing to learn rather than rereading passively.
- Feedback means exposing work to correction rather than dodging it.
- Retention deals with avoiding fast forgetting.
- Intuition emphasizes understanding deeply before stacking more knowledge.
- Experimentation pushes beyond default methods and comfort-zone habits.
