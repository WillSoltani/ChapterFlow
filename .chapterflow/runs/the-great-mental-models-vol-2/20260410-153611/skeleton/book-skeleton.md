## Chapter Order

1. `Relativity`: use perspective-shift and frame-dependence to prevent overconfidence in any single vantage point.
2. `Reciprocity`: use action-and-reaction logic to show how moves reshape the environment that answers back.
3. `Thermodynamics`: show how order, energy, entropy, and exchange pressures shape systems and habits.
4. `Inertia`: explain why habits, stories, and institutions resist change until enough force accumulates.
5. `Friction and Viscosity`: show how resistance, drag, and operating scale determine what moves easily.
6. `Velocity`: separate mere movement from movement in a useful direction.
7. `Leverage`: show how tools, positioning, and asymmetry amplify effort.
8. `Activation Energy`: explain why beginnings are costly and why threshold-crossing matters.
9. `Catalysts`: show how triggers speed change without being the whole cause.
10. `Alloying`: explain why strong systems combine unlike elements into a better composite.
11. `Evolution Part One: Natural Selection and Extinction`: show how adaptation pressure rewards fit and removes brittleness.
12. `Evolution Part Two: Adaptation Rate and the Red Queen Effect`: explain why relative adaptation speed matters in moving environments.
13. `Ecosystem`: show how interdependence and environmental stability shape resilience.
14. `Niches`: explain why fit between agent and environment matters more than abstract talent.
15. `Self-Preservation`: show how survival drives behavior but can harden into stagnation.
16. `Replication`: explain copying, transmission, and commander's-intent style reproducibility without mindless cloning.
17. `Cooperation`: show why large gains require coordinated mutual benefit, not only rivalry.
18. `Hierarchical Organization`: explain why hierarchy emerges, what it coordinates, and how it can drift.
19. `Incentives`: show how small reward structures steer choices harder than declared values.
20. `Tendency to Minimize Energy Output`: explain heuristics, least effort, and satisficing as efficiency moves with hidden costs.

## Rotation Plan For Examples

- early physics chapters: workplace, classroom, one family or friendship frame
- chemistry chapters: startup, team project, training habit, product launch
- biology chapters: organizational design, hiring, leadership, coalition behavior, public policy, personal learning system

## School-Setting Plan

- use school settings sparingly in Ch. 1-4 and Ch. 14-16
- avoid repeated teacher-student conflict scenes
- reserve one stronger school hierarchy example for Ch. 18

## Vocabulary Watchlist

- avoid pseudo-science, optimization cliches, and generic self-help verbs
- keep `entropy`, `niche`, `heuristics`, and `incentives` concrete and operational
- do not flatten `velocity` into `speed` or `leverage` into `manipulation`

## Motif / Callback Opportunities

- perspective limits from `Relativity` can echo inside `Niches` and `Incentives`
- threshold logic from `Activation Energy` can echo inside `Cooperation`
- adaptation pressure from evolution chapters can echo inside `Incentives` and `Tendency to Minimize Energy Output`

## Thin-Chapter Risk Notes

- `Catalysts`: easy to over-generalize if examples are not concrete
- `Self-Preservation`: can drift into life-coaching unless tied to cost and stagnation
- `Replication`: commander's-intent material must stay specific and not turn into management boilerplate

## Premium-Routing Candidates

- Ch. 1 `Relativity` because it sets the baseline voice and cognitive posture
- Ch. 11 `Natural Selection and Extinction` because biology support is thinner and drift risk is higher
- Ch. 19 `Incentives` because moral complexity and downstream transfer are both high
