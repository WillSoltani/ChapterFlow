Core Claim:
Inertia is the model that shows why existing motion keeps going. Habits, systems, beliefs, and institutions resist change unless real force is applied. That means people underestimate how much energy it takes to start a new direction and how long old momentum can keep shaping outcomes after the decision to change has already been made.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about motion persistence and resistance to change. It should not be confused with Thermodynamics, which focuses on upkeep and disorder. Here the core move is recognizing the force required to redirect what is already in motion.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that current direction tends to persist.
Then the author shows how people underestimate startup force and lingering momentum.
Then the author concludes that wise change efforts respect the drag of existing motion instead of treating decision as instant transformation.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Inertia: existing direction resists change.
- Startup force: extra effort required to begin a new path.
- Momentum carryover: old motion keeps affecting outcomes after intent changes.
- Directional trap: momentum strengthens the wrong path because it is already underway.

Specific Applications:
- changing team routines
- breaking entrenched personal habits
- evaluating whether a stated pivot has enough force behind it

Common Misreadings:
- treating inertia as laziness only
- assuming commitment language is enough to change direction

Counterarguments or Limits:
