Core Claim:
Adaptation rate is the model that asks not only whether a system can adapt, but whether it can adapt fast enough. The Red Queen effect adds that the landscape keeps moving because rivals, institutions, and environments change too. Strong judgment therefore measures progress relative to shifting conditions rather than assuming standing still preserves advantage.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about pace of adaptation under moving conditions, not Chapter 11's basic selection and extinction frame. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 11 because the core move is not asking what the environment selects, but whether the system can keep pace with that selection pressure over time.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that adaptation must be measured relative to a changing environment.
Then the author shows how systems can improve and still lose if the environment or rivals improve faster.
Then the author concludes that wise action focuses on adaptation rate, not just present status.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Adaptation rate: speed at which a system changes relative to environmental pressure.
- Relative improvement: progress measured against a moving baseline.
- Red Queen effect: the need to keep adapting because the environment and rivals also keep changing.
- Pace mismatch: a system improves, but not quickly enough to stay fit.

Specific Applications:
- strategy in competitive or changing markets
- skill maintenance in environments where standards keep rising
- personal routines that must adapt as life conditions change

Common Misreadings:
- treating any improvement as enough
- assuming current lead guarantees future safety

Counterarguments or Limits:
Not every setting changes at the same rate, and frantic adaptation can create noise or exhaustion. The chapter should not imply endless motion without directional judgment.
