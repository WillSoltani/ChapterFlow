Core Claim:
Reciprocity is the model that reminds you your move does not end with your move. Actions invite response, and those responses shape the next round. In repeated interactions, small positive or corrosive signals compound because people answer what they receive. The chapter should show why generous first moves can create leverage without becoming naive, and why forgiveness sometimes protects a longer game better than score-settling.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about feedback loops in relationships and repeated games. It is not generic kindness advice. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 1 because the core move is not changing perspective but anticipating the return move your own action will trigger.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that actions provoke responses.
Then the author shows how repeated interaction compounds those responses into trust, help, suspicion, or retaliation.
Then the author concludes that wise action accounts for the reply it will create, often by going positive first without becoming blind.

Counterarguments or Limits:
Positive first moves fail with bad-faith actors or one-shot exploitation. Reciprocity helps most where repeated interaction matters and where the return move can still be influenced.
