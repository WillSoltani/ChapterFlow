Core Claim:
Incentives are the model for understanding how systems steer behavior through rewards, penalties, and consequences. Strong judgment asks what behavior a structure is actually encouraging, whether the signal matches the stated goal, and how people adapt when incentives are badly designed.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 18 focused on layered coordination and decision ownership across levels. Chapter 19 should move from structure to the behavior-shaping forces operating inside that structure. The distinct move is from organization of roles to direction of action.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that behavior responds to incentives whether the system notices or not.
Then the author explains that badly designed incentives can produce behavior that defeats the stated aim.
Then the author concludes that wise action studies what the system rewards in practice rather than what it claims to reward.

Required Anchors:
- A setting where the reward structure pulls behavior in a predictable direction.
- A case where a stated goal is undermined by the incentive actually attached to it.
- A system where people adapt intelligently to the signal being sent.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Incentive: a reward, penalty, or consequence that nudges behavior in one direction.
- Signal mismatch: a gap between the stated goal and what the system actually rewards.
- Adaptive response: the way people adjust to incentive structures.
- Reward geometry: the practical pattern of gain and cost shaping action.

Specific Applications:
- workplace metrics and reward systems
- school grading or participation structures
- personal habit design through consequence and reward

Common Misreadings:
- treating incentives as only financial
- assuming stated values matter more than actual reward structure

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not reduce all behavior to crude payoff chasing. It should show that incentives are powerful without claiming they explain everything.
