Core Claim:
Velocity is the model that refuses to confuse speed with progress. Movement only counts as useful when rate and direction are aligned. A system can be busy, fast, and intense while still moving away from what matters. Strong judgment therefore checks heading and pace together instead of rewarding motion for motion's sake.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about speed plus direction, not friction's drag or inertia's persistence. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 5 because the core move is not resistance in the medium but whether movement is fast in the right direction at all.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that speed without direction is weak evidence of progress.
Then the author shows how people reward motion even when it is misaligned.
Then the author concludes that wise action measures pace and heading together and is willing to slow down to correct direction.
