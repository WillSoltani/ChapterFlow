Core Claim:
Alloying is the model that explains why the right combination can produce better properties than any single element alone. Complementary strengths can create resilience, flexibility, or effectiveness that purity by itself cannot. Strong judgment therefore looks for combinations that improve the system rather than chasing a false ideal of single-trait perfection.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about beneficial combination and complementary strengths, not catalysts changing reaction rate. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 9 because the core move is not acceleration but improved properties through mixture.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that combining elements can create new strengths.
Then the author shows how purity can become a weakness when it lacks complementary properties.
Then the author concludes that wise action looks for mixes that improve resilience, adaptability, or usefulness.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Alloying: combining elements so the mix has better properties than its parts alone.
- Complementary strength: one element offsetting the weakness of another.
- Purity trap: overvaluing uniformity when mixture would perform better.
- Combination design: intentionally choosing mixes for resilience or versatility.

Specific Applications:
- building better teams from complementary skills
- designing schedules or routines with balancing elements
- avoiding monocultures in projects or decision processes

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
Not every mix improves the system. Bad combinations can create conflict or weakness. The chapter should not imply that diversity without design is enough.
