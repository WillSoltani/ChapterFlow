Core Claim:
Catalysts are the model for influences that accelerate change without being used up themselves. Some people, structures, or conditions change the pathway so progress happens faster and with less waste. Strong judgment therefore looks for accelerators that alter how the reaction unfolds rather than assuming more direct force is the only path to speed.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about acceleration through facilitation, not activation energy's startup threshold. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 8 because the core move is not getting the reaction started at all, but changing how quickly it proceeds once the pathway is active.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that some influences speed reactions without being consumed.
Then the author shows how catalysts alter the pathway rather than simply adding more brute force.
Then the author concludes that wise action identifies and protects catalytic conditions in people, teams, and systems.

Required Anchors:

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Catalyst: an influence that speeds progress without being used up in the same way.
- Pathway change: altering how the reaction unfolds rather than merely increasing force.
- Facilitative condition: a structure or presence that makes good change easier to sustain.
- False catalyst: something noisy that looks helpful but does not actually accelerate the reaction.

Specific Applications:
- recognizing facilitative teammates or managers
- building conditions that help a project move faster without constant pushing
- distinguishing true accelerators from loud activity

Common Misreadings:
- treating catalysts as magical saviors
- confusing a catalytic influence with someone doing everyone else's work

Counterarguments or Limits:
Catalysts do not replace effort, and not every accelerator remains effective forever. The chapter should not imply that one helpful person or tool solves the whole system permanently.
