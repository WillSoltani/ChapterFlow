Core Claim:
Leverage is the model that shows why the placement of force matters as much as the amount of force. A small, well-positioned action can create outsized results, while large effort in the wrong place can accomplish very little. Strong judgment therefore looks for multipliers, fulcrums, and points of influence instead of assuming that more strain is always the answer.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about multiplied effect from structure and placement, not velocity's speed-plus-direction or friction's drag. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 6 because the core move is not judging rate but finding where force will do the most work.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that force can be amplified by structure.
Then the author shows how people waste effort by pushing hard in low-leverage places.
Then the author concludes that wise action finds the fulcrum before increasing strain.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Leverage: outsized effect from well-placed effort.
- Fulcrum point: the place where input changes outcomes most.
- Low-leverage strain: heavy effort applied where it barely moves the system.
- Multiplier search: scanning for structural advantages before adding force.

Specific Applications:

- identifying the one habit adjustment that unlocks several others
- choosing influence points in projects, relationships, or systems

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
