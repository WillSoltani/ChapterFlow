Core Claim:
Natural selection is the model that explains why fit is always relative to an environment. Strength alone does not decide what survives. Conditions select for some traits and eliminate others. Strong judgment therefore asks what the environment rewards, what it punishes, and how quickly a once-viable form can become obsolete when the selection pressure shifts.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about environmental selection and disappearance, not alloying's stronger combinations. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 10 because the core move is not building a better mix but understanding why some variants persist and others vanish under changing conditions.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that survival depends on fit to context rather than abstract strength.
Then the author shows how selection pressures preserve some forms and wipe out others.
Then the author concludes that wise action reads the environment before copying traits that succeeded somewhere else.

Required Anchors:

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Natural selection: differential survival based on fit to conditions.
- Selection pressure: the environmental force that rewards some traits and punishes others.
- Local fitness: strength that works in one setting but not necessarily in another.
- Extinction risk: failure when a system cannot survive the current environment.

Specific Applications:
- reading market, institutional, or social conditions before copying visible winners
- avoiding overconfidence from past success in changed environments
- designing habits or strategies around actual context rather than abstract strength

Common Misreadings:
- treating selection as proof that survivors are universally superior
- turning extinction into a moral judgment instead of an environmental outcome

Counterarguments or Limits:
Selection pressures are often messy and multicausal. Short-term survival is not identical to long-term wisdom. The chapter should not flatten everything into crude winner-loser rhetoric.
