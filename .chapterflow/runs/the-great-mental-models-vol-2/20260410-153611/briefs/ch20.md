Core Claim:
The tendency to minimize energy output is the model for understanding why systems often prefer lower-effort paths when they can. Strong judgment asks when effort conservation is adaptive, when it becomes complacency, and how environments steer systems toward easy or demanding routes.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 19 focused on behavior guided by incentives. Chapter 20 should move from rewarded behavior to the deeper pull toward easier, lower-cost pathways. The distinct move is from external signal to effort-saving tendency.

Then the author explains that easy paths can preserve resources or create stagnation depending on context.
