Core Claim:
Self-preservation is the model for understanding how systems protect the conditions necessary for continued survival. Strong judgment asks what a system is defending, what threats it is reacting to, and when protective behavior is preserving viability versus becoming over-defensive.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 14 focused on differentiated role fit inside a system. Chapter 15 should move from role structure to the protective behaviors or boundaries that keep the system alive when pressure rises. The distinct move is from functional fit to survival protection.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that functioning systems still need protection against threat.
Then the author explains that protective behavior can preserve viability or become excessive.
Then the author concludes that wise action distinguishes necessary defense from fear-driven overreaction.

Required Anchors:
- A setting where a system protects something essential to continued function.
- A case where missing a real threat creates avoidable damage.
- A case where overprotection starts harming the system it means to preserve.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Self-preservation: protecting the conditions required for continued survival.
- Viability: the system's ability to keep functioning.
- Threat response: a protective move taken against perceived danger.
- Over-defense: protective behavior that starts damaging the system it guards.

Specific Applications:
- organizations protecting core trust or continuity
- school or club systems guarding key constraints
- personal life through boundaries, rest, or resource protection

Common Misreadings:
- treating all caution as weakness
- treating all defense as wise by default

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not glorify paranoia or rigidity. Protection must remain proportional to real threat.
