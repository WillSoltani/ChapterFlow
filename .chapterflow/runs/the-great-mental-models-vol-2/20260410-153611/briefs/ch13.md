Core Claim:
An ecosystem is the model for understanding that outcomes arise inside networks of interdependence. What happens to one part of a system changes the conditions faced by other parts, so wise judgment looks beyond isolated actors and reads the web of relationships shaping the environment.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 12 focused on whether one system adapts quickly enough to moving conditions. Chapter 13 must widen the frame and show that the environment is often partly created by other connected systems. The distinct move is from pace under pressure to interdependence within a shared field.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author widens the lens from one adapting system to many connected systems.
Then the author shows that changes in one part alter the conditions elsewhere.
Then the author concludes that strong judgment reads relationships, spillovers, and system-level effects instead of isolating one actor.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Ecosystem: a set of connected actors sharing conditions and affecting one another.
- Interdependence: the way one part's behavior changes the environment for others.
- Spillover: an effect that moves beyond the original action or actor.
- System lens: a way of reading outcomes through relationships rather than isolated units.

Specific Applications:
- team dynamics where one policy changes behavior across the whole group
- school or club systems where one rule creates secondary effects
- personal environments where one household change reshapes everyone else's routines

Common Misreadings:
- treating outcomes as if they belong to one actor alone
- assuming local fixes stay local

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that every system is too complex to understand. The point is to widen causal reading, not to dissolve responsibility.
