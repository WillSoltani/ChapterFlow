Core Claim:
Friction and viscosity explain why movement is often slowed not only by internal hesitation but by the medium surrounding it. Progress can be drained by process drag, coordination cost, confusing interfaces, emotional resistance, or institutional thickness. Strong judgment therefore learns to distinguish useful resistance from avoidable friction and redesigns the environment before blaming the people moving inside it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about drag from the surrounding environment, not inertia's persistence from prior motion. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 4 because the core move is not momentum continuing but movement being slowed by contact, clutter, and thickness around it.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that movement can be slowed by the medium around it.
Then the author shows how hidden friction creates wasted effort and delay.
Then the author concludes that wise action removes unnecessary drag while respecting resistance that protects quality or safety.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Friction: resistance created by contact points in a system.
- Viscosity: thickness in the surrounding medium that makes movement slower and heavier.
- Drag audit: identifying where effort is being wasted by process design.
- Protective resistance: slowdown that preserves quality, safety, or accuracy.

Specific Applications:
- simplifying team approval chains
- redesigning personal routines so good choices are easier to start
- telling the difference between bureaucracy that protects and bureaucracy that merely slows

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
