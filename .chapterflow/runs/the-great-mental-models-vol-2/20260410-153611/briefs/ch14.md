Core Claim:
A niche is the model for understanding fit through differentiated role, position, or specialization within a shared environment. Strong judgment asks not only whether a system survives in the ecosystem, but what unique role it occupies and where overlap creates friction or confusion.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 13 widened the lens to ecosystems and interdependence. Chapter 14 should narrow the frame to how parts settle into differentiated roles inside that larger system. The distinct move is from shared web to role-specific fit.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that connected systems do not all serve the same function.
Then the author explains that role clarity and specialization shape fit within the shared environment.
Then the author concludes that wise action depends on understanding where you add distinct value and where overlap creates waste or conflict.

Required Anchors:
- A setting where two actors compete awkwardly because their roles overlap too much.
- A case where clearer role definition improves fit for the whole system.
- A system where specialization reduces friction or redundancy.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Niche: a distinct role or position within a larger environment.
- Role fit: how well a system's specific function matches surrounding conditions.
- Overlap friction: conflict or waste created when roles are poorly differentiated.
- Specialization: concentrating on a narrower function that improves system fit.

Specific Applications:
- teams with unclear ownership
- school groups where duplicated work leaves gaps elsewhere
- personal life where role confusion creates recurring conflict

Common Misreadings:
- treating niches as rigid labels that never change
- assuming specialization means isolation from the larger system

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not oversell narrow specialization when flexibility is still needed. Role clarity must remain adaptive rather than brittle.
