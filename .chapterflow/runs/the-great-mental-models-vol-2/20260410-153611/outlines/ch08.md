Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why starts often cost more than continuation and how to lower or redesign the threshold that keeps action from igniting.

Opening Move:
Open on a task or habit that keeps failing in the first few minutes even though it is manageable once underway.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 hard start and hidden ignition barrier
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 trigger design and startup subsidy
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 limits of startup help and sustaining the reaction

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live scene of repeated failure to begin
- P2: reveal threshold cost rather than weak goal
- P3: explain activation energy and ignition barrier
- P4: show trigger or setup reducing the startup cost
- P5: separate starting help from ongoing dependence
- P6: practical rule for lowering the barrier wisely
- P7 if needed: bridge to catalysts

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must separate hard starts from bad goals and ignition help from permanent carrying.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep alive the boundary between legitimate startup support and systems that never learn to run once started.

Threshold Question:
What is the real barrier to ignition, and how can it be lowered without pretending continuation will take care of itself?

Takeaway Count Lock:

- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

- Takeaway 1: starting often costs more than continuing
- Takeaway 2: threshold costs block good plans before they can run
- Takeaway 3: triggers and setup can lower the start barrier
- Takeaway 4: startup subsidy is not the same as carrying forever
- Takeaway 5: ignition is necessary but not sufficient for sustained success
