Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that systems persist across time by transmitting what matters reliably, and that true replication is different from mere repetition.

Opening Move:
Open on a process, lesson, or system that works once, then fails when someone else tries to carry it forward because the structure was not replicable enough.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 replication and persistence beyond one instance
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 transferability and fidelity
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 repetition trap and practical use

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live scene of something being passed on or copied
- P2: define replication as persistence through transmission
- P3: show transferability across work, school, or personal life
- P4: explain why low-fidelity transmission degrades results
- P5: distinguish replication from simple repetition
- P6: practical rule for designing what carries forward
- P7 if needed: bridge to cooperation

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must separate durable replication from repeating surface behavior.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep both truths visible: something must be transferable to persist, and copying without preserving core structure leads to drift.

Threshold Question:
What exactly is being carried forward here, and will it survive transmission with enough fidelity to matter?

- Takeaway 1: replication extends systems beyond one instance
- Takeaway 2: transferability determines what can persist
- Takeaway 3: fidelity matters when something is copied or taught
- Takeaway 4: repetition can fail when core structure is not preserved
- Takeaway 5: wise judgment designs what can survive transmission
