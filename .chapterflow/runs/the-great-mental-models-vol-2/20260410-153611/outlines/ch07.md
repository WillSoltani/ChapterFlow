Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why well-placed force can outperform larger effort in the wrong place, and how to search for leverage before simply pushing harder.

Opening Move:
Open on a team or person working intensely on the wrong part of a problem while a smaller structural change would matter more.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 outsized result from better placement
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 low-leverage strain versus fulcrum point
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 limits of leverage and anti-shortcut distinction

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live scene of heavy effort with weak movement
- P2: reveal a better leverage point
- P3: explain leverage and fulcrum
- P4: show structural multiplier in personal or team setting
- P5: separate leverage from lazy shortcut thinking
- P6: practical multiplier-search rule
- P7 if needed: bridge to activation energy

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must separate true leverage from superficial shortcut-seeking.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep alive the boundary between strategic placement and fantasy that hard work can always be bypassed.

Threshold Question:
Where would one unit of effort change the system most, and where is effort being wasted because it is poorly placed?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: leverage amplifies force through structure
- Takeaway 2: placement matters as much as effort amount
- Takeaway 3: low-leverage strain can look heroic while accomplishing little
- Takeaway 4: leverage is not a synonym for cheating
- Takeaway 5: some work still requires direct effort even after the multiplier is found

- Scenario 4 (decision_point, work): teaches choosing a leverage point over more overtime
