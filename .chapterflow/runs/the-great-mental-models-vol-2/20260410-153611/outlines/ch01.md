Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that better judgment begins by checking viewpoint, frame, and timescale before declaring what an event means.

Opening Move:
Open on a meeting where one person's plan sounds reckless until the missing constraints behind it become visible.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 workplace disagreement over a plan
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 article/report framing and hidden position
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 timeline extension that changes an early verdict

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live tension and misread behavior
- P2: show how hidden vantage point changes interpretation
- P3: move from people to framed information
- P4: explain why framed information is never neutral
- P5: add timeline shift as a second way to change viewpoint
- P6: draw the practical judgment rule
- P7 if needed: hard-depth boundary about over-analysis versus decision

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Perspective-taking is not agreement. The mechanism is reconstructing position and time horizon before treating your first interpretation as truth.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Perspective widens judgment, but it can also become a cover for delay if the reader never commits to a standard or a decision.

Threshold Question:
When does widening the frame sharpen judgment, and when does it become a sophisticated way to avoid choosing?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: every view is partial
- Takeaway 2: reconstructing the other frame
- Takeaway 3: framing changes interpretation
- Takeaway 4: timeline shifts change meaning
- Takeaway 5: perspective without standards becomes drift

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask the reader to predict how action changes the field in the next chapter. It cannot summarize reciprocity.
