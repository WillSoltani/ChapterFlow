Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand how catalysts speed reactions by changing the pathway, and how to distinguish true accelerators from noisy help.

Opening Move:
Open on a group or person making sudden progress once the right facilitator or condition appears, even though the total effort does not increase much.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 acceleration after the right catalyst appears
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 pathway change versus brute force
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 false catalyst and dependency limit

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live scene of stalled reaction beginning to move faster
- P2: reveal catalytic condition or person
- P3: explain catalyst as pathway changer
- P4: show facilitation in team or personal system
- P5: separate true catalyst from rescuer fantasy or noise
- P6: practical rule for identifying catalytic conditions
- P7 if needed: bridge to alloying

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must separate catalysts from brute force and from dependency on a heroic actor.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep alive the boundary between genuine acceleration and a system that only works when one person keeps carrying it.

Threshold Question:
What is actually accelerating the reaction, and is it changing the pathway or merely masking the underlying system weakness?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: catalysts change rate without being consumed the same way
- Takeaway 2: pathway change matters more than raw push
- Takeaway 3: catalytic conditions can live in people, structure, or environment
- Takeaway 4: false catalysts create noise without acceleration
- Takeaway 5: real catalysts should not become hidden dependency traps

- Scenario 1 (dialogue, work): teaches a teammate as pathway changer not rescuer
