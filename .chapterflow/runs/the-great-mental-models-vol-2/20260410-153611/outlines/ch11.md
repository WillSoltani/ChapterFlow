Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why survival depends on fit to conditions rather than abstract strength, and how environmental pressure can preserve some forms while eliminating others.

Opening Move:
Open on a strategy, team, or routine that looked strong until the environment changed and exposed a different requirement.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 changing environment and lost fit
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 selection pressure and local fitness
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 extinction risk and false lessons from survivors

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live scene of a once-winning form failing under new conditions
- P2: reveal the environment shift rather than a simple decline in effort
- P3: explain natural selection and local fitness
- P4: show selection pressure in work, school, or personal systems
- P5: separate survival from moral superiority
- P6: practical rule for reading the environment before copying winners
- P7 if needed: bridge to adaptation rate and the Red Queen effect

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must separate fit-to-environment from universal superiority.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep alive the boundary between current success and durable fitness when the environment keeps shifting.

Threshold Question:
What exactly is the environment selecting for right now, and how quickly could that pressure make today's advantage obsolete?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: survival depends on fit to conditions
- Takeaway 2: selection pressure rewards some traits and punishes others
- Takeaway 3: local fitness can be mistaken for universal superiority
- Takeaway 4: extinction can follow from environmental shift, not simple weakness
- Takeaway 5: wise judgment reads changing pressure before copying visible winners

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (before_after, work): teaches success reversed by changed market or rules
- Scenario 2 (predict_reveal, personal): teaches hidden environmental fit in a habit
- Scenario 3 (decision_point, school): teaches changing rules in a school setting
- Scenario 4 (dialogue, work): teaches survivor bias versus fit
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): teaches danger of copying a trait without matching the environment
- Scenario 6 (postmortem, school): teaches extinction logic after a format or rule shift

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, specify one sensory or situational detail to include.
- scoreboard or dashboard that no longer tracks the winning behavior
- club rules posted on a classroom whiteboard after a format change
- routine that worked in summer and breaks in a winter schedule
- once-busy storefront or inbox that now sits quiet under changed conditions

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what happens when adaptation must keep pace with ongoing selection. It must not summarize the Red Queen effect.

Genericity Risks:
- turning the chapter into winner-take-all rhetoric
- treating survival as moral proof
- losing the role of changing environments

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: crude social Darwinism language
- filler move 2: generic hustle slogans about winning
- invented authority move 1: unsupported species or genetics detail
- brief-language leak 1: unsupported zones phrasing in prose
