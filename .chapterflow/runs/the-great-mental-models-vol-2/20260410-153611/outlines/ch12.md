Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why adaptation must be measured against a moving environment, and how the Red Queen effect makes standing still dangerous even when a system is already strong.

Opening Move:
Open on a person, team, or system that keeps improving yet still loses ground because the landscape changes faster.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 relative improvement and moving baseline
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 adaptation rate and pace mismatch
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 Red Queen effect and limits of frantic churn

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live scene of improvement that is still insufficient
- P2: reveal the moving baseline or rival adaptation
- P3: explain adaptation rate and relative improvement
- P4: show Red Queen pressure in work, school, or personal life
- P5: separate healthy adaptation from frantic unstructured change
- P6: practical rule for reading pace relative to environment
- P7 if needed: bridge to ecosystems

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must separate absolute improvement from relative improvement under shifting conditions.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep alive the boundary between meaningful adaptation and frantic churn that moves fast without improving fit.

Threshold Question:
Compared with the speed of environmental change, is this system adapting fast enough and in the right direction?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: adaptation rate is relative to a moving environment
- Takeaway 2: improving can still mean losing ground
- Takeaway 3: the Red Queen effect keeps the baseline moving
- Takeaway 4: pace mismatch creates hidden decline
- Takeaway 5: wise judgment measures speed of adjustment without mistaking churn for adaptation
