Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that cooperation creates shared gain through coordinated action, and that it depends on conditions strong enough to keep actors aligned.

Opening Move:
Open on a setting where multiple actors create a better result together than any of them could as effectively alone.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 cooperation and shared gain
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 trust, structure, and coordination cost
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 defection pressure and practical use

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live scene of cooperative success
- P2: define cooperation as coordinated action toward shared gain
- P3: show structure or trust enabling cooperation
- P4: explain how coordination can fail under misaligned incentives
- P5: show temptation or defection pressure weakening cooperation
- P6: practical rule for building stable cooperation
- P7 if needed: bridge to hierarchy

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must separate real cooperation from vague group harmony talk.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep both truths alive: cooperation can create more value, and it remains fragile when trust, incentives, or structure break.

Threshold Question:
What makes cooperation worth sustaining here, and what would cause actors to stop cooperating?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: cooperation creates shared gain through coordination
- Takeaway 2: structure and trust support aligned action
- Takeaway 3: coordination has costs that must be justified by the gain
- Takeaway 4: defection pressure can unravel cooperation
- Takeaway 5: wise judgment designs conditions that keep cooperation stable

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dialogue, work): teaches cross-functional coordination
- Scenario 2 (before_after, personal): teaches shared household or relationship effort
- Scenario 3 (predict_reveal, school): teaches group coordination under time pressure
- Scenario 4 (decision_point, work): teaches building structure for coordination
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): teaches temptation to defect from shared effort
- Scenario 6 (postmortem, school): teaches cooperation breakdown analysis

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, specify one sensory or situational detail to include.
- shared tracker, timeline board, or meeting table
- kitchen schedule, chore board, or joint calendar
- presentation timer, project rubric, or equipment queue
- cross-team handoff document

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask how larger cooperative systems layer roles and control. It must not summarize hierarchy.

Genericity Risks:
- turning the chapter into generic teamwork praise
- assuming cooperation needs no structure
- forgetting the role of temptation and misalignment

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: harmony slogans
- filler move 2: unsupported evolutionary cooperation jargon
- invented authority move 1: named biology examples not supported in the frozen bundle
