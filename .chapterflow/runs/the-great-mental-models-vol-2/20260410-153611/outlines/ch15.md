Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that systems protect what they need to stay viable, and that wise judgment distinguishes necessary defense from overreaction that harms the system itself.

Opening Move:
Open on a system setting a protective boundary or defensive response because something essential to continued function is under threat.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 self-preservation and viability
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 threat response and protective boundaries
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 over-defense limits and practical use

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live scene of protection against a meaningful threat
- P2: define self-preservation as protecting viability
- P3: show practical defensive behavior in work, school, or personal life
- P4: explain why missing threats can damage survival
- P5: show how over-defense can become self-harm
- P6: practical rule for proportional protection
- P7 if needed: bridge to replication

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must separate viable protection from fear-driven over-defense.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep both truths visible: real threats require defense, and disproportionate defense can damage the system.

Threshold Question:
What is this system protecting, and is the current defense proportional to the real threat?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: self-preservation protects continued viability
- Takeaway 2: systems need boundaries against real threat
- Takeaway 3: missing genuine threat can produce avoidable damage
- Takeaway 4: over-defense can harm the protected system
- Takeaway 5: wise judgment makes defense proportional and targeted
