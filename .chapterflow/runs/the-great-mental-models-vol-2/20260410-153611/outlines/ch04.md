Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why existing motion keeps going and why changing direction requires more force than deciding to change.

Opening Move:
Open on a team that has announced a new plan while the old process keeps shaping daily behavior.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 old process still running after new decision
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 startup force and early resistance
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 momentum as asset or trap

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live scene of old direction persisting
- P2: reveal inertia at work
- P3: explain startup force
- P4: show carryover from old motion
- P5: separate useful momentum from lock-in
- P6: practical judgment rule
- P7 if needed: bridge to friction and viscosity

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Decision is not the same as redirection; inertia persists until force changes the path.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve the difference between momentum you should build and momentum you should interrupt.

Threshold Question:
When should you invest more force to redirect a system, and when should you use existing momentum instead of fighting it?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: current direction persists
- Takeaway 2: startup force is underestimated
- Takeaway 3: old motion lingers after new intent
- Takeaway 4: momentum can become leverage
- Takeaway 5: momentum can also lock in the wrong direction
