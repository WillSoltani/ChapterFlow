Core Claim:
The opening book gives the project a real enemy: Resistance, the inward force that attacks any meaningful call before it becomes actual work. Its job is to make Resistance feel universal, intimate, and dangerous without reducing it to ordinary laziness. The section has to show that fear, procrastination, self-sabotage, and rationalization belong to the same war. It also has to preserve one limit: Resistance is a sharp interpretive frame, not a license for fake certainty about every emotional struggle.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the naming chapter. It turns a vague feeling of blockage into a hostile force with recognizable signatures. It must feel more severe and more clarifying than a generic creativity pep talk, and it cannot steal Book Two's deeper discipline language or Book Three's spiritual framing.

Author Logic Chain:
First the section names the familiar but slippery force that rises whenever real work approaches.
Then it shows how Resistance behaves: invisibly, universally, and with special intensity around callings that matter.
Then it turns from description to consequence, arguing that ignoring Resistance buries the life a person feels called toward.
Finally it leaves the reader with a live problem: once you know the enemy's name, how do you fight it in practice?
