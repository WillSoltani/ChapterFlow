Core Claim:
Book Three pushes beyond discipline into vocation, service, and a larger source than ego. Its job is to show that turning pro keeps the work alive, but the work becomes truer and more durable when it is treated as obedience to something higher than vanity, fear, applause, or self-display. The chapter must preserve this upward move without inventing hidden doctrine or drifting into vague spiritual uplift.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the vocation-and-service chapter. It should feel less diagnostic than Book One and less procedural than Book Two. Its distinct pressure is that the worker is no longer asking only how to show up, but what the showing up is finally for.

Author Logic Chain:
First the section moves past routine by asking what kind of authority or calling the work answers to.
Then it contrasts ego-centered labor with work received as service, stewardship, or obedience.
Then it sharpens that move by showing how a larger frame can steady the worker against vanity, fear, and applause hunger.
Finally it lands on release: the work is given, not possessed, and the worker's task is faithful delivery rather than self-worship.
