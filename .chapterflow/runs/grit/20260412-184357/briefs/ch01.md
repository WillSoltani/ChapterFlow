Core Claim:
The opening chapter frames grit through a simple but punishing question: who keeps showing up when the pressure stops being glamorous and becomes daily attrition. Duckworth uses West Point's Beast Barracks because the entrants are already elite, which strips away the easy explanation that only weak candidates quit. The chapter's practical point is that long-term perseverance predicts survival in demanding systems more reliably than polished credentials alone. The chapter should teach grit as repeated return under strain, not as talent, hype, or personality branding.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is not generic success advice. It is defined by Beast Barracks as a highly selective environment where prestige has already been won but persistence is still tested, and by the opening move from admired credentials to the harsher question of who actually endures.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author opens on West Point's Beast Barracks as a demanding funnel where elite cadets are tested immediately.
Then the author uses that setting to show that selection, achievement, and promise do not guarantee staying power.
Then the author names grit as the better lens for understanding why some people keep returning to the work while others fall away.

Required Anchors:
- Beast Barracks as the opening proving ground with a 5:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. schedule, no weekends, and near-total separation from ordinary comforts; the friction is relentless daily structure, the consequence is that some cadets still wash out despite elite selection.
- West Point as a high-prestige institution filled with already accomplished candidates; the friction is that elite admissions do not settle the endurance question, the consequence is that talent-first explanations look incomplete.
- Duckworth's field-reporting frame that follows cadets under pressure rather than admiring them from a distance; the friction is repeated showing up, the consequence is the book's first concrete picture of grit.
