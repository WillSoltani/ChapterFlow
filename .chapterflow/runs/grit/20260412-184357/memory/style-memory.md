# Style Memory

- Write like an authored explainer, not a summary machine or a study-guide voice.
- Open from pressure, event, friction, or a live question, never with `This chapter`, a thesis announcement, or a slogan lead.
- Keep paragraphs on distinct jobs from the ledger: anchor, mechanism, tension, limit, implication, bridge.
- Stay concrete, chapter-specific, and source-bound; if a sentence could fit another chapter, cut or narrow it.
- Easy must feel accessible and pictureable, medium must explain mechanism plus application, hard must add boundary, cost, contradiction, synthesis, or a threshold question.
- Tone differences must be functional, not adjective swaps: gentle lowers resistance, direct clarifies the decision line, competitive sharpens stakes without becoming theatrical.
- No em dashes, fake neuroscience, generic self-help filler, contamination phrases, repeated endings, or recap-like support surfaces.
