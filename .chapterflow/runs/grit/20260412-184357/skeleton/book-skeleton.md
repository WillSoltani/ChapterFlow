## Rotation Plan For Examples

- keep two work, two school, and two personal examples per chapter
- rotate the six canonical formats exactly once per chapter
- reserve school and youth settings for Chapters 1, 4, 10, and 11 where the source bundle most clearly supports education-facing framing
- keep at least one adult professional example active in Chapters 2, 3, 7, 8, 9, and 12 so the book does not collapse into student-only advice

## School-Setting Plan

- rotate among classroom participation, team tryouts, music practice, college advising, family routines, and extracurricular culture
- avoid repeating seminar-room correction scenes or generic exam-prep stakes across adjacent chapters

## Vocabulary Watchlist

- avoid buzzword mush: mindset, hustle, resilience hacks, winners, grindset, consistency is everything
- use concrete verbs tied to the book's frame: persist, practice, return, train, quit, recommit, recover, contribute

## Motif / Callback Opportunities

- talent versus sustained effort
- passion that survives boredom
- effort as both builder and multiplier
- setback response as a trained pattern
- institutions either deepening or draining perseverance

## Thin-Chapter Risk Notes

- Chapter 6 through Chapter 12 are structurally supported by headings but still thin on chapter-local excerpt support, so briefs must stay conservative
- Chapter 13 can easily slide into generic uplift if it stops teaching concrete mechanism
- parenting and culture chapters risk flattening into universal prescriptions unless the brief keeps tradeoffs visible

## Premium-Routing Candidates

- Chapter 2 because talent critique can turn generic fast
- Chapter 3 because the effort formula invites sloganized restatement
- Chapter 9 because hope language drifts easily into unsupported optimism
- Chapter 12 because institution-level claims can outrun the frozen bundle
