Chapter Promise:
Show that grit can change over time without turning the claim into fantasy.

Opening Move:
Open on a moment when someone mistakes a current pattern for a permanent identity and then begin loosening that conclusion.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 title move from observation to growth
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 Chapter 4 carryover from repeated evidence to repeated change
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 limits, unevenness, and bridge to interest

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook
- P2: distinction
- P3: mechanism
- P4: implication
- P5: limit
- P6: bridge
- P7 if needed: sharpened caution

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
If grit is a pattern, then the chapter can ask whether the pattern is trainable rather than fixed.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Growth language clarifies possibility only if it keeps difficulty, slowness, and uneven progress visible.

Threshold Question:
What would it mean to say grit grows without promising miracles?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: observed patterns can change
- Takeaway 2: growth is slower than slogans suggest
- Takeaway 3: fixed-trait thinking as a trap
- Takeaway 4: limits of overpromising growth
- Takeaway 5: bridge to interest as an early ingredient

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): misreading a coworker's current pattern as permanent
- Scenario 2 (dialogue, school): teacher challenging fixedness in a student
- Scenario 3 (dilemma, personal): deciding whether a weak stretch defines you
- Scenario 4 (reset_moment, work): revising a no-growth assumption
- Scenario 5 (planning_choice, school): building a slow-change practice plan
- Scenario 6 (mistake_recovery, personal): recovering after overpromising instant change

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, specify one sensory or situational detail to include.
- red pen notes in the writing lab
- scuffed chairs in the practice room
- weekly habit tracker with missed boxes
- coffee ring beside a restarted plan

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what internal force helps persistence begin and stay alive, leading into interest.

Genericity Risks:
- turning growth into motivational mush
- pretending change is linear or easy
- repeating Chapter 4 without a new developmental move

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: anyone can do anything fast
- filler move 2: fixed forever rhetoric on the opposite side
- invented authority move 1: unsupported intervention claims or statistics
- brief-language leak 1: growth without fantasy phrasing copied mechanically into prose
