Core Claim:
Chapter 8 applies habit theory to social change. Duhigg argues that movements depend not only on persuasive ideas but on social habits: the routines and norms that tell communities how to act together. The chapter develops this through the Montgomery bus boycott and Saddleback Church.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Strongest at showing that habits scale through relationships, norms, and organizational form rather than isolated inspiration.
