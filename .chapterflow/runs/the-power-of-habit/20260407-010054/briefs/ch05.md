Core Claim:
Chapter 5 argues that willpower can become a habit. Duhigg uses Starbucks training to show why that matters. In difficult service moments, employees do not rely only on good intentions. They rely on scripts, scenarios, and practiced responses that were trained before the stressful moment appeared.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Strongest at reframing willpower as something trained through planned responses rather than summoned from nowhere.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes Starbucks training routines as willpower scaffolding.
Then the author develops stress planning as a way to automate the better response.
Then the author tests the idea against willpower as a habit rather than a pure trait.

Specific Applications:
- writing a script for a difficult conversation before it happens

- using a pre-decided fallback when work stress spikes

Common Misreadings:
- thinking willpower means brute force in the moment
- treating training scripts as fake instead of functional rehearsal

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter values rehearsal and scripting, but it does not imply that stress disappears; it shows how preparation changes the likely response.
