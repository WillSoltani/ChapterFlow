Core Claim:
Chapter 6 argues that crises can become catalysts for organizational habit change. Institutions often tolerate weak or dangerous routines because those routines are familiar, decentralized, or shielded by politics. A crisis alters that equilibrium. It makes the cost of the old pattern visible enough that resistance to change weakens.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Strongest at showing how breakdown makes hidden routines visible and therefore temporarily changeable.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes crisis as the moment routines become negotiable.
Then the author develops leaders using disruption to install new habits.
Then the author tests the idea against institutional accidents exposing the cost of bad automatic patterns.
