Core Claim:
Chapter 2 adds the missing force in habit formation: craving. Chapter 1 described the loop structurally, but a loop becomes durable only when the cue starts generating desire for the reward before the routine finishes. Duhigg makes that point through the Pepsodent and Febreze cases.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Strongest at showing that habits deepen when the brain starts wanting the reward before the routine begins.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes Claude Hopkins and Pepsodent as the classic manufactured-craving example.
Then the author develops the cue only matters once the reward becomes expected.
Then the author tests the idea against anticipation as the force that makes a loop sticky.

Required Anchors:
- Claude Hopkins and Pepsodent as the classic manufactured-craving example

Specific Applications:
- pairing a study ritual with an expected moment of relief or satisfaction
- designing a workout setup that creates desire before effort begins
- noticing why a phone check feels magnetic before any notification content appears

Common Misreadings:
- reducing craving to greed instead of reward expectation
- assuming cues work on their own without any learned anticipation

Counterarguments or Limits:
Craving explains why a habit gathers force, but it does not remove the need for structure or later substitution tactics.
