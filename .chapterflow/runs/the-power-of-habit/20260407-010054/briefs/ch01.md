Core Claim:
Chapter 1 opens with Eugene Pauly, whose memory was severely damaged by illness. He could not hold onto normal new memories for long, but he still moved through routines that had clearly become automatic. He could locate rooms in his home, repeat familiar actions, and respond to cues even when he could not explain what had just happened. Duhigg uses that case to show that habits operate through a system that can keep running outside explicit recall.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
