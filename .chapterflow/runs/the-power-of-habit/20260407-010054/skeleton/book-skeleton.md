# Book Skeleton

## Metadata
- Book: The Power of Habit
- Author: Charles Duhigg
- Locked edition: English Random House trade family (2012 launch family; 2014 paperback adds afterword)
- Numbered chapter range: 1-9
- Wave size: 2 by default
- Premium / solo routing candidates: ch06, ch08, ch09

## Rotation Plan For Examples
- Maintain the canonical six-format rotation once per chapter: decision_point, postmortem, dialogue, predict_reveal, dilemma, before_after.
- Maintain 2 work / 2 school / 2 personal examples in every chapter package.
- Rotate endings across broader_principle, self_directed_question, surprising_implication, cross_domain, common_trap, perspective_reframe.

## School-Setting Plan
- Reuse concrete but varied school contexts: lab partners, debate prep, campus clubs, study plans, classroom routines, and student government.
- Avoid flattening school examples into generic motivation advice.

## Vocabulary Watchlist
- Keep cue, routine, reward, craving, keystone habit, willpower, crisis, prediction, social norm, and responsibility precise and chapter-bound.
- Avoid fake neuroscience, generic self-help wrappers, validator language, and moralizing summaries detached from the source case studies.

## Motif / Callback Opportunities
- ch01 cue-routine-reward returns in change, willpower, marketing, and responsibility chapters.
- ch03 replacement logic echoes through willpower, crisis redesign, and social norms.
- ch04 keystone habits recur in Starbucks training, crisis response, and movement-building.
- ch09 should call back to earlier claims without collapsing into habit determinism.

## Chapter Plan
### ch01 The Habit Loop
- Section: The Habits of Individuals
- Intent: Establish the cue-routine-reward loop and show that habits can operate even when conscious memory fails.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: low
- Thin-chapter risk notes: none; keep the science tied to Eugene Pauly and the loop itself.
- Wave routing: wave 1

### ch02 The Craving Brain
- Section: The Habits of Individuals
- Intent: Show that craving is what locks a routine in place and explain how marketers and designers create habit energy.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: avoid drifting into generic marketing advice.
- Wave routing: wave 1

### ch03 The Golden Rule of Habit Change
- Section: The Habits of Individuals
- Intent: Explain why habits are replaced rather than erased and why belief and community stabilize change.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: none; keep AA and routine replacement visible.
- Wave routing: wave 2

### ch04 Keystone Habits, or The Ballad of Paul O'Neill
- Section: The Habits of Successful Organizations
- Intent: Show how one habit can reshape a wider system by changing incentives, attention, and small wins.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: avoid turning keystone habits into a magical category that applies everywhere.
- Wave routing: wave 2

### ch05 Starbucks and the Habit of Success
- Section: The Habits of Successful Organizations
- Intent: Make willpower practical by treating it as a trainable routine supported by scripts, practice, and predictable cues.
- Source richness: moderate
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: low
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep it concrete and not preachy.
- Wave routing: wave 3

### ch06 The Power of a Crisis
- Section: The Habits of Successful Organizations
- Intent: Explain why institutions often change only when crisis scrambles routines and creates permission for redesign.
- Source richness: moderate
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: high
- Thin-chapter risk notes: morally dense; keep the ethics of crisis leadership alive.
- Wave routing: solo/premium

### ch07 How Target Knows What You Want Before You Do
- Section: The Habits of Successful Organizations
- Intent: Show how companies infer, predict, and steer routines by reading the signals habits leave behind.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: high
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep the privacy / manipulation tension active.
- Wave routing: wave 4

### ch08 Saddleback Church and the Montgomery Bus Boycott
- Section: The Habits of Societies
- Intent: Explain how social habits, peer pressure, and organized routines make collective action possible.
- Source richness: moderate
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: high
- Thin-chapter risk notes: socially dense; avoid collapsing strong and weak ties into vague community language.
- Wave routing: solo/premium

### ch09 The Neurology of Free Will
- Section: The Habits of Societies
- Intent: Close the book by testing how far habit explains conduct before responsibility must return.
- Source richness: moderate
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: high
- Thin-chapter risk notes: do not flatten the chapter into either pure determinism or pure blame.
- Wave routing: solo/premium
