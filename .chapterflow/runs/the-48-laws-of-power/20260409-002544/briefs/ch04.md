Core Claim:
The fourth law argues that excessive speech reduces power because it gives away emotion, lowers mystery, and invites other people to measure you too closely. Greene supports that claim by contrasting Coriolanus, whose aura collapsed when he spoke too much in politics, with Louis XIV, whose sparse speech kept others uncertain and therefore deferential. Andy Warhol extends the same point into a modern register: brief, vague replies can make people project meaning and depth into silence. The chapter is strongest when it treats restraint as control over impression and leakage, not as empty coldness.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's cleanest argument that speech volume itself is a power variable. The chapter is not simply about being quiet. It is about how fewer words can force projection, preserve ambiguity, and prevent self-exposure.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that too much speech reveals weakness, emotion, and uncontrolled impulse.
Then the author shows that restraint can create mystery and make others project significance into you.
Then the author concludes that silence must stay tactical, because in some settings harmless chatter lowers suspicion better than hard reserve.

Required Anchors:
- Coriolanus as the warning case, with the friction that his reputation depended on distance and the consequence that his political speech disappointed audiences and led to downfall
- Louis XIV as the counterexample, with the friction that few people knew what he thought and the consequence that uncertainty reinforced fear and control
- Andy Warhol as the modern echo, with the supported claim that sparse, vague replies can create the appearance of depth and power
- the chapter's reversal that chatty-jester behavior can sometimes lower guard more effectively than stiff silence

Counterarguments or Limits:
Speech restraint becomes weak when it looks performative, contemptuous, or evasive. Some settings demand warmth, reassurance, or enough explanation for coordination. The chapter needs the line between controlled brevity and suspicious withholding.
