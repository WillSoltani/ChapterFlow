Core Claim:
The thirty-first law argues that power often increases when you shape the available options so other people move within a field you designed. Greene's point is not that force is always unnecessary or that every choice can be perfectly controlled. It is that many outcomes are effectively decided upstream when the menu of plausible options already channels the chooser toward one result. The chapter is strongest when it stays on framed choice, constrained menus, and apparent freedom rather than on generic manipulation slogans.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It shifts from controlling the appearance of accomplishment into controlling the architecture of choice itself. Chapter 30 showed how polished surfaces guide perception; Chapter 31 asks how the next layer of power shapes the options others feel they are selecting from freely.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that open conflict is often riskier than shaping the field before choice occurs.
Then the author shows that menu design, baited alternatives, and constrained paths can guide decisions without overt force.
Then the author concludes that influence is often strongest when others feel they chose the outcome themselves.

Specific Applications:
- noticing where outcomes are really set by the menu rather than by the chooser's final gesture
- designing options so the strongest path looks self-chosen instead of imposed
- distinguishing strategic framing from cornering so obvious it triggers resistance
- deciding when option control becomes unfair because relevant stakes or paths are hidden

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as praise for total mind control
- flattening it into generic persuasion advice
- assuming guided choice always removes the need for substance or incentives
- confusing elegant framing with deceptive concealment of material facts

Counterarguments or Limits:
Some situations require open negotiation, explicit stakes, or genuinely broad choice sets rather than engineered menus. The chapter overreaches if it becomes permission to hide essential information or trap people in unfair false alternatives. The useful limit is to shape the field and sequence of choices while leaving enough truth and room that the chooser is not being tricked about material reality.
