Core Claim:
The forty-eighth law argues that rigid form makes a person easier to predict, contain, and break, while adaptability preserves freedom of action under changing conditions. Greene's useful point is not that one should become chaotic or unprincipled. It is that fixed shape can become a target, while purposeful flexibility can protect power when circumstances shift.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It closes the book by moving beyond single tactics into a higher-order stance toward changing conditions. Chapter 47 asked how victory should be limited so success does not reverse into overreach. Chapter 48 asks how strategy survives by refusing to stay locked in one stable shape at all.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that rigid form becomes legible and vulnerable.
Then the author shows that flexibility and adaptation preserve room to respond.
Then the author concludes that power is often safer when it can change shape without losing direction.
