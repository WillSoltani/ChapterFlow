Core Claim:
The forty-seventh law argues that success often becomes unstable when a person keeps pressing beyond the point that already secured the goal. Greene's useful point is not that ambition is always bad or that one should stop too early. It is that victory can produce overconfidence, excess, and one move too many, while restraint after success can preserve gains that further pushing would endanger.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It shifts from moderating visible perfection to moderating victorious momentum. Chapter 46 asked how success should be softened so it does not provoke envy. Chapter 47 asks how success should be limited so it does not provoke reversal through overreach.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that victory creates the temptation to push past the original mark.
Then the author shows that excess after success can create backlash, exposure, or reversal.
Then the author concludes that knowing when to stop is part of securing victory rather than weakness after it.

Common Misreadings:

- assuming stopping early is always safer
- confusing consolidation with surrender

Counterarguments or Limits:
Some victories are incomplete and need follow-through before they are stable. In some settings stopping too soon invites a different kind of reversal. The chapter overreaches if it becomes fear of finishing, passive half-measures, or refusal to consolidate what has been won. The useful limit is to stop after the objective is secured, not before the position is defensible.
