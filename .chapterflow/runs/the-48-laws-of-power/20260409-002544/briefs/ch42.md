Core Claim:
The forty-second law argues that conflict, disorder, or resistance often become easier to break when the source of coordination, authority, or symbolic energy is directly targeted. Greene's point is not that every group is only one person deep or that visible leaders are always the sole cause. It is that many situations hold together through a central figure, and pressure on that figure can scatter followers faster than pressure on the whole field. The chapter is strongest when it stays on root concentration, symbolic centers, and coordination breakdown rather than generic aggression.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It shifts from the successor trapped by inherited greatness into the system organized around a present center. Chapter 41 warned against living inside another leader's shadow; Chapter 42 asks what happens when the active center itself is hit.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that many groups and conflicts concentrate energy around a central figure.
Then the author shows that striking the center can scatter followers more efficiently than attacking the whole group.
Then the author concludes that root-cause focus is often stronger than diffuse struggle, so long as the reader distinguishes a true center from a replaceable surface figure.

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as praise for cruelty
- flattening it into generic attack-the-leader advice
- assuming every group collapses when one visible person is removed
- confusing a scapegoat with a true organizing center

Counterarguments or Limits:
Some systems are resilient, some leaders are symbolic rather than operational, and some conflicts regenerate quickly even after a central figure is removed. The chapter overreaches if it becomes advice to personalize every problem or to ignore structure, incentives, and replacements. The useful limit is to identify a true center before concentrating force there.
