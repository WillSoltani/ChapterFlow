Core Claim:
The twenty-first law argues that appearing less sharp, informed, or threatening than you really are can reduce other people's caution and draw out their confidence. Greene's point is not that incompetence is power. It is that strategic underestimation can create room to observe, let others overextend, and prevent your intelligence from triggering premature defense. The chapter is strongest when it stays on lowered guard, mismeasurement, and tactical camouflage rather than contempt or clowning.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It shifts from withholding allegiance to shaping how readable your intelligence appears. Chapter 20 preserved leverage by refusing cheap commitment; Chapter 21 preserves room by letting others feel too certain that they have already sized you up.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that obvious cleverness can provoke suspicion, envy, or defensive caution.
Then the author shows that seeming simpler or weaker can invite disclosure, overconfidence, and lowered guard in the other person.
Then the author concludes that strategic camouflage can create better timing and more room to act than visible brilliance.

Specific Applications:
- noticing when showing full intelligence would trigger defensiveness too early
- letting a rival or counterpart reveal more because they feel safely superior
- distinguishing deliberate understatement from actual underperformance
- using restrained presentation to protect timing and access

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as advice to become incompetent
- flattening it into generic fake-humility performance
- turning it into contempt for less informed people
- assuming underestimation is always better than visible competence

Counterarguments or Limits:
Some situations require clear demonstration of competence, especially when trust, safety, or authority depends on it. The chapter overreaches if it becomes advice for chronic self-minimization or deliberate confusion. The useful limit is to lower guard without destroying credibility or surrendering actual control.
