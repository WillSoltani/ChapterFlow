Core Claim:
The twenty-third law argues that power grows when force is concentrated rather than diluted across too many fronts. Greene's point is not that every situation should become narrow or obsessive. It is that scattered effort weakens impact, while focused energy can create compounding advantage. The chapter is strongest when it stays on concentration, selectivity, and the cost of dispersion rather than on generic productivity slogans.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It shifts from surviving force and preserving initiative into choosing where to gather that saved initiative. Chapter 22 preserved power by not wasting it in the wrong collision; Chapter 23 asks how power strengthens once it stops spreading itself thin.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that scattered force loses depth and effect.
Then the author shows that concentrated resources, attention, or effort produce disproportionate results.
Then the author concludes that disciplined focus often outperforms broad but diluted activity.

Counterarguments or Limits:
