Core Claim:
The seventeenth law argues that predictability makes other people comfortable, while controlled unpredictability can create hesitation, caution, and strategic unease. Greene's claim is not that random chaos is powerful by itself. It is that when others cannot easily map your timing or reactions, they lose confidence in their forecasts. The chapter is strongest when it stays on expectation disruption, uncertainty effects, and restraint rather than on empty volatility.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's clearest move from scarcity into uncertainty. Instead of reducing presence, the chapter changes the readability of presence.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that stable patterns make people comfortable and easier to prepare.
Then the author shows that disrupted pattern and unreadable timing increase caution and hesitation.
Then the author concludes that unpredictability works best when it is controlled enough to unsettle others without collapsing trust or self-command.
