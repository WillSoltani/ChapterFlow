# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter: Use Selective Honesty and Generosity to Disarm Your Victim
Core concepts to test:
- selective honesty versus total disclosure
- calibrated generosity
- lowered suspicion
- trust asymmetry
- overexposure / obvious-bait limit
- Chapter 12 to Chapter 13 bridge

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate

Question plan:
- q01: define why selective openness can lower resistance more effectively than direct pressure
- q02: identify what trust asymmetry means in this chapter
- q03: identify why the law is not about total honesty or endless giving
- q04: named work scenario about using one real concession to lower resistance
- q05: school scenario about honest admission softening a guarded rival
- q06: personal scenario on generosity versus self-defeating overexposure
- q07: explain why obvious strategic gifts can fail
- q08: judge when candor or giving becomes so visible that suspicion returns
- q09: synthesize how Chapter 11 necessity leads into Chapter 12 disarmament
- q10: connect lowered resistance to Chapter 13's appeal to self-interest

Distractor notes:
- confuse honesty with total confession
- confuse generosity with constant self-sacrifice
- confuse lowered suspicion with permanent trust

Explanation diversity notes:
- avoid repeated openers
- each explanation should teach one piece of disarmament, trust, candor, or overexposure logic

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution roughly balanced across 0 / 1 / 2
