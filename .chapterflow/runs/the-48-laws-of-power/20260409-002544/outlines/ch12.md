Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand how selective honesty or generosity can lower suspicion, why the move works through defense reduction rather than pure virtue, and when overexposure reverses the advantage.

Opening Move:
Open on the weakness of direct pressure against a guarded person when one small concession or gift could have lowered the guard faster.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for lowered suspicion, strategic candor, and calibrated generosity
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for trust asymmetry and the mechanism by which defenses relax
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for overexposure risk, obvious bait, and the tactic's limit

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define why selective openness can disarm resistance better than force
- P2: explain how a small truth or gift can create outsized trust
- P3: distinguish strategic candor from total disclosure
- P4: distinguish calibrated generosity from self-sacrificing overgiving
- P5: apply the law to work, school, and personal settings through lowered vigilance
- P6: preserve the limit that obvious bait or overexposure can reactivate suspicion
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 13 on appealing to self-interest

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes measured disarmament from naive sincerity; the leverage comes from lowered defenses, not from moral purity.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the line between useful trust-lowering signals and openness so excessive that it destroys the strategic edge.

Threshold Question:
When does selective honesty or generosity lower resistance, and when does the tactic become so visible or excessive that it breeds new suspicion?
