Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand why isolation can make a person weaker, how circulation and contact preserve awareness, and where prudent boundaries differ from fortress-style withdrawal.

Opening Move:
Open on the apparent safety of retreat, then show how enclosure cuts off information and makes a person easier to misread the field from inside.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for isolation, comfort, and the false promise of safety
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for information loss, circulation, and adaptive contact
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for prudent boundaries, exposure limits, and the difference between privacy and fortress thinking

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define why isolation looks protective
- P2: explain how retreat cuts off information and movement
- P3: show why circulation preserves awareness and flexibility
- P4: distinguish contact from naive openness
- P5: apply the pattern to work, school, and personal settings
- P6: preserve the limit where privacy is useful but enclosure becomes blindness
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 19 on the danger of dealing with the wrong person

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes prudent boundaries from defensive isolation that narrows information and makes a person easier to trap.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the line between selective protection and self-defeating enclosure.

Threshold Question:
When does distance preserve judgment, and when does it start starving you of the information and contact needed to stay safe?
