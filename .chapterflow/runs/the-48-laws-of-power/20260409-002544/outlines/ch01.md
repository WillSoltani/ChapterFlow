Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand why the danger is not excellence itself but excellence that makes a superior feel reduced, and why redirected credit can change the same competence from a threat into an asset.

Opening Move:
Open on Fouquet's fatal celebration before Louis XIV, with the visible magnificence turning from gift to insult in a single evening.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for Fouquet and the status-threat mechanism
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for Galileo and redirected credit
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for complement versus substitute and superior-type limits

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: vivid opening scene at Vaux-le-Vicomte and the reversal from triumph to danger
- P2: explain status threat and why retaliation can exceed the real objective threat
- P3: shift to Galileo and the decision to redirect glory upward
- P4: name the complement-versus-substitute distinction
- P5: apply the law to modern hierarchies without becoming generic
- P6: introduce the limit cases of paranoid and genuinely secure superiors
- P7 if needed: bridge to the trust problem that Chapter 2 will sharpen

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The law distinguishes competence that complements a superior's status from competence that substitutes for it.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve that the same tactic changes meaning depending on what kind of superior you are dealing with.

Threshold Question:
When does making a superior feel bigger buy real protection, and when does it only postpone a reckoning with someone who cannot tolerate talent at all?
