Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand how overexposure can reduce perceived value, how selective absence can renew respect, and when scarcity turns from strength into unreliability.

Opening Move:
Open on the weakening effect of constant availability, where too much presence makes a person easier to overlook, undervalue, or take for granted.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for overfamiliarity, scarcity, and renewed attention
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for respect, honor, and perceived value under absence
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for backfire risk, trust, and the limit on irresponsible withdrawal

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define how constant presence can cheapen attention
- P2: explain why rarity or spacing can increase perceived value
- P3: distinguish strategic absence from flaky neglect
- P4: connect scarcity to respect and honor rather than simple mystery theater
- P5: apply the pattern to work, school, and personal settings
- P6: preserve the limit where too much withdrawal damages trust
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 17 on unpredictability

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes controlled scarcity that renews attention from careless disappearance that collapses reliability.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the line between value created by rarity and damage created by absence that breaks trust.

Threshold Question:
When does reduced presence increase respect by interrupting saturation, and when does absence become neglect that lowers trust instead?
