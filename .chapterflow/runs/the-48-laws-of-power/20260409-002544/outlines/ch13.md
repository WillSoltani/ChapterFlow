Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand why help requests work more reliably when they show visible benefit, how incentive framing differs from sentimental pleading, and when the appeal becomes so naked that it backfires.

Opening Move:
Open on the weakness of asking for help through pity or remembered loyalty when a clearer statement of mutual benefit would have worked better.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for self-interest, visible gain, and the weakness of mercy appeals
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for incentive framing and avoided-loss logic
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for transparent manipulation, insulting one-sidedness, and the tactic's limit

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define why self-interest is a stronger engine of help than mercy or gratitude
- P2: explain how visible gain or avoided loss reframes a request
- P3: distinguish mutual advantage from emotional pleading
- P4: distinguish incentive framing from crude bribery or flattery
- P5: apply the law to work, school, and personal settings through request design
- P6: preserve the limit that nakedly one-sided appeals can insult the listener and fail
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 14 on apparent friendship and hidden observation

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes aligned incentive from sentimental appeal; the leverage comes from giving the other side a reason it can accept as its own.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the line between realistic incentive framing and manipulative requests that expose too much self-interest on the speaker's side.

Threshold Question:
When does a request become more effective by making gain visible, and when does the self-interest appeal become so obvious or one-sided that it triggers refusal?
