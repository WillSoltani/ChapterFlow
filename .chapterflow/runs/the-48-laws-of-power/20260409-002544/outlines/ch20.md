Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand why premature commitment reduces leverage, how noncommitment preserves options, and where strategic independence differs from empty drift.

Opening Move:
Open on the pressure to choose a side, then show how visible commitment lets other people start counting on you for their purposes.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for commitment cost, side-taking, and reduced option space
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for leverage preservation, competition for your alignment, and capture risk
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for timing, necessary commitment limits, and the distinction between independence and drift

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define why commitment can make you easier to use
- P2: explain how noncommitment preserves leverage and options
- P3: distinguish strategic distance from passivity
- P4: show how sides try to recruit and spend your alignment
- P5: apply the pattern to work, school, and personal settings
- P6: preserve the limit where commitment can be necessary but timing still matters
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 21 on seeming weaker or simpler than you are

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes strategic independence from empty fence-sitting that preserves nothing and decides nothing.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the line between leverage-preserving noncommitment and sterile refusal to choose anything ever.

Threshold Question:
When does choosing a side secure a real gain, and when does it only make you easier to count, predict, and spend?
