## Chapter order and intent

1. `The Power of Purposeful Practice`
Intent: separate mindless repetition from practice that uses goals, stretch, feedback, and adjustment.
Source richness: high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: low

2. `Harnessing Adaptability`
Intent: show that bodies and brains adapt far more than talent stories admit, which widens the ceiling for improvement.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity: low

3. `Mental Representations`
Intent: explain why expert perception and self-correction depend on rich internal models, not just repetition.
Source richness: high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: medium

4. `The Gold Standard`
Intent: define deliberate practice as the stricter, coached, field-specific form of improvement that produces elite performance.
Source richness: high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: medium

5. `Principles of Deliberate Practice on the Job`
Intent: translate deliberate-practice logic into work settings where standards are messier and feedback loops are uneven.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity: medium

6. `Principles of Deliberate Practice in Everyday Life`
Intent: adapt the method for ordinary learning contexts without pretending every domain has formal coaching infrastructure.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: low

7. `The Road to Extraordinary`
Intent: map how ordinary improvement can become exceptional performance and where commitment, sequencing, and sacrifice intensify.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity: medium

8. `But What About Natural Talent?`
Intent: confront the strongest talent objection directly and separate genetic influence from lazy fatalism.
Source richness: high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: high

9. `Where Do We Go from Here?`
Intent: widen the argument from individual skill-building to education, institutions, and social expectations about human potential.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity: medium-high

## Rotation plan for examples

- ch01: work, music, school, personal skill-building
- ch02: physical training, language, memory, classroom
- ch03: diagnostics, chess, writing, sport
- ch04: elite coaching, structured drills, evaluative standards
- ch05: workplace learning, professional feedback, high-stakes teams
- ch06: hobbies, adult learning, self-training, everyday routines
- ch07: long-path development, commitment, sacrifice, plateau risk
- ch08: prodigy stories, genetics debate, selection effects, survivorship
- ch09: schools, organizations, policy, opportunity design

## School-setting plan

- alternate seminar room, rehearsal room, lab bench, gym, lecture hall, tutoring office, and library study table
- avoid repeating the same professor-student correction scene structure

## Vocabulary watchlist

- keep: practice, feedback, adaptation, representation, skill, standard, coach, plateau, stretch
- avoid overuse: talent, mastery, excellence, genius, potential
- ban drift into motivational slogans or pop-neuroscience filler

## Motif and callback opportunities

- repeat the distinction between comfort and stretch without using the same sentence frame
- carry forward the idea that experts see and correct differently because they perceive structure differently
- let later chapters answer earlier limits instead of merely repeating them

## Thin-chapter risk notes

- ch06 can drift into generic self-improvement if everyday-life transfer loses operational detail
- ch07 can become vague inspiration unless the cost of becoming extraordinary stays visible
- ch09 can flatten into policy optimism unless it keeps the training logic concrete

## Premium-routing candidates

- ch03 because the mental-representation concept can get abstract fast
- ch04 because deliberate-practice specifics must stay sharper than chapter-generic improvement advice
- ch08 because the genetics debate needs careful limits and anti-strawman discipline
