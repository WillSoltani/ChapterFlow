# Style Memory

- Teach the idea directly, not the existence of the chapter.
- Keep openings curiosity-first, pressure-first, or event-first. Never thesis-first.
- Prefer concrete nouns, clear mechanism, and one clean governing distinction over stacked abstractions.
- Easy is accessible and pictureable. Medium explains mechanism and application. Hard adds a boundary, contradiction, cost, or unresolved threshold.
- Tone differences must be functional, not louder copies of one another.
- Every paragraph needs a distinct job and should land once.
- Cut canned uplift, corporate phrasing, and poster-ready severity.
- Use only source-supported facts. If support is thin, narrow rather than invent.
