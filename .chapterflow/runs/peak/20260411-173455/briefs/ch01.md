Core Claim:
This opening chapter argues that improvement does not come from repetition alone. It comes from purposeful practice: specific goals, full concentration, fast feedback, and repeated work just beyond the current comfort zone. The chapter uses the digit-memory training story to show that ceilings that look natural often move once practice becomes precise enough.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's first mechanism chapter. Its job is to replace vague "practice makes perfect" thinking with a stricter account of how practice actually changes performance. It also sets up the later distinction between purposeful practice and the more structured gold-standard version of deliberate practice.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter rejects naive practice as plateau-producing repetition.
Then it uses Steve Faloon's digit-memory training to show that ordinary-looking limits can stretch under the right pressure.
Then it names the operating features of purposeful practice: narrow goals, focus, feedback, and stretch beyond comfort.
Then it preserves the limit that harder effort alone is not enough forever, which prepares the move to deliberate practice later.
