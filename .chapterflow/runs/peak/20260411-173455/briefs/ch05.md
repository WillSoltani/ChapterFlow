Core Claim:
This chapter argues that work settings rarely offer pure deliberate-practice conditions, but they can still borrow the method by isolating subskills, building feedback loops, and making weak performance more visible inside messy professional environments.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 4 defined deliberate practice in its strongest form. Chapter 5 translates that logic into jobs where standards are mixed, coaching is uneven, and outcomes often arrive late.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter admits that most jobs are not clean training arenas.
Then it shows how parts of deliberate practice can still survive through subskill isolation and feedback design.
Then it explains why professionals need stronger self-diagnosis and intentional review habits.
Then it preserves the limit that work adaptation is never identical to pure deliberate practice.

Required Anchors:
- most work settings are messy compared with music, chess, or sport training fields
- job improvement still benefits from isolating subskills and building review loops
- professionals need external feedback where possible and self-diagnosis where not
- the limit that workplace translation is partial, not a perfect copy of the gold standard
