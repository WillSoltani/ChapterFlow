Core Claim:
This chapter argues that extraordinary performance is not a sudden leap beyond ordinary improvement but the long accumulation of stronger practice loops, rising standards, and deeper commitment, with real costs and sequencing pressures that casual ambition usually avoids.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 6 showed how everyday learners can borrow deliberate-practice logic in bounded form. Chapter 7 extends that logic into the longer road where ordinary gains compound, expectations intensify, and sacrifice becomes harder to ignore.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter rejects the idea that extraordinary performance appears all at once.
Then it shows how improvement compounds through longer sequences of deliberate development.
Then it explains why stronger standards, deeper commitment, and sustained correction raise the cost of the path.
Then it preserves the limit that extraordinary development still depends on structure, sequencing, and sacrifice rather than pleasant inspiration.

Required Anchors:
- extraordinary performance grows from accumulated improvement rather than a sudden miracle

- commitment and sacrifice intensify as the road gets steeper
