Core Claim:
This chapter argues that the book's view of performance should change what schools, workplaces, and institutions expect from people, because human capacity is more trainable and environment-dependent than talent myths and static standards usually assume.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 8 weakened talent fatalism and reopened the question of responsibility. Chapter 9 widens the frame from individual development to education, institutions, and social design.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter asks what follows if performance depends heavily on development and opportunity.
Then it shows why institutions should redesign expectations, training systems, and support structures.
Then it explains that wasted potential is often a product of environment rather than fixed limits.
Then it preserves the limit that broader opportunity still requires serious standards and real developmental design.

Required Anchors:
- institutions should change how they think about human potential
- schools and workplaces often waste capacity by assuming fixed limits too early
- broader opportunity requires better training environments, not softer standards
- the chapter must end constructively without flattening into policy optimism or generic uplift
