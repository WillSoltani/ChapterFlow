Core Claim:
This chapter argues that the human body and brain adapt far more than everyday intuition suggests. Purposeful practice works because systems rebuild around repeated demand. The chapter pushes back against the idea that ordinary people hit fixed limits quickly, while also warning that adaptation follows the pressure you apply rather than your intentions alone.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 1 explained how better practice is designed. Chapter 2 explains why that design can work at all. Its job is to show that performance changes are not just motivational. They are adaptive. The chapter also keeps one caution alive: adaptation is real, but it is shaped by the quality and direction of the demand.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter widens the reader's picture of adaptation beyond obvious physical training.
Then it argues that mental performance also changes when demands stay specific and repeated.
Then it reframes supposed natural ceilings as often being ceilings of exposure and training.
Then it preserves the limit that adaptation can strengthen bad patterns as easily as good ones if the drill is poorly chosen.

Required Anchors:
- the body's visible adaptation to training demand
- the mind's comparable ability to adapt under repeated challenge
- the false comfort of fixed-limit stories
- the limit that adaptation follows the pattern of training, not merely effort in the abstract
