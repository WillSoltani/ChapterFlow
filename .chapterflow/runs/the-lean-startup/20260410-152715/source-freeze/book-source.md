## Narrow paraphrase summaries

### Introduction

The book frames entrepreneurship as management under extreme uncertainty. The central promise is that startups can reduce waste and learn faster by testing assumptions, gathering customer feedback, and adjusting strategy continuously.

### Chapter 1. Start

The opening chapter introduces the lean startup as a scientific management method for building new products in uncertainty. It argues that startups are not primarily trying to ship features quickly. Their real job is to discover how to build a sustainable business. Core concepts introduced here include startup-as-institution, continuous innovation, and the build-measure-learn loop as the steering system.

### Chapter 2. Define

Entrepreneurship is not limited to garage startups. It can exist inside large companies, government, or nonprofits whenever a team is creating something new under uncertainty. Traditional management fails in this setting because it assumes stable operating conditions. Validated learning becomes the primary progress measure.

### Chapter 3. Learn

Validated learning means proving with evidence that the team has discovered something true about the business model, customer, or product. Learning counts only when it is empirical. Ries links this to disciplined experimentation and to root-cause work such as the Five Whys.

### Chapter 4. Experiment

Every startup effort should be treated as an experiment testing business hypotheses. The chapter highlights value and growth hypotheses and argues that market research alone is weaker than real-world customer tests that produce actionable feedback.

### Chapter 5. Leap

Startups run on leap-of-faith assumptions. The riskiest assumptions deserve attention first because they govern whether the business can ever work. Value and growth hypotheses become the core tests that guide steering decisions.

### Chapter 6. Test

The minimum viable product exists to complete one build-measure-learn cycle with as little waste as possible. Low-fidelity MVPs, videos, concierge tests, or manually delivered experiences can all be valid if they generate useful learning about customer behavior.

### Chapter 7. Measure

Innovation accounting replaces vanity metrics with actionable, accessible, auditable measurement. The chapter emphasizes baselines, cohort analysis, and tuning the growth engine before making pivot-or-persevere decisions.

### Chapter 8. Pivot (or Persevere)

A pivot is a structured course correction to test a new core hypothesis. Founders should decide to pivot or persevere based on validated learning rather than emotional attachment. Runway depends on how many meaningful pivots remain, not just on time or cash alone.

### Chapter 9. Batch

Small batches accelerate learning by shortening the time between action and feedback. Large batches hide problems, create inventory-like waste, and delay correction. Continuous deployment and smaller releases fit the lean startup because they improve learning speed.

### Chapter 10. Grow

Sustainable growth comes from engines of growth rather than hope alone. The book distinguishes sticky, viral, and paid growth engines and argues that teams should identify the right engine and optimize the variables that drive it.

### Chapter 11. Adapt

Adaptive organizations use feedback loops to improve process and quality without slowing learning to a halt. The Five Whys appears here as a root-cause and organizational learning tool, especially for solving recurring technical problems that often trace back to human systems.

### Chapter 12. Innovate

Established organizations can still innovate if they create sandboxes, autonomy, protected resources, and meaningful incentives for entrepreneurial teams. Innovation requires structures that let new ideas learn without being crushed by the core business too early.

### Chapter 13. Epilogue: Waste Not

The epilogue reinforces the idea that waste in startups often comes from building without learning. Lean discipline is presented as a way to respect time, talent, and capital by turning effort into validated knowledge rather than unfocused output.

### Chapter 14. Join the Movement

The closing movement broadens the argument into a wider innovation practice. Lean startup principles are positioned as transferable across industries and organizations, not as advice only for venture-backed software startups.
