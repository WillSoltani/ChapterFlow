Core Claim:
This chapter argues that a minimum viable product exists to complete one build-measure-learn cycle with as little waste as possible. MVPs are learning instruments, not cheap products for their own sake. Ries allows low-fidelity, concierge, video, or manual MVPs when they generate useful evidence about important assumptions. The chapter therefore reframes product minimalism around learning speed and waste reduction rather than around stripped-down aesthetics.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 5 says test the riskiest assumptions first. Chapter 6 shows the practical instrument for doing that. Its distinctive move is redefining MVP as a learning device rather than a low-quality product.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author says the purpose of an MVP is one build-measure-learn cycle with minimum waste.
Then the author widens what an MVP can look like.
Then the author insists the standard is useful learning, not cheapness for its own sake.
