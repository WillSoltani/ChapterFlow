Core Claim:
This chapter argues that startups depend on leap-of-faith assumptions and should test the riskiest ones first. Ries uses value and growth hypotheses as the central leaps because they determine whether the product matters and whether the business can spread. Steering improves when teams stop testing what is convenient and start testing what would most damage the business if false. The chapter is therefore about ranking uncertainty by consequence.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 4 says startups are experiments. Chapter 5 asks which assumptions deserve the first experiment. Its distinctive move is prioritization: not all unknowns matter equally.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author says startups rely on leap-of-faith assumptions.
Then the author identifies the riskiest assumptions as the ones that deserve attention first.
Then the author ties that ranking back to value and growth hypotheses.
