Core Claim:
This chapter argues that a startup is not mainly a product-building sprint. It is a disciplined attempt to discover how to build a sustainable business under extreme uncertainty. The lean startup method matters because it shifts effort from output theater toward faster learning loops. Build-measure-learn is introduced as the steering system that keeps action tied to evidence rather than attachment. The chapter also treats entrepreneurship as management work, not inspirational chaos.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the first chapter that resets what success means. If this chapter goes generic, the rest of the book collapses into ordinary startup hustle advice. The chapter has to hold onto one sharp distinction: shipping, activity, and confidence do not count if they are not producing learning about the business.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that startups operate under extreme uncertainty.
Then the author shows that ordinary product-building effort can still produce failure if it does not generate learning.
Then the author introduces lean startup management and the build-measure-learn loop as the proper steering logic.

Required Anchors:
- Eric Ries's reframing of entrepreneurship as management under extreme uncertainty, with the consequence that discipline matters more than startup mythology.
- Lean manufacturing as the carrying analogy, with the friction that startup teams often confuse speed with progress.
- Build-measure-learn as the steering loop, with the consequence that progress is measured by what the team learns about a sustainable business.
