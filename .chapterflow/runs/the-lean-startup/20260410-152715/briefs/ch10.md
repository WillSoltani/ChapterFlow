Core Claim:
This chapter argues that sustainable growth comes from engines of growth rather than from hope, hype, or generic ambition. Ries distinguishes sticky, viral, and paid engines and says teams should identify the right engine and optimize the variables that drive it. The chapter therefore reframes growth as a mechanism to understand and tune.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 9 focuses on faster learning through batch size. Chapter 10 shifts into acceleration by explaining how growth actually sustains itself. Its distinctive move is replacing vague growth desire with specific engines and governing variables.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author says growth must be sustainable and mechanism-based.
Then the author distinguishes multiple engines of growth.
Then the author says teams should optimize the variables that drive their chosen engine.

Required Anchors:
- sustainable growth, with the consequence that expansion must have a repeatable logic
- engines of growth, with the friction that teams often chase growth without identifying the engine
- sticky / viral / paid engines, with the consequence that each demands different optimization focus

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- engine of growth: the mechanism that sustains expansion
- sticky growth: growth driven by retention and low churn
- viral growth: growth driven by users bringing in more users
- paid growth: growth driven by unit economics that fund acquisition

Specific Applications:
- teams diagnosing which growth mechanism they actually rely on
- founders stopping generic growth talk and focusing on engine variables
- operators separating retention-driven growth from acquisition-driven growth

Common Misreadings:
- all growth works the same way
- more users automatically means a healthy engine
- teams should optimize everything at once instead of the governing variable

Counterarguments or Limits:
