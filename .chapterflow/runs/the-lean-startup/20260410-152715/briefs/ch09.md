Core Claim:
This chapter argues that small batches accelerate learning because they shorten the distance between action and feedback. Ries contrasts them with large batches, which hide problems, build inventory-like waste, and delay correction. The chapter therefore reframes operational throughput around learning speed and earlier error visibility.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 8 makes strategic course-correction decisions. Chapter 9 shifts from strategy to operational cadence. Its distinctive move is treating batch size as a learning-speed decision rather than only an efficiency choice.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author says small batches shorten feedback loops.
Then the author explains how large batches hide errors and create waste.
Then the author ties smaller releases and continuous deployment to faster correction.

Required Anchors:
- small batches, with the consequence that learning arrives sooner
- large batches as delayed-problem containers, with the friction that they can feel efficient at first
- continuous deployment or smaller releases, with the consequence that correction becomes cheaper and faster
