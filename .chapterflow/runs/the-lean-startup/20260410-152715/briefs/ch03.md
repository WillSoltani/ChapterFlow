Core Claim:
This chapter argues that validated learning, not activity, is the real output of startup work. Learning counts only when it is tied to evidence strong enough to prove something about the customer, product, or business model. Ries uses experimentation and root-cause discipline to separate real learning from hopeful interpretation. The chapter therefore hardens the book's scorecard: teams must prove what they think they know.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapters 1 and 2 define the work and where it applies. Chapter 3 defines what counts as real progress within that work. Its sharp edge is the difference between claimed learning and empirical learning.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author says startups need learning, not just effort.
Then the author narrows the term by insisting that learning must be validated by evidence.
Then the author connects that standard to experimentation and root-cause discipline.

Required Anchors:
- validated learning as the startup's real output, with the consequence that activity alone is downgraded
- empirical proof versus intuition or narrative, with the friction that teams prefer stories that flatter effort
- experimentation / Five Whys as discipline, with the consequence that correction becomes operational rather than emotional
