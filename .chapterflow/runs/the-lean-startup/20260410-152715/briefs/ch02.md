Core Claim:
This chapter expands the meaning of entrepreneurship by defining startups through uncertainty rather than size, age, or cultural image. Ries argues that entrepreneurial work can appear anywhere new products or services are being created under unsettled conditions. That wider definition matters because it exposes why traditional management often fails in these settings. Validated learning remains the right progress metric because the basic facts are still in motion.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 1 reset what counts as progress. Chapter 2 resets who the method applies to. The chapter has to make the reader stop picturing startups as only garage companies and instead see uncertainty as the real boundary.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author breaks the stereotype that entrepreneurship belongs only to small startups.
Then the author defines startup work by uncertainty.
Then the author shows why stable-environment management fails there and why validated learning is still necessary.
