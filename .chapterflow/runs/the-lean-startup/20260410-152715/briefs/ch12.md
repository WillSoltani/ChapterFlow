Core Claim:
This chapter argues that established organizations can still innovate if they create sandboxes, autonomy, protected resources, and meaningful incentives for entrepreneurial teams. Ries says innovation needs structures that let new ideas learn without being crushed by the core business too early. The chapter therefore treats organizational design as a condition for internal entrepreneurship.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 11 focuses on adaptation and process learning inside organizations. Chapter 12 asks how larger organizations can preserve genuine innovation. Its distinctive move is specifying the structural protections that let entrepreneurial teams learn inside big systems.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author says established organizations can innovate under the right conditions.
Then the author names sandboxes, autonomy, resources, and incentives as those conditions.
Then the author ties protection and accountability together so innovation can learn without being smothered.

Required Anchors:
- sandboxes, with the consequence that new ideas need protected testing space
- autonomy and protected resources, with the friction that core systems can crush early learning
- meaningful incentives and accountability, with the consequence that internal innovation needs both freedom and responsibility
