An obstacle can grow without changing shape. The facts stay the same, but the mind starts racing ahead of them. One delay becomes a ruined month. One hard conversation becomes a broken relationship. One unfinished task becomes an imagined collapse crouching just beyond tomorrow. The present obstacle is already real, but now the future gets dragged backward into it. That extra burden is often imagined rather than earned, yet it can feel heavier than the real step in front of you.

That is why living in the present moment matters here. It is not a mystical idea and not permission to stop planning. It is the discipline of keeping attention attached to the move that is actually available now. The next call may be real. The next page may be real. The next sentence in the conversation may be real. The catastrophe you have started rehearsing may not be. Present attention keeps the obstacle sized to the current step instead of to a whole invented future.

Once you sort what is up to you, another problem appears: attention still wants to run away from that limited present task into every possible future discomfort. The control boundary tells you what is yours. Present-moment attention tells you where to place your force so that what is yours can actually be worked.

The main distortion here is not planning. Planning is often useful. The distortion is living mentally inside projection. A person can think ahead responsibly, make a plan, and return to the task. Or a person can keep forecasting outcome after outcome until the current move disappears under speculation. The difference matters because planning sharpens execution while projection steals from it.

This shows up everywhere. At work, people start thinking about whether the project will fail next quarter before they finish the next hour's task. In school, they imagine the final decision before they have handled today's preparation. In relationships, they begin responding to the feared ending of the conversation instead of to the sentence that was actually said. Future drag pulls force away from the execution window where anything useful can happen.

So the practical move is smaller than people expect. Return to the current step. Work the sentence in front of you. Finish the task inside today's window. Hear the actual question before answering the imagined accusation behind it. The obstacle may still be difficult, but it becomes less swollen when you stop feeding it with speculative futures.

This chapter keeps one limit in view. The future cannot be ignored completely, because responsible people still have to plan. The discipline is to visit the future without moving into it. Plan, note what matters, and come back. Once attention can stay with the present move, the next chapter can ask a different question: how do you think differently enough to avoid repeating the same stale response?
