Core Claim:
The chapter argues that progress begins when effort is narrowed to what is actually within choice, discipline, and response rather than spilled into outcomes, timing, or other people's decisions. Its job is to show that perspective becomes practical only when the reader can separate controllable ground from uncontrollable weather. The chapter should make control feel clarifying and effort-saving rather than passive or resigned. It should stay distinct from Chapter 5 by moving from widened interpretation to narrowed agency.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 5 widened the angle. Chapter 6 asks what portion of the widened field actually belongs to you. It is not another perspective chapter and not yet present-moment attention as its own chapter. It is the sorting move that prevents wasted force.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that obstacles feel bigger when readers confuse what they can influence with what they can command.
Then the author shows that force becomes useful when it is aimed at response, preparation, discipline, and choice.
Then the author concludes that peace and effectiveness both improve when effort is narrowed to what is actually up to you.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- control boundary: the line between what belongs to your choice and what does not
- wasted force: effort spent trying to command outcomes, timing, or other people
- agency pocket: the portion of the situation where response and discipline still matter

Specific Applications:
- focusing on preparation instead of obsessing over verdicts
- separating your response from another person's mood or decision
- directing effort toward process, conduct, and next move

Common Misreadings:
- treating control as total power over results
- confusing acceptance of limits with passivity or surrender
- using the idea of control to avoid responsibility for the part that is yours

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should acknowledge that control is often partial. Many obstacles include mixed territory. The task is not to deny influence or complexity, but to identify the portion that genuinely belongs to effort and character.
