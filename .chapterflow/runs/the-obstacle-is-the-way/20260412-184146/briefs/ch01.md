Core Claim:
The chapter argues that the first usable kind of power is interpretive rather than external. Before a person can act well, they have to stop surrendering judgment to fear, agitation, or reflexive storytelling about what an obstacle means. The chapter's job is to show that clearer perception enlarges agency because it reveals where choice still exists. It should make power feel like disciplined seeing, not bravado. It should stay narrow and avoid pretending the chapter has already earned the whole book's later action-and-will claims.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is the book's doorway into agency. It is not yet about persistence, tactics, endurance, or acceptance. It is about the moment before all of that, when the obstacle is still being mentally framed and the reader either gives away power through distortion or regains power by seeing cleanly.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that obstacles arrive through interpretation, not just through raw events.
Then the author shows that fear, emotional charge, and bad framing shrink the room for useful action.
Then the author concludes that disciplined perception restores a first layer of power by clarifying what can still be chosen.

Counterarguments or Limits:
Clearer perception does not magically remove the obstacle. It only prevents the reader from worsening it with distortion. The chapter should acknowledge that some constraints remain real even after the mind is steadied.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
The introduction frames adversity as a gift; this chapter turns that framing into the first practical discipline by asking what kind of power remains at the start.

Next Chapter Bridge:
Once power is recognized at the perceptual level, the next move is to steady the nervous system enough to keep using it under pressure.

Moral Complexity:
The chapter should not blame readers for suffering or imply that every obstacle is mentally self-created. It should frame perception as a strategic advantage, not a moral verdict on people under pressure.
