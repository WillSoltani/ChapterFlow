Core Claim:
The chapter argues that clear perception collapses quickly when the body floods with urgency, so composure is not cosmetic but operational. Its job is to show that a steadier nervous system protects judgment under pressure and keeps the obstacle from being amplified by panic. The chapter should make calm feel disciplined and practical rather than mellow or passive. It should stay narrower than later chapters about emotion and action by focusing on the immediate management of nerves under stress.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 1 separated event from interpretation. Chapter 2 asks what keeps that separation intact once pressure becomes bodily and immediate. It is not yet about fully mastering emotion, reframing the whole situation, or taking action. It is about not letting agitation seize the controls during the first hot moment.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that panic degrades perception even when the reader knows better in theory.
Then the author shows that composure preserves accuracy and keeps options visible under pressure.
Then the author concludes that steadier nerves are the bridge between clear seeing and any useful next move.

Specific Applications:

- resisting the urge to react from adrenaline alone
- protecting judgment in meetings, exams, or conflict when the body spikes first

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
Steadier nerves do not solve the obstacle by themselves. They only keep the reader from making the obstacle worse through a reactive surge. The chapter should acknowledge that some situations still require fast action once the first spike is controlled.
