# Style Memory

Core voice:
- Write like a sharp human explainer with taste.
- Specific, concrete, compressed, vivid, and authored beats generic, abstract, padded, or assembled prose.
- Teach the idea directly. Reader-facing surfaces should almost never narrate "the chapter."

Hard constraints:
- No invented facts, quotes, studies, or mechanisms.
- No em dashes.
- No filler neuroscience, fake precision, corporate tone, textbook tone, or self-help mush.
- No thesis-first breakdown openings and no slogan-first competitive openings.
- No repeated sentence, repeated paragraph job, repeated ending beat, or reusable template tail.

Depth contract:
- Easy: pictureable opening, one clean model, one usable implication, grade 8-9, exactly 3 takeaway points, no moreDetails.
- Medium: mechanism plus friction plus application, grade 10-11, singular selfCheckPrompt, 5-6 takeaways with real moreDetails.
- Hard: threshold question plus boundary/contradiction/cost/synthesis, grade 12, exactly 2 selfCheckPrompts, not longer-medium.

Tone contract:
- Gentle lowers resistance.
- Direct states the mechanism cleanly.
- Competitive sharpens stakes without sounding theatrical, corny, or poster-ready.
- Tone variants must differ in function, not just adjective intensity.

Examples and support surfaces:
- Scenarios must be vivid, modern, concrete, and socially realistic.
- whatToDo must describe a precise move, not generic coaching.
- whyItMatters must pay off the scenario's consequence.
- Review cards, recap, prompts, implementation surfaces, and moreDetails must all do different jobs.
