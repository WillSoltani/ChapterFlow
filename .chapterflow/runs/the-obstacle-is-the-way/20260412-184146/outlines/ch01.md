Chapter Promise:
By the end of the chapter, the reader should understand that their first real power in adversity is the ability to see clearly instead of reacting to a distorted mental story.

Opening Move:
Open with the felt moment when a problem arrives and the mind rushes to add fear, finality, or insult before any action has begun.

- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): Musashi used in P4 as the observing-versus-interpreting distinction
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): none; use conceptual pressure instead of inventing a weak anchor

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: anchor the arrival of an obstacle as an interpretive event before it becomes an external strategy problem
- P2: mechanism showing how fear and emotional over-reading shrink agency
- P3: Rockefeller anchor tied to calm objectivity under pressure
- P4: Musashi anchor tied to observing what is actually there
- P5: implication explaining how clear seeing restores usable power
- P6: limit stating that perception does not erase reality, but it stops self-sabotaging exaggeration

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's core distinction is event versus interpretation. Power begins when those two are no longer treated as the same thing.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must face the limit that clear perception is not magical control. It must ask what power remains when reality is still painful, unfair, or resistant.

Threshold Question:
How much of what feels like a crushing obstacle is the obstacle itself, and how much is the meaning I added before I looked clearly?
