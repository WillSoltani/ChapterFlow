# Chapter 7 Outline: Maneuvering

**6-paragraph structure**

---

**P1 — Hook**
The deviation paradox — the long route that becomes the direct one. Open from the image of a commander who started later but arrived first, and the opponent who cannot understand how. The advantage was not in speed; it was in the route chosen and the opponent misdirected.

**P2 — The forced-march cost schedule**
Precision about what speed costs — not a general caution but a specific diminishing-returns claim. 100 li: leaders captured, force scattered. 50 li: half arrives. 30 li: two-thirds arrive. The cost is not linear; it compounds. Speed is never free.

**P3 — Logistics as the invisible prerequisite**
Movement only works if the supply chain holds. Baggage, provisions, bases — without any one of them, the army is lost. Terrain knowledge and local guides are not optional enhancements but functional prerequisites. The deviation strategy is only available to the commander who has solved the logistics problem first.

**P4 — Morale as a time resource**
Morning spirit is keenest; by noon it flags; in the evening, the soldier's mind is bent on returning. Morale is not a fixed quantity but a time-sensitive one. Attack the flagging opponent; wait out the keen one. This is not patience — it is the art of choosing the moment.

**P5 — The seven prohibitions and the judgment they require**
Do not pursue simulated flight; do not attack keen temper; do not swallow bait; do not intercept homeward-bound forces; surround with an outlet; do not press a desperate foe. Hard depth marker: the prohibitions name the traps but cannot tell a commander when the traps are active. Knowing whether a retreat is simulated or real requires exactly the situational judgment the rule cannot supply.

**P6 — Bridge to Ch8**
Ch8 (Variation of Tactics): the moment when the standard rules stop applying. Ch7's prohibitions are themselves a set of rules with exceptions — Ch8 takes up what happens when flexibility becomes the rule itself.
