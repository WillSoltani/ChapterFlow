# Chapter 10 Outline: Terrain

---

## Easy Depth — 3 Key Takeaways (point only)

1. Six terrain types are categories of situational constraint. Knowing which type you are in tells you which moves are available and which are foreclosed before you take a single step.

2. Six calamities come from specific command failures, not from the enemy's strength: flight, insubordination, collapse, ruin, disorganization, rout — each traceable to a decision the general made, or failed to make.

3. Knowing the ground and knowing how to act on the ground are two different competences. A general who can name the terrain type but cannot translate that into a correct decision has information without leverage.

---

## Medium Depth — 5 Key Takeaways (point + moreDetails direction)

**T1 — Six terrain types: each carries a specific decision rule**
Point: Sun Tzu classifies terrain not by appearance but by what each type permits or forecloses. Accessible: occupy the high sunny position first and guard communications. Entangling: advance only if the enemy is unprepared; if you fail, you cannot return. Temporizing: neither side benefits from moving first; withdraw to lure, then strike when part of the enemy has come out. Narrow passes: hold fully if you arrive first; do not enter if the enemy holds it fully. Precipitous heights: occupy the high side first and wait; if forestalled, withdraw and entice. Great distance: do not provoke battle when forces are equal, as the engagement is structurally disadvantageous.
moreDetails direction: The decision rule is not "what the terrain looks like" but "what actions the terrain makes costly or unavailable." Accessible terrain makes first-mover advantage decisive; entangling terrain makes commitment irreversible; temporizing terrain converts patience into an offensive weapon. The classification work is prior to the decision work, and misclassification produces worse outcomes than having no rule at all.

**T2 — Six calamities: each is a command failure, not a force failure**
Point: Flight follows from being ordered to fight a force ten times larger. Insubordination follows from soldiers who are stronger than their officers. Collapse follows from officers who are stronger than their soldiers. Ruin follows from senior officers who fight on their own account from resentment before the commander is ready. Disorganization follows from a weak general with unclear orders and no fixed duties. Rout follows from a general who cannot estimate the enemy's strength and commits inferior forces against superior ones.
moreDetails direction: None of the six calamities is traceable to the enemy's skill or the force's inherent limitations. Every one is a structural failure in how the command relationship is organized. The calamities are diagnostic categories — each identifies a specific lever the general controls and has failed to use correctly.

**T3 — Knowing vs. using terrain: terrain knowledge is one factor, not the calculation**
Point: Sun Tzu names four elements of field competence: estimating the adversary, controlling the forces of victory, calculating difficulties, and assessing dangers and distances. Terrain is the context in which all four operate, but knowing the terrain type alone does not answer any of the four questions. A general with terrain knowledge who has not completed the remaining assessment has information without leverage.
moreDetails direction: The failure mode is treating terrain knowledge as sufficient when it is only one input into a multi-factor calculation. A general who correctly identifies the terrain type as entangling but has not estimated whether the enemy is actually unprepared (the condition that makes advancing correct) has done the classification work without completing the decision work. Terrain sets the feasible set; the remaining calculations narrow it.

**T4 — General's duty vs. sovereign's command: positional knowledge determines legitimate override**
Point: If fighting will produce victory, the general must fight even if the sovereign forbids it. If fighting will not produce victory, the general must not fight even if the sovereign commands it. The general's duty is to the state's welfare, not to the sovereign's instruction. The sovereign commands from a distance; the general holds the positional knowledge the sovereign cannot have.
moreDetails direction: The override authority is not a claim about the general's personal judgment being superior in all matters. It is a claim about the structure of information: field conditions are only knowable from the field. The sovereign's ignorance is not a failure of intelligence; it is a structural feature of distance. The general's positional knowledge licenses the override only when the divergence is about what is factually happening on the ground, not about strategic preference or political disagreement.

**T5 — Humaneness and discipline as a paired requirement**
Point: Regard soldiers as children and they will follow into the deepest valleys. But indulgence without enforcement produces "spoilt children" who are useless. Both are required. Caring for soldiers enables the authority to enforce; enforcing without prior care produces soldiers who resist even fair orders. The chapter prescribes neither softness nor severity but the combination, with care as the continuous foundation.
moreDetails direction: The failure mode is treating humaneness and discipline as opposite poles of a tradeoff, as if more of one means less of the other. The chapter's framing is sequential and additive: care is demonstrated continuously; discipline is applied within that ongoing care, not instead of it. The two together produce an army that follows into difficult ground because the soldiers trust that the ground is worth going into.

## Hard Depth — 5 Key Takeaways (distinct from medium; what hard moreDetails should add)

**T1 hard:** The terrain classification problem is a meta-problem. The commander must classify the terrain before applying the decision rule, and misclassification produces worse outcomes than having no rule at all. Accessible terrain treated as temporizing terrain — where neither side should move first — leaves an advantage unclaimed. Entangling terrain treated as accessible terrain commits a force to a position it cannot exit. Hard depth surfaces the classification difficulty itself: the six categories are cleanly distinct in the text but frequently ambiguous in the field, where a position may have features of two types, and the decision rule is only correct after the classification is correct.

**T2 hard:** Each calamity has a detection problem. By the time the failure becomes visible as an outcome — the army in flight, the ranks in disorganization — it is too late to correct in that engagement. The calamity must be diagnosed before it produces the outcome, which requires reading the command relationship structure rather than the battle result. Hard depth should make the detection problem explicit: what are the leading indicators of each calamity, and at what point in the causal sequence does the general still have the ability to intervene?

**T3 hard:** The multi-factor calculation is sequential, not simultaneous. Terrain establishes the feasible set. Enemy assessment narrows it. Supply and morale narrow it further. Skipping steps or running them in the wrong order produces false confidence: a general who assesses the enemy without first correctly classifying the terrain may identify a vulnerable moment that the terrain actually makes inaccessible. The sequencing failure is specific: each step in the assessment presupposes that the prior step has been completed correctly, because later assessments depend on what earlier assessments ruled in or out.

**T4 hard:** The general's override authority contains a recursive problem. If the general has the authority to diverge from the sovereign's command when the sovereign is ignorant of field conditions, who determines when the sovereign is ignorant? The general cannot self-certify the condition that licenses his own override, because any general who disagrees with a command can claim field conditions justify the divergence. Hard depth should surface this boundary: the override is structurally legitimate only when the divergence is about factual field conditions that the sovereign literally cannot observe from a distance, not when it is a strategic or political disagreement that the general prefers to frame as a factual field matter.

**T5 hard:** The sequencing failure mode for humaneness and discipline is specific and directional. Care demonstrated after an enforcement event is read as appeasement, not as genuine care — it arrives in a context that signals the care is a response to the soldier's reaction rather than a prior commitment to the soldier's welfare. The sequence must be established before any enforcement events occur. Hard depth should also address the boundary condition: in a new relationship or after a significant rupture, the full sequence is required before enforcement can be accepted. In a long-established high-trust relationship, a single enforcement event can precede a renewed demonstration of care without the same resistance, because the prior history of demonstrated care provides a buffer that the new-relationship situation cannot.

## Examples

**Ex1 — Dilemma (work)**
Character: Hiroshi, senior manager at a regional insurance company.
Concept: Entangling terrain — a commitment that is easy to enter and hard to exit (T1).
Scene: Hiroshi's division has been invited to pilot a new proprietary vendor platform. The vendor is offering favorable terms for early adoption. Leadership is enthusiastic. But Hiroshi has mapped the commitment: once the division's data and workflows are migrated to the platform, switching costs become prohibitive. The contract has a three-year minimum with auto-renewal and data portability restrictions. Hiroshi sees entangling terrain: easy to enter if the vendor performs as advertised, but with no viable path back if they don't. The dilemma is whether to advance into the position or decline and appear risk-averse in front of leadership that is moving toward yes.
Ending type: common_trap — the trap is that the favorable entry terms disguise the irreversibility. The chapter's rule for entangling terrain is to advance only if the enemy is unprepared — here, if the vendor's position is truly strong and the contract locks the division in, the favorable entry terms are the bait.

**Ex2 — Vignette (school)**
Character: Adaeze, a third-year medical resident.
Concept: General's duty vs. sovereign's command — positional knowledge licenses override (T4).
Scene: Adaeze is managing a patient overnight when the attending's written orders specify a medication dose that Adaeze, having just reviewed the patient's most recent labs, believes is contraindicated by a developing renal finding not present when the orders were written. The attending is not immediately reachable. Adaeze holds the field condition the attending does not have. She follows the escalation protocol, documents her clinical reasoning, and adjusts the treatment. When the attending calls back, Adaeze presents her reasoning. The attending confirms the adjustment was correct.
Ending type: consequence — the consequence is not dramatic, but the structural point is clear: Adaeze's positional knowledge was real and factually bounded; she did not override because she disagreed with the attending's general approach, but because a new fact changed the ground condition.

**Ex3 — Contrast (personal)**
Characters: Two adult siblings, Petra and Lionel, both dealing with a parent who has recently moved into assisted living.
Concept: Humaneness and discipline as paired requirement (T5).
Scene: Petra's approach is strict consistency: she holds her parent to the facility's schedule, enforces limits on late-night calls, and declines requests that the facility staff has advised against. Her parent complies but is withdrawn and does not share what is actually happening day-to-day. Lionel's approach is pure accommodation: he reshapes every visit around whatever his parent requests, never pushes back, and absorbs all complaints without redirection. His parent tells him everything but acts on nothing. Neither approach is producing what either sibling wants. The contrast is the chapter's paired requirement: Petra has the discipline without the demonstrated care that would make it trusted; Lionel has the warmth without the structure that would make it useful.
Ending type: perspective_reframe — the frame that both siblings are solving the same problem incorrectly from opposite directions. The solution is not a point between them on a dial but a combination neither of them is currently using.

**Ex4 — Decision Point (work)**
Character: Priya, operations lead at a mid-size e-commerce fulfillment center.
Concept: Six calamities — disorganization from weak authority and unclear roles (T2).
Scene: Priya has inherited a team from a manager who left abruptly. The team is technically capable but has no consistent handoff protocols: two people are doing the same checks, a third task has no owner, and there are three informal leaders who each give slightly different direction to the same group of four people. Nothing has failed yet — shipments are going out — but Priya can see the disorganization in the command structure that produced the unclear roles. She has to decide: wait until something fails and correct from evidence, or act now on the structural diagnosis before the failure event.
Ending type: decision — the chapter's diagnostic is structural, not outcome-based. By the time the calamity is visible in the outcome, the engagement is already lost. Priya acts on the structural diagnosis.

**Ex5 — Before/After (personal)**
Character: Tomasz, a recreational tennis coach who runs weekend clinics for adults.
Concept: Narrow passes — first-mover advantage in a contested chokepoint (T1, terrain types).
Before: Tomasz has been holding his clinic at a public park facility with courts available on a first-come, first-served basis. He and a competing coach have been informally managing the scheduling conflict by arriving early. The competing coach begins reserving the courts through the facility's new advance reservation system the week it launches. Tomasz, unaware of the new system, arrives to find the courts reserved.
After: Tomasz learns the reservation system and immediately registers a standing weekly reservation for his clinic time. He secures the scheduling chokepoint through the same channel the competing coach used. The lesson is not that he fought better; it is that once he held the narrow pass, the competition was resolved without any direct contest.
Ending type: reversal — the before state is one of informal contest; the after state converts the chokepoint into a settled advantage through first-mover occupation.

**Ex6 — Inner Monologue (school)**
Character: Zephyr, a graduate student in an urban planning program who is also a teaching assistant for an undergraduate policy course.
Concept: Knowing vs. using — the three-part victory formula (T3: know your own readiness, know the opponent's openness, know the ground).
Scene: Zephyr is preparing to give feedback on a student's paper that has fundamental conceptual problems. He knows what is wrong with the argument. He is also aware that this student has been visibly struggling and has twice approached him after class in a state of frustration. Zephyr pauses before writing his comments. He runs the three-part check inward: Does he know his own readiness — is he writing from genuine pedagogical intention or from the fatigue of grading twenty papers? Does he know the student's openness — is this student currently in a state where critical feedback will land constructively? Does he know the ground — what has the student actually been told before about this type of problem, and does the student have the framework to hear this feedback as guidance rather than judgment? He realizes he does not know the third factor. He schedules a brief conversation before writing the comments.
Ending type: open_question — knowing that you are going only halfway toward the goal is itself a form of field intelligence. Zephyr does not know yet whether the conversation will resolve the ground condition or reveal that more is needed.

## implementationPlan Direction

The implementation plan should focus on terrain classification before action: the practice of identifying what type of situation you are in before deciding what to do in it. Three concrete applications:

(1) Work: Before agreeing to any new commitment that involves significant switching costs or exit barriers, explicitly classify the terrain — is this an entangling position, and if so, is the condition for advancing (the other party is genuinely unprepared or disadvantaged) actually met? Name the exit conditions in advance.

(2) School: Before giving feedback or making a judgment call in a teaching or evaluation context, run the three-part readiness check: What is my own readiness? What is the other person's openness to receiving what I am about to say? What do I actually know about the conditions on the ground for them? If any of the three is uncertain, address the uncertainty before acting.

(3) Personal: Identify one relationship where you have been either too indulgent or too strict. Name specifically which failure mode it is (humaneness without enforcement, or enforcement without prior care). Then identify what the missing element would actually look like if you added it — not as a general attitude shift but as a concrete behavior change in the next two weeks.

The plan should be explicit that all three applications share the same underlying move: classify first, act second. The temptation in each domain is to act from familiarity (use the terrain rules that worked last time) rather than from an accurate read of the current situation.
