# Chapter 6 Outline: Weak Points and Strong

**6-paragraph structure**

---

**P1 — Hook**
Open from the first-in-field image — one commander arrives rested, the other arrives breathless after a forced march to respond. The gap between them was decided before the engagement, by timing.

**P2 — Imposing the agenda (two mechanisms)**
The commander who does not control the agenda is fighting on someone else's terms. Two mechanisms: lure with advantage (positive pressure) and threaten what they must defend (negative pressure). Both move the opponent; both keep the agenda yours.

**P3 — Concentration vs. division: the knowledge asymmetry**
Discover their dispositions, conceal yours. The result is whole against fractions. The commander who knows and is unknown can concentrate a unified force against separated parts — and at every point of contact, be many against few.

**P4 — Attack undefended, defend inattackable — and the selection problem**
The maxim is seductive but has a selection problem: undefended positions may be undefended because they are not valuable. Hard depth marker: the principle requires knowing which gaps matter, which requires the intelligence the chapter begins to describe but does not fully resolve.

**P5 — Formlessness / water analogy**
Adaptation as the final principle: water has no constant shape, finds the low ground, shapes its course to the terrain. Do not repeat tactics; let methods be governed by infinite variety of circumstances. Hard depth: formlessness requires the most accurate situational awareness of any principle in the chapter — a commander who does not know the terrain and the opponent's actual position cannot be formless, only inconsistent.

**P6 — Bridge to Ch7**
Ch7 (Maneuvering): movement is how the first-in-field advantage is actually achieved. Arriving first requires knowing how to move — and what it costs.
