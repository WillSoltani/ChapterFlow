## Questions

**q01** — recall
What does "the artifice of deviation" mean? How does taking the long, circuitous route become an advantage rather than a liability?

**q02** — recall
What three things does Sun Tzu say an army cannot afford to be without — and what happens if any one of them is missing?

**q03** — apply
Apply the morale-timing principle to a non-military setting. If morale is a time-sensitive resource that peaks, flags, and bends toward withdrawal, how does knowing this change when you act?

**q04** — named scenario (work, Hendrik)
Hendrik is deciding whether to take the obvious, fast route to market or a longer route that no competitors are watching. What does the deviation principle say about this choice — and what conditions must hold for the longer route to work?

**q05** — analysis
The forced-march cost schedule (100 li / 50 li / 30 li) is a diminishing-returns argument, not a simple caution about overextension. What is the difference — and why does it matter for how a commander thinks about speed?

**q06** — named scenario (personal, Naomi)
Naomi needs to have a difficult conversation with her father. She has tried before when he was defensive and the conversation collapsed. How does the morale-timing principle apply to choosing when to raise the subject — and what does it require her to observe?

**q07** — analysis
The seven prohibitions (do not pursue simulated flight, do not attack keen temper, etc.) are rules about when not to act. Why do they require exactly the situational judgment that the rules themselves cannot provide?

**q08** — limit
The deviation principle depends on the long route remaining undetected. When does the deviation principle fail — and what does that failure reveal about the conditions under which the strategy is available?

**q09** — synthesis
Ch6 described formlessness as the highest adaptive state — shaping response to the opponent's actual position. Ch7 describes deviation as turning the circuitous into the direct. How do these two principles relate? Does deviation require formlessness, or can it be executed as a fixed plan?

**q10** — predict
Ch7 ends with a set of prohibitions — things a commander must not do even when initiative seems to call for action. What problem does Ch8 (Variation of Tactics) likely take up, given that Ch7's prohibitions are themselves rules with exceptions?
