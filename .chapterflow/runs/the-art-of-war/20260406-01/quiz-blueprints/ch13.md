# Chapter 13 Quiz Blueprint — The Use of Spies

Chapter: The Use of Spies (Ch 13)

## Core concepts to test
- concept 1: Foreknowledge from human sources only — the categorical rejection of spirits, inductive experience, and deductive calculation
- concept 2: The five spy types as a mutually enabling system rather than a list of alternatives
- concept 3: The converted spy's priority — hinge of the system, most valuable and most dangerous
- concept 4: The doomed spy's function — deliberate sacrifice as a deception delivery mechanism
- concept 5: Intelligence as the precondition for all prior strategic principles (Ch1-Ch12)

## Bloom ceiling
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate (level 5). Create (level 6) is not supported because Ch13 does not teach how to generate new intelligence systems; it teaches how to understand and manage an existing one.

## Question plan
- q01: basic recall — what Sun Tzu identifies as the source of foreknowledge (and what he rules out). Orienting distinction.
- q02: anchor comprehension — identify the correct function of the converted spy among plausible distractors (most valuable because he holds the structural key to the other four types, not because he is loyal or experienced).
- q03: concept application — given a scenario where someone discovers an information leak, identify which response reflects the converted spy logic correctly (convert the channel, do not terminate it).
- q04: named-scenario application (work) — Meera, chief of staff at a fast-growing startup, discovers that a board member has been sharing confidential executive discussions with a competing investor. Which response most accurately reflects the five-spy framework applied to her situation?
- q05: named-scenario application (school) — Tobias, PhD candidate, realizes his advisor has begun briefing a rival student on Tobias's unpublished research in exchange for access to that student's data. Which action most precisely reflects the chapter's distinction between terminating a compromised channel and converting it?
- q06: named-scenario application (personal) — Liang, a family mediator, discovers that one sibling is selectively filtering what Liang says to the other sibling in ways that favor the first sibling's position. Which approach reflects the doomed spy mechanism applied constructively rather than the naive response?
- q07: distinction / trap — distinguish the chapter's claim that all five types must operate simultaneously from the misreading that any one type is sufficient for foreknowledge. Tempting distractor: "the surviving spy is sufficient because she actually returns with intelligence."
- q08: limit or misreading — identify the chapter's unanswered problem correctly: either the regress problem (meta-intelligence about sources), the double-agent recursive problem, or the impenetrability gap. Tempting wrong: "Sun Tzu says to trust the converted spy completely."
- q09: deeper synthesis — what does Chapter 13 add to Chapter 1's framework of temple calculations? Evaluate explanation candidates.
- q10: previous-chapter connection (book loop) — given that this is the final chapter, q10 should close the loop: which claim from Chapter 1 does Chapter 13 most directly fulfill, and how? The question points backward rather than forward.

## Distractor notes
- Tempting wrong type 1: treating the converted spy as important because of loyalty or seniority rather than because of structural knowledge of the enemy's intelligence system (for q02, q04).
- Tempting wrong type 2: treating the doomed spy mechanism as simply "disinformation" without the structural requirement that the spy is sacrificed or deceived about her own fate (for q06, q07).
- Tempting wrong type 3: reading "foreknowledge cannot come from calculation" as a preference for intuition rather than a categorical epistemological claim (for q01, q09).
- Tempting wrong type 4: treating the five types as alternatives rather than complementary roles in a system where each enables the others (for q03, q07).

## Explanation diversity notes
- Each direct explanation must begin with a different opening construction.
- No "The strongest answer...", "The best answer...", "The correct response...".
- Varied openers to rotate across: "Sun Tzu's claim here...", "The converted spy's value...", "What the question tests...", "Notice that...", "The trap in option X...", "Here, the distinction...", "This turns on...", "The mechanism is...", "The misreading in option X...", "Picture what happens when...", "The chapter's ruling on this...", "The structural requirement is...".
- No two direct explanations should share 4+ opening words.

## CorrectIndex plan
- Target roughly balanced distribution across 0 / 1 / 2 over 10 questions (aim for approximately 3-4 / 3-4 / 3-4).
- Actual placement decided at write time; do not force balance at the cost of correctness.

## Connection policy for q10
- q10 is a retrospective connection question pointing back to Chapter 1. The prompt should ask which claim from Chapter 1 Chapter 13 most directly completes, giving the reader the experience of recognizing the book as a loop. The question should name the Chapter 1 image explicitly (the general making calculations in the temple) and ask what Chapter 13 adds to it.

## Forbidden question shapes
- Any prompt that starts with "Which of the following best applies to..."
- Any prompt that quotes the chapter title.
- Any prompt that requires the reader to memorize the numerical order of the five spy types verbatim.
- Any prompt that reduces the chapter to "spying is good and important."
- Any prompt that presents the doomed spy scenario without acknowledging the sacrifice structure.
