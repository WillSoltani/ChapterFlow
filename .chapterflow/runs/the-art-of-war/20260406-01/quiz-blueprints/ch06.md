# Quiz Blueprint: Chapter 6 — Weak Points and Strong

## Questions

**q01** — recall
What does arriving "first in the field" produce, and what happens to the force that arrives second?

**q02** — recall
What are the two mechanisms Sun Tzu describes for controlling the agenda — the ways a commander forces the opponent to move on the commander's terms?

**q03** — apply
Apply the concentration-vs-division principle to a non-military setting. How does a knowledge asymmetry (knowing more about the other side than they know about you) enable you to concentrate effort against divided opposition?

**q04** — named scenario (work, Zahara)
Zahara identifies a market segment before her competitors notice it and moves resources there before anyone else. How does her situation illustrate the first-in-field principle, and what does she risk if she delays?

**q05** — analysis
Why does "attack undefended positions" require knowing which undefended positions actually matter? What is the selection problem embedded in this maxim?

**q06** — named scenario (personal, Saoirse vs. other party)
Saoirse needs to have a difficult conversation. She chooses the setting, frames the agenda, and opens before the other party has prepared a counter-position. How does entering on her own terms reflect the agenda-control principle — and where does the analogy break down?

**q07** — analysis
Formlessness is presented as the highest adaptive state. Why does it require more accurate situational awareness than any other principle in the chapter — and what happens to a commander who attempts formlessness without sufficient intelligence?

**q08** — limit
The concentration/division dynamic describes adversarial situations where one party benefits from dividing the other's attention. In what kinds of relationships does this dynamic not apply — and why does importing it there cause harm?

**q09** — synthesis
Ch3 introduced the preserve-intact principle: the best victory avoids destroying what you seek to win. How does Ch6's concentration-vs-division logic relate? Can you concentrate against fractions while still preserving intact — or do the two principles pull in opposite directions?

**q10** — predict
Given Ch6's emphasis on arriving first and controlling the agenda, what problem must Ch7 (Maneuvering) address? What does "arriving first" actually require in execution?
