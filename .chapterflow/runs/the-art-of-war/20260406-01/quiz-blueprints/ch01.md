# Chapter 1 Quiz Blueprint — Laying Plans

Chapter: Laying Plans (Ch 1)

## Core concepts to test
- concept 1: Strategy as pre-battle assessment, not in-battle heroism
- concept 2: The five constant factors as an interconnected system (not a checklist)
- concept 3: The seven comparative questions as a relational audit
- concept 4: Deception as the operational method for protecting a correct assessment
- concept 5: The epistemic tension between assessment and deception (hard / deeper questions)

## Bloom ceiling
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate (level 5). Create (level 6) is not supported because Ch1 does not teach how to generate new strategic systems; it teaches how to run the existing one.

## Question plan
- q01: basic recall — what Sun Tzu says war is (state-survival framing). Orienting distinction.
- q02: anchor comprehension — identify the five constant factors correctly among plausible distractors.
- q03: concept application — apply the seven comparative questions to a simple comparison task.
- q04: named-scenario application (work) — Lena, product manager pre-mortem. Which move reflects a genuine comparison ritual rather than enthusiasm dressed up as planning?
- q05: distinction / trap — distinguish "the audit of the five factors" from "a checklist of business variables". Tempting distractor: the generic business-school reading.
- q06: named-scenario application (personal) — Aisha relocation decision. Which option honors the relational nature of the audit instead of optimizing only one side?
- q07: transfer — apply the deception-as-execution idea to a non-military setting. Tempting wrong: reading it as a permission slip for manipulation.
- q08: limit or misreading — identify the Clausewitzian or Confucian counterargument correctly.
- q09: deeper synthesis — why does the chapter need both halves (audit + deception)? Evaluate explanation candidates.
- q10: previous-chapter connection — none (this is Ch1). Instead, preview the Ch2 tempo question. q10 should ask the reader to predict what Chapter 2 will add to Chapter 1's framework.

## Distractor notes
- Tempting wrong type 1: generic business-school reading of "the five factors" as a checklist (for q02, q05).
- Tempting wrong type 2: reading deception as universal permission for manipulation rather than as an operational method inside a correctly diagnosed adversarial contest (for q07).
- Tempting wrong type 3: treating assessment and deception as two unrelated pieces of advice rather than as a sequence (audit first, then shape the opponent's perception of your audit) (for q09).
- Tempting wrong type 4: confusing the chapter's preference for disciplined calculation with a general recommendation for "more planning meetings" (for q01, q03).

## Explanation diversity notes
- Each direct explanation must begin with a different opening construction.
- No "The strongest answer...", "The best answer...", "The correct response...".
- Varied openers to rotate across: "Sun Tzu's framing...", "The relational audit...", "What this question tests...", "Notice that...", "Here, the contrast...", "The trap is assuming...", "This turns on...", "The mechanism is...", "The misreading in option X...", "Picture what happens when...".
- No two direct explanations should share 4+ opening words.

## CorrectIndex plan
- Target roughly balanced distribution across 0 / 1 / 2 over 10 questions (aim for approximately 3-4 / 3-4 / 3-4).
- Actual placement is decided at write time; do not force balance at the cost of correctness.

## Connection policy for q10
- q10 is a prediction question about Chapter 2 rather than a retrospective connection to a previous chapter (there is none). The prompt should ask the reader to predict which new element Ch2 will most likely emphasize, given Ch1's audit-and-deception frame.

## Forbidden question shapes
- Any prompt that starts with "Which of the following best applies to..."
- Any prompt that quotes the chapter title.
- Any prompt that requires the reader to memorize the order of the seven comparative questions verbatim.
- Any prompt that reduces the chapter to a fortune-cookie maxim.
