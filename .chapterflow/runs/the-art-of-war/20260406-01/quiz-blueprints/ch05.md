# Chapter 5 Quiz Blueprint — Energy

Chapter: Energy (Ch 5)

## Core concepts to test
- concept 1: Direct method joins battle; indirect method wins victory — they are complements, not alternatives, and their combination produces inexhaustible variation
- concept 2: Energy (accumulated potential) vs decision (trigger release) — the timing gap between building force and releasing it is the strategic art
- concept 3: Simulated disorder requires real discipline — the appearance of chaos or weakness is only achievable through extreme internal order
- concept 4: Combined energy — the system carries the outcome; the commander does not require extraordinary performance from individuals
- concept 5: The inexhaustibility of indirect methods — like the five notes, five colors, five tastes: finite elements, infinite combinations

## Bloom ceiling: evaluate (level 5)

## Question plan

### q01: recall — what direct method does vs what indirect method does
Test whether the reader can accurately distinguish the roles Sun Tzu assigns to each method: direct joins battle (engages the opponent's main force, holds attention), indirect wins victory (exploits the gap the direct engagement creates). Wrong answers should include: "direct wins, indirect sets up" (inversion), "they are equivalent" (symmetry error), "direct is superior" (hierarchy error).

### q02: recall — the energy/decision metaphor
Test whether the reader can accurately name both elements of the metaphor: energy is the bending of the crossbow (accumulated potential); decision is the releasing of the trigger (the precise moment of release). Wrong answers should include: "energy is the trigger, decision is the target" (reversal), "energy and decision are the same act" (collapse the distinction), "decision is the planning stage" (common reframe that loses the timing point).

### q03: application — apply direct/indirect to a non-military setting
Present a scenario outside the military domain (e.g., a negotiation, a product launch, or a classroom presentation) and ask which move corresponds to the direct method and which to the indirect. Wrong answers should include: identifying the indirect move as the main engagement (inversion), collapsing the two into one action (missing the structural distinction), labeling the indirect method as "deceptive" rather than strategic (moralized misread).

### q04: named-scenario (work) — an orchestra director using combined energy
Ingrid is preparing her state-school orchestra ensemble for a regional competition. She has spent twelve weeks on foundational technique (every section reliable, no missing entries, no tempo drift). In the final week, she introduces dynamic variations and unexpected phrasing that she expects will differentiate the performance. A violinist, Tobias, asks why they spent so much time on the foundational work when the impressive parts are the variations.

The question should ask what Ingrid's preparation strategy demonstrates about the relationship between direct and indirect methods, and why Tobias's framing misses it. Wrong answers: "the variations were the real work and the foundational practice was just rehearsal," "the direct work could have been shortened to leave more time for the indirect," "the indirect elements are more important and should have come first." Correct direction: the indirect elements (variations) only work because the direct foundation (technique) is secure. Without the direct discipline, the indirect moments are unanchored and land as errors rather than effects.

### q05: analysis — why simulated disorder requires real discipline
Ask the reader to explain the paradox in §16-18: that appearing to be in disorder, or appearing weak, requires a higher level of internal discipline than appearing orderly. The question should require engaging with the mechanism, not just restating the quote.

Wrong answers: "simulated disorder is just acting" (misses the structural requirement), "Sun Tzu means you should appear confident even when you are not" (generic reframe), "real discipline allows you to recover from disorder" (misses that the disorder itself is simulated). Correct direction: to sustain a false appearance under pressure — to maintain the simulation when the opponent responds to it — requires the actual resources the simulation denies. You can only convincingly simulate weakness if you have real strength ready. The simulation is a load-bearing act, not a performance.

### q06: named-scenario (personal) — someone building a case over time before acting
Remi has a difficult conversation to have with a close friend Farrukh about a longstanding pattern in their relationship. For eight weeks, Remi has been writing notes, clarifying what the actual problem is, documenting specific examples, and thinking about what a constructive resolution would look like. Remi has not said anything yet. A mutual friend tells Remi this is avoidance.

The question should ask which element of the chapter's energy-decision framework best describes what Remi is actually doing, and whether the mutual friend's "avoidance" reading is consistent with the chapter's logic. Wrong answers: "Remi is using indirect methods to avoid the direct conversation," "delaying is always a sign of avoidance in Sun Tzu's framework," "Remi should have acted sooner to maintain the advantage." Correct direction: Remi is in the energy-accumulation phase (bending the crossbow). The mutual friend is applying a different framework — one that treats delay as passivity. Sun Tzu's framework distinguishes between delay that builds potential and delay that simply postpones. Remi's eight weeks have produced something; the delay is load-bearing.

### q07: analysis — the infinite-combinations claim
Sun Tzu says there are only two methods (direct and indirect), yet their combination "gives rise to an endless series of maneuvers." Ask the reader to evaluate this claim: is the infinite-combinations argument persuasive, and what does it depend on?

The question should require engaging with the combinatorial logic rather than accepting or rejecting the claim without reasoning. Wrong answers: "the claim is false because two methods cannot produce infinite results" (misses the combinatorial structure across conditions and timing), "the claim is true because war is complex" (vague restatement), "Sun Tzu is using hyperbole" (dismisses without analysis). Correct direction: the infinite-combinations claim is a structural argument about how the same two elements behave differently across different conditions (terrain, timing, scale, opponent behavior). Like five notes producing more melodies than can be heard, the claim is about the variability of application rather than the number of primitive elements.

### q08: limit — the indirect-vs-indirect problem (threshold question)
Ask directly: Sun Tzu says indirect methods win victory. If both sides in a contest have internalized this principle and both are deploying indirect methods while running direct engagements, what does Ch5's framework predict will happen?

This question has no clean answer in the text. It should ask the reader to reason from the chapter's logic to the edge case. Wrong answers: "the better direct engagement wins" (ignores the indirect principle), "Sun Tzu says this cannot happen" (text does not address it), "the side with more resources wins" (resource argument, not structure argument). Correct direction: the chapter's logic implies that if both sides use indirect methods, the differentiating variable shifts inward — to the internal discipline that makes simulated disorder sustainable. The side with greater real discipline sustains the simulation longer and executes the combination with more precision. The chapter does not state this directly; arriving at it requires extending the framework.

### q09: synthesis — relationship between Ch4 accumulated advantage and Ch5 energy
Ch4 ended with pent-up waters and a pound weighed against a grain — images of accumulated advantage reaching a threshold. Ch5 opens the question of how that force is structured and released. Ask the reader to identify the specific claim Ch5 adds that Ch4 left open.

Wrong answers: "Ch5 is about offense and Ch4 is about defense" (misses the continuity), "Ch5 contradicts Ch4's no-mistakes principle" (wrong relationship), "Ch4 and Ch5 address the same concept from different angles" (too vague). Correct direction: Ch4 established that accumulated advantage exists and that its release feels inevitable when the gap is sufficient. Ch5 names the structure of that accumulation (direct builds, indirect wins) and the timing mechanism (energy vs decision). Ch5 is the inside of the image Ch4 presented from outside.

### q10: predict — what Ch6 will address
Ask the reader to predict, based on Ch5's closing emphasis on combined energy and indirect methods, what Ch6 is most likely to address. The prediction should be grounded in the logic of the text.

Wrong answers: "Ch6 will explain how to manage troops in large formations" (addresses opening of Ch5 rather than its close), "Ch6 will address the moral law" (Ch4's theme), "Ch6 will be about speed" (Ch2's theme). Correct direction: Ch5's indirect method works by finding the gap in the opponent's position. The natural next question is: where are those gaps, and how does a commander identify what is weak and what is strong in the opponent's formation? That is precisely Ch6's subject (Weak Points and Strong). The indirect method requires a map of where to aim; Ch6 provides it.

## Distractor notes
- Tempting wrong type 1: inversion of direct and indirect roles (direct wins, indirect sets up). Questions q01 and q04 should have this inversion available as a distractor.
- Tempting wrong type 2: collapsing the energy-decision distinction into a generic "plan then act" frame. Question q02 and q06 should make this available.
- Tempting wrong type 3: reading simulated disorder as dishonesty or "acting" rather than a structural requirement. Question q05 should have this reading as the primary tempting wrong answer.
- Tempting wrong type 4: treating combined energy as "teamwork" (generic) rather than the system-carries-the-outcome principle. Question q04 should include this distractor.
