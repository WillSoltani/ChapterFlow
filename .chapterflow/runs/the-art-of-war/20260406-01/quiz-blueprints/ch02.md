# Chapter 2 Quiz Blueprint — Waging War

Chapter: Waging War (Ch 2)

## Core concepts to test
- concept 1: Tempo as structural constraint, not style preference
- concept 2: The compounding-cost chain (dulled weapons → damped morale → exhausted treasury → opportunistic rivals)
- concept 3: Forage on the enemy as a logistics rule to protect the home state
- concept 4: The stupid-haste vs decisive-finishing distinction
- concept 5: The governance framing (general as arbiter of the people's fate) — hard/deeper questions

## Bloom ceiling
Evaluate (level 5) is the highest justified level. Create (level 6) is not supported at this chapter's depth.

## Question plan
- q01: basic recall — what is the opening cost estimate about? (orienting)
- q02: anchor comprehension — identify the compounding-cost chain correctly
- q03: concept application — apply the forage-on-the-enemy idea to a non-military setting
- q04: named-scenario application (work) — Naomi startup runway conversation
- q05: distinction / trap — stupid haste vs decisive finishing
- q06: named-scenario application (personal) — Theo family lawsuit postmortem
- q07: transfer to another context — apply the tempo argument to a learning setting
- q08: limit or misreading — the hustle-culture misreading of Sun Tzu
- q09: deeper synthesis — the ethical weight of the governance framing
- q10: previous-chapter connection — connect Ch2's tempo constraint to Ch1's relational audit

## Distractor notes
- Tempting wrong type 1: "faster is always better" flattening of the thesis (for q01, q05).
- Tempting wrong type 2: treating foraging as generic "work smarter" (for q03, q07).
- Tempting wrong type 3: reading the governance framing as an endorsement of authoritarian decision-making (for q09).
- Tempting wrong type 4: treating the audit (Ch1) and the tempo constraint (Ch2) as unrelated ideas (for q10).

## Explanation diversity notes
- Each direct explanation must begin with a different construction.
- No "The strongest answer", "The best answer", "The correct response".
- Varied direct openers to draw from: "Sun Tzu's cost estimate is...", "The compounding-cost chain...", "Notice that the forage doctrine...", "Here, the distinction...", "The trap in Option X...", "Ch2's tempo constraint...", "The governance framing...", "Picture what happens when..."
- No two direct explanations should share 4+ opening words.

## CorrectIndex plan
- Rough target: A=3-4, B=3-4, C=3-4. Actual placement decided by correctness, not balance.

## Connection policy for q10
q10 connects Ch2's tempo constraint back to Ch1's relational audit. The supported connection is that the audit produces a forecast that has an expiration date, and the tempo constraint is what makes the expiration date matter.

## Forbidden question shapes
- Any "best applies" or "best reflects" prompt.
- Any prompt that reduces the chapter to "faster is better".
- Any prompt that treats the silver cost estimate as a modern-currency conversion exercise.
- Any prompt that asks the reader to memorize the exact ratio from the foraging doctrine (the number 20 is less important than the principle).
