**q01** — Bloom's: Remember | Depth: Easy | Concept: B (signal-reading vocabulary)

Topic: Identifying what specific environmental signals indicate in the chapter.

Prompt direction: Ask the reader what it means, according to Sun Tzu, when birds suddenly take flight in the area near the army's position.

Correct answer: It is a sign of ambush — someone or something is hiding nearby that has disturbed the birds.

Wrong answer 1 (tests misconception: environmental signals are coincidental background noise): The birds are reacting to weather; their behavior has no tactical significance.
Wrong answer 2 (tests misconception: any animal disturbance means the same thing): The birds signal that the enemy is making a full frontal advance.
Wrong answer 3 (tests misconception: only human-produced signals count): The chapter does not address birds; only human behavior carries tactical meaning.

**q02** — Bloom's: Understand | Depth: Easy | Concept: D (affection-before-discipline)

Topic: Understanding the logic behind Sun Tzu's sequencing of humanity before enforcement.

Prompt direction: Ask why Sun Tzu says punishment must not come before attachment — what breaks down when the sequence is reversed?

Correct answer: Soldiers punished before they have grown attached do not become submissive — they comply without commitment, making them practically useless when conditions require genuine effort.

Wrong answer 1 (tests misconception: affection is the endpoint, not a precondition): Sun Tzu is saying discipline should be avoided altogether in favor of voluntary loyalty.
Wrong answer 2 (tests misconception: the sequence is irrelevant, only outcomes matter): The order does not matter — any mix of care and strictness produces the same result if applied consistently.
Wrong answer 3 (tests misconception: the problem is about morale, not functional effectiveness): The issue is purely psychological — attached soldiers feel better, but their performance outcomes are unchanged.

**q03** — Bloom's: Apply | Depth: Medium | Concept: C (word-versus-deed contrast)

Topic: Applying the verbal-versus-behavioral signal principle to a non-military setting.

Prompt direction: Ask the reader to apply the principle that humble words paired with increased preparations signal an advancing enemy to a workplace situation. A colleague verbally agrees to share a key project resource but simultaneously schedules the resource for exclusive team use over the next three weeks. What does Sun Tzu's signal framework say about how to interpret this?

Correct answer: The behavioral action (scheduling the resource exclusively) is the high-reliability channel; the verbal agreement is low-reliability. The colleague's stated agreement does not change the interpretation of the behavioral evidence — preparation is the signal, speech is noise.

Wrong answer 1 (tests misconception: verbal commitment overrides behavior): The verbal agreement is the relevant data; the scheduling may be an oversight rather than a signal of intent.
Wrong answer 2 (tests misconception: behavioral signals require multiple confirmations before acting): One behavioral instance is not enough to read; Sun Tzu would counsel waiting for a pattern to emerge over several weeks before acting.
Wrong answer 3 (tests misconception: the principle only applies in overtly adversarial contexts): The framework only applies when parties are actively competing; in collaborative workplaces, stated agreements are the primary reliable channel.

**q04** — Bloom's: Apply | Depth: Medium | Concept: B + C | Named character: Kwame (senior engineer, logistics company)

Topic: Applying behavioral signal-reading to identify a system failure before formal dashboards reflect it.

Prompt direction: Kwame's warehouse routing dashboards show green. But drivers are taking longer routes than optimal, dock supervisors are filing a rash of small complaints, and two experienced operators have stopped attending optional system reviews. Kwame has to decide how to interpret these signals. What does the chapter's observational framework say about the relationship between formal indicators and behavioral signals?

Correct answer: Formal dashboards are lagging indicators; behavioral signals from people closest to the physical system are leading indicators. The cluster of behavioral signals Kwame is seeing — each a physical consequence of an underlying condition — is the high-reliability channel. Acting on them before the dashboard catches up is the chapter's method.

Wrong answer 1 (tests misconception: formal indicators are more reliable because they are systematic): The dashboard reflects the actual state of the system; behavioral complaints are anecdotal and should be weighted below the formal data until they reach threshold volume.
Wrong answer 2 (tests misconception: each signal must be individually confirmed before it carries meaning): Each individual signal — the route deviation, the complaints, the absence from reviews — is inconclusive on its own and should be investigated in isolation before drawing any connection.
Wrong answer 3 (tests misconception: signal-reading is relevant only when formal data is unavailable): The observational framework only applies when dashboards do not exist; once formal monitoring is in place, behavioral signals are redundant.

**q05** — Bloom's: Apply | Depth: Medium | Concept: C (word-versus-deed) | Named character: Ingrid (high school principal)

Topic: Applying the word-versus-deed contrast to an institutional conflict where verbal agreement and behavioral action diverge.

Prompt direction: After a mediated session, two department heads at Ingrid's school both verbally commit to collaboration. Over the following weeks, one department's scheduling decisions consistently block the other's key events, shared planning periods disappear through small calendar changes, and informal contact between the two groups drops to zero. Ingrid must decide what to act on. How does the chapter's framework identify which data to trust?

Correct answer: The behavioral data — scheduling patterns, calendar changes, informal contact frequency — is the high-reliability channel. Verbal commitment in a mediated setting is easily produced without commitment; the logistical decisions that follow are harder to fake and carry more information about actual intent.

Wrong answer 1 (tests misconception: verbal agreements in formal settings are binding and override subsequent behavior): The verbal commitment made in the mediated session is the governing data point; the scheduling conflicts are likely unintentional and should be raised as coordination problems, not evidence of bad faith.
Wrong answer 2 (tests misconception: behavioral signals require formal corroboration before action): Ingrid should not act on behavioral signals until she has formal documentation — written complaints, formal grievances — because observable behavior is ambiguous without institutional backing.
Wrong answer 3 (tests misconception: confronting behavioral signals directly will make the situation worse): The chapter would counsel patience; naming the behavioral pattern directly before more time has passed risks damaging the relationship the mediation was designed to repair.

**q06** — Bloom's: Apply | Depth: Medium | Concept: D (affection-before-discipline sequence) | Named character: Cora (family therapist, private practice)

Topic: Applying the affection-before-discipline sequence to a professional relationship where authority must be established before it can be used.

Prompt direction: Cora has a new client family. In the first session, she identifies a pattern of avoidance she knows she will eventually need to challenge directly. A colleague suggests she name the pattern in the second session to establish her credibility as a direct practitioner. Cora waits eight months before making the direct challenge. What principle from the chapter explains her timing?

Correct answer: Authority that arrives before attachment fails. Cora waits because the family must first experience her as safe and on their side — not as a threat — before a direct challenge can land as care rather than attack. The delay is not hesitation; it is the required sequence for making the intervention functional.

Wrong answer 1 (tests misconception: the sequence can be shortened if the practitioner is sufficiently skilled): A highly skilled therapist can establish credibility through early directness; the sequence Cora follows is appropriate for less experienced practitioners but not a universal requirement.
Wrong answer 2 (tests misconception: affection and discipline can be built simultaneously with the right approach): Cora could have used early directness paired with warmth to build both trust and authority at the same time; waiting eight months is unnecessarily conservative.
Wrong answer 3 (tests misconception: the affection-before-discipline principle applies only in military command, not in cooperative professional relationships): Sun Tzu's sequence is specific to hierarchical military command; therapeutic relationships operate on different dynamics and do not require the same ordering.

**q07** — Bloom's: Analyze | Depth: Hard | Concept: F (signal-forgery limit)

Topic: Analyzing the recursive vulnerability in the chapter's word-versus-deed framework.

Prompt direction: The chapter teaches that behavioral signals are more reliable than verbal ones — increased preparations signal attack, regardless of what the enemy says. What happens when a sophisticated opponent knows you read behavioral signals? Does the chapter's framework contain a vulnerability it does not address directly?

Correct answer: Yes. The chapter teaches you to read behavioral signals, but that teaching also implicitly tells a sophisticated adversary which signals to fake. Staged preparations, deliberate posture signals, and manufactured environmental disturbances can all be used to produce false reads. The chapter does not address this recursive problem — it assumes the signals are genuine products of underlying conditions, not crafted outputs. This is the limit of the observational framework.

Wrong answer 1 (tests misconception: the sheer volume of signals makes forgery impractical): Sun Tzu catalogues so many signals that an adversary cannot fake them all simultaneously; the breadth of the framework is its defense against forgery.
Wrong answer 2 (tests misconception: the chapter addresses this through the word-versus-deed contrast itself): Sun Tzu already accounts for deception by teaching that deeds are more reliable than words; a sophisticated adversary using behavioral signals against you is just a version of the verbal-deception problem the chapter has already solved.
Wrong answer 3 (tests misconception: only the highest-level conceptual signals, not physical environmental ones, can be faked): Physical signals like dust columns and bird flight cannot be staged; only behavioral signals like speech and posture are forgeable, so the environmental half of the catalogue is immune to this problem.

**q08** — Bloom's: Analyze | Depth: Hard | Concept: B + E (false-positive problem in signal clusters)

Topic: Analyzing why single signals are insufficient and what the chapter implies about the epistemology of field reading.

Prompt direction: Sun Tzu says birds taking flight signal ambush, soldiers leaning on spears signal hunger, dust in a high column signals chariots. But birds take flight for many reasons, soldiers lean for many reasons, and dust rises in many patterns. What does this mean for how the chapter's signal catalogue should be used — and what does the chapter assume about the relationship between individual signals and reliable judgment?

Correct answer: No single signal is diagnostic; the catalogue is meant to be read in clusters and in context. The chapter assumes that the trained observer will see multiple signals converging before acting — one indicator is a prompt for closer attention, not a basis for action. The epistemology is probabilistic and comparative, not rule-following. The reader who treats each signal as a direct trigger has misread the chapter's own method.

Wrong answer 1 (tests misconception: each signal in the catalogue is sufficient on its own for the indicated conclusion): Sun Tzu lists each signal with a specific conclusion attached; treating them as sufficient individually is the intended application — the catalogue is a lookup table for field decisions.
Wrong answer 2 (tests misconception: the false-positive problem makes the whole catalogue unreliable): Because any individual signal can occur for innocent reasons, the entire observational framework is unreliable and should be supplemented with formal intelligence before any signal-based action is taken.
Wrong answer 3 (tests misconception: the chapter privileges environmental signals over behavioral ones because environmental signals are harder to fake): Sun Tzu treats environmental signals like bird flight as more reliable than behavioral ones like speech precisely because animals cannot dissemble; therefore environmental signals are always sufficient individually.

**q09** — Bloom's: Evaluate | Depth: Hard | Concept: E + Ch8 connection

Topic: Evaluating the relationship between Ch9's observational method and Ch8's requirement for flexible variation of tactics.

Prompt direction: Ch8 argues that a commander must vary tactics according to circumstances — fixed rules applied mechanically will fail. Ch9 argues that judgment is built from accumulated field observation. Do these two claims support each other, or do they create a tension? Can trained pattern recognition become its own form of rigidity?

Correct answer: They are in tension as well as in support. Accumulated observation builds the pattern recognition that makes situational variation possible — you cannot vary intelligently without knowing what you are varying in response to. But the same accumulated experience can also produce category-lock: a commander who has trained their field reading on past campaigns may misread a genuinely novel situation by forcing it into a familiar pattern. Ch9's method is the foundation for Ch8's flexibility, but it does not guarantee that flexibility. The tension the reader should hold: trained observation is expertise, but expertise has its own failure mode.

Wrong answer 1 (tests misconception: accumulated observation directly produces flexibility with no conflict): Ch9 and Ch8 are mutually reinforcing without tension — more field experience always produces more flexible judgment because the observer has more patterns to draw from.
Wrong answer 2 (tests misconception: the two chapters describe different generals, not different aspects of the same general's development): Ch8 is describing a strategist operating at the level of campaign design; Ch9 is describing a field officer operating at the tactical level. They are not in tension because they address different roles.
Wrong answer 3 (tests misconception: Ch8's flexibility is prior to and independent of Ch9's observation): Situational variation requires no prior observational training — a general with strong abstract reasoning can vary tactics without the field-reading expertise Ch9 describes.

**q10** — Bloom's: Evaluate | Depth: Hard | Concept: D + cross-chapter (Ch9 to Ch10 preview)

Topic: Evaluating which account of why authority fails before attachment is structurally more accurate — and what Ch10 (Terrain) likely adds given Ch9's close attention to physical conditions.

Prompt direction: Sun Tzu says authority fails when it arrives before attachment, and also fails when attachment exists without enforcement. Two readers draw different conclusions. Reader A says: the failure is purely psychological — people resist those they don't trust. Reader B says: the failure is structural — without attachment, the general has no information network, no informal feedback, and therefore cannot enforce effectively even if they try. Which account is more consistent with how the chapter actually argues — and what does Ch10's likely focus on terrain suggest about where the chapter sequence is heading?

Correct answer: Reader B's account is more consistent with the chapter's logic. Ch9 is fundamentally about information — reading signs, knowing conditions, building the perceptual vocabulary that makes command functional. A general without the troops' attachment lacks not just their goodwill but their behavioral data: the informal signals, the honest reporting, the ground-level knowledge that only flows through a relationship. Authority without that information network cannot enforce effectively because it is partly blind. Ch10's move to terrain continues the same theme: the general must know the physical ground as the troops know it — terrain is another information environment that must be read before it can be commanded.

Wrong answer 1 (tests misconception: Reader A's psychological account is sufficient): Reader A captures the chapter's full argument — the issue is the troops' subjective experience of trust, which determines whether they follow. No structural claim beyond psychology is needed.
Wrong answer 2 (tests misconception: neither account is relevant because the affection-discipline sequence is a purely practical logistical rule, not a structural or psychological claim): Sun Tzu is not making a claim about psychology or structure; he is giving a practical sequencing rule derived from military experience, and over-theorizing it misreads the chapter's intent.
Wrong answer 3 (tests misconception: Ch10 will address command authority rather than terrain, making the preview claim above incorrect): Given the chapter sequence, Ch10 most likely continues the command-authority theme by addressing what generals must do when troops refuse orders — terrain is a secondary concern the chapter will mention only briefly.
