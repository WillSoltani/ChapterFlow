## Hook quality
Strong. The first sentence creates a specific picture: a general, a room, a lamp, a map. It asks the reader to consider that the part of war worth taking seriously is the part nobody watches. That earns the reader's attention without announcing itself.

## Paragraph-job distinctness
- P1: Scene hook and framing of where the chapter actually takes place.
- P2: State-survival framing; why disciplined auditing is serious.
- P3: The five factors as an interconnected system.
- P4: The seven questions as a relational audit ritual.
- P5: The turn — deception as an operational method for corrupting the opponent's audit.
- P6: The contradiction — assessment vs deception, resolved at the level of epistemic competition.
- P7: Temple callback and the bridge to Ch2's tempo question.

No two paragraphs do the same job. Each earns its place.

## Easy-mode convertibility
Good. The core moves (war is a state-survival problem; plan by comparing; act through perception; the temple is where outcomes are shaped) translate cleanly into grade 8-9 language without losing specificity. The converter will have enough to build an easy breakdown that is a doorway rather than a compressed medium.

## Meta-distance score
Strong. No "the chapter teaches" or "the book says" anywhere in the reader-facing prose. Author name "Sun Tzu" appears five times in breakdown-support positions, which is within the allowed sparing-use envelope for a chapter that is specifically about one author's argument.

## Hard-edge preservation
The hard-depth tension from the outline survives the prose. The contradiction between assessment-as-forecast and deception-as-operational-method is explicit in P6. The threshold question ("does disciplined planning reward the better strategist, or the one who succeeds at making the better strategist's calculations wrong?") is preserved in substance if not in exact wording; the converter will need to surface it as the hard-depth threshold.

## Conceptual repetition risk
Low. Each concept (state-survival framing, five factors, seven questions, deception catalogue, contradiction, temple image) appears once as the load-bearing element of its paragraph. Nothing is restated for emphasis.

## Weakest paragraph
Paragraph 3 is the slightly weaker one. The sentence "A brilliant commander with broken supply lines is still going to lose" lands the point but with a flatter cadence than the paragraphs on either side. It is clear and accurate, but the phrasing "is still going to lose" is the only moment in the chapter where the voice sounds close to generic. Not bad enough to require a repair pass; worth keeping in mind if the converter needs a sharper key-takeaway sentence for this concept.

## Strongest sentence
"The clash of armies is downstream of the clash of calculations."

This sentence is the chapter's whole thesis compressed into ten words, and it is the line most readers will underline. It is also the line that makes the hard-depth contradiction possible. The converter should consider using this as the backbone of the hard-depth breakdown or the competitive key takeaway card.

None detected. The three approved verbatim quotes from the source ledger are referenced in paraphrase only. The five-factor list is stated using Sun Tzu's own terms (Moral Law, Heaven, Earth, Commander, Method and discipline) because these are proper nouns within the translation, not stylistic choices.
