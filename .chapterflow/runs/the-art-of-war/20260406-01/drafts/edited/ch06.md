# Weak Points and Strong

An exhausted messenger arrives at the ridge three hours after dawn. The commander who called the meeting has been there since first light, with tents pitched and ground already known. The messenger is not less capable. He arrived second, and second means starting from conditions someone else chose. Whoever arrives first sets the terms, frames the engagement, and occupies the prepared position. The latecomer arrives depleted and must respond on terrain he did not select.

Two mechanisms prevent you from being the one who arrives second. The first is positive: offer what the opponent must come toward, drawing them onto your prepared ground through attraction. The second is negative: threaten what they cannot afford to abandon, imposing a cost on any choice that does not direct them toward your position. Both mechanisms move the opponent toward you. Neither requires you to chase them. The skilled commander shapes the situation so that the opponent's best available options lead toward a position already prepared.

Behind both mechanisms sits a knowledge requirement. Discover the opponent's dispositions while concealing your own. That asymmetry is what makes concentration possible: knowing where the opponent is thin while keeping your own distribution opaque allows you to bring a whole force against divided fractions. The discovery function requires active probing. The concealment function requires actively shaping what the opponent's intelligence sees. Passive observation and silence are not sufficient for either.

Two targeting rules follow. Attack only undefended positions; concentration wins reliably there. Defend only positions the opponent cannot attack; commitment is not required there. Both rules restrict engagement to favorable terms before the commitment is made, which is how reliable outcomes in both directions become achievable rather than hoped for.

Water has no constant shape but always flows to the low ground. A method that produced a victory in the first engagement revealed an approach to every opponent who observed the result. Repeating it converts surprise into signal. The heaven-born captain modifies methods in relation to the specific opponent and moment, with no fixed form and no repeatable recipe. That is the discipline governing everything else in the chapter.
