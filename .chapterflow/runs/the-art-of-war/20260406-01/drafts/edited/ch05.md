# Energy

A soldier holds a crossbow with the bolt locked and the string drawn back to its limit. Nobody watching from outside can see the tension in the bow. When the bolt finally releases, the strike appears effortless, almost spontaneous. Sun Tzu uses this image to introduce shi, energy or momentum: potential stored before it is needed and released at the moment of maximum effect. The image matters because it separates energy from mere force. Force applied continuously at full effort is expenditure. Energy accumulated and then released at the right moment converts the same effort into a multiplied outcome. The strategic discipline lives in the gap between the two.

Two methods govern how a skilled force operates. The direct method, zheng, holds the engagement: it joins battle, keeps the opponent's attention fixed, maintains contact, and prevents retreat. The indirect method, qi, decides the outcome, working from an angle the opponent did not model at a moment when his attention is already occupied. Both methods attack. The distinction is between the function of holding the engagement and the function of deciding it. A force that uses only direct methods fights on terrain the opponent prepared for. Adding the indirect layer means the decisive pressure arrives from outside the opponent's prepared response.

From these two elements, Sun Tzu argues, infinite variations arise, grounded in the mathematics of combination: five notes, five colors, five flavors each produce more possibilities than a lifetime can exhaust. The practical claim is that no tactical response can exhaust the indirect options available to a skilled commander, because every defensive response the opponent makes to the direct engagement creates new angles for the indirect.

Simulated disorder demonstrates the principle at the level of the whole force. A skilled commander can make a disciplined force appear chaotic, fragmented, overwhelmed. The organizational implication is specific: only a force with real internal structure can manufacture convincing disorder. A genuinely disorganized force cannot simulate chaos on purpose, because simulation requires control over the gap between what is projected and what is real. Visible confusion is available only to forces whose invisible coordination is intact.

Combined energy applies the same logic to the system as a whole. Seek the energy of the combined force rather than requiring exceptional individual output. A round stone placed on a slope generates momentum from its position, not from any internal quality of the stone. The commander's skill is in constructing the slope, placing the force in conditions where position generates energy, so that the release is the product of accumulated systemic potential rather than exceptional individual effort under pressure.
