# Maneuvering

A commander starts after his opponent. He takes the road the opponent is not watching. He arrives first. The opponent, who took the obvious route, spends the whole journey wondering how that happened.

Sun Tzu calls this the artifice of deviation: the circuitous route becomes the direct one when the opponent's attention is committed to the short path. Two conditions must hold simultaneously. The lure must keep the opponent watching the wrong road. The deviation route must actually be clear. If either fails, the deviation adds distance without gaining position.

Speed does not dissolve this cost. It imposes its own. Forcing the march one hundred li strips out all leaders. Fifty li leaves half the force behind. Thirty li costs a third. The schedule is not a warning about excessive effort. It is a specific diminishing-returns function: force integrity declines as march speed increases, precisely and predictably. A perfect positional advantage reached by a force too small to hold it is not a victory.

An army without baggage is lost. Without provisions, lost. Without supply bases, lost. Each dependency is a distinct constraint and each is a lever the opponent can cut without engaging in direct combat. Movement is not displacement. It is sustained displacement, and the chain that sustains it must hold at every link. Alliance knowledge, terrain knowledge, and local guides are the intelligence prerequisites that make movement reliable rather than a gamble on unverified conditions.

Morale runs on a daily clock. Morning spirit is keenest; noon it flags; evening it is spent. Attack the flagging opponent. Wait out the keen one. The timing principle extends to every other situational variable: wait at ease while they toil, be fed while they are hungry, be near the goal while they are still far from it. The commander who stacks all these advantages before the engagement begins has made the fight structurally unequal in their favor.

Gongs and drums carry unity across noise. Banners and flags extend coordination across distance. A dispersed force becomes one organism when these signals function. Sight and attention are unified at scale by the tools that carry beyond the reach of any single voice.

Seven prohibitions close the chapter. Simulated flight, a keen opponent's temper, bait troops, homeward-bound soldiers, a surrounded army, a desperate foe: each names a situation whose surface appearance is an opportunity and whose underlying structure is a trap. The prohibitions name what not to do. The judgment required to recognize when a specific situation is an instance of the trap is the skill they identify but cannot teach.
