# Chapter 7 Brief: Maneuvering

**Source:** VII. MANŒUVERING (Lionel Giles)
**Core intent:** "Movement succeeds only when logistics, timing, and morale all hold."

---

## Core Concepts (priority order)

1. **The artifice of deviation:** Turn the long circuitous route into the direct one. Entice the opponent away, start after him, arrive before him. §3-4.

2. **Cost of forced marches:** A strict diminishing-returns schedule. Force-march 100 li: leaders captured, force scattered. 50 li: half arrives. 30 li: two-thirds arrive. Speed trades capacity for time. §6-10.

3. **Logistics prerequisites:** An army without baggage is lost; without provisions, lost; without supply bases, lost. Alliance knowledge, terrain knowledge, local guides. §11-14.

4. **Morale as time-sensitive:** Morning spirit keenest; noon it flags; evening, bent on returning. Attack when they are sluggish; wait when they are keen. §27-32.

5. **Seven "do not" prohibitions:** Do not pursue simulated flight; do not attack keen temper; do not swallow bait; do not intercept homeward-bound forces; surround with an outlet; do not press desperate foe. §33-37.

## Threshold Question (hard depth)

The seven "do not" prohibitions are exceptions to the chapter's general encouragement of initiative and movement. Hard depth: these exceptions require exactly the situational judgment that the rules themselves cannot specify. When does a pursuing force know it is pursuing simulated flight vs. real retreat? When does it know the foe's temper is "keen" vs. merely defensive? The prohibitions name the traps but cannot tell a commander when they are active.
