# Chapter 6 Brief: Weak Points and Strong

**Source:** VI. WEAK POINTS AND STRONG (Lionel Giles)
**Core intent:** "Impose your will by forcing the opponent to divide."

---

## Core Concepts (priority order)

1. **Initiative / first-in-field:** Whoever arrives first is fresh; the latecomer exhausted before the fight begins. Impose your will — do not let the opponent impose theirs.

2. **Control the agenda:** Two mechanisms — positive pressure (lure with advantage) and negative pressure (threaten what they must defend). Both move the opponent; both keep the agenda yours.

3. **Concentration vs. division:** Discover opponent's dispositions while revealing nothing of yours. The knowledge asymmetry lets you concentrate a whole against their fractions.

4. **Attack undefended / defend inattackable:** The maxim has a selection problem — undefended positions may be undefended because they are not valuable. Requires knowing which gaps matter.

5. **Formlessness / water analogy:** Water has no constant shape; it finds the low ground. Do not repeat tactics; let methods be governed by infinite variety of circumstances. §29-34.

## Threshold Question (hard depth)

If formlessness is the ideal adaptive state, it requires the most accurate situational awareness of any principle in the chapter. A commander who does not know the terrain and the opponent's actual position cannot be formless — they will simply be inconsistent. Does Sun Tzu resolve this dependency, or does formlessness implicitly require the intelligence infrastructure the chapter does not describe?

---

## Moral Complexity

Watch for paranoid-workplace import. The chapter's concentration/division dynamic can be misread as manipulative agenda-setting in all relationships — flag where it applies (adversarial, agenda-driven) and where it does not (cooperative, trust-building).
