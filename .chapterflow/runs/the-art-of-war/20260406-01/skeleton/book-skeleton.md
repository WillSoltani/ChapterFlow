## Governing voice guardrails for the whole book
- Sun Tzu is a strategic classic, not a universal win-at-all-costs manual.
- Preserve anti-waste, anti-vanity, anti-rage cautions.
- Frame deception, espionage, coercion as strategic awareness, not endorsement.
- Do not flatten into "hustle culture" or paranoid workplace slogans.
- Gentle tone in modern scenarios must reach outside martial imagery into classroom, family, coaching, health settings.
- Cooperative domains (trust-building, co-creation, long-run institutions) are where the book fails; flag this in chapters where the temptation is highest (3, 6, 13).
- Do not import battlefield paranoia into domains where transparency outperforms secrecy.

## Vocabulary watchlist (build across chapters)
- "strategy" — limit to essential uses; do not open a chapter with the word.
- "win/winning" — rotate with "prevail", "hold position", "secure an outcome", "come out ahead" (cautiously, per gentle tone).
- "battle" — prefer "contest", "confrontation", "decision", "engagement" depending on context to avoid martial drone.
- "enemy" — prefer "opponent", "rival", "adversary", "counterpart" in modern scenarios.
- "leverage" — capped at 1 per chapter per the repo validator's vocabulary check.
- "ask yourself" — limited per the repo validator.
- "The ______ is not ______. It is ______." — banned sentence skeleton, audit every chapter.

## Thin-chapter risk notes
- **Chapter 12 (The Attack by Fire)**: source richness = limited, concept density = medium. Use the restraint ethic (anger, vengeance, irrecoverable destruction) as the load-bearing mechanism. Do NOT pad with abstract metaphors about fire. Consider solo wave.
- **Chapter 8 (Variation in Tactics)**: concept density = medium. Load-bearing content is the five besetting sins. Lean on character-failure analysis to keep hard depth real.

## Premium-routing candidates
- **Chapter 11 (The Nine Situations)**: longest and psychologically densest. Strong candidate for solo wave. Needs extra editor pass on "desperate ground" framing to avoid glamorizing coercion.
- **Chapter 13 (The Use of Spies)**: morally uncomfortable (bribery, double agents, lethal secrecy). Needs extra critic pass for strategic-awareness framing.
- **Chapter 3 (Attack by Stratagem)**: "break resistance without fighting" tempts euphemizing intimidation. Needs distance language.

## Cross-chapter tensions to preserve (load-bearing in later hard depth)
- Ch1 favors deception; Ch12 and Ch13 favor restraint and honest intelligence. The contradiction is productive, not a flaw — use it as the book's internal ethic.
- Ch3 says winning whole and intact; Ch11 says throw soldiers into positions of no escape. The psychology of cohesion pulls against the aesthetic of preservation.
- Ch4 says "wins by making no mistakes"; Ch11 says "rapidity is the essence of war". Structural patience vs speed — both true, depending on terrain.
