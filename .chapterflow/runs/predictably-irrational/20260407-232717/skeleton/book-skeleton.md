# Book Skeleton

## Metadata
- Book: Predictably Irrational
- Author: Dan Ariely
- Locked edition: Revised and expanded English Harper trade family
- Numbered chapter range: 1-13
- Wave size: 2 by default under `serial_safe`
- Premium / solo routing candidates: ch05, ch11

## Rotation Plan For Examples
- Maintain the canonical six-format rotation once per chapter: `decision_point`, `postmortem`, `dialogue`, `predict_reveal`, `dilemma`, `before_after`.
- Maintain `2 work / 2 school / 2 personal` examples in every chapter package.
- Rotate endings across `broader_principle`, `self_directed_question`, `surprising_implication`, `cross_domain`, `common_trap`, and `perspective_reframe`.

## School-Setting Plan
- Reuse school contexts that can carry real tradeoffs: exam prep, project teams, student clubs, part-time work, course shopping, dorm life, and campus buying decisions.
- Avoid flattening every school example into generic motivation advice.

## Vocabulary Watchlist
- Keep relativity, anchor, arbitrary coherence, zero price, social norms, market norms, arousal, procrastination, endowment effect, expectations, placebo, honesty, cash, and behavioral economics precise and chapter-bound.
- Avoid generic nudging language, moral grandstanding, and filler psychology claims not tied to Ariely's experiments.

## Motif / Callback Opportunities
- ch01-ch03 build the baseline claim that preferences feel stable but are highly context-sensitive.
- ch04-ch06 shift from prices and norms into state changes and self-control failures.
- ch07-ch10 intensify how ownership, open options, expectations, and price labels distort experience.
- ch11-ch13 turn from consumer irrationality toward honesty, public trust, and actionable "free lunches."

## Chapter Plan
### ch01 The Truth about Relativity
- Intent: Show how easy comparisons, decoys, and relative judgments push people away from absolute value.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: low
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep the decoy logic concrete and avoid turning relativity into a vague complaint about envy.
- Wave routing: calibration 1

### ch02 The Fallacy of Supply and Demand
- Intent: Show how arbitrary anchors and coherent arbitrariness shape willingness to pay more than stable demand curves do.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: low
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep pearls, arbitrary anchors, and first-price effects visible.
- Wave routing: calibration 2

### ch03 The Cost of Zero Cost
- Intent: Explain why "free" creates an emotional distortion that makes people ignore downside and net value.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: low
- Thin-chapter risk notes: do not treat zero price as merely a bigger discount.
- Wave routing: wave 3

### ch04 The Cost of Social Norms
- Intent: Show why adding money can collapse social cooperation and trigger colder, more selfish market behavior.
- Source richness: medium_high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep the distinction between social and market norms alive instead of turning the chapter into "money is bad."
- Wave routing: wave 3

### ch05 The Influence of Arousal
- Intent: Show how hot states change preferences, risk tolerance, and moral judgment more than people predict in cold states.
- Source richness: medium_high
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: high
- Thin-chapter risk notes: do not flatten arousal into a narrow sex-ed lesson; keep the general hot-state logic visible.
- Wave routing: solo/premium

### ch06 The Problem of Procrastination and Self-Control
- Intent: Explain why present bias beats long-term plans and why external structure can outperform self-promise.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: low
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep the MIT deadline experiment central and avoid generic productivity advice.
- Wave routing: wave 4

### ch07 The High Price of Ownership
- Intent: Show how ownership, effort, and imagined possession inflate value and distort exchange.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep the endowment effect and seller-buyer gap concrete.
- Wave routing: wave 4

### ch08 Keeping Doors Open
- Intent: Show how preserving options can drain attention and make people sacrifice the path they actually value.
- Source richness: medium
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: low
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep the chapter on option-hoarding and switching costs, not generic commitment advice.
- Wave routing: wave 5

### ch09 The Effect of Expectations
- Intent: Show how expectations reshape actual experience, not just later interpretation.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep the beer experiment and stereotype logic distinct.
- Wave routing: wave 5

### ch10 The Power of Price
- Intent: Show how price can work as a placebo signal and change perceived effectiveness.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep the medical and consumer ethics tension visible.
- Wave routing: wave 6

### ch11 The Context of Our Character, Part 1
- Intent: Show how ordinary people cheat a little when temptation is close and moral reminders are weak.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: high
- Thin-chapter risk notes: do not collapse the chapter into "people are bad"; keep the honesty-monitor claim precise.
- Wave routing: solo/premium

### ch12 The Context of Our Character, Part 2
- Intent: Show why distance from cash lowers moral friction and makes cheating easier.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: high
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep tokens, nonmonetary currency, and real-world analogues concrete.
- Wave routing: wave 7

### ch13 Beer and Free Lunches
- Intent: Close by showing how image management distorts choice and how behavioral economics offers practical "free lunches."
- Source richness: medium_high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep the final chapter from dissolving into generic optimism.
- Wave routing: wave 7
