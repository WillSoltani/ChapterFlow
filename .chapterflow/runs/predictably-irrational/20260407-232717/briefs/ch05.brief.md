- Paper Hints: Sexual Arousal and Decision making
- Objective: Ariely uses the sexual-arousal experiments to show that people in calm states drastically underestimate how much a hot state can change desire, risk tolerance, and moral judgment. The same students answer questions one way while calm and another way while aroused, revealing a cold-hot empathy gap: the calm self cannot imagine the hot self accurately, and the hot self does not honor calm promises reliably. The chapter is not only a lesson about sex. It is a lesson about state-dependent preferences and about why willpower plans built in cold states often fail in hot ones. Keep the experiment trail central, keep the moral complexity visible, and avoid turning the chapter into shallow shock value or generic self-help. The practical discipline is precommitment: decide earlier, add friction before the hot state arrives, remove avoidable temptation, and design safeguards that do not depend on improvising well while judgment is narrowed.

## Keep Visible
- Calm-state predictions often fail because hot states feel psychologically distant until they arrive.
- Sexual arousal is the chapter's core experiment path, but the deeper lesson is about state-dependent judgment.
- Hot states can change willingness to take risks and cross lines the calm self rejects.
- The failure is not only weak character; it is misprediction about what kind of person the hot state will produce.
- The practical answer is precommitment, boundaries, and environment design rather than confidence alone.
