- Objective: Ariely uses placebo-style evidence to show that price can act as a signal of efficacy. A higher-priced treatment or product can feel more effective even when the underlying substance is the same. Keep the chapter on price as an expectation carrier with real felt consequences, not just on willingness to pay. Preserve the ethical tension: this mechanism sits at the border between medicine, marketing, and manipulation. The practical lesson is to separate true effectiveness from the power of the price cue when possible. The limit is that price is not always meaningless; the distortion appears when the tag is doing more of the healing or persuading than the thing itself.
