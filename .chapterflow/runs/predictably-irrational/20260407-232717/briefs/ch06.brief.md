- Paper Hints: Procrastination Deadlines and Performance
- Objective: Ariely uses the MIT deadline experiment to show that self-control problems are predictable, not accidental. Students with evenly spaced external deadlines perform best, students allowed to set their own deadlines do somewhat better than total freedom, and students with only one final deadline perform worst because present bias keeps stealing time until the cost becomes urgent. The chapter is not generic productivity advice. It is a design argument: if people know they procrastinate, then freedom without structure can be a trap, and good constraints can be a form of protection rather than oppression. Keep the experiment trail visible, keep the practical lesson on binding intermediate commitments, and avoid drifting into empty slogans about discipline.

## Keep Visible
- The MIT paper-deadline experiment is the chapter's core evidence path.
- Present bias makes immediate comfort win against distant cost.
- Self-imposed deadlines can help, but they are weaker than externally enforced ones.
- Total flexibility often feels attractive up front and harmful later.
- The chapter's practical answer is earlier structure, staged commitments, and real consequences.
