# Chapter Brief

- Chapter: 7 The High Price of Ownership
- Chapter ID: ch07-the-high-price-of-ownership
- Source IDs: unt_catalog_toc, wikipedia_summary, supersummary_guides, duke_papers_index
- Paper Hints: Value Buyer and Seller, Auction Fever, Tom Sawyer Value
- Objective: Ariely uses the endowment effect to show that ownership changes valuation. Once people possess something, imagine possessing it, or work to obtain it, they often value it more than a neutral outsider would. The seller-buyer gap is the cleanest version: sellers demand far more to give up an item than buyers are willing to pay to acquire it. The chapter keeps this concrete through ticket cases, ownership experiments, and the way effort or imagined possession can inflate value. The practical lesson is to separate 'mine' from actual worth by asking what you would pay today if you did not already own it. Keep the chapter grounded in ownership distortion rather than broad anti-attachment philosophy.

## Keep Visible
- The endowment effect is the chapter's core mechanism.
- Ownership inflates valuation even when the object itself has not changed.
- Seller-buyer gaps reveal that giving something up feels worse than gaining it feels good.
- Effort and imagined possession can trigger similar inflation.
- The practical answer is to reprice the item from a non-owner's perspective before acting.
