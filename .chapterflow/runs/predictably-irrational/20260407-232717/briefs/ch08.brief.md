- Chapter: 8 Keeping Doors Open
- Chapter ID: ch08-keeping-doors-open
- Source IDs: unt_catalog_toc, supersummary_guides, duke_papers_index
- Paper Hints: Keeping Doors Open, Sequential Choice Group Settings
- Objective: Ariely uses the disappearing-door experiment to show that people can become so anxious about closing options that they waste time and value protecting doors they are not even using. The distortion is not commitment itself but the fear of irreversible loss. People keep bouncing back to preserve options, even when that behavior hurts performance on the path they actually care about. Keep the chapter on option-hoarding, switching cost, and the mispricing of unused possibilities. The practical lesson is to close low-value doors on purpose so attention can go to the path with real value.

## Keep Visible
- The disappearing-door experiment is the chapter's central evidence path.
- People overvalue options that are about to disappear.
- Preserving options can drain attention and reduce performance on the chosen path.
- The loss of possibility can feel more painful than the actual value of the lost option.
- The practical answer is strategic closure rather than endless maintenance.
