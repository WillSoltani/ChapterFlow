# Chapter Outline

## Opening
- Ariely asks readers to notice how differently a calm person predicts a hot moment than a person inside that hot moment actually chooses. The chapter uses sexual arousal to make that gap impossible to ignore.

## Core Moves
- The experiment compares calm-state answers with aroused-state answers from the same people.
- Hot states change desire, risk tolerance, and moral judgment more than calm selves predict.
- The cold-hot empathy gap makes people overtrust willpower and underbuild safeguards.
- The lesson generalizes beyond sex to other hot states without losing the sexual-arousal experiment as the core evidence.
- The practical defense is precommitment: decide early, add friction, remove temptation, and respect state change.

## Reinforcement

## Exit
- If a hot state can hijack a single moment so thoroughly, what happens when the problem is not one hot episode but a long series of ordinary failures to do what we already planned?
