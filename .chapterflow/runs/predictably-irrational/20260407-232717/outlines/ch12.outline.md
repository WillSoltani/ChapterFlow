# Chapter Outline

## Opening
- Ariely extends the dishonesty argument by showing that equivalent value feels easier to misuse once it stops looking like literal cash.

## Core Moves
- People feel stronger moral resistance to taking cash than to taking tokens, credits, goods, or redeemable stand-ins.
- Distance from money lowers moral friction and makes rationalization easier.
- The same value can trigger more cheating once it becomes abstract enough to stop feeling like theft.
- Real systems repeat this pattern through points, reimbursements, supplies, gift cards, and other indirect currencies.
- The practical lesson is to make value concrete before the decision rather than trusting abstract balances to police themselves.
