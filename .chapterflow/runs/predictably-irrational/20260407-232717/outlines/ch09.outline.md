# Chapter Outline

## Opening
- Ariely uses the beer experiment because it shows that expectation can change the tasted experience itself.

## Core Moves
- Preloaded expectations can shape perception before judgment is reported.
- Knowing about the balsamic ingredient before tasting changes the beer experience differently than learning afterward.
- Labels, stories, and stereotypes can bend experience, not just later explanation.
- The chapter is about expectation entering the experience, not about people lying after the fact.
- The practical lesson is to protect clean signal when possible and to notice when the setup is already doing part of the tasting.

## Reinforcement
- The User Test That Changed After the Premium Label Went On (decision_point, work)
- The Study Group Taste Test That Shifted Once the Ingredient Was Named (postmortem, school)
- The Conversation About Why the Fancy Coffee Tasted Better Before the Blind Pour (dialogue, personal)
- The Wellness App Trial That Felt Stronger After the Team Called It Elite (predict_reveal, work)
- The Course Rating Dilemma After Everyone Heard the Professor Was Supposed to Be Brilliant (dilemma, school)
- Before and After the Family Started Hiding Brand Names at Dinner (before_after, personal)

## Exit
- If expectation can change experience before reflection begins, what happens when price itself starts acting like a signal of effectiveness?
