# Chapter Outline

## Opening
- Ariely opens Chapter 2 with Tahitian black pearls because they reveal how demand can be engineered. The pearls were not naturally greeted as obvious luxury goods. They became luxurious after marketers displayed them beside rare gems, wrapped them in elite symbolism, and assigned them strikingly high prices. That initial positioning mattered because it created the first anchor. Ariely uses the story to introduce arbitrary coherence: the opening value can be arbitrary, but later values become coherent with it.

## Core Moves
- The black-pearl story shows how a first luxury price can manufacture later willingness to pay.
- The anchor can be arbitrary even when the later reasoning around it is coherent.
- Unfamiliar goods invite anchoring because there is no strong internal value meter to resist it.
- Demand is partly shaped by prior prices and frames, not only by internal desire.
- Practical judgment starts by isolating and testing the anchor.
