# Chapter Outline

## Opening
- Ariely closes by showing that public choice often mixes real preference with image management, especially when people order or choose in front of others.

## Core Moves
- Public ordering can distort what people say they want because social image enters the decision.
- Sequence and visibility matter because people react to what others have already chosen.
- The result is that people can leave with choices that look better socially than they feel personally.
- Behavioral economics becomes practical here: simple design changes can reduce these distortions.
- The chapter's "free lunches" are low-cost, humane fixes that make it easier for people to choose what they actually value.

## Exit
- Behavioral economics matters because once a distortion is predictable, people can often redesign the environment and recover a real preference at surprisingly low cost.
