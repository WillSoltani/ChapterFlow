# Chapter Outline

## Opening
- Ariely frames the chapter with a simple problem: ask someone for a favor and one set of norms appears, but add a small payment and the relationship starts feeling like a job. That shift matters because the price tag does not just add compensation. It changes the rules of the exchange.

## Core Moves
- Social norms support help, reciprocity, and identity-based effort.
- Market norms support explicit trade, wages, and contractual clarity.
- Small payments can perform worse than no payment because they crowd out social motivation.
- The daycare fine and low-paid-help cases show that pricing a social obligation can weaken behavior and make reversal difficult.
- The practical defense is to choose the norm system deliberately rather than mixing them carelessly.
