# Chapter Outline

## Opening
- The Effect of Expectations says that expectations can change what people actually experience, not just how they later describe it. Ariely uses the beer experiment in which knowing about balsamic vinegar changed perceived taste to prove that the pattern is not just intuition.

## Core Moves
- Ariely's main mechanism is that expectations can change what people actually experience, not just how they later describe it.
- Ariely makes the claim visible through the beer experiment in which knowing about balsamic vinegar changed perceived taste.
- The chapter's warning is that labels, stories, and stereotypes can rewrite experience before evidence arrives.
- Ariely's practical response is to separate signal from sensation and notice when expectation is doing the tasting for you.
- Its important limit is that the chapter is not saying expectation is fake; it says expectation becomes part of the experience.
