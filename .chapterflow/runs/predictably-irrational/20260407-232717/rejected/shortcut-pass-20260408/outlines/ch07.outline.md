# Chapter Outline

## Opening
- The High Price of Ownership says that once people own or imagine owning something, they value it more than nonowners do. Ariely uses the endowment effect, the seller-buyer gap, and Duke-ticket style examples of inflated asking prices to prove that the pattern is not just intuition.

## Core Moves
- Ariely's main mechanism is that once people own or imagine owning something, they value it more than nonowners do.
- Ariely makes the claim visible through the endowment effect, the seller-buyer gap, and Duke-ticket style examples of inflated asking prices.
- The chapter's warning is that ownership, effort, and imagination make exchange harder and distort fair value.
- Ariely's practical response is to compare from the outside and ask what you would pay if you did not already own it.
- Its important limit is that the chapter does not deny attachment; it warns against mistaking attachment for market value.
