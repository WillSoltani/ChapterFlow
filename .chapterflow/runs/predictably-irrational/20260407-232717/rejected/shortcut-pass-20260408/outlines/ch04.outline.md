# Chapter Outline

## Opening
- The Cost of Social Norms says that social norms and market norms motivate effort through different meanings. Ariely uses the moving-help example, token-pay experiments, and the daycare fine that increased lateness to prove that the pattern is not just intuition.

## Core Moves
- Ariely's main mechanism is that social norms and market norms motivate effort through different meanings.
- Ariely makes the claim visible through the moving-help example, token-pay experiments, and the daycare fine that increased lateness.
- The chapter's warning is that small payments can crowd out goodwill and turn moral pressure into purchasable permission.
- Ariely's practical response is to decide whether an interaction should stay relational or become clearly transactional before adding money.
- Its important limit is that the chapter is not anti-money; it warns that money changes the category of the relationship.
