# Keeping Doors Open

Keeping Doors Open argues that people waste effort preserving options long after those options stop serving their real goals. Ariely does not leave that claim at the level of slogan. He builds it through the shrinking-door game and examples of option hoarding that sacrifice progress for imagined flexibility, using the example as a clean demonstration of what the mind does under the relevant pressure.

The chapter matters because it explains why the distortion survives self-confidence. fear of closing doors creates distraction, switching, and abandonment of the path that matters most. Once that happens, people interpret the resulting choice as if it emerged from stable judgment rather than from a bent frame or state.

Ariely then shows that the mechanism is not trapped inside one experiment. It appears across commitment, switching, focus, which is why the chapter functions as a field guide rather than a trivia lesson. The broader pattern makes the warning harder to dismiss.

The practical move is disciplined redesign: identify the option that truly deserves commitment and stop paying to preserve every possibility. Ariely repeatedly shows that good intentions alone are weak once the critical pressure is active. The stronger answer is to change the setup before the moment of distortion arrives.

The limit keeps the chapter intellectually honest. the chapter is not against flexibility itself; it attacks compulsive option preservation. Ariely is identifying a dependable bend in judgment, not claiming that every instance of behavior under this heading is fake or corrupted.

In that sense the chapter does two jobs at once. It explains a mechanism and forces a defensive posture toward it.
