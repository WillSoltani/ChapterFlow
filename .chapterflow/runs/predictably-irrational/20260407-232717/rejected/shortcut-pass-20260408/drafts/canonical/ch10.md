# The Power of Price

The Power of Price says that price can signal effectiveness so strongly that it changes felt results. Ariely uses the placebo study where a more expensive painkiller worked better than a discounted version to prove that the pattern is not just intuition.

What makes the chapter useful is the risk it isolates: people mistake price for proof and then feel the product through the signal. Once that pressure appears, people stop noticing the real tradeoff and start obeying the frame or state that got there first.

The chapter then extends the mechanism into ordinary decisions across placebo, signals, pricing. That reach matters because the distortion is not confined to the original experiment.

Ariely's practical answer is to treat price as information to inspect, not as direct evidence of underlying power. The chapter's limit is equally important: the chapter is not only about medicine; it is about price labels changing experience.
