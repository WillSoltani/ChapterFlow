# Keeping Doors Open

Keeping Doors Open says that people waste effort preserving options long after those options stop serving their real goals. Ariely uses the shrinking-door game and examples of option hoarding that sacrifice progress for imagined flexibility to prove that the pattern is not just intuition.

What makes the chapter useful is the risk it isolates: fear of closing doors creates distraction, switching, and abandonment of the path that matters most. Once that pressure appears, people stop noticing the real tradeoff and start obeying the frame or state that got there first.

The chapter then extends the mechanism into ordinary decisions across commitment, switching, focus. That reach matters because the distortion is not confined to the original experiment.

Ariely's practical answer is to identify the option that truly deserves commitment and stop paying to preserve every possibility. The chapter's limit is equally important: the chapter is not against flexibility itself; it attacks compulsive option preservation.
