# The Cost of Zero Cost

The Cost of Zero Cost says that free is psychologically different from merely cheap. Ariely uses the truffle-versus-Kiss candy experiment and the pull of free shipping, trials, and bonus offers to prove that the pattern is not just intuition.

What makes the chapter useful is the risk it isolates: zero removes the felt downside and makes people ignore total value, future cost, and clutter. Once that pressure appears, people stop noticing the real tradeoff and start obeying the frame or state that got there first.

The chapter then extends the mechanism into ordinary decisions across promotions, shipping, subscriptions. That reach matters because the distortion is not confined to the original experiment.

Ariely's practical answer is to run the one-cent test and recalculate the full outcome before accepting the free option. The chapter's limit is equally important: the chapter does not say every free offer is bad, only that free deserves more comparison, not less.
