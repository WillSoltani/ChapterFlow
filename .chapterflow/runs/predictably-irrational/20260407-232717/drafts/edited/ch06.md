# The Problem of Procrastination and Self-Control

Chapter 6 matters because it turns procrastination from a character insult into a design problem. Ariely's MIT deadline experiment shows that people often understand their own weakness just well enough to admit it, but not well enough to build the structure that would really protect them. External deadlines spread across a semester work best because they keep the future from staying abstract too long. Self-imposed deadlines help somewhat because people know total freedom is risky, but the self usually leaves itself too much room to bargain later. One final deadline feels flexible and mature until the work piles up and the cost becomes urgent. The practical lesson is not to worship structure or to treat discipline as shame. It is to respect present bias enough to create real milestones, visible commitments, and consequences before delay starts writing the schedule for you.

## Key Takeaway Card
- The chapter's key move is to treat deadlines and structure as tools for defending long-term goals against a brain that keeps preferring today.
