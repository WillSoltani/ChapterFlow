# Beer and Free Lunches

Chapter 13 ends the book by showing that people do not always choose in public the way they would choose in private. Social image, sequencing, and visibility can make a preference look like a statement about taste, status, or originality. That is why a public order can drift away from real enjoyment. Ariely then gives the chapter its closing value: if these distortions are predictable, then small design changes can reduce them. Private ordering, quieter comparison, and better choice framing can produce behavioral-economics "free lunches," meaning practical gains that cost little and help people get closer to what they actually want.

## Key Takeaway Card
- The chapter's key move is to turn social-image distortion into a design problem that can often be improved cheaply and humanely.
