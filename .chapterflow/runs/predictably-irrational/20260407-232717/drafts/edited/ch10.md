# The Power of Price

Chapter 10 sharpens the expectation story by showing that price itself can act like a placebo cue. A higher-priced treatment, supplement, or product can feel more effective even when the underlying substance or function is unchanged. That is why the chapter is not merely about cost or willingness to pay. Price becomes part of the experienced result. Ariely keeps the chapter morally tense because the same effect can appear in medical settings and ordinary consumer settings, where the line between reassurance and manipulation matters. The practical lesson is to separate what the item actually does from what the price tag instructed you to feel about it, while preserving the chapter's limit: price is not meaningless, but it becomes dangerous when the cue does more work than the thing itself.

## Key Takeaway Card
- The chapter's key move is to notice when the premium price is acting like an ingredient.
