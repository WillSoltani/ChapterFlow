# The High Price of Ownership

Chapter 7 works because Ariely does not argue that people become irrational only when they buy. He argues that they become differently irrational once they already have something. Ownership changes the reference point. What was once an optional acquisition becomes something that feels like a loss if given up, and that loss feels larger than the matching gain would have felt from the outside. That is why seller prices and buyer prices diverge so sharply. The same object looks different once it is wrapped in 'mine.' Ariely keeps the chapter practical by widening ownership beyond physical objects. Effort, customization, and imagined possession can all create the same inflation. The practical move is to step outside the owner's story and ask the harder question: if this were not already mine, what would I actually pay for it today?

## Key Takeaway Card
- The chapter's key move is to strip the word 'mine' out of the valuation long enough to see what the item or project is really worth now.
