# The Context of Our Character, Part 2

Chapter 12 sharpens Ariely's honesty argument by isolating one especially useful mechanism: people resist stealing cash more than they resist taking something that can later become cash. Tokens, points, credits, office supplies, reimbursable perks, and similar stand-ins lower the emotional force of the act even when the value is the same. That is why the chapter matters. It shows that dishonesty is not only about desire or character. It is also about how directly the loss is felt. The practical lesson is to make value concrete earlier, keep abstract currencies visible, and stop assuming that a redeemable token will trigger the same moral friction as money in the open.

## Key Takeaway Card
- The chapter's key move is to show that distance from cash lowers moral friction and quietly expands the room for rationalized cheating.
