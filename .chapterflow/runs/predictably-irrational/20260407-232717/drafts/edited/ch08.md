# Keeping Doors Open

Chapter 8 works because Ariely turns a common fantasy into a measurable cost. People like to imagine that preserving more options is always safer and wiser. The disappearing-door experiment shows the opposite can happen. Once an option seems close to vanishing, people start spending effort to keep it alive even when it is not the best path and even when protecting it harms performance elsewhere. The loss of possibility starts feeling bigger than the value the option was delivering. That is why the chapter is not anti-choice. It is anti-unexamined option-hoarding. The practical lesson is to stop paying attention taxes on weak alternatives just because closure feels scary. Shut low-value doors with intention so the path you actually want can receive real time and depth.

## Key Takeaway Card
- The chapter's key move is to price the cost of maintaining weak options before you mistake open doors for real freedom.
