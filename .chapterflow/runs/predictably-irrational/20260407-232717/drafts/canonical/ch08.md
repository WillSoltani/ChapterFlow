# Keeping Doors Open

Ariely uses disappearing doors to show that people often overvalue options that are about to close. Instead of focusing on the path they are actually using, they spend time and energy keeping weak alternatives alive. The problem is not that options are always bad. It is that the fear of losing a possibility can outweigh the actual value the possibility was delivering. That makes performance worse on the choice that matters. The practical lesson is to close low-value doors deliberately so attention can stop paying a maintenance tax on possibilities you are not truly pursuing.

## Key Takeaways
- The chapter's core evidence is the disappearing-door experiment.
- Imminent loss can make unused options feel more valuable than they are.
- Maintaining too many doors can reduce depth and performance.
- The pain of closure can be stronger than the value of the option.
- The practical move is strategic closure, not endless preservation.
