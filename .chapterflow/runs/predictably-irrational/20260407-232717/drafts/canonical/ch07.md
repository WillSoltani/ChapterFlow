# The High Price of Ownership

Ariely uses Chapter 7 to show that ownership changes value. Once something is mine, giving it up feels more painful than getting it would have felt rewarding. That is why sellers often demand far more than buyers want to pay for the same item. The object did not change. The point of view changed. Ariely widens the lesson beyond literal possession by showing that effort and imagined possession can inflate value too. The practical defense is to step outside the owner's frame and ask what you would actually pay today if you did not already have the item, project, or process in your hands.

## Key Takeaways
- The endowment effect is the chapter's core distortion.
- Sellers and buyers start from different emotional positions.
- Loss aversion helps explain why giving something up feels so expensive.
- Effort and imagined possession can create similar inflation.
- The best correction is to price from the outside instead of from 'mine.'
