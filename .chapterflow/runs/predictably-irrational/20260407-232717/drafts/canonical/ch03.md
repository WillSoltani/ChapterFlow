# The Cost of Zero Cost

The candy experiment matters because it shows that zero price changes the decision qualitatively, not just quantitatively. When both the Lindt truffle and the Hershey's Kiss cost something, many people choose the better chocolate. When each price drops by one cent and the Kiss becomes free, the preference flips. Ariely's point is that free removes the felt downside of the choice and creates an emotional bonus that ordinary discounts do not produce. Once the price hits zero, people stop comparing total value with the same discipline. That is why free shipping, trials, samples, and bonus offers can pull attention away from hidden costs, future lock-in, and simple clutter.

## Key Takeaways
- Free is a special psychological category, not just a larger discount.
- Zero removes the sting of loss and weakens net-value comparison.
- A tiny price change can create a large preference reversal when it crosses into free.
- Sellers exploit zero through shipping offers, trials, and add-on logic.
- The one-cent test restores the comparison that zero suppresses.
