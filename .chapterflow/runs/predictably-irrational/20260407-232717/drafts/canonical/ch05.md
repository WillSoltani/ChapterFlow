# The Influence of Arousal

Ariely uses sexual arousal because it makes a larger truth hard to ignore: people in calm states are bad at predicting what they will want, tolerate, or justify in hot states. The same person answers one way when calm and another way when aroused. That gap matters because it reveals more than temptation. It reveals state-dependent judgment. Hot states can narrow foresight, raise risk tolerance, and loosen moral restraints that felt solid a short time earlier. The lesson is not that people lose all agency. It is that they routinely overestimate how much calm-state values will survive without protection when desire spikes.

## Key Takeaways
- The chapter's core evidence is the calm-versus-aroused comparison.
- Sexual arousal reveals the broader cold-hot empathy gap.
- Hot states change what feels acceptable, not just what feels attractive.
- Confidence in future restraint is often misplaced when the future self will be in a different state.
- The practical lesson is to precommit while calm instead of improvising while hot.
