# The Cost of Social Norms

Ariely's central move in Chapter 4 is to show that a price tag can change the meaning of a relationship, not just the payoff. Under social norms, people help because of reciprocity, belonging, care, and shared identity. Under market norms, they help because the compensation makes the exchange worth it. The danger appears when a social situation gets a tiny fee attached to it. Then the stronger social motive can collapse and get replaced by a weak market motive. Ariely uses the small-payment experiments, the AARP lawyer example, and the daycare fines to show that token payments and symbolic prices can reduce effort, increase selfish behavior, and make it difficult to return to the earlier spirit of cooperation.

## Key Takeaways
- Social norms and market norms are different systems, not slightly different versions of the same thing.
- Small payments can reduce effort by crowding out reciprocity.
- Daycare fines can make lateness worse when guilt becomes a fee.
- Once a relationship is priced, the earlier social expectation can be hard to recover.
- The practical lesson is to choose one frame clearly instead of mixing them badly.
