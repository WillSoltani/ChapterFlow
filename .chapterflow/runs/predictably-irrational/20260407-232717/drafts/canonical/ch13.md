# Beer and Free Lunches

Chapter 13 closes the book by showing that public choice often turns into a performance. When people order in front of others, react to a sequence, or try to manage how they look, they can drift away from what they actually want. Ariely's point is not only diagnostic. It is practical. If visibility and social image distort preference, then small design changes such as private ordering, better defaults, and cleaner decision setups can help people get closer to their real interests. That is why the chapter ends with "free lunches": low-cost fixes that improve behavior without demanding heroism or heavy punishment.

## Key Takeaway Card
- The chapter's key move is to connect image-distorted choice with practical, low-cost design fixes that help people choose what they actually want.
