Core Claim:
Hands and fingers often reveal how much control, tension, ease, and self-soothing are active in the moment. The chapter should teach expressive and regulatory hand behavior, not pretend that one hand gesture solves motive.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 6 tracked the arms as barriers and invitations. Chapter 7 moves to the finer-grained signals of the hands and fingers, where confidence, soothing, tension, concealment, and emphasis become easier to see.

Author Logic Chain:
First show a mismatch between calm words and stressed or hidden hands. Then explain why hands matter: they are highly expressive, often regulate stress, and are hard to keep perfectly neutral. Then map visible palms, hidden hands, finger interlacing, rubbing, self-touch, and open or closed emphasis. Finish with contextual limits and a bridge into the face chapter.

Required Anchors:
- A work scene where hand visibility, finger tension, or rubbing reveals comfort or strain before verbal clarity.
- A school scene where hands show confidence, stress, or self-soothing during a presentation or pitch.
- A personal scene where hands reveal welcome, concealment, or regulation before speech does.

Common Misreadings:
- treating open palms as proof of honesty
- treating hidden hands as proof of deceit
- reading one finger or hand cue without timing, transition, and surrounding body support

Counterarguments or Limits:
Hands are expressive but also shaped by habit, culture, props, tables, temperature, and social style. The chapter must keep the gain practical: better reading of confidence, stress, and regulation, not certainty.
