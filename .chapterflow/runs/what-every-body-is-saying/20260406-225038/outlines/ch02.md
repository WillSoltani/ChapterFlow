Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader knows how to observe nonverbal behavior without turning one cue into a fantasy of certainty.

Opening Move:
Open in Maya's performance review. Colin looks normal until one project topic appears, then his whole physical pattern changes.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2, P5
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4, P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live change-over-baseline scene in the review.
- P2: define baseline and why change matters more than static habit.
- P3: classroom context normalizes what would look suspicious in isolation.
- P4: dinner-table incongruence shows why clusters beat single cues.
- P5: method paragraph — context, cluster, congruence, caution.
- P6: limitation paragraph — even good observers can still misread.
- P7: bridge to the limbic-response chapter.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish between a cue dictionary and a disciplined method. The method reads change, context, and clusters. The dictionary pretends gestures have fixed meanings.

Hard-Depth Tension:
What happens when the observer has a good cluster but the wrong context?

Threshold Question:
How much confidence is justified before an observer crosses from careful inference into projection?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 6
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- baseline is situational, not personality destiny
- change matters more than isolated habit
- clusters are stronger than single cues
- congruence and incongruence shape interpretation
- context can rescue or ruin a read
- good method still leaves uncertainty alive
