Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader sees body language as an early-warning layer of information rather than a mystical answer key.

Opening Move:
Open in the interview waiting room. The candidate says she is ready. Her feet keep angling toward the exit.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3, P5
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4, P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live scene in the waiting room; body gets there before speech.
- P2: widen the frame from one cue to whole-body observation.
- P3: school hallway example; polite words vs physical withdrawal.
- P4: seminar example; agreement that does not look settled.
- P5: caution paragraph — signal is useful, certainty is dangerous.
- P6: payoff paragraph — the value is better questions, not psychic confidence.
- P7: bridge to the method chapter.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Signal is not verdict. The chapter's distinction is between noticing a physical shift and claiming you now know the hidden cause.

Hard-Depth Tension:
If the body gives early clues, what stops the observer from turning those clues into projection, accusation, or control fantasy?

Threshold Question:
How do you use early signals without fooling yourself about what they mean?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- whole-body observation beats face-only reading
- bodies show comfort and discomfort before tidy words
- one cue is weak; patterns are stronger
- context changes meaning
- the method gives better questions, not certainty
- misuse begins when observation becomes accusation

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dialogue, work): words and feet disagree
- Scenario 2 (decision_point, work): manager pauses before over-reading tension
- Scenario 3 (before_after, school): hallway conference ends physically before verbally
- Scenario 4 (dilemma, school): student agreement may be social pressure, not true ease
- Scenario 5 (postmortem, personal): missed dinner-party discomfort reconstructed later
- Scenario 6 (predict_reveal, personal): first impression corrected by context

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least 4 scenarios should include physical details such as chair placement, notebook grip, hallway angle, glass in hand, or doorway orientation.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what rules make nonverbal reading responsible instead of flashy.

Genericity Risks:
- vague promises about reading minds
- pop-psych language about "energy"
- face-only advice

Forbidden Drift:
- turning the chapter into a top-ten list
- acting as if one cue proves a motive
- leaking future body-part chapter details too early
