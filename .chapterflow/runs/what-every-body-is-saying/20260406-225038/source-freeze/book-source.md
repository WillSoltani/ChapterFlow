## Authorized preview takeaways
Google Books describes the work as a guide to reading people through nonverbal behavior rather than words alone. The preview-page concept list highlights:
- survival instincts as a driver of body language
- caution about relying on the face alone
- interpretive value in thumbs, feet, and eyelids
- confidence and sentiment displays
- trust and authority cues

## Author-authored secondary takeaways
- Navarro repeatedly argues that nonverbal reading should involve the whole body, not only the face.
- Comfort and discomfort are more supportable categories than confident lie-detection claims.
- Hands, fingers, skin, torso orientation, and feet often reveal tension, confidence, hesitation, withdrawal, or engagement.
- Deception should be treated cautiously. Behavioral shifts can indicate stress, fear, discomfort, or hidden knowledge, but not deception by themselves.

## Run-safe interpretation rules
- Stay closest to what the chapter titles and preview copy support.
- Prefer body-region specificity over abstract psychology.
- Prefer discomfort / confidence / engagement / withdrawal language over certainty claims about motives.
- Keep the conclusion synthetic and ethical; do not add a new framework not visible in the frozen bundle.
