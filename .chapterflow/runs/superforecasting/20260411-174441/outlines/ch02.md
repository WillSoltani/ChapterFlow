Chapter Promise:
The reader will understand why confidence often outruns understanding and why disciplined evidence is the only reliable corrective.

Opening Move:
Start with a scene or statement that feels authoritative, then show how little verified knowledge sits underneath it.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 illusion of knowledge and confident explanation
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 Archie Cochrane and evidence-based medicine
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P5 Cochrane misdiagnosis and the cost of specialist certainty

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: open on the seduction of fluent certainty
- P2: mechanism for how confidence gets mistaken for knowledge
- P3: anchor in Cochrane's push for evidence
- P4: tension through Cochrane's own misdiagnosis and delayed pathology
- P5: implication for judgment under uncertainty
- P6: bridge to scoring, calibration, and Chapter 3

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between feeling like you understand and having claims disciplined by evidence and feedback.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must keep the live problem of acting before certainty without turning evidence standards into paralysis.

Threshold Question:
How do you act decisively under uncertainty without letting the feeling of knowing outrun what has actually been tested?

- Hard: 5

- Takeaway 1: confident explanation is not proof
- Takeaway 2: expertise can outrun evidence
- Takeaway 3: evidence disciplines ego
- Takeaway 4: action still has to happen before certainty
- Takeaway 5: scoring and revision are the structural antidote
