Chapter Promise:
The reader will understand why perpetual beta may be one of the most important superforecasting traits, and why learning from feedback is harder than it sounds.

Opening Move:
Open on the desire to become finished, then contrast that with perpetual beta as endless trial, feedback, and revision.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 growth mindset versus fixed mindset
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 ambiguous language and hindsight bias as learning obstacles
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 perpetual beta and feedback hunger as durable improvement

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: introduce growth mindset and the refusal of fixed self-concepts
- P2: show why improvement matters more than static talent
- P3: explain how vague language blocks feedback
- P4: explain hindsight bias and distorted memory of error
- P5: define perpetual beta as endless revision and scrutiny
- P6: argue why this may matter more than intelligence and bridge to teams

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is that forecasting skill improves only when the forecaster keeps records clear enough to learn from and remains willing to revise the self as well as the forecast.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the difference between wanting feedback and actually learning from it without distortion.

Threshold Question:
How does a forecaster keep improving when memory, ego, and vague records all interfere with learning?
