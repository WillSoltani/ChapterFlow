Chapter Promise:
The reader will understand why updating skill is not just reading more news, and why proportionate revision matters more than constant motion by itself.

Opening Move:
Open on the discomfort of seeing forecasts move after new reports arrive, then separate disciplined updating from noise-driven twitching.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 stronger priors and the myth that news volume explains everything
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 Bill Flack and update sizing
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 dilution effect, underreaction, overreaction, and Tim Minto

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: narrow the news-volume myth
- P2: show why original forecast quality still matters
- P3: use Bill Flack to model measured updating
- P4: explain underreaction versus overreaction
- P5: introduce dilution effect and irrelevant information
- P6: land Tim Minto's many small updates and bridge to self-improvement

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is that forecasting edge comes from better update sizing, not merely from higher information intake.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the difference between responsiveness and instability.

Threshold Question:
How much should a new piece of information actually move the forecast?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: original forecast quality still matters
- Takeaway 2: updating is a separate skill from information intake
- Takeaway 3: underreaction and overreaction are twin failures
- Takeaway 4: dilution effect can distort revision
- Takeaway 5: many small updates can outperform rare dramatic swings
