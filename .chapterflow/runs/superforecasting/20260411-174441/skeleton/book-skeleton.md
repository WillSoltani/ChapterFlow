# Book Skeleton

## Metadata

- Title: Superforecasting: The Art and Science of Prediction
- Authors: Philip E. Tetlock, Dan Gardner
- Working edition: Crown 2015
- Chapter order: 12 numbered chapters
- Variant family: EMH

## Chapter Order

1. An Optimistic Skeptic  
Intent: establish the book's governing posture by rejecting both pundit confidence and fatalistic anti-prediction.
Source richness: high
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: low

2. Illusions of Knowledge  
Intent: show why confidence and explanatory fluency make bad forecasters feel smart.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: low

3. Keeping Score  
Intent: make feedback, scoring, and calibration the book's discipline rather than a side note.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: low

4. Superforecasters  
Intent: identify what the strongest forecasters actually do differently in practice.
Source richness: high
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: low

5. Supersmart?  
Intent: test whether raw intelligence explains forecasting edge.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: low

6. Superquants?  
Intent: test whether quantitative skill alone explains forecasting edge.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: low

7. Supernewsjunkies?  
Intent: separate useful information diets from compulsive information consumption.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: low

8. Perpetual Beta  
Intent: make self-updating and anti-finality the behavioral core of good judgment.
Source richness: high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: medium

9. Superteams  
Intent: show how disagreement, aggregation, and team process improve forecasts.
Source richness: high
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: medium

10. The Leader's Dilemma  
Intent: explore how leaders structure teams without crushing independent judgment.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: medium

11. Are They Really So Super?  
Intent: test the limits and durability of superforecasting claims.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: medium

12. What's Next?  
Intent: extend the argument into institutions, technology, and the future of judgment.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: medium

## Rotation Plan For Examples

- Work examples: meetings, hiring, investment memos, product bets, operations, leadership reviews
- School examples: debate prep, research design, seminar corrections, group projects, admissions choices, lab work
- Personal examples: travel plans, health decisions, family bets, budgeting, friend conflict, hobby improvement
- Keep examples probabilistic and decision-focused rather than purely motivational

## School-Setting Plan

- Use seminar rooms, debate teams, student newspapers, lab groups, admissions committees, and capstone projects
- Avoid repeating professor-correction scenes after Chapter 2 unless the lesson materially differs

- experts versus disciplined amateurs
- confidence versus calibration
- scoring versus storytelling
- update speed versus stubbornness
- team process versus lone genius

## Thin-Chapter Risk Notes

- Chapters 5 through 7 risk sounding like small variants of the same "what makes superforecasters special?" argument
- Chapter 12 risks turning into loose futurism if not kept tied to the book's disciplined forecasting frame

## Premium-Routing Candidates

- Chapters 3, 8, 9, 10, and 11 carry the highest risk of conceptual flattening and may need extra editorial scrutiny
