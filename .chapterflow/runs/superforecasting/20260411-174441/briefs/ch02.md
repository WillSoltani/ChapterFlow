Core Claim:
The chapter argues that bad forecasting often feels persuasive because people mistake confidence and explanatory fluency for actual knowledge. The problem is not only ignorance. It is ignorance padded by stories, status, and a false sense of understanding. The cure is evidence-minded humility: test claims, score them, and distrust the feeling of certainty when proof is thin. Archie Cochrane gives the chapter a hard edge by showing how even elite professionals can act as if they know more than the evidence warrants.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 2 turns inward. Chapter 1 established that forecasting can improve; Chapter 2 explains why ordinary thinking so often sabotages that improvement before feedback ever arrives.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show that confidence is often detached from actual explanatory depth.
Then they expose how professionals and institutions defend stories that feel complete.
Then they push toward evidence, testing, and revision as the antidote.

Required Anchors:
- Archie Cochrane as the champion of randomized testing and evidence-based medicine.
