Core Claim:
One of the deepest superforecasting traits is perpetual beta: an endless willingness to learn, test, revise, and improve. The chapter uses growth mindset, hindsight bias, and feedback hunger to argue that forecasting skill depends on treating oneself as improvable rather than finished. Ambiguous language and hindsight bias both block learning, while constant scrutiny and trial-and-error improvement keep the forecaster alive to feedback.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's self-improvement chapter. It stops asking what superforecasters know and starts asking how they keep changing themselves.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors connect superforecasters to growth mindset instead of fixed mindset.
Then they show why learning from mistakes is harder than it sounds because vague language and hindsight bias distort feedback.
Then they argue that perpetual beta may matter more than intelligence because the best forecasters keep improving without closure.
