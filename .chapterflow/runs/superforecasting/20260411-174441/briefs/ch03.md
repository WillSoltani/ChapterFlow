Core Claim:
Forecasting improves only when vague claims are turned into measurable ones and then checked against what happens. Numerical probability language, calibration, resolution, and Brier scoring make judgment accountable in a way verbal hedging does not. The chapter also argues that fox-like thinking and aggregation outperform charismatic hedgehog certainty when scorekeeping becomes real.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's operational pivot. It turns skepticism about confidence into a discipline of explicit probabilities, measurable horizons, and scored feedback.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show why vague forecasting language avoids accountability.
Then they introduce numerical discipline through Sherman Kent, calibration, resolution, and Brier scoring.
Then they connect scorekeeping to cognitive style by contrasting hedgehogs and foxes.

Required Anchors:
- Sherman Kent's attempt to quantify intelligence language after ambiguity proved costly.

- Hedgehogs versus foxes as a contrast between confident big-idea thinking and adaptive detail-oriented thinking.
