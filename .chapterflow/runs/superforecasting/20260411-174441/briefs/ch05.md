Core Claim:
Superforecasters are smart, but not because they are rare geniuses. The more important edge is how they use intelligence: they decompose messy questions, reach for outside-view base rates, test the opposite case, and enjoy hard thinking enough to stay with uncertainty longer than most people do. The chapter also keeps personality narrow by pointing to need for cognition, openness, and self-critical thinking rather than to a single magic trait.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is where the book refuses the flattering myth that superforecasters are just born intellectual prodigies. It shifts the reader from talent worship toward trainable habits of thought.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors narrow the intelligence claim by showing that superforecasters are above average without usually being geniuses.
Then they use Fermi-style decomposition and Bill Flack's outside-view reasoning to show what smart forecasting looks like in action.
Then they turn to cognitive style and personality, arguing that enjoyment of effortful thinking and self-correction matter more than raw horsepower.
