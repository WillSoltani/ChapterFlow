Core Claim:
Superforecasters are comfortable with numbers, but advanced math alone does not explain their edge. Their advantage comes from probabilistic thinking: handling uncertainty without panic, distinguishing kinds of uncertainty, using finer-grained estimates, and updating in measured steps instead of lurches. The chapter uses intelligence-community probability spreads, Barbara Mellers's granularity findings, and Tim Minto's many small revisions to show that calibrated judgment matters more than numerical swagger.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter removes the fantasy that forecasting excellence is basically hidden quantitative genius. It turns numbers into a language of nuanced judgment instead of a badge of mathematical prestige.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors narrow the numeracy claim by saying math helps but is not the whole story.
Then they show how leaders and analysts must think with probability ranges and disagreement instead of demanding certainty.
Then they argue that finer granularity and many small calibrated updates outperform blunt numerical confidence.
