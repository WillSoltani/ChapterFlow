Core Claim:
Superforecasters do absorb incoming news aggressively, but their edge is not just information volume. It comes from update discipline: starting from stronger priors, revising when evidence matters, resisting attachment to old narratives, and avoiding overreaction to irrelevant signals. The chapter uses Bill Flack's modest Arafat update, the dilution effect, and Tim Minto's many small revisions to show that update sizing is a forecasting skill in its own right.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter shifts the focus from traits and number sense to dynamic contact with the news stream. It tests whether constant updating is a cheap explanation and then makes updating itself the harder skill.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors reject the idea that superforecasters win only because they consume more news.
Then they show that updating is hard because forecasters can underreact from attachment or overreact from noise.
Then they argue that frequent, proportionate updates outperform both inertia and drama.
