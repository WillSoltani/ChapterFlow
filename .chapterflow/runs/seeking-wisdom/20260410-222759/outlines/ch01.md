Chapter Promise:
The reader will understand that many bad decisions recur in recognizable patterns and that the first protection is learning to diagnose the pattern before commitment locks it in.

Opening Move:
Open on the uncomfortable fact that people often notice a mistake only after it has become expensive because the mistake felt unique while it was happening.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The core distinction is between treating an error as a one-off event and treating it as a recurring pattern that can be reviewed before action.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Pattern recognition can protect judgment, but it can also tempt the reader into lazy labeling and false certainty.

Threshold Question:
How do you use familiar error patterns to sharpen judgment without letting the label do your thinking for you?
