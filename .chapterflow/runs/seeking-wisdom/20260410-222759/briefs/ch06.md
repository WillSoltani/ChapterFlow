Core Claim:
Knowledge and process still fail when fear, ego, memory drift, and emotional pressure overpower execution. Better judgment requires a temperament that can stay steady enough to use what it already knows. This chapter should make emotional self-management feel like a practical condition of judgment, not a soft add-on.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is about the behavioral limit on knowledge. The chapter must stay concrete about pressure, overreaction, vanity, fear, and memory drift. It is not a generic self-help chapter.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that people can know the right move and still lose it under emotion or pressure.
Then the author frames temperament as a practical defense against distorted execution.
Then the author pushes the reader to build steadier habits that preserve judgment when the moment gets heated.
