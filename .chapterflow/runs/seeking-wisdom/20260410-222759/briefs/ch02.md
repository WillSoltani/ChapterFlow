Core Claim:
People often explain themselves with clean reasons while behaving under the pull of self-interest, status, reciprocity, fairness, and social approval. Better judgment starts when you read motives and rewards together instead of taking stated reasons at face value. This chapter should make incentive-reading feel concrete rather than cynical.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is about motive-reading, not generic bias cataloging. The chapter should keep human nature visible through reputation, fairness, reciprocation, and self-interest rather than drifting into abstract strategy talk.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that behavior is shaped by self-interest, fear, reward, status, reciprocity, and fairness.
Then the author shows that people often imitate, conform, or rationalize under those pressures.
Then the author pushes the reader toward motive-reading as a protection against naivete.

Required Anchors:
- people act in self-interest even when their stated reasons sound cleaner
- reciprocity and fairness as strong motivators in social life
- conformity, shame, and status pressure changing behavior in groups

Counterarguments or Limits:
Motive-reading can become corrosive if it collapses into lazy cynicism. The chapter must keep mixed motives visible.
