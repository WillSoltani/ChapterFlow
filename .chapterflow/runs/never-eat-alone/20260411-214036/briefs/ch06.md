Core Claim:
This chapter argues that networking fails fastest when it becomes visibly self-centered, manipulative, or extractive. Ferrazzi's point is not simple politeness. It is that people withdraw from relationships that feel transactional, performative, or status-hungry. The distinct move is to define what off-putting networking looks like from the receiving side.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's contamination chapter. If it goes generic, it becomes manners advice. It has to explain why jerk behavior destroys trust and blocks the long-game benefits the earlier chapters depend on.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author identifies the behaviors that make networking feel fake or predatory.
Then the author shows that self-centered energy pushes people away even when the words sound polished.
Then the author concludes that warmth, memory, and genuine interest are stronger than visible status hunger.

- The chapter title as a warning label.
