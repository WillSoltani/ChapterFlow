Core Claim:
This chapter argues that relationship-building gets shallow when it is disconnected from a larger direction. Ferrazzi's point is not to turn networking into rigid life-planning. It is to show that a clear mission helps a person know which relationships to build, how to create value in them, and why random contact accumulation is weaker than connection organized around meaningful work.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's direction-setting chapter. If it goes generic, it becomes self-help mush. It has to connect purpose to relationship quality and selection, not just to motivation.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that relationship-building without direction becomes scattered and performative.
Then the author shows that a mission helps a person identify relevant people, ideas, and communities.
Then the author concludes that the network becomes stronger when it is tied to meaningful contribution rather than random accumulation.

Required Anchors:
- The chapter title itself as a pressure question that turns networking away from drift and toward direction.

- The related support that clarity around mission lets a person contribute more effectively because they know what they are building toward.
- The warning that collecting contacts without purpose creates width without useful depth.
