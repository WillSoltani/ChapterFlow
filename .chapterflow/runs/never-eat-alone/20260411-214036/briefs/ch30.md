Core Claim:
This chapter argues that success, access, and network power can rot when they turn into ego, entitlement, or self-importance. Within the frozen support boundary, Ferrazzi's point is that relational strength has to be protected from hubris if it is going to remain generous, disciplined, and sustainable.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's anti-hubris chapter. If it goes generic, it becomes vague humility advice or moralizing. It has to show why ego is not only a moral problem but also a relational and strategic failure.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author moves from influence and convening into the danger that success itself can distort character and relationships.
Then the author shows that hubris weakens connection by making a person less attentive, less generous, and less trustworthy.
Then the author concludes that discipline against ego is necessary if network strength is going to stay humane and durable.
