Core Claim:
This chapter argues that relationship-building fails when every interaction becomes a ledger. Ferrazzi's point is not naive self-sacrifice. It is that generosity creates stronger, more durable bonds than transactional tally-keeping, and that people who contribute broadly often create a multiplier effect of trust and help.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's first moral filter. If this chapter drifts into generic kindness, it gets soft. If it drifts into disguised extraction, it betrays the opening chapter. It must show why generosity is both humane and strategically stronger.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author rejects the idea that networking should be a running tally of favors.
Then the author argues that contribution and willingness to help create stronger relationships and wider trust.
Then the author concludes that generosity should be broad, practical, and free of tight scorekeeping because influence changes over time.

- The official "Don't keep score" line from the authorized publisher summary, with the pressure that ambition can quietly turn every relationship into arithmetic.

- The specific secondary claim that small favors, repeated help, and the question "How can I help you?" build stronger ties over time.
