Core Claim:
This chapter argues that relationships are weakest when they are activated only at the moment of need. Ferrazzi's point is that trust, familiarity, and goodwill have to be built before pressure arrives. The distinct move is to make timing the issue: preexisting connection turns an ask into a continuation of a relationship instead of an opportunistic interruption.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's anti-emergency chapter. If it goes generic, it becomes "network early" fluff. It has to show why advance relationship-building changes the emotional quality of later requests.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author rejects last-minute relationship-building driven by urgent need.
Then the author argues that trust compounds when contact happens before favors are required.
Then the author concludes that investing early makes later asks cleaner, warmer, and more credible.

Required Anchors:
- The chapter title as a timing principle.
- Secondary frozen support that relationships should be built before they are needed.
- The practical claim that early contact and repeated light maintenance create trust and make later requests easier.
