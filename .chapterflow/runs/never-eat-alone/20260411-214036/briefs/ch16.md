Core Claim:
This chapter argues that relationship growth comes from moving beyond familiar contacts into adjacent circles without becoming random, cold, or socially diffuse. Within the frozen support boundary, Ferrazzi's point is that network expansion works best through warm relevance and neighboring communities rather than through breadth for its own sake.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's expansion chapter. If it goes generic, it becomes bland advice to meet more people. It has to show how circles widen without losing warmth, fit, or continuity.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author turns from high-leverage connectors to the broader problem of staying socially boxed in.
Then the author shows that adjacent networks create growth paths without requiring random social sprawl.
Then the author concludes that expanding outward works when it preserves warmth and relevance instead of chasing breadth alone.
