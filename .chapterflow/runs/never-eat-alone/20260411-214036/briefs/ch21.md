Core Claim:
This chapter argues that a healthy network is often stabilized by a small number of especially important relationships that deserve deliberate care. Within the frozen support boundary, Ferrazzi's point is that some relationships function like anchors for a wider community, so they should be fed with real attention rather than taken for granted.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's anchor-relationship chapter. If it goes generic, it becomes favoritism advice or vague "invest in key people" rhetoric. It has to explain why a few high-value relationships can stabilize broader connection without making everyone else disposable.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author moves from light maintenance of many relationships to the question of which few relationships hold disproportionate weight.
Then the author shows that some people or relationships anchor access, trust, continuity, and community stability.
Then the author concludes that those anchors need ongoing care, generosity, and thoughtful investment rather than occasional extraction.
