Few social moves feel thinner than reaching out only when you need something. The message might be polite, even warm, but the timing gives it away. Ferrazzi's chapter on building it before you need it makes timing the whole issue. A relationship built under pressure feels like an interruption. A relationship built earlier feels like a continuation.

That is the chapter's first useful distinction. Trust is not created best in the same moment that it is asked to carry weight. Familiarity, goodwill, and memory work better when they have been built before the favor arrives. The frozen support for the chapter is simple but strong on this point: relationships should be built before they are needed.

This does not mean everyone deserves constant contact. Ferrazzi's point is lighter than that. It is about pre-need investment, not endless maintenance theater. A thoughtful check-in, a useful note, a shared article, a brief reconnection before life gets urgent: these forms of contact make later outreach warmer because they prove the bond existed before the ask.

The chapter is really about credibility. When someone hears from you only in emergencies, they naturally wonder whether the relationship matters on its own. When they have seen you before the pressure point, the later request lands differently. It sounds less like opportunism and more like a real extension of an existing bond.

Still, the chapter weakens if it becomes mechanical. Maintenance can turn fake fast. Forced contact, overmessaging, and artificial persistence do not build trust. They create noise. So the chapter needs restraint built into it. Presence matters, but it has to feel human rather than managed.

By the end, Ferrazzi has turned timing into a relationship principle. Build trust early, stay in light contact where it is real, and do not wait until urgency makes your outreach sound hollow. Once that is clear, the next question becomes bolder: if some relationships are worth building in advance, how much audacity should you bring when you reach beyond your current circle?
