The weakest kind of network is often the busiest one. A person says yes to every room, collects names quickly, and mistakes social motion for progress. Ferrazzi interrupts that drift by asking a harder question first: what are you actually trying to build your life around? Without some answer, relationship-building stays scattered.

That is why the chapter title matters. "What's Your Mission?" is not a grand declaration. It is a sorting question. A mission helps a person decide which rooms deserve repeated attention, which conversations have real relevance, and which relationships fit the work they are trying to do. The chapter is not calling for rigid certainty. It is trying to cut through drift.

That change in direction improves the quality of connection. When you know the kind of work, contribution, or impact you care about, your outreach becomes more coherent. You can recognize who matters, what you can learn, and what value you may be able to offer. Mission does not only sharpen the ask. It sharpens the contribution.

The chapter also blocks random accumulation. A wide contact list can still be weak if the relationships are not organized around anything real. Width alone is flimsy. The frozen support keeps the claim practical: goals and direction help identify the people who can matter more deeply. That does not make the relationships cold. It gives them shape.

Still, mission can go bad if it hardens into ego theater. Direction is useful because it organizes effort. It gets worse when it becomes brittle certainty or self-branding dressed up as purpose. People often clarify what matters partly through relationships, not only before them. So the chapter needs a looser clarity: enough focus to avoid drift, enough flexibility to keep learning.

By the end, purpose has become a networking principle rather than a speech. Strong relationships are not collected at random. They are built around a direction that helps a person choose better rooms, offer more relevant value, and avoid performative busyness. Once that is in place, the next question is timing: if you know which relationships matter, why wait until you desperately need them?
