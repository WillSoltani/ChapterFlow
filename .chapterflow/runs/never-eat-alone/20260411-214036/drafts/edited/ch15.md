Spotting a well-connected person in a crowded room can trigger a very specific kind of urgency. Someone notices the speaker badge, the cluster of attention, or the reputation attached to a name and immediately starts thinking about access. Ferrazzi's chapter is strongest when it refuses that reflex.

The problem with access hunger is not only that it looks needy. It also distorts judgment. A connector stops being a person with real demands, preferences, and context and becomes a shortcut. The chapter argues that this is the wrong frame from the start. High-leverage people are not best approached as trophies.

What improves the approach is contribution. If there is a useful idea, a relevant introduction, a thoughtful follow-up, or some other form of real fit, the interaction stops looking like pure extraction. The chapter is not saying that connectors should be flattered into generosity. It is saying that approach quality rises when the first move carries value instead of appetite.

That is why relevance matters as much as effort. Not every connector should be pursued, and not every possible approach deserves to be made. The chapter works best when it shows restraint. The stronger question is not, how do I get in front of this person? It is, what would make this interaction useful or fitting for both sides?

This is also why attention and trust enter the chapter. People who are highly connected usually face too many asks. A contribution-first approach signals that the relationship is not being treated as a one-way channel. The contact becomes less noisy because it is shaped by usefulness rather than by status excitement.

The chapter also needs a hard limit. Fake helpfulness is still extraction. If contribution becomes a costume for access hunger, the same problem returns in cleaner language. The chapter only holds if usefulness is real enough that it would still make sense even without guaranteed payoff.

That sets up the next chapter cleanly. Once the reader understands how to approach connectors well, the next issue is how to move beyond familiar contacts more broadly. Expansion should not mean indiscriminate reach. It should mean widening the circle without losing warmth or fit.
