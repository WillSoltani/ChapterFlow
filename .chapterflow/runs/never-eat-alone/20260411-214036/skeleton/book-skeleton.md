# Book Skeleton

## Metadata

- Book: Never Eat Alone, Expanded and Updated
- Authors: Keith Ferrazzi, Tahl Raz
- Locked edition: 2014 Crown expanded and updated edition
- Chapter count: 33
- Wave size: 2
- Source posture: official excerpt plus authorized preview plus narrow chapter-map cross-checks

## Chapter order and one-line intent

1. Becoming a Member of the Club
   Show that relationship-building is not extracurricular polish but a core path into opportunity, belonging, and upward movement.
2. Don't Keep Score
   Reframe networking around generosity and mutual lift rather than transactional tally-keeping.
3. What's Your Mission?
   Tie relationship-building to a clear personal direction so contacts cluster around purpose instead of vanity.
4. Build It Before You Need It
   Argue for building trust and familiarity long before a request creates pressure.
5. The Genius of Audacity
   Show how bold outreach can work when it is prepared, targeted, and anchored in real value.
6. The Networking Jerk
   Define the behaviors that make networking feel extractive and self-defeating.
7. Do Your Homework
   Make research and preparation the baseline for respectful, useful connection.
8. Take Names
   Turn memory, note-taking, and personal details into proof of care rather than superficial collection.
9. Warming the Cold Call
   Show how to reduce the social and practical friction of first contact.
10. Managing the Gatekeeper Artfully
   Treat intermediaries as relationships to honor, not obstacles to bulldoze.
11. Never Eat Alone
   Make ordinary meals and daily routines into durable relationship infrastructure.
12. Share Your Passions
   Use genuine interest and enthusiasm as connection fuel instead of forced small talk.
13. Follow Up or Fail
   Show that consistency after the first interaction is what converts interest into trust.
14. Be a Conference Commando
   Turn crowded professional settings into deliberate, generous relationship practice instead of random exposure.
15. Connecting with Connectors
   Explain how to approach high-leverage people by contributing value rather than chasing access.
16. Expanding Your Circle
   Move beyond familiar contacts into adjacent networks without losing warmth or relevance.
17. The Art of Small Talk
   Redeem small talk as a gateway to signal care, curiosity, and conversational ease.
18. Health, Wealth, and Children
   Show how recurring life concerns create natural paths to deeper connection.
19. Social Arbitrage
   Use insight across communities and contexts to create value through introductions and pattern-spotting.
20. Pinging All the Time
   Normalize light, regular outreach as maintenance for living relationships.
21. Find Anchor Tenants and Feed Them
   Identify the few high-value relationships that can stabilize a broader network and invest in them well.
22. Tap the Fringe
   Look to edge contacts and looser ties for fresh access, ideas, and serendipity.
23. Become the King of Content
   Use ideas, curation, and online sharing to stay visible and useful in the digital age.
24. Engineering Serendipity
   Create environments where useful chance encounters become more likely.
25. Be Interesting
   Build a life, mind, and range of experiences that make connection richer and more memorable.
26. Build Your Brand
   Shape a recognizable reputation around real value rather than self-promotion alone.
27. Broadcast Your Brand
   Extend that reputation across channels without sliding into noise or vanity.
28. Getting Close to Power
   Approach influence and elites with strategic humility, contribution, and clarity.
29. Build It and They Will Come
   Show how hosting, convening, and creating gatherings can compound network strength.
30. Never Give In to Hubris
   Warn that status and access can curdle into arrogance that corrodes relationships.
31. Find Mentors, Find Mentees, Repeat
   Make reciprocal guidance part of a healthy long-game network.
32. Balance Is B.S.
   Reject neat work-life formulas in favor of integrated priorities and relational tradeoffs.
33. Welcome to the Connected Age
   Close by repositioning connection as an ongoing operating system for modern work and life.

## Rotation plan for examples

- Work-heavy chapters should vary across consulting, startups, corporate leadership, creative industries, and nonprofit settings.
- School examples should skew toward office hours, group projects, student leadership, recruiting, alumni access, and campus events.
- Personal examples should rotate family gatherings, friendships, neighborhood communities, hobby groups, and milestone events.
- Avoid repeating conference and coffee-meeting scenes too often; save them for chapters where the mechanic truly depends on them.

## School-setting plan

- Use undergrad career fairs, seminar rooms, alumni mixers, club leadership, lab groups, and graduate recruiting contexts.
- Keep chapters 1, 7, 8, 9, and 17 especially strong for school examples because they map naturally to early-career social friction.

## Vocabulary watchlist

- Avoid generic networking clichés: hustle, leverage your network, maximize connections, build your tribe, add value at scale.
- Avoid cold corporate phrasing: stakeholders, touch base, circle back, optimize your relationships.
- Prefer social verbs with consequence: notice, remember, invite, introduce, follow up, host, reconnect, contribute.

## Motif and callback opportunities

- Class exclusion versus earned belonging
- Meals and gatherings as infrastructure, not ornament
- Visibility versus extraction
- Generosity as strategic strength, not softness
- Connector profiles as mirrors of different operating styles

## Thin-chapter risk notes

- Ch14 can collapse into generic conference tips unless anchored in status, energy, and concrete room-reading.
- Ch16 can drift into vague "meet more people" advice without a sharper distinction.
- Ch17 risks turning into generic charisma content unless it stays on conversational function.
- Ch30 can become a sermon unless tied to the social cost of ego in networks.

## Premium-routing candidates

- Ch01 because it sets the baseline voice and the class-to-connection argument.
- Ch03 because mission-setting can easily go generic.
- Ch15 because connector strategy has high leverage and moral complexity.
- Ch19 because social arbitrage can drift toward opportunism if not framed carefully.
- Ch23 because digital-age advice can become dated or generic fast.
- Ch28 because power access needs precision and moral distance.
- Ch32 because the title invites sloganized overreach.
- Ch33 because the close must synthesize without becoming a recap speech.
