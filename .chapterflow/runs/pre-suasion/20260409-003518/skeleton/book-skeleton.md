# Book Skeleton

## Metadata
- Book: Pre-Suasion
- Author: Robert Cialdini
- Locked edition: 2016 Simon & Schuster English text family
- Wave size: 2 by default
- Premium / solo routing candidates: ch07, ch09, ch11, ch13

## Rotation Plan For Examples
- Use the canonical six-format rotation once per chapter: decision_point, postmortem, dialogue, predict_reveal, dilemma, before_after.
- Keep the 2 work / 2 school / 2 personal split in each chapter package.
- Rotate ending types across broader_principle, self_directed_question, surprising_implication, cross_domain, common_trap, perspective_reframe.

## School-Setting Plan
- Rotate seminar corrections, club leadership, admissions pressure, group projects, dorm negotiation, and coaching moments.
- Avoid generic study-hack examples unless the chapter truly points there.

## Vocabulary Watchlist
- Keep pre-suasion, opener, receptivity, attention, association, and timing precise.
- Avoid drifting into broad charisma talk, manipulator clichés, or unsupported lab terminology.

## Motif / Callback Opportunities
- ch01 establishes the before-the-ask frame that later chapters specify.
- ch02 and ch03 can echo the idea that attention is not neutral but channeling.
- ch07 to ch09 should develop the association logic that the opener chapter only previews.
- ch13 should callback to the ethical warnings seeded in ch01.
- ch14 should close by showing that aftereffects matter because influence does not end at agreement.

## Chapter Plan
### ch01 PRE-SUASION: An Introduction
- Intent: Establish that persuasive advantage is often won before the message itself through preparation of receptivity.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Do not reduce the chapter to "first impressions matter." Keep the opener logic and trust/anchor examples load-bearing.
- Wave routing: wave 1

### ch02 Privileged Moments
- Intent: Show that some moments carry disproportionate influence power because attention and readiness temporarily peak.
- Source richness: low-medium
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Source support is thin, so the brief must stay narrow and should not invent specific studies.
- Wave routing: wave 1
