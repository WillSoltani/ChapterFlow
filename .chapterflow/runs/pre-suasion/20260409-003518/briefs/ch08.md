Core Claim:
Chapter 8 argues that messages become associated not only with their own merits but also with the surrounding place, including physical settings and psychological states, and that these contexts can reduce resistance and increase agreement.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 7 focused on favorable associations attaching to a focal concept. Chapter 8 shifts to where those associations are built, making placement and setting the central mechanism.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author claims that persuasive response depends partly on the context in which a message is encountered.
Then he argues that physical settings and psychological states attach traces to the message itself.
Then he shows why those places can be used as pre-suasive contexts that lower resistance.
Then he preserves a limit: a favorable setting can smooth reception without proving the offer's merits.
