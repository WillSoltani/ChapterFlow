Core Claim:
Chapter 2 argues that some moments carry outsized persuasive leverage because attention is unusually open, concentrated, or self-relevant right before a decision. The chapter's job is to move from the general discovery of pre-suasion to the more specific claim that timing and mental readiness are not random. Communicators can recognize or create moments in which a later request will land with greater force.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 1 proved that the before-the-ask stage matters. Chapter 2 specifies that not every before-the-ask moment matters equally. It has to make "privileged moments" feel like a real selection rule, not just another name for good timing.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author narrows the focus from pre-suasion in general to moments of unusual receptivity.
Then he argues that what is elevated in attention at the moment of choice gains special influence.
Then he shows that a question or cue tied to the listener's self-image can make a later request feel more natural.
Then he implies that skilled persuaders notice or create these receptive windows before making the real ask.
