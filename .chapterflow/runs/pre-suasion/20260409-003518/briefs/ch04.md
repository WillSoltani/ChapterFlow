Core Claim:
Chapter 4 argues that people often treat what is focal in attention as if it were causal. The chapter's job is to show that a highlighted feature can start to feel like the explanation for an outcome simply because it is the feature currently in view.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 3 argued that attention changes importance. Chapter 4 sharpens that by claiming focality can also distort causal judgment. It must feel like a distinct escalation, not a restatement.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author claims that focal cues are often mistaken for causes.
Then he argues that attention can make one feature look explanatory rather than merely noticeable.
Then he shows how this matters for persuasion and interpretation.
Then he preserves a limit: focality can mislead when visibility outruns actual causation.
