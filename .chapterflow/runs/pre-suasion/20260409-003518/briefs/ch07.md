Core Claim:
Chapter 7 argues that whatever concept receives focal attention can become more important and attractive because it gets linked to favorable associations already present in the audience.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 6 was about keeping attention on a cue. Chapter 7 shifts to what starts attaching to that cue while it stays active, making the chapter about linkage rather than staying power alone.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author claims that focal attention does not stay isolated from the rest of the response system.
Then he argues that attended concepts can pick up favorable associations already available in the audience.
Then he shows why this matters for persuasion before the main request lands.
Then he preserves a limit: associative lift is not proof that the linked concept deserves the positive weight it receives.

- borrowed positivity: favorable affect or meaning transfers to a focal target

Counterarguments or Limits:
