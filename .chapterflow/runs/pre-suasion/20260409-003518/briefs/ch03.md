Core Claim:
Chapter 3 argues that attention does not merely select information. What receives heightened attention also starts to feel more important. The chapter's job is to show that attentional foregrounding changes perceived significance, which is why directing attention before the ask can alter judgment later.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 2 argued that some moments are privileged. Chapter 3 explains why those moments matter by linking attention to perceived importance itself. It cannot drift into generic focus advice.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that attention carries evaluative weight, not just visibility.
Then he claims that what is brought forward in the mind starts to feel more important than what remains background.
Then he shows why this matters for persuasion and judgment.
Then he implies that pre-suaders can use attentional privilege without changing the underlying object itself.
