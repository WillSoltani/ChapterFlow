## Official book framing
- The book argues that persuasive success often depends on the moment before the message, not only on the message itself.
- The key move is to make an audience receptive before the request or proposal appears.
- The overview emphasizes redirected attention, mental state, and timing as major levers.

## Authorized excerpt support

### Chapter 1 opener and governing frame
- Cialdini opens by describing his undercover work in persuasion training programs across sales, marketing, fundraising, recruiting, and related fields.
- He reports that the highest performers spent more time preparing the psychological frame before making a request than merely polishing the request itself.
- He uses the gardener analogy: good seeds still depend on prepared ground.
- He defines pre-suasion as arranging for recipients to be receptive to a message before they encounter it.

### Concrete supported examples inside the frozen excerpt
- A Toronto-based consultant reduced price resistance by joking that he would not charge a million dollars before naming a $75,000 fee.
- Cialdini cites research showing that earlier numbers, long versus short lines, and even national music in a wine shop can shift later judgments or choices.
- He recounts the salesperson Jim, who staged a small act of forgetfulness so prospects would let him back into the house, associating him with trust before the pitch began.

### Early conceptual support
- What comes first changes how people experience what comes next.
- Openers can function as frames, anchors, primes, mindsets, and first impressions.
- The book revisits the six persuasion principles from *Influence* but shifts attention toward when and how receptivity is prepared.
- Ethical use matters, and the book explicitly flags the ethical risk of creating opportune moments of influence.
