Chapter Promise:
Show that some moments have unusual persuasive leverage because attention and self-relevance are especially alive right before the ask.

Opening Move:
Open on a person being asked a small identity-linked question before the real request, and show how that first move changes the climate of the second one.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> privileged moments as identifiable points of unusual receptivity
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4 -> what is elevated in attention at the moment of decision gains special privilege
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-5 -> helpful / adventurous self-image pre-question examples

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: narrow the chapter from pre-suasion generally to privileged moments specifically
- P2: explain that attention at the moment of decision is not neutral
- P3: show why elevated attention gains special weight in judgment
- P4: introduce self-relevance as a way to create or identify a privileged moment
- P5: preserve the ethical limit on identity-linked pre-questions
- P6: bridge into the next chapter's deeper focus on attention itself

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish ordinary earlier moments from privileged moments that matter because the audience's focus and self-relevance are unusually active right before the ask.

Hard-Depth Tension:
A privileged moment can help a worthy message meet a ready mind, but it can also become a manufactured identity trap if the speaker is trying to steer the person's self-concept instead of inform their judgment.

Threshold Question:
When does timing a request to a receptive moment respect the listener's judgment, and when does it start borrowing the listener's identity to push a result?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: not all moments before the ask carry equal leverage
- Takeaway 2: whatever is elevated in attention at decision time gains special influence
- Takeaway 3: self-relevant pre-questions can prime later consistency
- Takeaway 4: skilled persuaders notice or create privileged moments
- Takeaway 5: ethical limits matter when identity cues are involved
