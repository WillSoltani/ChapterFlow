Chapter Promise:
Show why what receives attention starts to feel more important, and explain why that matters for later judgment.

Opening Move:
Open on a decision that shifts because one feature gets the spotlight and therefore starts to feel like the main thing.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> chapter-title claim that attention becomes importance
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4 -> elevated attention gains special weight at decision time
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-5 -> foregrounding changes perceived significance before the ask

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: restate the chapter's central equation between attention and importance
- P2: explain why foregrounded items gain weight
- P3: show how this changes judgment without changing the object itself
- P4: connect the mechanism back to pre-suasion
- P5: preserve the limit between helping notice and distorting significance
- P6: bridge into focal-as-causal logic

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish between seeing something and treating it as important. Its claim is that attention can shift the second, not only the first.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Foregrounding can guide people toward what deserves attention, but it can also counterfeit importance by making the most visible feature feel decisive.

Threshold Question:
When does directed attention clarify what matters, and when does it merely inflate the importance of what happens to be most visible?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: attention changes felt importance
- Takeaway 2: foreground weight is not the same as truth
- Takeaway 3: importance can be shaped without changing the object
- Takeaway 4: pre-suasion uses this to prepare later judgment
- Takeaway 5: ethical and analytical limits on spotlighting

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): which product feature to foreground before a client choice
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): why a class presentation made one issue feel more important than the rest
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): explaining how spotlighting one detail changed a family decision
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): forecasting how one highlighted risk changes a meeting
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing whether to foreground urgency or fairness in a student appeal
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing a neutral request with one where one feature is spotlighted first

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like a highlighted line on a slide, a circled note on a handout, a meeting-room whiteboard, or a text thread where one issue gets repeated.

Preview Constraint:
