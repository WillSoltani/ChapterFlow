Chapter Promise:
Show that some cues attract attention faster than others, and why that matters before persuasion even begins.

Opening Move:
Open on a room instantly pulled toward one standout cue, then show that the pull came from the cue's attractor properties.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> chapter-title claim that attractors command attention
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4 -> threat, novelty, and goal-fit as attention magnets
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-5 -> captured attention shaping later persuasion

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define attractors as commanders of attention
- P2: explain why some cues seize attention quickly
- P3: show threat / difference / relevance as major families
- P4: tie capture back to persuasion
- P5: preserve the limit between grabbing attention and deserving attention
- P6: bridge into magnetizers

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish between what captures attention and what merits later judgment.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Attractors can help important cues get seen, but they can also over-reward cues built to seize attention regardless of merit.

Threshold Question:
What makes some cues win the spotlight before the audience has decided whether they deserve it?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: attractors seize attention fast
- Takeaway 2: threat, difference, and relevance are major attractor families
- Takeaway 3: persuasion benefits when one cue wins early attention
- Takeaway 4: captured attention is not earned importance
- Takeaway 5: Chapter 6 asks what keeps attention in place

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): whether to lead with the most standout issue
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): a room chased the noisiest cue instead of the most useful one
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): explaining why one personally relevant detail stole attention
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): forecasting what a threatening headline will do to attention
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing between a novel frame and a fuller frame
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing a flat message with a goal-fit message

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like a red headline, a standout slide, an unusual comparison item, or a cue linked to the listener's active goal.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what keeps attention from leaving once it has been captured.
