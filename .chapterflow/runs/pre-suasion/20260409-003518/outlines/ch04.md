Chapter Promise:
Show how what is focal can start to feel causal, and why that matters for persuasion and judgment.

Opening Move:
Open on a room that treats the most visible feature as the reason for what is happening, then reveal the distortion.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> chapter-title claim that focality breeds causal weight
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4 -> visibility starts to look explanatory
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-5 -> helping-norm / highlighted-need framing as an example of focal-causal pressure

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: state the focal-to-causal move
- P2: explain why visibility feels explanatory
- P3: show how that distorts judgment
- P4: tie the mechanism back to persuasion
- P5: preserve the limit between real cause and visible cause
- P6: bridge into the attractors chapter

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish between what is merely visible and what is actually causal.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Focality can help people notice a real cause, but it can also counterfeit causation by making the easiest feature feel like the explanatory one.

Threshold Question:
When does spotlighting a feature help people see the real cause, and when does it simply make visibility masquerade as explanation?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: focality often feels causal
- Takeaway 2: visible is not the same as explanatory
- Takeaway 3: persuasion can exploit this explanatory shortcut
- Takeaway 4: highlighted need or risk can dominate interpretation
- Takeaway 5: causal discipline requires looking beyond the spotlight

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): whether to foreground one visible problem before a decision
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): a committee blames the most visible issue after a failure

- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): forecasting what happens when one risk is spotlighted repeatedly
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): deciding whether to highlight need or context in an appeal
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing a narrow blame frame with a wider causal frame
