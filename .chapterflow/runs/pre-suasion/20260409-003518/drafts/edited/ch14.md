A persuasive win is not finished when the room says yes. Chapter 14 turns to that overlooked remainder. After a book built its case around preparing attention, association, and receptivity before the ask, the final chapter asks what happens after the moment has been won. Its title, *Post-Suasion: Aftereffects*, signals the shift clearly. The concern is no longer only how influence begins, but how its effects endure.

The frozen support keeps the chapter narrow. It says pre-suaders want to do more than create temporary changes through momentary shifts in attention. They want those changes to become durable. That matters because an influence move can feel successful while it is happening and still dissolve once the setting changes, the feeling cools, or the prepared cues disappear. A momentary opening is not the same thing as lasting change.

That is why Chapter 14 treats durability as a separate problem. Frontloading attention may help a message land. Association may ready meaning. Unity may soften resistance. But none of those things guarantees that the resulting shift will take root after the privileged moment passes. The chapter asks what has to happen next if the initial change is supposed to remain.

The authorized support gives one further claim, and it has to be handled carefully. The chapter says behavioral science provides evidence for two kinds of procedures that increase the likelihood that initially generated changes take root and last beyond pre-suasive moments. The frozen bundle does not identify those procedures, so the chapter must not invent them. What it can say is narrower and still useful: lasting change requires some kind of follow-on support beyond the moment of influence itself.

That makes the final chapter an important correction to any shallow reading of the book. Pre-suasion is not only about winning the first response. It is about preparing conditions under which a later response can begin honestly and then remain. Chapter 14 keeps the reader from confusing immediate movement with settled change.

The closing effect is to complete the book's arc. The opening chapters asked what can be done before a request to shape receptivity. The final chapter asks what has to be true after the request if the result is going to last. Influence, in that fuller sense, becomes accountable on both sides of the moment: in the preparation before it and in the durability after it.
