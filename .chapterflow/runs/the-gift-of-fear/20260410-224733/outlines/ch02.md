Chapter Promise:
By the end of the chapter, the reader should understand intuition as fast perception of real cues rather than mystical certainty.

Opening Move:
Open on a person feeling something is off in a room before they can explain why, then show that judgment starts arguing against perception.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5, P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: name the common experience of noticing before explaining
- P2: define intuition as technology or process, not magic
- P3: show how judgment talks people out of perception
- P4: explain why naming the cue is better than worshiping the feeling
- P5: separate intuition from worry and paranoia
- P6: bridge to prediction as the next expansion

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Perception notices the cue first; judgment often interferes by demanding a socially tidy story too early.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must keep the limit that intuition is only as useful as the concrete signal it can eventually point back to.

Threshold Question:
How do you trust fast perception without giving vague certainty too much authority?
