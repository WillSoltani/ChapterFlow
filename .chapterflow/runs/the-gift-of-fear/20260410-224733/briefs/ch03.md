Core Claim:
Prediction in this chapter is not fortune-telling. It is the disciplined reading of patterns, pre-incident cues, and repeated behavior so that danger looks legible earlier than it otherwise would. The chapter extends intuition outward: instead of noticing one live mismatch, it asks how people learn to see sequences and likely outcomes from human behavior. The core discipline is to treat recurring patterns as data without claiming impossible certainty.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 2 explained early perception. Chapter 3 scales that logic into forecast. It is about how accurate prediction grows from reading observable behavior chains rather than from charisma, confidence, or mystical instinct.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author moves from isolated cues to recurring patterns.
Then the author argues that prediction improves when people stop dismissing repeated behavior as coincidence.
Then the author concludes that reliable forecasting comes from disciplined attention to behavior sequences, not from dramatic certainty.
