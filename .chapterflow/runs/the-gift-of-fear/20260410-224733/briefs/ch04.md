Core Claim:
This chapter names the signals that often appear before danger becomes undeniable. It turns the earlier work on intuition and prediction into a more explicit signal vocabulary. The main point is that recognizable behaviors and pressure patterns can warn earlier than overt harm, and that people become safer when they stop dismissing those signals as isolated quirks.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Earlier chapters taught why signals matter and how patterns accumulate. This chapter is more concrete about the kinds of cues that should draw attention before harm fully develops.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that danger often broadcasts itself through early signals.
Then the author shows that these signals are behavioral and social, not mystical.
Then the author concludes that naming and respecting the signals improves timing and self-protection.
