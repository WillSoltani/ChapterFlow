Core Claim:
Intuition is not magic in this chapter. It is rapid pattern recognition that notices meaningful signals before conscious explanation catches up. The book's pressure comes from the claim that judgment often interferes with perception by demanding socially neat explanations too early. The chapter argues that safety improves when people name the cue they noticed instead of talking themselves out of it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 1 proved that an early signal can matter. This chapter explains what that early signal is doing and why people mistrust it. It is not yet a catalog of violence indicators. It is a chapter about the mechanics of perception, interference, and signal-reading discipline.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that intuition is a real process rather than mysticism.
Then the author shows that judgment and social rationalization often override perception.
Then the author concludes that people should identify the concrete cue their intuition noticed and act from that narrower evidence.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter can be misused if people treat intuition as mystical certainty. Its own guardrail is concreteness: identify the cue, keep the signal narrow, and do not let worry impersonate perception.
