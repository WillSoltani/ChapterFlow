## Official Author-Site Doctrine Notes

### Fear versus worry
From the official Gavin de Becker site:
- True fear is tied to something perceived in the environment or circumstance.
- Unwarranted fear or worry is tied to imagination or memory rather than a present signal.
- Worry is framed as manufactured and counterproductive because it delays constructive action.
- Constant fear dulls the signal that should stand out when danger is real.

### Intuition and safety
From official site material and chapter-linked notes:
- Intuition is treated as rapid pattern recognition, not mystical certainty.
- Judgment can interfere with perception when social scripts and rationalizations override a signal.
- The practical task is not to become anxious about everything, but to notice concrete cues and let them inform action.

## Chapter 1 Support Notes: In the Presence of Danger
- Secondary chapter summary and notes consistently identify Kelly as the opening case.
- Kelly is carrying groceries up to her apartment when a man insists on helping despite her refusal.
- Her discomfort shows up early, but social pressure and politeness keep overriding it.
- The assault escalates after the man gets inside the apartment.
- A later detail makes Kelly realize she is in immediate mortal danger, and that signal triggers decisive action.
- The chapter's core function is to establish that fear can be a survival signal and that politeness can bury it.

## Chapter 2 Support Notes: The Technology of Intuition
- The chapter frames intuition as a usable technology of perception rather than a supernatural gift.
- A key distinction is between perception and the judgment that talks people out of what they noticed.
- The chapter pushes readers to name concrete signals instead of accepting vague unease or dismissing it.
- The practical lesson is disciplined attention: identify what triggered the feeling, trust the pattern when the evidence is real, and avoid manufacturing fear where no present cue exists.
