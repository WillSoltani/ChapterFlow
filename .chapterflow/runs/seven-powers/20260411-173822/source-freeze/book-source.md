# Frozen Book Source Bundle

## Official bibliographic anchor
- Title: *7 Powers: The Foundations of Business Strategy*
- Author: Hamilton Helmer
- Publisher family: Deep Strategy LLC
- Published year: 2016
- Core book promise from the authorized metadata: strategy is about building power, defined as conditions that create the potential for persistent differential returns; power requires both a benefit and a barrier; invention comes first; and the Power Progression helps match powers to origination, take-off, or stability.

## Supported chapter-level notes

### Introduction setup
- Power is not generic advantage. It is a configuration that can sustain superior returns even against capable competition.
- Each power has a benefit and a barrier. The benefit improves cash flow through better pricing, lower cost, or lower investment needs. The barrier keeps competitors from arbitraging the benefit away.
- Strategy statics asks what power looks like once it exists. Strategy dynamics asks how a firm reaches that position.

### Ch01 Scale Economies
- Scale economies exist when per-unit cost falls as volume rises.
- Common sources named in the frozen notes include fixed costs, density/distribution effects, learning effects, purchasing leverage, and scale-related bargaining advantages.
- The chapter-level examples repeatedly cross-check to Intel, Netflix, and other firms where volume created durable cost advantage.

### Ch02 Network Economies
- Network economies exist when the value to one customer rises as more customers use the same offering.
- The frozen notes emphasize winner-take-most pressure, boundedness by network type, and the importance of early product execution.
- The bundle supports social/professional network examples and complement-driven reinforcing loops, but not speculative platform lore outside the frozen notes.

### Ch03 Counter-Positioning
- A newcomer adopts a superior business model that incumbents resist because copying it would damage their current profit pool or installed logic.
- The frozen notes consistently use disruption shaped by incumbent self-cannibalization and organizational unwillingness as the barrier.
- This chapter must keep the tension between newcomer superiority and incumbent inability/unwillingness visible.

### Ch04 Switching Costs
- Customers stay because switching would destroy value they have built up with the incumbent.
- The frozen notes break switching costs into financial, procedural, and relational forms.
- The chapter is about lock-in and continuity value, not about brand affection alone.

### Ch05 Branding
- Branding creates higher willingness to pay for an objectively similar offering because historical signals have shaped expectation and feeling.
- The key barrier is time and uncertainty in building the same trust and emotional meaning.
- Branding is not exclusive by default; competing brands can both be strong.

### Ch06 Cornered Resource
- Cornered resource means preferential access on attractive terms to a coveted resource that independently raises value.
- The bundle supports examples involving unique talent pools, patents, and control of scarce assets.
- The chapter must distinguish genuinely cornered inputs from ordinary good assets.

### Ch07 Process Power
- Process power is rare.
- It appears when a deeply embedded set of interlocking activities yields lower cost or better product and rivals would need prolonged commitment to match it.
- The frozen bundle supports Toyota and Amazon-style examples, but operational excellence alone does not qualify.

### Ch08 The Path to Power
- Operational excellence is not strategy.
- Firms often need invention or a crux directional choice before any real power can emerge.
- "Me too" positioning is insufficient because it leaves no barrier.

### Ch09 The Power Progression
- Power forms fit different stages: origination, take-off, and stability.
- The frozen notes align on the typical sequence: counter-positioning and cornered resource in origination; scale economies, network economies, and switching costs in take-off; process power and branding in stability.
- Timing matters. Missing the stage when a power can be established can permanently narrow later options.

## Support limits
- The bundle supports the seven powers, benefit/barrier reasoning, and the broad examples repeatedly named in the frozen sources.
- It does not support long exact quotations, hidden anecdotes, or detailed financial claims beyond what the frozen notes state.
- All downstream chapter artifacts must stay narrower rather than filling gaps with remembered strategy lore.
