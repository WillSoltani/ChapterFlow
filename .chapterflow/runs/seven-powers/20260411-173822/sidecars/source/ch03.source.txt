Supported source notes:

1. Counter-positioning means a newcomer adopts a superior business model.
   - The frozen notes define it as a newcomer adopting a new, superior business model.
   - The chapter is not about novelty alone. The model has to be better on relevant economics or value delivery.

2. The barrier comes from incumbent unwillingness to copy.
   - The frozen notes say the incumbent does not mimic the move because of anticipated damage to the existing business.
   - The core mechanism is self-cannibalization or analogous harm to the current model.

3. The chapter subtitle matters.
   - "Scylla and Charybdis" supports the idea that the incumbent is trapped between painful options.
   - Use that only as framing support, not as a mythological detour.

4. Practical limit supported by the frozen bundle:
   - If the incumbent can copy the entrant without much damage, the barrier weakens.
   - The entrant's advantage does not come from cleverness alone. It comes from forcing a painful incumbent tradeoff.

5. Book-level support:
   - The authorized bundle says invention comes first.
   - Counter-positioning should be framed as one route where invention matters because the old leader is tied to the old model.
