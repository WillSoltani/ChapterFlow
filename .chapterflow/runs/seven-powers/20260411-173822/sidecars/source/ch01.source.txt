Supported source notes:

1. Scale economies are declining unit costs as business size increases.
   - SellingSherpa gives the chapter title and the core definition.
   - The chapter is not about size in the abstract. It is about cost economics that improve with volume.

2. The chapter is governed by benefit plus barrier.
   - A larger firm gets the benefit through lower unit cost.
   - The barrier appears because smaller rivals must chase scale from a worse starting position and inherit inferior economics while doing it.

3. Named cost drivers supported in the frozen bundle:
   - fixed costs
   - volume / area relationships
   - distribution network density
   - learning economies
   - purchasing economies

4. Netflix is the clearest named company example in the frozen notes.
   - The frozen notes say original content shifted a major expense from variable toward fixed cost.
   - As subscriber scale rose, the unit burden of that content fell for Netflix.
   - Smaller streamers faced worse economics trying to match the same move.
   - Do not invent financial numbers or detailed chronology.

5. Intel appears in the frozen notes as a timing example.
   - Intel's success is tied to takeoff-stage decisions that helped it widen advantage through scale and switching costs.
   - For this chapter, use Intel narrowly to support the idea that scale power depends on timing and market significance.
   - Do not invent operational details beyond that.

6. Practical limit supported by the bundle:
   - Growth is not strategic by itself.
   - A business needs a route to continuing power in a significant market.
   - If bigger size does not bend the cost curve or leave followers structurally worse off, the firm may be getting bigger without getting stronger.
