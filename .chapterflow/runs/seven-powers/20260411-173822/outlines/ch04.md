Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will understand that switching costs protect an incumbent when customers would lose built-up value by moving to a rival.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between liking a product and being costly to leave. Show that customers can see a rival clearly and still stay because migration destroys accumulated value.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1 definition of switching costs and continuity value
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2 financial, procedural, and relational forms
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3 rival comparison after transition pain is counted
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P4 distinction from brand affection
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P5 limit where portability reduces the barrier
- anchor 6 used in paragraph(s): P6 bridge to branding

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define switching costs precisely
- P2: break the costs into financial, procedural, and relational forms
- P3: explain how the rival's offer becomes weaker once migration pain is included
- P4: separate lock-in from mere positive feeling
- P5: preserve the limit where switching becomes easier
- P6: bridge to the next power

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The central distinction is between affection and accumulated value loss. The mechanism is that moving to a rival destroys or forces recreation of financial, procedural, or relational value.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard must ask when the customer is truly locked in by built-up continuity value and when the supposed stickiness is weak because portability or low setup burden makes exit easier.

Threshold Question:
What has the customer built up with the incumbent that would have to be paid again during a switch?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: switching costs come from sacrificed value, not admiration alone
- Takeaway 2: financial, procedural, and relational costs can stack together
- Takeaway 3: the rival must beat both the incumbent offer and the transition bill
- Takeaway 4: portability and standards can weaken the barrier
- Takeaway 5: lock-in differs from branding because the customer loses setup value by leaving
