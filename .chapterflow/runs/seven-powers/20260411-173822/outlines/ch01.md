Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will see that scale becomes power only when larger size lowers unit cost in a way smaller rivals cannot economically copy.

Opening Move:
Open on the trap of mistaking growth for power, then pivot quickly into the harsher test: does getting bigger actually bend the cost curve in your favor?

Paragraph Job Map:

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The central distinction is between bigness and scale power. The mechanism is declining unit cost that leaves smaller rivals structurally disadvantaged as they try to match the leader.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard must ask when scale investment is strategic and when it is just expensive ambition. It must preserve the possibility that chasing volume too early can weaken a company instead of strengthening it.

Threshold Question:
How can you tell whether increasing volume is creating a durable barrier or merely creating a larger version of the same business?
