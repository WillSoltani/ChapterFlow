Core Claim:
Operational excellence is not strategy. Firms often need invention or a crux directional choice before any real power can emerge, and "me too" positioning is insufficient because it leaves no barrier. The chapter asks how a firm begins moving toward a power-bearing position instead of merely performing well inside an existing game.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is the cleanest transition from static analysis to dynamic construction. It resists broad motivational strategy talk by keeping the focus on invention, crux choices, and why imitation without a barrier does not create durable advantage.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author rejects operational excellence as a substitute for strategy.
Then he shows that firms often need invention or a crux directional move.
Then he explains why "me too" positioning fails because it offers no barrier.
Then he frames the problem as moving toward power rather than merely improving execution.
Then he leaves the reader with the practical question: what non-obvious move could begin creating a path to real power?

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Strategy dynamics: the problem of building power rather than merely describing it.
Crux choice: the directional move that changes the path toward power.
Invention: a non-obvious move that opens a route to a barrier.
Me-too failure: imitation that improves execution without creating protection.

Specific Applications:
- separating operational improvement from genuine strategic movement
- testing whether a plan contains a barrier-creating crux or only better execution
- recognizing when imitation leaves the firm inside the same exposed game

Common Misreadings:
- treating better execution as equivalent to strategy
- using invention as a synonym for random novelty
- assuming any market entry creates a path to power

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter weakens if it drifts into abstract strategy sermonizing without concrete links to invention and barrier creation. Hard depth should preserve that a move matters only if it opens a route to power rather than leaving the firm in me-too competition.
