Core Claim:
Power forms fit different stages: origination, take-off, and stability. The typical sequence in the frozen notes places counter-positioning and cornered resource in origination; scale economies, network economies, and switching costs in take-off; and process power and branding in stability. Timing matters because missing the stage when a power can be established can permanently narrow later options.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is the book's timing map. It closes the loop between the earlier powers and the dynamic problem of building them by showing that not every power is equally buildable at every stage. It must stay concrete about stage matching rather than drift into general life-cycle talk.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author defines the three stages: origination, take-off, and stability.
Then he matches different powers to the stages where they are typically buildable.
Then he explains that timing changes what options remain open.
Then he shows that missing a stage can permanently narrow strategic choices later.
Then he leaves the reader with the practical question: which powers are actually available at this stage, and which windows have already closed?

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

Take-off stage: scaling phase where other powers become available.
Stability stage: mature phase where still different powers dominate.
Timing pressure: the fact that delayed action can close certain power windows.

Specific Applications:
- testing which powers are actually plausible at a firm's current stage
- avoiding stage mismatch in strategic ambition
- understanding why some late moves cannot recover missed early opportunities
