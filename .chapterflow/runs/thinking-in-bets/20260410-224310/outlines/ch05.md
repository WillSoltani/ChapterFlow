# Chapter Outline

Chapter Promise:
Show why a trusted thinking partner improves judgment by challenging self-serving stories, weak evidence, and sloppy updates.

Opening Move:
Open from the fact that people are poor referees of their own certainty and revision.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2, P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3, P4

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Show why solitary judgment is vulnerable to self-serving narration.
- P2: Define the buddy system as a truth-seeking correction tool.
- P3: Explain what a good thinking partner does differently from a cheerleader.
- P4: Show how outside correction improves updates and accountability.
- P5: Bridge toward stronger group disagreement and dissent.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish a real truth-seeking buddy from someone who mainly offers comfort, agreement, or status protection.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard mode must keep the tension between psychological safety and real challenge: how can a partner be trusted enough to hear and sharp enough to confront?

Threshold Question:
What kind of outside voice helps you think better instead of just feel better?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: self-audit is not enough when ego is involved
- Takeaway 2: a good buddy challenges stories, not just outcomes
- Takeaway 3: truth-seeking feedback is different from reassurance
- Takeaway 4: outside correction improves updates and accountability
- Takeaway 5: better partners prepare the ground for healthy dissent

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dialogue, work): a teammate questions a forecast before the room locks in
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, personal): a friend catches a self-serving rewrite after a setback
- Scenario 3 (decision_point, school): a student chooses who to ask for real challenge before a competition
- Scenario 4 (before_after, work): a team review improves once one partner starts asking sharper questions
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): someone mistakes comfort for correction
- Scenario 6 (predict_reveal, school): a research partner asks what evidence would truly change the estimate

Scenario Vividness Targets:
- Scenario 1: conference room with a forecast slide paused on the wall
- Scenario 2: kitchen table postmortem with a notebook and one crossed-out explanation
- Scenario 4: meeting notes split between old reassurance and new challenge questions
- Scenario 6: seminar worksheet with a prediction and a revision column

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask how a whole group can use disagreement well instead of just one trusted partner.

Genericity Risks:
- drifting into generic friendship or mentorship advice
- making the buddy system sound like emotional support only
- skipping the harder truth-seeking standard in favor of comfort language

Forbidden Drift:
- no therapy clichés
- no generic teamwork slogans
- no implication that any supportive person is automatically a good thinking partner
- no brief-language leakage
