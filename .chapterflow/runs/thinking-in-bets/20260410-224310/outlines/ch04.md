# Chapter Outline

Chapter Promise:
Show how bet framing improves learning by helping readers read noisy feedback as the future unfolds.

Opening Move:
Open from the frustration of wanting a fast verdict when the evidence is arriving in pieces.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2, P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3, P4

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Show why people crave immediate clarity from incomplete feedback.
- P2: Define the unfolding future as a stream of evidence rather than a single reveal.
- P3: Explain how bet framing helps sort signal from noise without reverting to resulting.
- P4: Show how updating works without collapsing into overreaction.
- P5: Bridge toward the social learning and accountability of the next chapter.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish disciplined updating from emotional overreaction to early outcomes.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard mode must preserve the tension between openness to revision and stability under noisy short-run evidence.

Threshold Question:
How do you learn from early results without letting each new result rewrite the whole decision?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: the future unfolds in evidence slices, not clean verdicts
- Takeaway 2: early outcomes often mix signal with noise
- Takeaway 3: updating requires proportional revision rather than emotional swings
- Takeaway 4: process review remains necessary while feedback is incomplete
- Takeaway 5: learning discipline prepares the ground for outside correction

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (predict_reveal, work): a rollout gets mixed early signals and the team has to avoid snap judgment
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, personal): one bad week tempts a person to abandon a sound plan
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, school): classmates argue over whether one result proves a broader forecast
- Scenario 4 (decision_point, work): a manager decides whether to update a forecast after partial evidence
- Scenario 5 (before_after, personal): a person shifts from verdict-thinking to staged review
- Scenario 6 (dilemma, school): a finance-class team debates whether to trust a noisy early indicator

Scenario Vividness Targets:
- Scenario 1: rollout dashboard with one metric up and another down
- Scenario 2: notebook margin with one red result circled too heavily
- Scenario 4: conference room timeline with future checkpoints marked
- Scenario 6: classroom spreadsheet projected with cells changing color

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask how other people can help keep updates honest once beliefs and feedback start moving together.

Genericity Risks:
- restating Chapter 1 resulting lessons instead of focusing on learning over time
- turning updating into vague flexibility advice
- making the chapter sound like "trust the process" cliche

Forbidden Drift:
- no generic resilience talk
- no unsupported stats jargon
- no outcome-minimizing language that weakens accountability
- no brief-language leakage
