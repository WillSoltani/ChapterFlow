# Chapter Outline

Chapter Promise:
Make the reader stop treating outcomes as clean verdicts and start seeing decision-making as a bet placed under hidden information.

Opening Move:
Open on the Pete Carroll pass call because it is vivid, controversial, and instantly exposes the human habit of calling a decision stupid just because it failed.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2, P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3, P4

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Start with the Seahawks play and the speed of hindsight certainty.
- P2: Explain why outcome alone cannot carry the judgment because the decision was made under hidden information.
- P3: Introduce poker versus chess as the book's governing contrast.
- P4: Make "I'm not sure" feel like disciplined honesty, not weakness.
- P5: Close by positioning the rest of the book as a tool for better bets and bridge toward explicit betting language in the next chapter.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must separate outcome evaluation from process evaluation. The non-generic mechanism is that hidden information and luck make real life structurally more like poker than chess.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard mode must preserve the accountability problem: if outcomes are noisy, how do you avoid using uncertainty as cover for sloppy thinking?

Threshold Question:
How do you stay humble about outcomes without becoming evasive about the quality of your choices?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: resulting mistakes the scoreboard for the whole truth
- Takeaway 2: hidden information changes what a fair judgment looks like
- Takeaway 3: poker is useful because it normalizes incomplete knowledge and luck
- Takeaway 4: calibrated confidence is stronger than false certainty
- Takeaway 5: uncertainty should sharpen accountability, not erase it

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): a manager must judge a failed launch without collapsing into hindsight blame
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): a student team separates bad luck from poor prep after a competition loss
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): two friends argue over whether a failed decision was actually foolish
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): a hiring choice looks brilliant or terrible only after noisy early results
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): a student must state confidence honestly instead of pretending certainty
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): a person shifts from right-wrong self-judgment to probabilistic reflection

Scenario Vividness Targets:
- Scenario 1: projector glow in a Monday postmortem room
- Scenario 2: debate ballots and half-empty water bottles on a classroom table
- Scenario 3: text thread after a breakup decision
- Scenario 4: quarterly dashboard with one red metric and one green metric

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask the reader to predict what changes when every belief and choice is treated explicitly as a bet.

Genericity Risks:
- drifting into a generic "life is uncertain" sermon
- using poker only as a metaphor without making the judgment mechanism concrete
- softening accountability so much that the chapter sounds evasive

Forbidden Drift:
- no gambling glamour language
- no unsupported football-detail inflation
- no motivational certainty language
- no brief-language leakage
