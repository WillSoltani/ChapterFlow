## Official Book Frame

- Title: *Thinking in Bets*
- Subtitle: *Making Smarter Decisions When You Don't Have All the Facts*
- Author: Annie Duke
- Publisher: Portfolio/Penguin
- First locked English edition: 2018

Official framing from the publisher page:
- The book argues that decision quality and outcome quality are not the same thing.
- It uses poker as the governing analogy for life under incomplete information.
- It treats uncertainty, luck, and hidden information as unavoidable features of decision-making.
- It aims to make readers more comfortable saying "I'm not sure" and more disciplined about probabilities.

## Narrow Chapter Support Notes

### Introduction

- The introduction positions the book as a decision-making book rather than a poker memoir.
- Pete Carroll's Super Bowl XLIX pass call is used to separate process quality from result quality.
- The core frame is that uncertainty is always present, even when people pretend otherwise.

### Chapter 1: Life Is Poker, Not Chess

- Core distinction: life resembles poker more than chess because hidden information and luck are always in play.
- Main trap: "resulting," or treating an outcome as proof that the underlying decision was good or bad.
- Duke stresses that accurate self-description begins with admitting uncertainty.
- Useful pressure point: people want certainty so badly that they distort the connection between outcome and choice.

### Chapter 2: Wanna Bet?

- The chapter reframes every decision as a bet on an uncertain future.
- Beliefs become visible when a wager forces a person to state confidence more honestly.
- Confidence should be treated as calibrated probability rather than all-or-nothing certainty.
- Motivated reasoning and stubborn belief attachment distort judgment.

### Chapter 3: Bet To Learn

- Outcomes are noisy feedback, not clean verdicts.
- Learning requires separating luck from skill instead of claiming all good outcomes as personal credit and all bad outcomes as bad luck.
- The chapter emphasizes more objective outcome-fielding and better habit loops around truth-seeking.
- Comparative ego and self-serving bias interfere with learning from both our own outcomes and other people's outcomes.

### Chapter 4: The Buddy System

- Better decision-making is easier inside a truth-seeking pod than in isolation.
- Good groups need explicit norms for accuracy, accountability, and viewpoint diversity.
- The chapter draws a sharp line between confirmatory thought and exploratory thought.
- A productive group helps people confront blind spots they cannot reliably surface alone.

### Chapter 5: Dissent To Win

- Duke uses CUDOS as a framework for truth-seeking group culture:
  - Communism: relevant information belongs to the group.
  - Universalism: claims are judged by standards, not source prestige.
  - Disinterestedness: conflicts of interest must be recognized and contained.
  - Organized skepticism: dissent should be civil, active, and structurally welcomed.
- The chapter argues that withholding uncomfortable facts is often a signal that the omitted fact matters most.

### Chapter 6: Adventures In Mental Time Travel

- Better decisions come from bringing past and future selves into present choices.
- Regret is more useful when moved in front of the decision rather than processed only afterward.
- Tools include 10-10-10 thinking, precommitment, scenario planning, backcasting, and premortems.
- A major thread is reducing impulsive, present-biased decisions by rehearsing multiple futures.
