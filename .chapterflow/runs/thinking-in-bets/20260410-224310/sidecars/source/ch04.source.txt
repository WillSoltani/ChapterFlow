Core claim:
- The future reveals itself in pieces, so outcomes should be used as staged feedback rather than as instant verdicts.
- Bet framing helps readers learn from those pieces by separating signal from noise and process from result.
- Updating is strongest when it is proportional rather than reactive.

Distinct mechanism:
- The chapter treats the future as an unfolding stream of evidence instead of a single answer key.
- It shows how bet language helps sort noisy outcomes into learning rather than ego defense.
- It reframes review as repeated adjustment rather than one final judgment.

Required anchors:
- The unfolding future as gradual evidence.
- Short-run outcomes mixing signal and noise.
- Process review staying alive while feedback remains incomplete.

Common misreadings:
- Results barely matter if the future unfolds gradually.
- Updating means changing your mind every time a new data point appears.

Specific applications:
- Review mixed project metrics without overreacting.
- Stage forecast updates as new evidence arrives.
- Separate one noisy outcome from the longer learning loop.

Hard-edge requirement:
- Hard mode must preserve the tension between revising honestly and overreacting to noise.

Threshold question:
- How do you update from partial evidence without letting each result become a final verdict?
