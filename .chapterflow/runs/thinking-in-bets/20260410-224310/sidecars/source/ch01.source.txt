Core claim:
- This is not primarily a poker memoir or poker strategy book.
- The book is about decision-making under uncertainty, hidden information, and luck.
- The opening case makes the reader confront how quickly people confuse bad outcomes with bad decisions.

Distinct mechanism:
- The chapter uses the Pete Carroll goal-line pass as an opening test case for resulting.
- It introduces poker versus chess as the book's master contrast for real life.
- It reframes "I'm not sure" as intellectual honesty instead of weakness.

Required anchors:
- Pete Carroll's Super Bowl XLIX decision looked foolish because the pass was intercepted, but the outcome alone is not enough to judge the choice.
- Chess models near-perfect information while poker normalizes uncertainty and luck.
- Decision quality depends on process under the information available at the time, not on hindsight certainty alone.

Common misreadings:
- Thinking the chapter excuses bad choices by blaming luck.
- Thinking the poker comparison is decorative rather than structural.

Specific applications:
- Postmortems should ask what was known at the time.
- Confidence statements should reflect uncertainty instead of pretending certainty.
- Readers should prepare to treat later chapters as tools for better bets, not for perfect prediction.

Hard-edge requirement:
- Hard mode must keep the tension between humility and accountability visible. Uncertainty complicates judgment, but it does not erase responsibility for process.

Threshold question:
- If outcomes are noisy, what makes a decision praiseworthy or blameworthy in the first place?
