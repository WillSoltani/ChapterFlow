Core claim:
- Beliefs become more honest when they are expressed as bets rather than as unpriced declarations.
- Bet language reveals the gap between verbal certainty and real willingness to commit.
- The value of "wanna bet?" is calibration and learning, not gambling swagger.

Distinct mechanism:
- The chapter turns belief into something that can be priced instead of merely asserted.
- It uses stake language to expose hidden uncertainty and overconfidence.
- It reframes disagreement as a chance to compare confidence levels more honestly.

Required anchors:
- "Wanna bet?" as a calibration move.
- The mismatch between confident speech and actual willingness to stake.
- Bet language as a practical way to make beliefs measurable.

Common misreadings:
- The chapter is advocating literal gambling.
- Bet framing is mainly about social dominance.

Specific applications:
- Ask what stake fits a strong forecast.
- Use percentage or stake language to make disagreement clearer.
- Catch when your own confidence is performative rather than real.

Hard-edge requirement:
- Hard mode must preserve the tension between honest calibration and status performance.

Threshold question:
- What happens when a belief has to survive contact with a stake?
