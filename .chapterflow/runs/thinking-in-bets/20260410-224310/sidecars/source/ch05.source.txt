# Source Sidecar

Chapter: ch05 - The buddy system
Part: Outside correction and truth-seeking partnership
Richness: medium
Source IDs: src-openlibrary-edition, src-tedneward-summary
Canonical edition: 2018 Portfolio/Penguin English edition, ISBN 9780735216358
Quote posture: paraphrase_first

Core claim:
- Better decision-making needs another person who can challenge self-serving stories, weak evidence, and sloppy revisions.
- A useful buddy is not mainly a cheerleader. The role is truth-seeking correction.
- Outside accountability improves judgment because solo reflection is too easy to bend around ego.

Distinct mechanism:
- The chapter uses a trusted thinking partner to reduce bias in forecasting, reviewing, and updating.
- It separates supportive challenge from agreement-seeking comfort.
- It frames better feedback as a method for keeping beliefs and revisions honest.

Required anchors:
- The buddy system as a decision and review tool.
- Outside challenge as protection against biased self-narration.
- The difference between correction and reassurance.

Common misreadings:
- The chapter is mostly about emotional support.
- Any supportive friend is automatically a good decision buddy.

Specific applications:
- Review forecasts with a trusted challenger.
- Ask another person to test your evidence and confidence level.
- Use a decision buddy to catch self-serving rewrites after outcomes arrive.

Hard-edge requirement:
- Hard mode must preserve the tension between trust and challenge.

Threshold question:
- What kind of partner helps truth more than comfort?
