Core Claim:
This chapter argues that strong decision groups need real dissent rather than polite agreement. Duke uses dissent to show how truth-seeking improves when disagreement is invited, surfaced clearly, and handled without ego collapse. The chapter has to present dissent as a performance standard for better judgment, not as interpersonal friction for its own sake.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the chapter that escalates from one trusted buddy to a culture of useful disagreement. It must show how group quality rises when people argue toward truth instead of toward status, comfort, or premature harmony.

Author Logic Chain:
First Duke shows why agreement feels easier than truth-seeking.
Then she explains how suppressed dissent weakens decisions and learning.
Then she reframes disagreement as a productive input rather than a social threat.
Then she shows how better groups create room for challenge without chaos.

Required Anchors:
