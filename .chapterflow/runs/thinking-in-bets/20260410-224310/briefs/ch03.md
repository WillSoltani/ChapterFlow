Core Claim:
This chapter argues that beliefs become clearer and more honest when they are expressed as bets instead of as flat declarations. Duke uses bet language to expose overconfidence, force calibration, and make disagreement more productive. The chapter has to show that "wanna bet?" is not about gambling style. It is a tool for revealing how strong a belief really is and for separating conviction from posture.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the chapter that converts the book's big analogy into a practical conversational move. If Chapter 2 names the environment, Chapter 3 changes the language used inside that environment. The chapter must show how explicit wager language sharpens accountability without turning every interaction into combat.

Author Logic Chain:
First Duke shows that people speak with more certainty than they would ever stake.
Then she uses bet language to expose the real confidence level hiding underneath absolute statements.
Then she shows how bets improve clarity, accountability, and self-awareness.
Then she frames the move as a learning tool rather than as social aggression.

Required Anchors:
- "Wanna bet?" as a calibration tool.

- Bet language as a way to price belief and reveal uncertainty honestly.

Specific Applications:

- Reframe team disagreement by asking how strongly each person would price the claim.
- Catch self-deception when internal certainty exceeds real willingness to commit.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter must acknowledge that not every situation needs a literal wager and that social context matters. Its point is not to turn friendship or work into a casino. Its point is that stake-like language reveals confidence better than vague certainty claims do.
