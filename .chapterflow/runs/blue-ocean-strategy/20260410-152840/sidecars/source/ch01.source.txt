Chapter 1 frozen source notes: Creating Blue Oceans

- Official book page frames the core contrast as red oceans, where rivals fight over shrinking profit pools, versus blue oceans, where organizations create untapped market space and new demand.
- The same page says the argument is based on a study of 150 strategic moves across more than 100 years and 30 industries.
- O'Reilly's authorized contents page confirms Chapter 1 is the opening of Part One and that the expanded edition later adds chapters on alignment, renewal, and red-ocean traps, so this chapter must establish the broad logic rather than close every loop.
- Official FAQ says blue ocean strategy does not hinge on new technology and that success depends on linking innovation to value. It gives examples like Cirque du Soleil, yellow tail, and Starbucks as moves not driven mainly by technology.
- Official value-innovation page defines the core mechanism as pursuing differentiation and low cost simultaneously, and says value innovation happens only when buyer utility, price, and cost line up.
- Official Canon example gives one narrow, authorized strategic-move pattern: a shift in target buyer from office purchasing managers to office users that changed what mattered in the copier market.
- Official article on invention versus innovation reinforces that invention and blue-ocean success are different things. A move fails if it is original but does not unlock a leap in buyer value or commercial viability.
- Safe chapter emphasis: competitive crowding, strategic move rather than permanent industry fate, value innovation, and the promise of a systematic process to follow.
- Unsafe zone: detailed retelling of specific company histories beyond the small official references above.
