Core Claim:
This chapter argues that blue-ocean growth comes less from segmenting existing customers more finely and more from reaching beyond existing demand. Its public anchor is the three tiers of noncustomers framework, which distinguishes soon-to-be noncustomers, refusing noncustomers, and unexplored noncustomers. The chapter's burden is to move the reader away from narrower customer focus and toward latent demand that the current market leaves outside.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's chapter on demand expansion. Chapter 4 clarified the visible strategic picture. Chapter 5 asks who that picture still excludes and how a team can broaden demand instead of fighting harder over the current customer pool.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter argues that customer-centric strategy can become too narrow when it only refines current segments.
Then it shifts attention to noncustomers as the more strategic source of demand expansion.
Then it introduces the three tiers of noncustomers as the public discipline for seeing soon-to-be, refusing, and unexplored demand.
Then it argues that blue-ocean moves often unlock commonalities across those outside the market rather than chasing finer differences inside it.

Required Anchors:
- reach beyond existing demand as the governing move
- three tiers of noncustomers as the public framework
- the three tiers: soon-to-be, refusing, and unexplored noncustomers
- common demand across noncustomers mattering more than narrower segmentation inside the current market
