Core Claim:
This chapter argues that blue oceans are not found by brainstorming harder inside the same industry box. They are found by reconstructing market boundaries systematically. Its public anchor is the six paths framework, which gives teams a disciplined way to look across alternative industries, strategic groups, buyer groups, complementary offerings, functional-emotional orientation, and time.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's first full chapter on search logic. Chapter 2 shows how to see the current curve and challenge factors. Chapter 3 asks where a team should look when the existing market definition itself is too narrow.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter argues that most strategy search stays trapped because managers look within accepted industry boundaries.
Then it introduces boundary reconstruction as the answer to that search risk.
Then it presents the six paths framework as a systematic search surface rather than a creativity slogan.
Then it shows that commercially compelling blue oceans come from recombining what the market had kept separate.

Common Misreadings:
- treating the six paths as an ideation game instead of a search discipline
- turning the chapter into an exhaustive list of examples rather than a search method
- assuming every path must produce one idea instead of using the paths to sharpen judgment

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter can become vague if it is taught as creativity rhetoric rather than boundary reconstruction. It also risks unsupported overreach if case details are invented beyond what the frozen official pages support.
