Core Claim:
This chapter argues that strategy quality improves when teams step back from spreadsheet competition and use visual strategy work to see the whole pattern of competition more clearly. Its public anchor remains the strategy canvas as a one-page visual analytic, but the chapter's job is different from Chapter 2. Here the point is not just diagnosing the current curve. It is seeing strategic sameness, possible divergence, and choice at the level of the whole picture rather than through isolated numbers.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's chapter on visual judgment. Chapter 3 widened the search field by reconstructing market boundaries. Chapter 4 explains why those options must be seen comparatively and visually, or the team will slide back into fragments, spreadsheets, and category habits.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter argues that numbers and line-item competition can hide strategic sameness.
Then it re-centers visual strategy work as the way to see the current strategic picture more honestly.
Then it treats the strategy canvas as the main public instrument for seeing comparative patterns and possible new value curves.
Then it argues that big-picture judgment matters because strategic choice collapses when teams drown in factors without seeing the whole shape.

Counterarguments or Limits:
