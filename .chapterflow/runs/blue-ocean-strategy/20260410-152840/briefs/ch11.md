Core Claim:
This chapter argues that firms fall back into red oceans when they let competition-first assumptions govern strategy again. The frozen official support is narrow but sufficient: red ocean strategy means competing for existing demand in known market space, benchmarking rivals leads to imitation rather than innovation, and fighting over existing customers narrows strategic imagination.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's trap chapter. Chapter 10 framed renewal positively through the portfolio lens. Chapter 11 turns to the negative side and names the mindset errors that drag firms back toward ordinary competition.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter re-establishes what red-ocean logic is.
Then it warns that benchmarking rivals and trying to beat the competition recreate that logic.
Then it warns that fighting over existing customers narrows demand creation.
Then it closes the book by treating trap avoidance as a discipline of keeping buyer value and new demand ahead of imitation.
