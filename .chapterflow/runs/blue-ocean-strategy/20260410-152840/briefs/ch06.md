Core Claim:
This chapter argues that a blue-ocean move is not complete when demand looks large or exciting. It must also pass a strategic sequence. The public support in the frozen bundle anchors buyer value, price, and cost alignment through official value-innovation material and the invention-versus-innovation article's four-question idea-index summary. The chapter's burden is to make commercial order feel like a pressure test rather than a checklist.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's viability chapter. Chapter 5 widened demand beyond existing customers. Chapter 6 asks whether that opportunity can actually hold once buyer utility, price, cost, and adoption pressures are tested in order.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter argues that promising demand is not enough if the move fails commercially.
Then it frames strategic sequence as the discipline that prevents attractive ideas from collapsing in practice.
Then it anchors the sequence in buyer value, price, cost, and adoption.
Then it argues that a move only deserves commitment when those pressures align instead of fighting one another.

Required Anchors:
- get the strategic sequence right as the governing move
- buyer utility / buyer value first
- price and cost needing to align commercially
- adoption as the final reality test
