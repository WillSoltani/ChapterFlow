Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand why fighting harder inside a crowded market is a different game from creating uncontested demand, and why value innovation is the opening chapter's decisive mechanism.

Opening Move:
Open on the suffocating logic of rivals slicing at the same demand pool, then pivot into the idea that some strategic moves escape that water entirely.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for red-ocean pressure and the blue-ocean contrast
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for the 150 strategic moves and the strategic-move unit
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for value innovation and the break from the usual trade-off

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the felt pressure of crowded competition
- P2: sharpen the contrast between red-ocean rivalry and blue-ocean creation
- P3: establish the study base so the chapter does not read like inspiration-only strategy talk
- P4: explain why the unit of analysis is the strategic move, not the permanent nature of an industry
- P5: define value innovation as more than novelty or technology
- P6: bridge toward the need for tools and process in Chapter 2

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must clearly distinguish mere competitive differentiation from value innovation that changes the buyer-value and cost logic enough to open new demand.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must feel the difference between expensive novelty inside a red ocean and a move that genuinely changes the market's basis of competition.

Threshold Question:
How do you know whether a bold new offer is opening uncontested space or simply decorating the same fight at higher cost?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- red-ocean rivalry as a shrinking logic
- strategic move as the portable unit of learning
- value innovation versus invention theater
- competition-irrelevant demand creation
- the risk of mistaking differentiation spend for a blue ocean
