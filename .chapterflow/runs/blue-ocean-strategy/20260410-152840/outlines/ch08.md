Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand why execution becomes more durable when strategy is built through fair process rather than handed down through opaque decisions.

Opening Move:
Open on a team that can tolerate an unfavorable outcome better than an opaque process because people can live with a hard decision more easily than a process they distrust.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for process legitimacy and execution
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for fair process and its three elements
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for committed execution and the bridge beyond the chapter

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show why outcomes alone do not determine cooperation
- P2: explain fair process as an execution mechanism rather than a courtesy
- P3: establish engagement
- P4: establish explanation and expectation clarity
- P5: show why fair process can sustain cooperation without consensus
- P6: bridge toward the book's broader synthesis

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish fair process from consensus and from post-hoc messaging.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must feel that people may still dislike a decision and yet choose to execute when the process felt fair enough to trust.

Threshold Question:
What would prove that execution failed less because of the decision itself and more because people experienced the process as unfair or opaque?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- why process legitimacy shapes execution
- fair process as the chapter's governing move
- engagement
- explanation and expectation clarity
- why durable follow-through does not require full consensus

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): leader decides whether to invite real input before a difficult strategic choice
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): group accepts the outcome only after realizing the process lacked explanation
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): siblings debate whether fairness means agreement or transparency plus clear expectations
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): manager thinks morale is the issue but discovers expectation clarity is missing
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): seminar group must choose between fast opaque decision and slower fair-process discipline
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): organizer changes follow-through by explaining the reasoning and the role expectations after the choice

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include annotated decision notes, visible role expectations, margin comments on proposals, or meeting recap boards.

Preview Constraint:
The preview should point toward the book's later synthesis without inventing unsupported chapter mechanics.

Genericity Risks:
- consensus cliches
- HR trust language with no strategic edge
- treating explanation like spin
- flattening expectations into vague accountability talk

Forbidden Drift:
- invented legal fairness claims
- generic servant-leadership prose
- participation language detached from decisions
- brief-language leakage
