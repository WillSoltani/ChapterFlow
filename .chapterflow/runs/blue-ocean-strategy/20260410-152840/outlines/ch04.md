Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand why strategic choice gets clearer when teams compare the whole pattern visually instead of treating spreadsheets and line items as the strategy itself.

Opening Move:
Open on a room full of charts and metrics where nobody can tell whether the offer is meaningfully different, then shift to the relief of seeing competing curves on one page.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for why numbers can hide sameness
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for visual strategy work and the strategy canvas as whole-pattern comparison
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for why big-picture judgment matters when choosing among boundary-shift options

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show how line-item competition obscures the strategic picture
- P2: explain why whole-pattern visibility matters more than metric accumulation
- P3: reintroduce the strategy canvas as the public visual surface
- P4: show how comparative curves expose sameness or divergence
- P5: argue that choice requires seeing the whole strategic pattern, not isolated factor wins
- P6: bridge to noncustomers by asking what demand the visible pattern still ignores

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish between using numbers as inputs and letting numbers fragment the strategic picture until the team cannot tell whether it is still obeying the same market logic.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must feel how visual clarity can improve judgment without turning into hand-wavy anti-analytic rhetoric.

Threshold Question:
What would prove that a team is seeing a genuinely different strategic pattern rather than just a spreadsheet with more favorable cells?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- why numbers alone can hide strategic sameness
- visual strategy work as comparative judgment
- strategy canvas as whole-pattern instrument
- why choice requires seeing the entire value profile
- how this sets up the move toward noncustomers in Chapter 5

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): team must choose between adding more dashboard metrics or putting competing value curves on one wall
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): student group realizes its spreadsheet argument never showed whether the offer was strategically different
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): siblings compare a service idea visually and notice that one factor is hiding the same old pattern
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): product team expects a pricing fight but discovers the whole curve still mirrors the market
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): seminar team debates whether a visually different pattern justifies dropping a favored metric
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): independent coach stops optimizing line items and reframes the offer around a clearer overall curve

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include printed charts, taped curves, wall comparisons, marked-up canvases, or annotated factor lists.

Preview Constraint:
