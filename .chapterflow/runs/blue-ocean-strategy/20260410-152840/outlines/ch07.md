Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand why execution barriers should be attacked as a few named organizational hurdles rather than as a vague culture problem.

Opening Move:
Open on a strategy that passed the commercial test but is still stalled because the organization cannot see the urgency, free the resources, move the key people, or neutralize internal blockers.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for why execution fails after strategic viability
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for tipping point leadership and the four hurdles
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for concentrated leverage and the bridge to fair process

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the gap between commercially sound strategy and organizational motion
- P2: explain why broad change language is too weak for the problem
- P3: establish tipping point leadership as focused leverage
- P4: distinguish cognitive, resource, motivational, and political hurdles
- P5: show why concentrated effort beats diffuse reform
- P6: bridge to Chapter 8 by pointing toward execution built into strategy

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish diffuse change management from concentrated leadership against a few organizational bottlenecks.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must feel that execution is neither automatic nor solved by generic inspiration; it moves when leverage hits the right hurdles.

Threshold Question:
What would prove that a strategy failed not because it was weak, but because the organization never moved through its key hurdles?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- why viable strategy still stalls in organizations
- tipping point leadership as leverage
- cognitive and resource hurdles
- motivational and political hurdles
- why Chapter 8 must build execution into strategy next

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): leader decides whether to spread resources thinly or target one bottleneck team
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): class venture sees a strong plan fail because nobody cleared cognitive hurdles
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): siblings debate whether motivation or politics is the true blocker in a shared project
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): manager assumes budget is the problem but discovers political resistance is decisive
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): seminar group must choose which hurdle to attack first in a campus initiative
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): organizer replaces broad pep talks with a focused leverage move that shifts participation

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include blocked calendars, marked-up resource maps, visible bottleneck boards, or stakeholder pressure notes on one page.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must point toward fair process without teaching its three elements in advance.

Genericity Risks:
- generic change-is-hard prose
- leadership cliches detached from specific hurdles
- motivation talk that erases politics or resources
- invented case heroics

Forbidden Drift:
- unsupported turnaround stories
- culture jargon with no leverage point
- collapsing all hurdles into morale
- brief-language leakage
