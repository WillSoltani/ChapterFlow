Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand how the pioneer-migrator-settler map helps leaders judge whether their portfolio supports future growth strongly enough to renew blue oceans over time.

Opening Move:
Open on a portfolio that looks comfortable in the present but may be weak on future growth because too much of it has settled.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for renewal and the risk of treating success as permanent
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for settlers, migrators, and pioneers distinctly
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for future-growth judgment and the bridge to red-ocean traps

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: warn that blue oceans must be renewed over time
- P2: introduce the portfolio view
- P3: establish settlers, migrators, and pioneers distinctly
- P4: explain how the PMS map helps judge future growth
- P5: show the danger of a comfortable but settled portfolio
- P6: bridge toward the traps that pull firms back into red oceans

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish stable settlers from growth-driving pioneers without treating settlers as worthless.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must feel that a present-comfort portfolio can still be strategically weak if it underweights future growth.

Threshold Question:
What would prove that a portfolio feels stable now but is too settled to renew blue oceans effectively over time?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- why blue oceans require renewal
- settlers, migrators, and pioneers distinctly
- the PMS map as a future-growth lens
- why settled comfort can mask strategic weakness
- why renewal leads into red-ocean traps

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): leader judges whether a portfolio leans too hard on settled business
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): team realizes its strongest current offering says little about future growth
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): siblings debate whether stable settlers are enough without pioneers
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): manager thinks the portfolio is healthy until the map shows too little pioneer movement
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): studio team must choose between protecting settled comfort and renewing future growth options
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): organizer stops viewing initiatives one by one and starts using a portfolio-growth lens

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include portfolio boards, category columns, sticky-note maps, or future-growth ranking sheets.

Preview Constraint:
The preview should point toward red-ocean traps without importing those traps early.

Genericity Risks:
- generic innovation-funnel talk
- treating pioneers as automatically superior in every way
- flattening settlers, migrators, and pioneers into vague buckets
- drifting into unsupported portfolio math

Forbidden Drift:
- invented financial ratios

- generic disruption slogans
- brief-language leakage
