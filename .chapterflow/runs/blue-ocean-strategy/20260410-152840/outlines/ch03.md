Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that market creation starts by reconstructing boundaries systematically, not by inventing louder ideas inside the same market box.

Opening Move:
Open on a team that keeps benchmarking direct rivals until someone asks whether the real comparison set, buyer group, or surrounding use experience has been defined too narrowly from the start.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for why search gets trapped inside accepted boundaries
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for the six paths framework as the disciplined answer
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for what the paths do to strategic judgment and buyer-value recombination

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the trap of searching only against known rivals
- P2: explain boundary reconstruction as the real shift
- P3: introduce the six paths framework as systematic search
- P4: summarize the six directions without turning the chapter into a list dump
- P5: show why cross-boundary insight can produce new demand
- P6: bridge to big-picture visualization in Chapter 4

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish between disciplined boundary reconstruction and free-form idea generation that still assumes the industry's current borders.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must feel the difference between widening search intelligently and multiplying options that never leave the same competitive frame.

Threshold Question:
How do you know whether a team is genuinely reconstructing market boundaries instead of just brainstorming variations on what the industry already offers?
