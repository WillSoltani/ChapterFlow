Chapter Title: Never Enough
Position In Book: 4 of 22

Core Claim:
The desire for "a little more" can turn abundance into self-destruction. Status comparison and the inability to define enough make people take risks they do not need to take.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is the book's appetite warning. It focuses less on scarcity and more on the danger of insatiability, especially when high achievement stops feeling like security and starts feeling like a ranking contest.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that having a lot does not automatically create a stopping point.
Then the author explains how comparison and status make "more" feel permanently unfinished.
Then the author argues that the most dangerous risk is often the risk you take when you already had enough.
Then the author concludes that defining enough is a protective skill, not a timid one.
Then the author bridges to compounding by showing that staying in the game matters more than squeezing every extra point of return.

Specific Applications:
- define what "enough" means before success expands your appetite
- avoid risking stability for prestige
- separate admiration of others from imitation of their lifestyle or risk tolerance

Common Misreadings:
- the chapter condemns ambition
- the chapter says all wealth is morally dangerous
- the chapter says growth should stop early in every context

Counterarguments or Limits:
Ambition and growth are not the problem by themselves. The problem is risking irreversible downside for a comparison game with no finish line.
