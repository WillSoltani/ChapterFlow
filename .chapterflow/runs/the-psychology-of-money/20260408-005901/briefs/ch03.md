Chapter Title: Luck & Risk
Position In Book: 3 of 22

Core Claim:
Outcomes are shaped by forces that can look identical on the surface but mean opposite things underneath. Luck and risk both move results, which means observers should be slower to assign full credit or blame and quicker to judge process with humility.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter widens Chapter 2's humility from biography to randomness. It focuses on the danger of reading outcomes too confidently, especially when good results may hide luck and bad results may hide disciplined process.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that luck and risk are siblings because both distort the clean link between effort and result.
Then the author shows why people over-read visible outcomes and under-read randomness.
Then the author explains why this makes hero worship and moral condemnation unreliable.
Then the author concludes that process deserves more attention than outcome snapshots.
Then the author bridges to Never Enough by showing that success interpreted too cleanly can become appetite and overreach.

Counterarguments or Limits:
