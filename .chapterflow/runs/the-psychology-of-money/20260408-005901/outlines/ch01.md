# CH01 Outline

## Hook

- Open with the contrast between Ronald Read and Richard Fuscone.
- Use the curiosity gap: how can a janitor and gas station attendant leave a fortune while a highly credentialed executive ends in bankruptcy?
- Make the reader feel that intelligence is not the clean explanation they want.

## Build

- Show what Ronald Read represents: ordinary income, low visibility, patience, and behavior that stayed intact for decades.
- Show what Richard Fuscone represents: elite knowledge, social polish, leverage, image maintenance, and behavior that cracked when conditions changed.
- Shift from the story to the broader claim that money is lived in emotional environments, not abstract laboratories.
- Bring in the blackboard-versus-dinner-table contrast.
- Name the forces that bend decisions: ego, envy, fear, marketing, incentives, family expectations, and identity.

## Deliver

- State the chapter's real insight: money is a soft skill where behavior often dominates technical knowledge.
- Add the limit clearly: arithmetic still matters, but it has to survive human behavior to matter in practice.
- Explain why the book uses short stories. Money behavior becomes visible in scenes, not just formulas.

## Bridge

- Reward the reader with one clean rule: do not ask only whether a decision is mathematically correct; ask whether a real person can actually keep it.
- Open the next chapter by asking why sensible people can look at the same financial choice and see different risks, goals, and meanings.

## Distinct Scenario Lesson Map

1. Work / decision_point:
   classify a "smart" plan by whether the team can stick with it under public pressure
2. School / postmortem:
   learn from a technically strong model that failed because the humans using it panicked
3. Personal / dialogue:
   surface the hidden emotional drivers inside a household money disagreement
4. Work / predict_reveal:
   show how polished expertise can still break when status maintenance becomes load-bearing
5. School / dilemma:
   choose between a strategy that looks impressive and one that is behaviorally survivable
6. Personal / before_after:
   shift from tracking only numbers to tracking emotional triggers around money
