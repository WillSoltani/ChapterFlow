# CH02 Outline

## Hook

- Start with the accusation built into the title: other people's money behavior looks crazy.
- Flip the accusation quickly by showing how decisions feel from the inside of a different life.

## Build

- Explain that each person's money memory is a narrow slice of history seen at close range.
- Show how depression, inflation, unemployment, bull markets, or easy credit change what risk feels like.
- Bring in the recency of modern investing tools and retirement systems.
- Make clear that many people are operating without centuries of stable precedent.
- Emphasize that explanation is not endorsement.

## Deliver

- Land the chapter's insight: disagreement about money often starts in biography, not stupidity.
- Add the practical rule: before copying advice or judging someone else, ask what world they think they are living in.
- Connect this to self-knowledge: your own instincts are also history-shaped.

## Bridge

- Reward the reader with a humility upgrade: understanding a decision's origin improves judgment.
- Open the next chapter by noting that even after you understand a person's behavior, luck and risk still shape outcomes beyond anyone's control.

## Distinct Scenario Lesson Map

1. Work / decision_point:
   pause judgment and ask what market or career history is behind a cautious or aggressive colleague
2. School / postmortem:
   review a club conflict by mapping the different home and economic histories students brought in
3. Personal / dialogue:
   turn a household argument into a conversation about money memory and safety
4. Work / predict_reveal:
   discover that a conservative CFO's past crisis history was shaping present caution
5. School / dilemma:
   choose between copied online advice and a strategy matched to your own emotional tolerance
6. Personal / before_after:
   reframe family money habits once the older generation's history is made visible
