# CH02 Source Sidecar

Chapter: No One's Crazy

## Frozen-support facts

- The chapter title and book-level framing support the claim that money behavior is deeply shaped by experience.
- Stable public summaries of the book consistently describe this chapter as arguing that different money decisions can feel rational given different histories.
- Public discussion of the chapter repeatedly points to inflation, depression, unemployment, and market booms as examples of experience-shaped risk perception.
- Public summaries also note that many common retirement tools are historically recent, which supports the chapter's humility about treating current norms as timeless.

## Safe paraphrase zones

- personal money experience is a tiny slice of history but a large share of worldview
- generational and economic context shape what feels safe or reckless
- explanation is not endorsement
- copied advice often fails because the adviser and the reader are not starting from the same history

## Avoid

- exact quotations
- unsupported numerical precision about historical returns or survey data
- presenting one generation's experience as universally correct
