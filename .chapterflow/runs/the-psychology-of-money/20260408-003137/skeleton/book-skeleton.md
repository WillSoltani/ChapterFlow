# The Psychology of Money Skeleton

## Metadata

- Title: `The Psychology of Money`
- Author: `Morgan Housel`
- Edition lock: Harriman House Limited, `2020-09-08`, ISBN `9780857197689`
- Chapter packaging: 22 reader-facing units including Introduction, Confessions, and Postscript

## Chapter Order

1. `Introduction: The Greatest Show On Earth`
   - intent: Money works like a behavioral subject before it works like a spreadsheet problem.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: low
   - thin-chapter risk: low
2. `No One's Crazy`
   - intent: People bring different money histories to the table, so behavior that looks irrational from one life can look sensible from another.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
3. `Luck & Risk`
   - intent: Luck and risk are opposite forces that often look identical in the final score.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
4. `Never Enough`
   - intent: The hardest part of money is often deciding what counts as enough before appetite turns success into self-destruction.
   - source richness: medium-high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: high
   - thin-chapter risk: low
5. `Confounding Compounding`
   - intent: Compounding looks magical only because the most important variable is usually duration, not brilliance.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: low
   - thin-chapter risk: low
6. `Getting Wealthy vs. Staying Wealthy`
   - intent: Getting rich often rewards boldness, but staying rich rewards endurance, restraint, and survival.
   - source richness: medium-high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
7. `Tails, You Win`
   - intent: In many money systems, a small number of outsized wins explain most of the total result.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium-high
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
8. `Freedom`
   - intent: The highest return money can buy is control over your time and attention.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: low
   - thin-chapter risk: low
9. `Man in the Car Paradox`
   - intent: People buy status objects hoping to be admired, but observers usually admire themselves in the object rather than the owner behind it.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
10. `Wealth Is What You Don't See`
   - intent: Real wealth is the pile of resources you have not yet spent, which means it is usually invisible from the outside.
   - source richness: medium-high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
11. `Save Money`
   - intent: Saving is powerful because it creates flexibility before it creates a specific purchase or forecast.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: low
   - thin-chapter risk: low
12. `Reasonable > Rational`
   - intent: The best money plan is often the one you can live with consistently, even if a model says another plan is more efficient.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium-high
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
13. `Surprise!`
   - intent: History is steered by surprises so often that planning as if surprise were rare is itself a major forecasting mistake.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium-high
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
14. `Room for Error`
   - intent: Margin is not wasted space in a financial plan. It is the price of surviving uncertainty and your own imperfect judgment.
   - source richness: medium-high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: low
   - thin-chapter risk: low
15. `You'll Change`
   - intent: People underestimate how much their future priorities will differ from the priorities they defend most confidently today.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
16. `Nothing's Free`
   - intent: Good returns always charge a fee, and in investing the fee is usually emotional discomfort rather than a line item on a statement.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
17. `You & Me`
   - intent: People make costly mistakes when they copy financial behavior from someone who is playing a different game with different incentives and horizons.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium-high
   - moral complexity: high
   - thin-chapter risk: low
18. `The Seduction of Pessimism`
   - intent: Pessimism feels sophisticated because danger is vivid, immediate, and emotionally legible, while progress is slow and easy to overlook.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
19. `When You'll Believe Anything`
   - intent: People reach for tidy stories when outcomes are messy, especially in markets where randomness and complexity leave gaps that narrative rushes to fill.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium-high
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
20. `All Together Now`
   - intent: The book's strongest money advice only makes full sense when humility, patience, savings, enough, and independence are treated as one coherent temperament.
   - source richness: medium-high
   - concept density: medium-high
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
21. `Confessions`
   - intent: Confessions works because it admits that even universal truths about money still have to be applied through a personal lens.
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
22. `Postscript: A Brief History of Why the U.S. Consumer Thinks the Way They Do`
   - intent: Modern American money behavior was shaped by a specific postwar history, not by timeless human nature.
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: medium-high
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low

- risk: sounding overconfident where public source support is thin
- risk: losing the book's humane tone while trying to sound smart
