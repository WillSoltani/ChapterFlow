Focus claims:
- Pessimism feels sophisticated because danger is vivid, immediate, and emotionally legible, while progress is slow and easy to overlook.
- The mind notices crashes, frauds, and losses faster than it notices decades of compounding improvement, so bad news gets mistaken for deeper truth.
- The practical lesson is to pair realism about fragility with long-run optimism about adaptation, progress, and human problem-solving.
