When You'll Believe Anything

Supported by frozen bundle sources: Harriman House official contents page, Google Books bibliographic preview, and catalog listings where needed.

Focus claims:
- People reach for tidy stories when outcomes are messy, especially in markets where randomness and complexity leave gaps that narrative rushes to fill.
- After the fact, the mind prefers a coherent explanation to an honest admission that many things were uncertain, surprising, or partly random.
- The practical lesson is to distrust explanatory confidence that arrives faster than evidence and to leave more events labeled uncertain than the crowd prefers.
