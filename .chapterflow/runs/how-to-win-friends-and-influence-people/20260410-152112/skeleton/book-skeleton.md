# Book Skeleton

## Metadata
- Book: How to Win Friends and Influence People
- Author: Dale Carnegie
- Locked edition: 2022 Simon and Schuster revised trade line
- Numbered chapter range: 1-30
- Wave size: 2 by default
- Premium / solo routing candidates: ch01, ch02, ch10, ch18, ch20, ch22, ch25, ch30

## Rotation Plan For Examples
- Maintain the canonical six-format rotation once per chapter: decision_point, postmortem, dialogue, predict_reveal, dilemma, before_after.
- Maintain 2 work / 2 school / 2 personal examples in every chapter package.
- Rotate endings across broader_principle, self_directed_question, surprising_implication, cross_domain, common_trap, and perspective_reframe.

## School-Setting Plan
- Rotate school contexts across class-team conflict, debate prep, student government, dorm friction, club leadership, scholarship interviews, lab partnerships, case competitions, and teacher-student feedback moments.
- Keep school examples socially concrete rather than turning Carnegie into generic study advice.

## Vocabulary Watchlist
- Keep criticism, appreciation, interest, argument, cooperation, pride, face, resentment, importance, and motive chapter-bound.
- Avoid leadership-summit filler, emotional-intelligence buzzwords, empty positivity slogans, and modern productivity jargon.

## Motif / Callback Opportunities
- The opening warning about criticism in ch01 should echo through later chapters about argument, fault-finding, and leadership correction.
- The desire to feel important in ch02 should keep resurfacing as the hidden motive under liking, agreement, cooperation, face-saving, and praise.
- The shift from self-interest to the other person's motives in ch03 should operate as a spine across persuasion and leadership chapters, not a one-off rule.
- Part Three should feel like escalation from basic rapport into disagreement handling, while Part Four should feel like pressure-tested application under authority.
