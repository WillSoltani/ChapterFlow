Core Claim:
The chapter argues that early agreement can shape the emotional and conversational direction of persuasion before resistance has a chance to harden. Small truthful points of agreement create forward motion. Early contradiction often creates drag and defensive positioning. The point is not to trick someone into compliance. It is to begin from real alignment so that momentum works with the conversation instead of against it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 13 focused on warmth at the start. Chapter 14 narrows further and asks what kind of early content shapes momentum once the opening is warm. It cannot become compliance hacking, rhetorical funneling, or fake consensus. Its distinct work is to show that early truthful yes-moments can keep persuasion cooperative, while also naming the manipulation risk directly.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter assumes that conversational momentum matters and that early positions become harder to reverse once resistance starts.
Then it shows that real early agreement keeps the exchange moving in a cooperative direction.
Then it keeps the chapter from thinning out by insisting that agreement must be honest and non-coercive rather than staged.

Common Misreadings:

- using early yes-moments to bypass the other person's genuine judgment

Counterarguments or Limits:
Agreement extracted dishonestly can destroy trust, and some conversations do not offer much early common ground. The prose should keep the chapter balanced: early yes works when it grows out of reality, not pressure.
