Core Claim:
The chapter argues that genuine interest in other people is the fastest social welcome signal because it shifts attention off self-preoccupation and onto the other person's experience. People feel the difference between being managed, impressed, or entertained and being actually noticed. That difference changes whether a room opens.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 3 was about building requests from the other person's side. Chapter 4 widens that move into social presence. It cannot become networking advice, extroversion worship, or fake curiosity scripts. The point is that welcome follows attention.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter treats self-focused presence as socially narrowing.
Then it suggests that people respond warmly when they feel genuine interest instead of self-display.
Then it turns that interest into the opening social technique for liking and trust.

Required Anchors:
- the title-level promise that one simple move makes you welcome widely
- the contrast between self-display and real interest in other people
- the limit that interest has to be genuine rather than performed for gain alone

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

- attention shift: moving the center of presence away from the self and toward the other person

Specific Applications:
- entering a meeting, class, or gathering by noticing others instead of rehearsing your own impression management
- asking questions that arise from real attention rather than from self-branding
- building trust in friendships, clubs, and teams through active interest instead of self-centered performance

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as a networking hack
- assuming the chapter rewards loud sociability rather than focused attention
- mistaking question-asking alone for genuine interest

Counterarguments or Limits:
Not every setting rewards immediate warmth, and not every person wants the same level of social attention. The chapter also risks sounding manipulative if interest is treated as a tactic detached from sincerity. The prose should keep the human contact real.
