Core Claim:
The chapter argues that people respond strongly to being personally recognized, and names are one of the clearest signals of that recognition. Forgetting or flattening names pushes interaction toward generic politeness. Remembering and using a name makes the other person feel more specifically received. The point is not memory performance. It is regard.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 5 focused on visible warmth at first contact. Chapter 6 makes that warmth more personal by attaching it to recognition. It cannot become a chapter about clever memory systems, networking etiquette, or fake personalization. Its distinct work is to show why names matter emotionally.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter assumes that people want to feel personally rather than generically handled.
Then it shows that remembering and using a name is a direct signal of that personal regard.
Then it keeps the chapter from thinning out by insisting that names matter because of recognition, not because of mnemonic skill.

Specific Applications:
- greeting coworkers, classmates, and neighbors in a way that makes them feel specifically remembered
- using names in meetings, classrooms, and service exchanges without sounding performative
- noticing when forgetting names creates distance beyond the simple memory lapse itself

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as a memory-hack chapter
- using names so often that the interaction sounds manufactured
- assuming names matter only in business or networking contexts

Counterarguments or Limits:
Not every setting calls for repeated name use, and overuse can sound calculated. Some names are unfamiliar or easy to miss at first. The prose should keep the principle humane: the important thing is personal regard, not verbal overperformance.
