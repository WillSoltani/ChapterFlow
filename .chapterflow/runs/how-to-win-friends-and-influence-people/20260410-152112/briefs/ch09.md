Core Claim:
The chapter argues that liking rises quickly when another person can feel your genuine respect and importance toward them. Goodwill does not come mainly from performance, charm, or fast praise. It comes from making regard legible in a way that feels human rather than flattering. The point is not manipulation. It is immediate warmth grounded in sincere value.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 8 centered the other person's interests. Chapter 9 asks what happens when that attention becomes so unmistakable that goodwill appears almost at once. It cannot become compliment technique, reputation strategy, or social sugar. Its distinct work is to separate genuine importance from flattery while keeping the promise of quick liking.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter assumes that people respond quickly when they feel distinctly valued.
Then it shows that visible respect creates goodwill faster than self-display or charm performance does.
Then it keeps the chapter from thinning out by insisting that praise without sincerity turns unbelievable and can reverse the effect.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- visible regard: making sincere respect legible enough to be felt
- importance signal: a concrete social cue that tells the other person they matter here
- flattery fog: praise that feels strategic, inflated, or detached from reality

Specific Applications:
- showing respect in work, school, and personal settings without sounding performative
- noticing when admiration stays private and therefore never becomes socially legible
- distinguishing truthful appreciation from fast compliments designed to win favor

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as empty praise technique
- assuming instant liking means manipulation or fake charm
- acting as if making someone feel important requires exaggeration

Counterarguments or Limits:
People notice when appreciation sounds inflated or generic, and no one can force instant liking in every situation. The prose should keep the chapter balanced: quick goodwill is plausible when regard is truthful and visible enough to register.
