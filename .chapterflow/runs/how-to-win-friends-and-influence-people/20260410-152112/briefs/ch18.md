# Chapter 18 Brief

- title: What Everybody Wants
- section: Part Three: How to Win People to Your Way of Thinking
- core claim: sympathy for the other person's ideas and desires lowers defensiveness because people want their inner position to be understood, but sympathy must stay distinct from agreement
- bridge from prior chapter: Chapter 17 made motives more legible; this chapter turns that legibility into more precise sympathy
- bridge to next chapter: once defensiveness drops, appeals to nobler motives can land more cleanly
- moral complexity: high

## Required Emphases

- preserve the line between sympathy and agreement
- keep sympathy precise rather than vague
- show that people want their ideas and desires to be understood
- avoid therapeutic or validating-by-default language
