Core Claim:
The chapter argues that first impressions often form before explanations do, so visible warmth matters immediately. A sincere, open expression can make another person feel received before the conversation has carried very far. The point is not cosmetic cheerfulness. It is that the face can either invite contact or make the room feel harder to enter.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 4 focused on genuine interest as the inner source of welcome. Chapter 5 asks how that welcome becomes visible at first contact. It cannot turn into body-language trivia, fake positivity, or salesy advice about appearing upbeat. Its distinct work is to show that visible warmth is one of the earliest social signals people read.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter assumes that people register atmosphere before they understand content.
Then it shows that visible warmth can lower strain and make another person feel received.
Then it keeps the move credible by insisting that forced expression often feels false.

Required Anchors:
- the title-level promise that one simple move improves first impressions
- the claim that visible warmth reaches people before many words do
- the limit that first-impression warmth has to be sincere rather than pasted on

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- visible warmth: outward expression that makes another person feel received
- first-contact signal: the nonverbal social cue that arrives before explanation
- forced-cheer failure: when visible friendliness feels manufactured rather than human

Specific Applications:
- greeting a coworker, classmate, or neighbor with an expression that lowers strain instead of adding distance
- entering a meeting or classroom with visible openness before trying to prove competence
- noticing when a flat or guarded face is sending a colder signal than intended

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as advice to grin constantly
- reading visible warmth as manipulation rather than welcome
- reducing the chapter to appearance management instead of sincerity

Counterarguments or Limits:
Not every setting invites the same level of outward warmth, and some people are naturally more reserved in expression. The chapter also becomes hollow if it is reduced to pasted-on friendliness. The prose should keep the principle human and situational.
