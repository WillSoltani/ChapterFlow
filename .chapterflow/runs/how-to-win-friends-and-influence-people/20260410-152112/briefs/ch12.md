Core Claim:
The chapter argues that when you are truly wrong, fast and honest admission is often stronger than defensiveness because it removes the fuel that keeps attack alive. People often push harder when they meet resistance. They often soften when the fault is already openly owned. The point is not groveling. It is truthful self-correction that restores room for cooperation and movement.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 11 showed how superiority makes enemies. Chapter 12 presents the opposite posture: when the fault is yours, own it before the conflict hardens. It cannot become image strategy, manipulative self-blame, or ritual apology language. Its distinct work is to show that honest admission can dissolve attack energy faster than self-protection can.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter assumes that defensiveness often invites more attack rather than less.
Then it shows that quick admission can remove the other person's need to keep pressing the case.
Then it keeps the chapter from thinning out by insisting that admission must be truthful and proportionate rather than theatrical or false.

Specific Applications:

- noticing when self-defense is prolonging a conflict you could shorten by telling the truth
- distinguishing proportionate ownership from exaggerated self-abasement

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as strategic groveling
- assuming any admission must be dramatic to count
- using false self-blame to control the other person's reaction

Counterarguments or Limits:
Not every accusation is accurate, and over-admitting can create new distortions. The prose should keep the chapter balanced: admit fault quickly when it is real, but do not turn honesty into theater or false confession.
