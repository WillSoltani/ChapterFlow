Chapter Promise:
Show that first impressions are shaped by visible warmth before much explanation begins, and that sincere expression opens contact faster than forced cheerfulness.

Opening Move:
Open on how quickly people read the face and tone of welcome before they understand what is being said.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 -> first impressions form early and visible warmth matters
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 -> expression can invite contact before words do
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 -> forced cheer fails and bridge into names/recognition

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: establish that first impressions start before explanation
- P2: show visible warmth as an early social signal
- P3: explain why another person feels received by sincere expression
- P4: contrast honest warmth with mechanical friendliness
- P5: keep the chapter from collapsing into grin advice or body-language trivia
- P6: bridge into Chapter 6 by moving from expression to recognition

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
People read visible warmth quickly, but they also read strain and performance quickly, so the chapter depends on sincerity rather than on expression as a trick.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How can a face carry welcome without turning welcome into a mask?

Threshold Question:
When does visible warmth feel like human ease, and when does it feel like borrowed friendliness?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- first impressions begin before explanation
- visible warmth changes the room quickly
- sincere expression makes people easier to approach
- forced brightness feels false
- warmth is invitation, not performance

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): hallway greeting before a first meeting
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): volunteer table or seminar doorway where one greeter felt easy to approach and one felt closed
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): family or friendship moment where expression changes the tone before the talk begins
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): onboarding moment where competence display loses to visible welcome
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): student host wants to seem friendly without feeling fake
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): neighbor or roommate exchange shifts when guarded expression softens

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include a lanyard, seminar handout, half-open door, paper cup, name tag, or text notification.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must point toward Chapter 6 by asking how welcome becomes personal recognition instead of only atmosphere.

Genericity Risks:
- body-language cliche
- fake positivity advice
- thin "just smile more" simplification

Forbidden Drift:
- no charm-performance jargon
- no wellness language about positivity
- no contamination phrasing
- no brief-language leakage into reader-facing prose
