Chapter Promise:
Show that good conversation grows more reliably from disciplined listening and follow-up attention than from self-display.

Opening Move:
Open on why many conversations fail because both people are waiting to perform rather than to receive.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 -> self-display versus conversational room
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 -> listening and follow-up as the real engine of conversation
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 -> reject fake-questioning and bridge into interest

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: expose performance-minded conversation as thin
- P2: show what listening changes in the room
- P3: name follow-up attention as the chapter's main move
- P4: explain why people open around what matters to them
- P5: keep the chapter from collapsing into question scripts or passive silence
- P6: bridge into Chapter 8 on interest

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Listening works here because it creates room for the other person's real interest, but it fails when the questions are procedural and the answers are not truly received.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How do you listen in a way that feels like hospitality instead of extraction or delay before your own turn?

Threshold Question:
When is a question an invitation, and when is it only a tool to keep the conversation looking alive?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- self-display can thin out conversation
- listening creates conversational room
- follow-up attention is stronger than stock questions
- people open around what matters to them
- fake listening hollows the chapter out

- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): student leader wants to keep talking without sounding scripted
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): ordinary exchange changes when follow-up becomes real instead of procedural
