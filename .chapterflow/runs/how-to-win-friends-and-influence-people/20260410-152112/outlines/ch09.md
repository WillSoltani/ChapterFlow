Chapter Promise:
Show that people warm quickly when your respect becomes visible enough that they feel genuinely important rather than socially managed.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between admiring someone silently and making that regard legible in a way the person can actually feel.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 -> instant liking promise and visible-regard mechanism
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 -> why importance signals create goodwill faster than self-display
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 -> reject flattery and bridge into argument

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: frame instant liking as the result of visible regard rather than charm theater
- P2: explain why feeling important creates fast warmth
- P3: name importance signals as the chapter's main move
- P4: show why silent respect is weaker than legible respect
- P5: keep the chapter from collapsing into flattery
- P6: bridge into Chapter 10 on arguments and relationship defeat

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Goodwill rises when another person can feel sincere importance, but the chapter fails if the signal sounds inflated, generic, or strategically flattering.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How do you make another person feel important without crossing into exaggeration that makes the respect unbelievable?

Threshold Question:
When does visible appreciation feel like truthful recognition, and when does it become social inflation?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- instant liking comes from visible regard, not performance
- importance signals create warmth faster than self-display
- private admiration is weaker than legible respect
- truthful appreciation must stay concrete
- flattery hollows the chapter out

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): manager chooses whether to acknowledge a colleague's contribution concretely or stay generally pleasant
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): student senate interaction fell flat because appreciation stayed generic
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): one person becomes warmer when the other notices something specific and true
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): onboarding or cross-team meeting improves through visible regard instead of self-presentation
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): student leader wants to show importance without sounding fake
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): ordinary exchange changes when specific respect replaces vague niceness

Preview Constraint:
