Chapter Promise:
Show that names matter because they make another person feel personally recognized rather than generically processed.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between polite interaction and specific recognition.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 -> generic politeness versus personal recognition
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 -> names as a direct signal of regard
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 -> overuse and memory-trick drift rejected; bridge into conversation

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: establish why generic politeness can still feel impersonal
- P2: show what a name changes in the interaction
- P3: connect names to recognition rather than memory display
- P4: show why forgetting names creates more than a technical lapse
- P5: keep the chapter from becoming mnemonic or networking advice
- P6: bridge into conversation and listening

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter works because a name signals specific attention, but it fails when name use starts sounding performative or transactional.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How do you use names in a way that feels like regard rather than like a social tactic?

Threshold Question:
When does naming someone feel personal, and when does it feel deployed?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- people want personal rather than generic handling
- names are a direct recognition signal
- forgetting names creates emotional distance
- names are about regard, not memory tricks
- overuse can sound calculated

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): greeting a new teammate by name instead of staying generic
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): student panel where forgetting a name changed the feel of the exchange
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): family or neighbor conversation feels more direct once the person is specifically addressed
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): service or onboarding interaction shifts when someone is personally remembered
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): student leader wants to use names without sounding forced
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): ordinary exchange changes once generic greetings become specific recognition

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include a name tag, sign-in sheet, lab badge, rehearsal list, text thread, or folded program.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must point toward Chapter 7 by asking what happens when recognition turns into the kind of conversation people want to stay inside.

Genericity Risks:

- overclaim that names alone solve warmth

Forbidden Drift:
