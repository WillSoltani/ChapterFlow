Chapter Promise:
Show that quick, honest admission of fault is often stronger than defensiveness because it removes the fuel that keeps attack alive.

Opening Move:
Open on the strange relief that appears when someone owns a fault before the other person has to force the admission.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 -> title instruction and attack-fuel removal
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 -> why defensiveness prolongs conflict and honest admission shortens it
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 -> keep admission truthful rather than theatrical and bridge into Chapter 13

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: frame admission as strength rather than loss
- P2: show how attack energy falls when fault is openly owned
- P3: name defensive drag as the chapter's main failure mechanism
- P4: explain why honest self-correction restores movement faster than excuse-making
- P5: keep the chapter from collapsing into groveling or false confession
- P6: bridge into Chapter 13 on friendly openings

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Admission works because it removes the other person's need to keep proving the point, but the chapter fails if ownership sounds exaggerated, false, or theatrical.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How do you admit fault fully enough to disarm conflict without turning admission into performance or self-erasure?

Threshold Question:
When does self-defense create more conflict than the original mistake did?

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- admission can be stronger than defense
- attack-fuel removal lowers pressure
- defensive drag lengthens conflict
- truthful ownership differs from groveling
- self-correction can restore cooperation quickly
