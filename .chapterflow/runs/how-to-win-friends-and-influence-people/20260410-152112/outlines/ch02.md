Chapter Promise:
Show why people move more willingly when they feel genuinely appreciated and significant than when they feel pushed, corrected, or used.

Opening Move:
Open on the stark claim that the only lasting way to get someone to do something is to make that person want to do it.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 -> willing action versus pressure
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 -> desire to feel important across ordinary and distorted forms
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 -> Charles Schwab and honest appreciation versus flattery

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: announce the chapter's hard positive claim
- P2: explain why threats and punishments produce weak energy
- P3: widen into the desire for importance
- P4: show how that desire can take healthy or distorted forms
- P5: Charles Schwab as praise-over-fault operator
- P6: separate honest appreciation from flattery and bridge forward

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
People do not move deeply because you want something. They move more willingly when the action connects to their own motives and sense of importance.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How do you recognize and feed the need for importance without becoming manipulative, false, or addicted to using praise as a tool?

Threshold Question:
When is appreciation still honest recognition, and when does it become flattery dressed up as strategy?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- willing action beats pressured compliance
- importance drives more behavior than people admit
- appreciation works because it recognizes real value
- distorted quests for importance show the motive's power
- flattery fails where honest appreciation can still work

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): whether to motivate a teammate through criticism or sincere recognition
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): scholarship or lab feedback where praise changed effort more than pressure did
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): friend or partner feels unseen until something specific is appreciated
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): sales or management request framed around the speaker's wants versus the listener's
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): student leader trying to praise without sounding fake
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): home environment shifts when specific appreciation replaces only-noticed-fault

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include a resume folder, project prototype, sticky note on a monitor, robotics bench, text thread, or dinner table.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what happens when you stop thinking about what you want and begin from what the other person wants.

Genericity Risks:
- generic positivity language
- flattery-sounding praise
- making importance sound like simple vanity

Forbidden Drift:
- no shallow compliment culture language
- no fake neuroscience or HR jargon
- no contamination phrasing
- no brief-language leakage into reader-facing prose
