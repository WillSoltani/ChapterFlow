Chapter Promise:
Show that arguments are usually unwinnable because defeating the point often defeats persuasion, goodwill, or cooperation too.

Opening Move:
Open on the hidden disappointment of proving a point while feeling the relationship tighten instead of open.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 -> title claim and point-victory trap
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 -> why arguments harden resistance instead of changing minds
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 -> preserve standards while rejecting ego escalation and bridge into Chapter 11

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: frame argument as a practical loss even when the facts go your way
- P2: show how point-victory produces relational defeat
- P3: name ego-escalation as the chapter's main failure mechanism
- P4: explain why defeated people rarely become more persuadable
- P5: keep the chapter from collapsing into passivity or fake agreement
- P6: bridge into Chapter 11 on certainty and enemies

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Arguments fail because public defeat hardens identity and resistance, but the chapter fails if restraint sounds like surrendering truth or standards.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How do you refuse the ego logic of argument without drifting into avoidance that leaves real conflict untouched?

Threshold Question:
When does being right stop helping you influence the other person?
