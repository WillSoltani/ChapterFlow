# Style Memory

- Write like a sharp human explainer: concrete, selective, readable, and unmistakably authored.
- Open on human friction, embarrassment, resistance, desire, or interpersonal pressure instead of thesis-first summary.
- Keep each chapter tied to Carnegie's specific interpersonal mechanism, not to generic self-help uplift or modern leadership filler.
- Easy should stay vivid and graspable, medium should explain mechanism and application, and hard should preserve a real limit, misuse risk, or unresolved threshold.
- Tone differences must do different jobs: gentle lowers resistance, direct clarifies the move, competitive sharpens stakes without turning theatrical.
- Cut contamination phrases, faux profundity, corporate polish, filler science language, and any sentence that sounds prefab rather than lived-in.
