Many people try to become interesting by asking others to care about what they themselves already care about. Carnegie's next move reverses that pressure. People become more interested when the conversation enters what already matters to them.

That shift works because attention rises faster around live concern than around imported agenda. When you move toward the other person's preferred world, the conversation gains traction. The other person no longer has to manufacture energy for your topic first. Interest is already waiting on the other side.

The chapter stays stronger when it keeps that move sincere. It is not praising opportunistic mirroring or topic theft. It is praising real engagement with what the other person actually cares about. That is why the move feels social rather than manipulative when it is done well.

This is also why self-topic control can feel heavy. A person may be vivid, informed, or enthusiastic, yet still ask the room to carry too much of that energy. Carnegie is not saying your own interests never matter. He is saying other people's interests are the faster route to their engagement.

That move leads naturally into the next chapter. If you can make another person interested by entering the world that already matters to that person, what happens when that interest becomes so visible that liking begins almost immediately?
