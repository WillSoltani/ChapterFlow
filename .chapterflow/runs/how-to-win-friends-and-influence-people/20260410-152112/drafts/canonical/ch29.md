People do not repair faults well when the task feels crushing. Carnegie treats manageable framing as leverage because hope can keep a person engaged long enough to improve.

That is why this chapter says to make the fault seem easy to correct. The point is not to lie about difficulty. It is to present the change as workable enough that the person does not collapse before the repair even begins.

The chapter becomes weak if ease is treated as denial. Real change can be costly and effortful. The useful version keeps the challenge visible while removing the sense of hopeless impossibility that shuts action down.

This matters because discouragement can become its own barrier. If the person feels doomed, incompetent, or permanently stuck, effort thins out fast. A more manageable frame can preserve willingness to keep trying.

So the chapter is not asking for false comfort. It is asking for credible hope. The fault should feel repairable, not trivial.

This leads naturally into the next chapter. Once action feels possible, the book can close by asking how to make people glad to do what you want.
