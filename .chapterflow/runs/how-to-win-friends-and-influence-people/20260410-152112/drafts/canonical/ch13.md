People often think persuasion begins when the main point is stated. Carnegie asks us to look earlier. The opening tone already changes what kinds of persuasion remain possible.

That is why friendliness matters here. A warm start can keep the other person reachable in ways a sharp or pressured opening cannot. Resistance does not wait for the full argument. It starts forming as soon as the atmosphere tells the other person what kind of encounter this will be.

This is why the chapter treats friendliness as more than decoration. A harsh beginning can close options before the real content even arrives. A friendly beginning can preserve room for agreement, movement, or cooperation that would otherwise narrow too quickly.

The chapter does not praise sugary performance or surface niceness. It asks for genuine warmth strong enough to shape the opening climate without replacing clarity or substance. Friendliness is the condition, not the whole content.
