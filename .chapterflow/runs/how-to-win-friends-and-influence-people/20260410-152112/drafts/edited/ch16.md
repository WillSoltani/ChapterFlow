People often try to get cooperation by increasing pressure. Carnegie points in a different direction. People work with more energy when the action feels partly theirs rather than merely required of them.

That difference matters because compliance can produce movement without producing commitment. Someone may go along under pressure while withholding initiative, care, or imagination. Cooperation is stronger when the person feels some ownership in what is being done.

This is why the chapter is not really about softness. It is about agency. When people feel they are participating rather than being maneuvered, they contribute differently. The work can become more durable because it is not held together only by external force.

The chapter also keeps a necessary warning in view. Ownership can be faked. If the language of participation is only theater wrapped around a fixed demand, people eventually feel the manipulation underneath.

That opens the way to the next chapter. If cooperation depends on real agency, then it matters even more to understand what the other person thinks they are protecting or pursuing.
