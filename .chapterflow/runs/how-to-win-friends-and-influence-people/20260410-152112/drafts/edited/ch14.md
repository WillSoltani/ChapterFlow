People often think agreement matters only after the main issue is fully on the table. Carnegie asks us to notice something earlier. The first few yes-moments can already shape where the conversation is headed.

That matters because momentum hardens quickly. When the exchange begins from real shared ground, the conversation can keep moving in a cooperative direction longer. When it begins from contradiction, resistance often arrives before the harder point is even fully developed.

This is why the chapter treats early agreement as directional rather than decorative. Honest alignment points make it easier for later disagreement to stay inside a shared frame. The move weakens fast, however, if agreement is manufactured instead of real.

The chapter does not praise fake consensus or compliance tricks. It asks for truthful early yes-moments that the other person can actually mean. Agreement works here because it is discovered, not extracted.
