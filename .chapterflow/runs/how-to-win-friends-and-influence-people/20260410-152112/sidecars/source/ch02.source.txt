Chapter 2 frozen-source note:
The chapter's supported core is narrow and strong. Carnegie argues that the most reliable way to move people is to connect with what they already want and, underneath that, with their desire to feel important and appreciated. Threats, punishments, and fault-finding may force temporary compliance, but they do not create willing energy. Honest appreciation does.

Safest supported anchor set:
- the chapter's central claim that the only lasting way to get someone to do something is to make that person want to do it
- the recurring idea that nearly everyone wants to feel important
- Charles Schwab as the cleanest praise-over-fault example
- Rockefeller, Christopher Wren, and other builders as examples of how the need for importance can be routed into creation
- the counterpoint that some people seek importance through criminal notoriety, domination, invalidism, or fantasy
- the chapter's warning that flattery is cheap while honest appreciation has force
