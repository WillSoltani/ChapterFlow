Chapter 1 frozen-source note:
The chapter opens Carnegie's method by attacking the instinct to criticize. The strongest lawful support in the frozen bundle is that Carnegie uses notorious wrongdoers and everyday authority figures to show the same pattern: people justify themselves even when the outside world sees them as clearly wrong. Criticism wounds pride, invites resentment, and rarely produces lasting change by itself.

Safest supported anchor set:
- Two-Gun Crowley as the opening case of a violent man still seeing himself as justified.
- Al Capone and Dutch Schultz as examples of criminals who still framed themselves as benefactors rather than villains.
- Lincoln's unsent rebuke to General Meade after Gettysburg as the chapter's strongest restraint example.
- Bob Hoover's response to a mechanic's fuel mistake as a modern example of refusing to crush someone who already knows he failed.
- "Father Forgets" as an emotional domestic counterweight about constant fault-finding inside family life.
