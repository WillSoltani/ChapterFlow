# Book Skeleton

## Metadata
- Book: Atomic Habits
- Author: James Clear
- Locked edition: 2018 English trade family
- Numbered chapter range: 1-20
- Wave size: 2 by default
- Premium / solo routing candidates: ch02, ch09, ch10, ch18, ch19, ch20

## Rotation Plan For Examples
- Maintain the canonical six-format rotation once per chapter: decision_point, postmortem, dialogue, predict_reveal, dilemma, before_after.
- Maintain 2 work / 2 school / 2 personal examples in every chapter package.
- Rotate endings across broader_principle, self_directed_question, surprising_implication, cross_domain, common_trap, perspective_reframe.

## School-Setting Plan
- Vary school contexts across exam prep, lab habits, practice drills, dorm routines, group projects, club leadership, and teacher feedback loops.
- Keep school scenarios tied to the chapter mechanic rather than generic student productivity talk.

## Vocabulary Watchlist
- Keep atomic habits, 1 percent better, systems, identity, cue, craving, response, reward, friction, environment, tracking, accountability, boredom, and deliberate practice precise and chapter-bound.
- Avoid fake neuroscience, generic willpower sermons, smooth but empty self-help uplift, and unsupported study detail.

## Motif / Callback Opportunities
- ch01 compounding and systems should echo in ch16 tracking and the conclusion without turning into slogan repetition.
- ch02 identity should return in ch16 and ch20 as the reason consistency matters beyond a single result.
- ch03 installs the habit loop and Four Laws; later law chapters should feel like expansions of that engine, not disconnected tips.
- ch06 environment and ch12 friction should callback to each other so law-1 and law-3 feel structurally linked.
- ch18-ch20 should test simplistic readings of the earlier laws by adding talent fit, boredom, review, and deliberate adjustment.

## Chapter Plan
### ch01 The Surprising Power of Atomic Habits
- Section: The Fundamentals: Why Tiny Changes Make a Big Difference
- Intent: Establish that tiny repeated changes compound, that systems matter more than isolated goals, and that habits shape long-run trajectory.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: low
- Thin-chapter risk notes: keep the chapter on compounding and systems, not generic motivation.
- Wave routing: wave 1
