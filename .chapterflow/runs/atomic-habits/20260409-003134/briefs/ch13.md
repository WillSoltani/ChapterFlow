Core Claim:
A habit can be made easier to start by shrinking its opening move to a two-minute version. The chapter argues that reducing the first action to something almost too small to resist helps overcome procrastination and keeps the behavior alive.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the tiny-entry chapter. It should feel distinct from Chapter 12 because the emphasis is no longer on friction in general. The emphasis is on reducing the first step of a desired habit to a nearly effortless starting action.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author narrows least effort into the first two minutes of a habit.
Then the author argues that starting is often the real barrier, so the opening move should be made tiny.
Then the author shows that a small entry action can lead naturally into larger repetitions over time.

Required Anchors:
- use a two-minute version to make the habit easier to start
- the first step should be tiny enough to resist procrastination less
- a small beginning can open the door to larger repetition later

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that tiny starts are the whole habit forever. The narrower claim is that a small opening move lowers resistance and makes consistent starting more likely.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 12 made the path easier; Chapter 13 makes the first move small enough to begin immediately.
Next Chapter Bridge: Chapter 14 can show how easier starts become harder to ignore once cues stay visible and satisfying.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:

- the tiny version is an entry point, not the full standard forever

Moral Complexity:
Medium; the chapter can drift into self-deception if it forgets that the tiny action is meant to open the real habit rather than replace it permanently.
