Core Claim:
Good habits create efficiency, but they can also make behavior mindless. Improvement stalls when automation replaces conscious review.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the anti-autopilot chapter. Chapter 19 explained how to keep a habit motivating through calibrated challenge. Chapter 20 explains why even successful repetition can become a trap if the person stops noticing, reflecting, and refining.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author explains that habits reduce the need for attention and can therefore hide mistakes.
Then the author argues that mastery requires review rather than repetition alone.
Then the author closes by showing that reflection and deliberate adjustment keep good habits from turning into blind automaticity.

Required Anchors:
- automation is useful but can make behavior mindless
- repetition alone does not guarantee mastery
- deliberate review and reflection protect improvement from stagnation

Common Misreadings:

- turning the chapter into a generic anti-routine message
- skipping the role of review and drifting into empty recap

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that every habit needs constant analysis. The narrower claim is that mastery requires periodic review because automaticity can hide stagnation and error.
