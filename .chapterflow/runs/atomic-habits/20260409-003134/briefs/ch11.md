Core Claim:
Progress comes from repetition and continued motion, not from waiting for a flawless version of the habit. The chapter argues that habits improve through practice and accumulated reps, even when early attempts are imperfect.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the reps-and-motion chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 10 because the emphasis is no longer on diagnosing causes. The emphasis is on doing the behavior repeatedly enough for it to improve and stabilize.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from choosing the right response to repeating it.
Then the author argues that motion and practice matter more than early perfection.
Then the author reframes consistency as staying in action long enough for the habit to become easier and cleaner over time.

Required Anchors:
- repetition matters more than waiting for a perfect start
- practice and accumulated reps improve the behavior
- progress comes from continuing to move rather than from demanding ideal execution first

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that quality never matters. The narrower claim is that quality improves after repeated action, and many people stall because they demand polish before practice.
