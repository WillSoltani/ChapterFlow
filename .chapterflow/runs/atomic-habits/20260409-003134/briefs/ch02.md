Core Claim:
Lasting behavior change is easier to sustain when the change reaches identity instead of stopping at outcomes. Habits do not only help you get a result. Repeated actions also become evidence for the kind of person you are becoming. The chapter's main job is to move the reader from outcome chasing toward identity-based repetition.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's identity chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 1 because the focus is no longer delayed results alone. The focus is how repetition builds or reinforces a self-concept, and why that deeper layer makes habits more durable than goal-only change.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author distinguishes outcomes, processes, and identity as different layers of change.
Then the author argues that most people try to change habits for results while neglecting the identity beneath the behavior.
Then the author shows that repeated action becomes evidence for a self-concept, which is why identity-based habits are more stable than temporary goal pursuit.

Required Anchors:
- the three-layer distinction between outcomes, processes, and identity
- identity-based habits as the chapter's core mechanism
- repeated behavior as evidence for a desired self rather than mere wishful thinking

Specific Applications:
- study like the kind of student who prepares before the panic window arrives
- practice reliability at work by keeping one repeatable promise instead of admiring the result
- shift a health or reading goal by acting from the identity you want to reinforce

Common Misreadings:
- treating identity as positive-affirmation language with no repeated behavior behind it
- assuming one good action or one bad day settles identity permanently
- using identity labels as a prison instead of as a direction

Counterarguments or Limits:
Identity language can become manipulative or brittle if it turns into slogans, shame, or rigid self-labeling. The chapter needs to preserve that repeated action is evidence, not instant proof, and that identities should stay revisable rather than trapped by old stories.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 1 argued that systems shape results over time; Chapter 2 asks what those repeated systems are doing to the person who keeps repeating them.
Next Chapter Bridge: if identity change depends on repeated action, the next question is how habits are actually built and repeated in the first place.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- identity makes habits more durable, but Chapter 3 still has to explain the mechanics that let the repetition happen
- self-concept can stabilize change, but later chapters must prevent it from becoming rigid self-mythology

Moral Complexity:
Medium; identity can empower change, but it can also harden excuses, shame, or false certainty if handled carelessly.
