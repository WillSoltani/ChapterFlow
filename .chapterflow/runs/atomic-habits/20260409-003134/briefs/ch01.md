Core Claim:
Small repeated actions matter because they compound over time and steer your trajectory long before the results become obvious. Goals can point you in a direction, but systems determine whether you keep moving there. The chapter's main job is to make the reader respect tiny changes that look unimpressive in the short run but become decisive in the long run.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's compounding-and-systems chapter. The chapter should feel impossible to confuse with later tactic chapters because it is about delayed payoff, direction, and structural trajectory rather than cue design or motivation hacks.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that tiny changes look trivial in the moment but accumulate.
Then the author uses the systems lens to explain why outcomes lag behind underlying habits.
Then the author concludes that winning requires trusting the process of small repeated improvement rather than chasing visible breakthroughs.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

Specific Applications:
- build a study routine by protecting the daily rep instead of waiting for motivation
- improve team output by fixing repeatable process steps rather than chasing one big push
- use small financial or health behaviors to change direction before the scoreboard changes

Common Misreadings:
- treating small habits as motivational fluff rather than structural compounding
- hearing systems over goals as a reason to stop setting direction entirely
- assuming delayed results mean the system is not working

Counterarguments or Limits:
Tiny improvements can become an excuse for vagueness if the system is never specified clearly enough to repeat. Patience is not enough by itself; the repeated action still has to point in the right direction.

Previous Chapter Bridge: the introduction frames change through lived experience; this chapter generalizes why small adjustments can produce big outcomes.
