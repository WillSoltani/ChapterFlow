Core Claim:
New habits start more reliably when the cue is specific, visible, and attached to an existing routine or a precise context. The chapter's main job is to make implementation intentions and habit stacking feel like the practical ignition system for new behaviors.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the cue-design chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 4 because the focus is no longer just noticing cues. The focus is deliberately placing and specifying them so the loop starts cleanly.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that vague intentions fail because they do not create a dependable cue.
Then the author shows that implementation intentions and habit stacking make the start of a habit more specific.
Then the author makes cue design feel like the practical first-law engine for starting new routines.

Required Anchors:
- specific cues beat vague intentions
- implementation intentions as explicit start conditions
- habit stacking as attaching a new habit to an existing routine

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Implementation intentions; habit stacking; cue design; specific start condition; existing routine as anchor.

Specific Applications:
- attach a short work review to a standing calendar event
- begin study prep after a known classroom or after-school transition
- tie a personal health or reading rep to a routine that already happens daily

Common Misreadings:
- turning the chapter into generic consistency advice
- treating a vague plan as if it were an implementation intention
- using habit stacking without a stable anchor routine

Counterarguments or Limits:
Specific cues improve starting odds, but they do not remove all friction or guarantee follow-through by themselves. The chapter must stay on ignition and cue clarity rather than claiming it solves every later barrier.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 4 made cues visible; Chapter 5 shows how to place and specify those cues deliberately.
Next Chapter Bridge: once cues can be placed deliberately, Chapter 6 can widen the frame to environment design.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- cue precision matters, but later chapters still have to address motivation, friction, and reward
- habit stacking needs a stable anchor, not a routine that is chaotic or optional

Moral Complexity:
Low; the challenge is practical specificity, not moral ambiguity.

Concept Budget:
- target concept count: 4
- rationale: implementation intentions, habit stacking, cue specificity, and anchor stability are enough for the chapter's core.

Hard-Depth Minimum:
Preserve the tension that a cue can be specific on paper and still fail if the anchor routine is unstable or the start condition is too fuzzy in practice. Hard depth must keep alive the question of what makes an anchor reliable enough to stack onto.

Unsupported Zones:

- no drifting into full environment design before Chapter 6
- no reducing the chapter to generic planning talk
- no fake psychology about why lists magically work
