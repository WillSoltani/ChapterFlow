Core Claim:
Motivation lasts best when the task is neither too easy nor too hard. People stay engaged when the challenge sits near the edge of their current ability.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the calibrated-challenge chapter. Chapter 18 focused on choosing the right game. Chapter 19 explains how to stay motivated inside that game by keeping the difficulty level in a productive middle zone.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author explains why boredom and discouragement both kill motivation.
Then the author introduces the Goldilocks Rule as the zone of workable challenge.
Then the author argues that habits stay engaging when they remain just difficult enough to demand attention without crushing confidence.

Required Anchors:
- motivation stays stronger when the challenge is just manageable enough
- boredom and overwhelm both weaken repetition
- the ideal zone is slightly beyond current comfort but not far beyond capacity

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Goldilocks Rule; manageable difficulty; boredom; overwhelm; engagement; calibrated challenge.

Specific Applications:
- raise the challenge slightly after the habit becomes too easy
- reduce the challenge when failure becomes demoralizing
- use fit plus calibration rather than intensity alone

Common Misreadings:
- turning the rule into constant novelty seeking
- claiming easy tasks are always bad
- drifting into mastery plateau mechanics before the chapter's own job is done

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that every moment must feel perfectly calibrated. The narrower claim is that long-term motivation improves when the challenge level stays near the edge of the person's current ability.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 18 identified the right game; Chapter 19 explains how to keep that game engaging once fit exists.
Next Chapter Bridge: Chapter 20 can move from motivation and mastery into the downside of automaticity and the danger of mindless repetition.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- fit gives the right arena, but calibration keeps the arena engaging
- too much ease produces boredom, while too much difficulty produces discouragement

Moral Complexity:
Medium; the chapter can drift into optimization fetishism if it forgets that real routines move through imperfect days and uneven difficulty.

Hard-Depth Minimum:
