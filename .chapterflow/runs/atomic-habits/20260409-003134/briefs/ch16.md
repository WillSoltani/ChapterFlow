Core Claim:
Habit tracking and visible evidence can make good habits more satisfying and easier to sustain, but the deeper rule is to avoid letting one miss become a second miss.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the visible-continuity chapter. Chapter 15 explained why immediate satisfaction matters. Chapter 16 shows how tracking, streaks, and visible evidence make that satisfaction repeatable across days while also warning against perfection traps.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows why tracking and visible evidence feel satisfying.
Then the author explains that habits stay alive when misses are repaired quickly.
Then the author argues that the key recovery rule is never miss twice.

Required Anchors:
- tracking can make progress visible and satisfying
- visible evidence helps habits stick
- the important recovery rule is to avoid missing twice in a row

Specific Applications:
- check the habit off right after completion
- use a visible record that makes continuity easy to see
- if a day is missed, repair the chain on the next opportunity instead of treating the lapse as collapse

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as streak worship only
- claiming one missed day ruins the habit
- reducing the chapter to tooling choices instead of visible reinforcement plus recovery

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that tracking is mandatory for every habit or that perfect consistency is the goal. The narrower claim is that visible evidence and quick recovery help habits stay alive.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 15 explained why habits need some immediate satisfaction; Chapter 16 shows how visible tracking can carry that satisfaction forward.
Next Chapter Bridge: Chapter 17 can move from self-tracking into social accountability and outside pressure.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- immediate satisfaction matters in the moment, but visible evidence helps satisfaction persist over time
- one miss is noise; repeated misses change identity and momentum

Medium; the chapter can drift into perfectionism if it forgets that repair matters more than spotless streaks.
