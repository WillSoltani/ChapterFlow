Core Claim:
Habits can be understood as a practical four-step loop: cue, craving, response, and reward. The chapter's job is to make that loop feel like the governing engine behind the rest of the book so later law chapters read as targeted interventions on a shared mechanism rather than as disconnected tips.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the engine chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapters 1 and 2 because the focus is no longer compounding or identity alone. The focus is the repeatable sequence that makes habits happen and the four laws that map directly onto that sequence.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author identifies cue, craving, response, and reward as the recurring structure behind habits.
Then the author explains what job each step performs and why the sequence matters.
Then the author shows that the Four Laws are practical levers on those four steps, which is why the rest of the book can be organized around them.

Required Anchors:
- cue, craving, response, and reward as the four-step loop
- the loop as the governing structure behind the book's later law chapters
- the Four Laws as practical interventions on the loop rather than as free-floating advice

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

Specific Applications:
- redesign a work routine by clarifying the cue and lowering the response friction
- improve study consistency by making the trigger and immediate reward easier to spot
- diagnose a personal habit by finding which step in the loop is weak

Common Misreadings:
- treating the loop as abstract pseudo-science instead of a practical diagnostic tool
- acting as if motivation alone explains craving and reward
- flattening the Four Laws into generic advice without tying them back to the four steps

Counterarguments or Limits:
The loop is a useful simplification, not a claim that every human behavior is mechanically identical. The chapter must stay practical and avoid fake precision. It should show the loop as a strong working model while keeping later chapters responsible for the detailed interventions.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 2 argued that repeated actions build identity; Chapter 3 explains the mechanism that makes repeated action recur.
Next Chapter Bridge: because cue begins the loop, the next chapter can focus on awareness and noticing what has become automatic.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- the loop explains habits broadly, but later chapters still need distinct practical depth
- craving and reward matter, but the chapter should not drift into casual neuroscience or overclaiming

Moral Complexity:
Low; the main risk is confusion or abstraction, not moral endorsement.
