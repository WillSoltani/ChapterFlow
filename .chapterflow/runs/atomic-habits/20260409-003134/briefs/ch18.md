Core Claim:
Habits are easier to sustain and excel at when they fit a person's natural inclinations, abilities, and context. Genes and predispositions matter, but they matter most in shaping where effort compounds best rather than deciding whether change is possible at all.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the fit-and-inclination chapter. The earlier chapters explained how habit loops work broadly. Chapter 18 asks where those loops work best for a given person and why some pursuits feel more aligned than others.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author explains that people differ in natural tendencies and capacities.
Then the author argues that success improves when the habit matches those tendencies.
Then the author clarifies that the point is to choose a suitable game, not to use talent as an excuse to stop improving.

Required Anchors:
- genes or predispositions affect what comes more naturally
- habits stick better when they match the person
- the goal is to choose a field or approach that suits your inclinations

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that talent makes habits effortless or that less-natural areas are worthless. The narrower claim is that fit influences where effort compounds most effectively.
