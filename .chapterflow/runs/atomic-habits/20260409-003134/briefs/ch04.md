Core Claim:
Awareness comes before redesign. Many habits feel invisible because repetition makes them automatic, so change begins when the behavior becomes noticeable enough to inspect. The chapter's main job is to make the reader respect awareness as a practical prerequisite rather than as vague mindfulness language.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the awareness chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 3 because the focus is no longer the full loop alone. The focus is the first law's starting move: noticing cues and automatic behavior that have become too familiar to see clearly.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that automatic behavior can hide in plain sight.
Then the author shows that awareness is necessary because unseen habits cannot be redesigned.
Then the author turns that insight into a practical noticing discipline rather than a generic call for self-reflection.

Required Anchors:
- awareness precedes change
- automatic behavior becomes hard to notice through repetition
- the first law begins by making cues and routines visible enough to inspect

Counterarguments or Limits:
Awareness alone does not fix a habit, and the chapter should not pretend it does. Its job is narrower: make the invisible visible enough that later design moves become possible.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 3 explained the loop; Chapter 4 starts with the first practical requirement for changing it by noticing what already triggers automatic behavior.
Next Chapter Bridge: once cues are visible, Chapter 5 can turn that awareness into deliberate cue design and habit stacking.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:

- awareness helps without turning into soft self-observation detached from action
