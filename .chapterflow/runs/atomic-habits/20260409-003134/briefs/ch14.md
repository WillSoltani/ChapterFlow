Core Claim:
Habits become far more likely when the environment makes the good action almost unavoidable and the bad action unusually difficult. The chapter argues that setup and environment can shift behavior from merely easier to close to inevitable.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the inevitability chapter. It should feel distinct from Chapter 13 because the emphasis is no longer on a tiny entry move alone. The emphasis is on shaping the environment so strongly that the good habit is hard to ignore and the bad one is hard to carry out.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author widens easy starts into full environment setups.
Then the author argues that people repeat what the environment makes obvious and available.
Then the author shows that good habits become more likely when the environment favors them strongly and bad habits become less likely when the environment blocks them.

Required Anchors:
- make good habits more inevitable through setup and availability
- make bad habits harder or nearly impossible through stronger barriers
- environment can reduce reliance on constant self-control

Specific Applications:

Common Misreadings:
- turning inevitability into a fantasy of total control
- acting as if self-control never matters again
- drifting into satisfaction-law material before the chapter's own job is done

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that environment control is perfect. The narrower claim is that stronger setup changes the odds dramatically and reduces how much constant willpower is needed.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 13 made the opening move smaller; Chapter 14 makes the whole setup harder to ignore and easier to obey.
Next Chapter Bridge: Chapter 15 can move from inevitability into how rewards and immediate satisfaction reinforce repetition.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- tiny starts help the habit begin, but full setup helps the habit keep appearing as the obvious next move
- inevitability helps behavior without pretending that control becomes absolute

Moral Complexity:
Medium; the chapter can drift into overcontrol fantasies if it forgets that environment design changes odds rather than guaranteeing perfection.

Concept Budget:
- target concept count: 4
- rationale: inevitability, availability, stronger barrier, and room setup are enough for the chapter core.

Hard-Depth Minimum:
Preserve the tension that environment can do a great deal of work, but no setup removes all choice or all failure. Hard depth must keep alive the difference between shifting odds dramatically and claiming total control.

Unsupported Zones:
- no invented certainty claims or percentages
- no claim that self-control becomes unnecessary forever
- no drift into reward-law mechanics as the main point
- no generic optimization slogans
