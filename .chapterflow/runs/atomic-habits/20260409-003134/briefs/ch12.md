Chapter Title: The Law of Least Effort
Position In Book: second chapter of the 3rd Law section; turns repetition into friction design by showing that habits follow the path of least resistance
Source Sidecar Path: sidecars/source/ch12.source.txt

Core Claim:
People naturally repeat the option that requires less friction. The chapter argues that good habits become more likely when the environment makes them easier, while bad habits weaken when they become harder to perform.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the friction-and-path chapter. It should feel distinct from Chapter 11 because the emphasis is no longer on collecting reps in spite of awkward beginnings. The emphasis is on shaping the path so the desired rep asks for less effort.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author treats least effort as a basic tendency rather than a moral defect.
Then the author shows that the easiest visible path often wins repeated behavior.
Then the author argues that good habit design reduces friction for the desired action and increases friction for the unwanted one.

Required Anchors:
- people tend to follow the path of least resistance
- reducing friction increases the odds that the habit repeats
- making bad habits harder can weaken their default pull

- acting as if all effort is bad
- collapsing into Chapter 13 environment reset mechanics too early

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that ease eliminates discipline completely. The narrower claim is that friction changes repetition odds, and design can help the better choice compete more fairly.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 11 argued that repeated action matters; Chapter 12 asks what path repeated action will most naturally follow.
Next Chapter Bridge: Chapter 13 can move from least effort into environment redesign and reset cues.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- repetition builds familiarity, but friction still shapes what gets repeated most
- ease can help good habits without turning effort itself into the enemy

Moral Complexity:
Medium; the chapter can drift into excuses for comfort if it forgets that least effort is a design principle, not a moral ideal.

- target concept count: 4

Hard-Depth Minimum:
Preserve the tension that humans naturally conserve effort, but healthy design uses that tendency instead of pretending it does not exist. Hard depth must keep alive the question of when convenience serves growth and when it only serves avoidance.

Unsupported Zones:
