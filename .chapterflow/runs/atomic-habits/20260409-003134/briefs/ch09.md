Core Claim:
Habits feel more attractive when they align with the people and groups around us. The chapter argues that family, friends, and culture shape what seems normal, desirable, and worth repeating.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the social-attraction chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 8 because the emphasis is no longer on making a routine attractive through private association alone. The emphasis is on how belonging and imitation make behaviors appealing.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author widens attraction from personal desire to social desire.
Then the author shows that people imitate the habits of the close, the many, and the powerful.
Then the author reframes behavior change as partly a group-selection problem in which the right culture makes the good habit feel normal.

Required Anchors:
- habits become more attractive when they help us belong
- people imitate the habits of close relationships, larger groups, and admired people
- joining or shaping the right environment can make the desired behavior feel more normal and desirable

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Social norms; belonging; imitation; the close, the many, and the powerful; cultural attraction.

Specific Applications:
- study with people who already normalize review and repetition
- join a fitness environment where the default behavior is movement
- choose peers or routines that make the desired habit feel ordinary instead of exceptional

Common Misreadings:
- turning the chapter into total peer determinism
- drifting into identity content from early chapters instead of social imitation here
- flattening the chapter into generic networking advice

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that social influence erases agency. The narrower claim is that behavior becomes easier to repeat when the surrounding people make it feel normal, respected, or expected.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 8 made habits more attractive through private craving and bundling; Chapter 9 asks how attraction is also shaped by groups.
Next Chapter Bridge: Chapter 10 can turn from social attraction to diagnosing and rewiring the causes of bad habits.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- a routine can be privately desirable but socially unsupported
- belonging can strengthen good habits or bad ones depending on the group

Moral Complexity:
Medium; the chapter can drift into fatalism or blame if it is not kept structural.

Concept Budget:
- target concept count: 4
- rationale: belonging, imitation, social norms, and group selection are enough for the chapter's core.

Hard-Depth Minimum:
Preserve the tension that social influence is powerful without making the person passive. Hard depth must keep alive the question of what it means to choose a culture that will keep choosing you back.

Unsupported Zones:
- no invented sociology claims or statistics
- no deterministic language that removes agency
- no drift into Chapter 10 cause-rewiring language
- no vague advice about popularity or charisma
