Core Claim:
Behavior that feels satisfying in the short term is more likely to be repeated, while behavior that feels immediately unpleasant is less likely to stick. The chapter argues that immediate reinforcement matters because the brain favors outcomes it can feel now.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the immediate-satisfaction chapter. It should feel distinct from Chapter 14 because the emphasis is no longer on availability and setup alone. The emphasis is on why habits repeat when they deliver some form of immediate payoff.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author states the cardinal rule that rewarded behavior gets repeated.
Then the author explains why immediate outcomes dominate delayed ones in daily behavior.
Then the author argues that good habits stick better when some form of immediate satisfaction follows them.

Required Anchors:
- satisfying behavior is more likely to be repeated
- immediate outcomes influence behavior more strongly than delayed ones
- good habits need some immediate reinforcement if they are going to stick reliably

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that instant pleasure is the only factor in behavior. The narrower claim is that immediate feedback strongly influences repetition, especially early in habit formation.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 14 made the habit more available; Chapter 15 explains why repeated behavior also needs to feel rewarding now.
Next Chapter Bridge: Chapter 16 can move from immediate reinforcement into how tracking and visible evidence keep habits satisfying.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:

- immediate payoff matters even when the larger benefits arrive much later

Moral Complexity:
Medium; the chapter can drift into simplistic pleasure-maximizing language if it forgets that healthy habits often need designed immediate reinforcement because their biggest rewards are delayed.
