Core Claim:
Habits become easier to sustain when another person or system makes the cost of failure more immediate and visible, because accountability adds consequence to the choice.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the outside-consequence chapter. Chapter 16 focused on self-visible continuity and repair. Chapter 17 shows how another person or agreement can raise the cost of not following through.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author explains why accountability changes behavior.
Then the author shows that habits stick better when the cost of failing is more immediate.
Then the author argues that an accountability partner or contract can help good habits survive weak moments.

Required Anchors:
- accountability increases follow-through
- immediate consequence helps behavior change
- another person or agreement can raise the cost of not acting

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

Specific Applications:
- tell another person what you will do and when
- create a concrete consequence if the habit is skipped
- use outside visibility when private intention keeps failing

Common Misreadings:
- turning accountability into humiliation
- claiming self-direction never works without outside pressure
- drifting into broad social belonging instead of this chapter's consequence logic

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that outside accountability is required for every habit. The narrower claim is that some habits benefit when another person or agreement makes non-compliance more costly in the moment.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 16 made continuity visible to the self; Chapter 17 adds another person or contract that makes lapses harder to ignore.
Next Chapter Bridge: Chapter 18 can move from the habit laws into advanced differences in talent, fit, and specialization.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- self-tracking can support continuity, but outside accountability can strengthen follow-through when private resolve weakens
- immediate reward helps a habit repeat, while immediate cost can help prevent quitting or drifting

Moral Complexity:
Medium; the chapter can drift into coercive or shame-based framing if it forgets that the goal is supportive consequence, not humiliation.
