Core Claim:
A habit becomes more compelling when the cue is linked to desire and anticipation. The chapter argues that craving, not cue visibility alone, is what gives a habit its motivational pull, and it uses temptation bundling and attractive association as practical tools.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the attraction-and-craving chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 7 because the emphasis is no longer on seeing the cue less often. The emphasis is on why certain cues feel appealing enough to pull behavior toward them.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from cue exposure to craving and attraction.
Then the author argues that anticipation helps make habits feel desirable before the action happens.
Then the author shows how temptation bundling can attach a needed behavior to a wanted behavior so the routine becomes more attractive.

Required Anchors:
- cravings are the motivational force behind habits
- anticipation and attractive association help explain why a cue feels hard to ignore
- temptation bundling can make a useful habit more appealing by pairing it with something already desired

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

Specific Applications:

- bundle admin work with a preferred cafe or music ritual
- make a study block feel more attractive by attaching a wanted pre-routine

Common Misreadings:
- turning the chapter into a neuroscience sermon with unsupported claims
- flattening it into pure reward after the habit instead of attraction before action
- confusing temptation bundling with bribing yourself after the fact

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that attraction solves every habit problem. The narrower claim is that cues become more powerful when they point toward a state the person wants, and bundling can help create that pull without eliminating the need for structure.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 7 reduced temptation exposure; Chapter 8 asks why some remaining cues still feel so magnetic.
Next Chapter Bridge: Chapter 9 can widen attraction from personal desire into social and cultural influence.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:

- reduced exposure weakens bad habits while attractive association can strengthen good ones
