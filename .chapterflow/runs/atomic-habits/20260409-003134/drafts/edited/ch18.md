Two people can apply the same habit rules with equal seriousness and still get very different results from the same activity. Chapter 18 begins there. The chapter argues that habits compound best when they fit the person's natural inclinations, abilities, and context. The goal is not to worship talent. The goal is to understand where effort has the best chance to keep paying off.

That is why predisposition matters in this chapter. Some tasks feel more natural, more repeatable, or more engaging to one person than to another. The chapter does not claim that genes decide everything. It claims something narrower and more useful: different people often find it easier to sustain and improve in different kinds of games.

This changes how the reader should understand struggle. A mismatch does not prove the habit laws failed, and it does not prove the person lacks discipline. Sometimes the problem is that the chosen habit or arena does not fit the person's strengths, style, or circumstances very well. In that case, more force alone may not be the smartest answer.

That is where choosing the right game matters. A person may need to select a version of the task, an environment, or a role that suits them better. The chapter becomes strongest when it treats this as strategic fit rather than as surrender. The reader is not being told to quit growth. The reader is being told to stop confusing bad fit with personal deficiency.

The limit matters because this chapter can easily drift into fatalism. Talent is not destiny here. Inclination is guidance. The useful claim is that effort compounds more effectively when it is pointed at a field that matches the person, not that less-natural areas are hopeless or worthless.

That is why Chapter 18 follows the habit laws so well. The earlier chapters explained how to make a habit obvious, attractive, easy, and satisfying. Chapter 18 asks where those laws are most likely to produce exceptional results for a particular person. The book is now less concerned with building any habit at all and more concerned with building the right one.

That shift leads directly to Chapter 19. Once the person has found a better-fitting game, the next problem is how to stay challenged enough to remain motivated instead of sliding into boredom or discouragement.
