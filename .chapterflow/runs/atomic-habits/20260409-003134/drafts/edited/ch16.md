The satisfaction of a habit grows stronger when progress stops being invisible. Chapter 16 begins there. A person can finish the routine, feel a small payoff, and then lose that feeling by the next day unless some visible record carries it forward. The chapter argues that tracking matters because it gives the habit a sign that can be seen again.

That is why visible evidence is so powerful. A mark on the calendar, a checked card, a line on the board, or some other simple record can turn repetition into something concrete. The person does not have to guess whether the habit is real. The evidence is sitting there. That visible continuity can make the routine feel satisfying in a way that survives beyond the moment of completion.

The chapter stays strongest when it keeps the claim narrow. Tracking is not magic, and it is not mandatory for every behavior. The deeper point is that habits stick more easily when the person can see proof that the effort happened. What is visible becomes easier to value, easier to remember, and easier to continue.

This is where the streak becomes useful and dangerous at the same time. A visible chain can motivate the next repetition because breaking the line feels costly. But the chapter refuses to make perfection the goal. One missed day is not the real problem. The larger danger is letting one miss turn into a second miss, because that is when the pattern begins to change.

That recovery rule is the practical heart of the chapter. Never miss twice. A lapse does not need to become a collapse. The person does not win by protecting a flawless record forever. The person wins by repairing the habit at the next reasonable opportunity so the identity and momentum stay intact.

That limit matters because visible evidence can become brittle if the reader turns it into a test of purity. The chapter is not asking for streak worship. It is asking for continuity plus repair. The tracker helps because it shows the chain, but it also helps because it makes the break visible early enough to fix.

That is why Chapter 16 follows Chapter 15 so naturally. Immediate satisfaction gets the routine to pay something back today. Tracking lets that satisfaction stay visible tomorrow. Once that visible continuity exists, Chapter 17 can ask what changes when another person starts reinforcing the chain too.
