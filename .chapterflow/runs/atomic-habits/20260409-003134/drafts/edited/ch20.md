A habit can be working well and still start hiding problems. Chapter 20 begins there. The chapter argues that automaticity is powerful because it lowers friction, but that same efficiency can create blindness if the person stops paying attention to how the routine is actually going.

That is the downside of creating good habits. Repetition makes behavior easier, faster, and more stable. Yet the very ease that makes a habit valuable can also make it mindless. A person may keep performing the routine while missing drift, errors, or signs that improvement has stalled.

This changes how the reader should think about mastery. Repetition alone is not the same as refinement. A habit can remain consistent without becoming better. The chapter becomes strongest when it separates automatic performance from deliberate practice and says that excellence still requires moments of conscious review.

That is why reflection matters here. A person may need to pause, inspect the routine, measure results, or notice where small mistakes have become invisible through familiarity. The point is not to overanalyze every repetition. The point is to re-enter awareness often enough that the habit stays sharp instead of hardening into stale competence.

The limit matters because this chapter is not attacking habits themselves. Good habits still save time, energy, and decision-making. The narrower claim is that automaticity is a tool, not a final stage. Once a routine becomes easy to run, the person still needs periodic review so the process does not quietly flatten into mindless repetition.

That is why Chapter 20 follows Chapter 19 so well. Chapter 19 explained how to keep a habit motivating through calibrated challenge. Chapter 20 explains why even a well-calibrated routine still needs reflection if the person wants mastery rather than mere consistency. The book closes the numbered chapters by pairing strong habits with conscious refinement.

That close matters because the four laws were never supposed to produce sleepwalking. They were supposed to create a system that works. Chapter 20 adds the final warning: once the system runs smoothly, the person must still step back, notice what the system is producing, and refine it on purpose.
