Chapter 8 source focus: cues become powerful not only when they are visible, but when they are linked to craving, anticipation, and attractive association.

- Official support is strongest for the cue -> craving -> response -> reward loop and for the broader claim that habits need motivational pull, not just exposure.
- Secondary support can reinforce that anticipation helps make a cue feel desirable and that temptation bundling can attach a wanted behavior to a needed behavior.
- Keep the claims narrow: the chapter is about attraction and craving inside habit formation, not about unsupported neuroscience spectacle.
- The hard edge is interpretive. A cue can be obvious and still weak if it is not tied to a desired state.
- Avoid drifting into Chapter 9 social norms or into later reward mechanics as the main explanation here.
