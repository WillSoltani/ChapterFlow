Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that strong self-control often comes from encountering tempting cues less often rather than resisting them heroically every time.

Opening Move:
Open on someone who keeps assuming they have a willpower problem when the real issue is that the tempting cue keeps appearing in front of them all day.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3 for self-control as reduced exposure
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for visible temptation cues keeping bad habits active
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for cue removal and avoidance as practical self-control

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: challenge the myth of heroic constant resistance
- P2: explain why repeated cue exposure keeps the bad habit alive
- P3: show how fewer tempting situations changes the fight
- P4: connect cue removal to practical self-control rather than moral weakness
- P5: show how avoidance and removal support consistent behavior
- P6: preserve the limit that agency still exists even when structure helps
- P7 if needed: bridge from first-law completion to attraction in Chapter 8

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes self-control as repeated late-stage resistance from self-control as designing life so the cue appears less often in the first place.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Reducing temptation exposure can look less heroic than resisting it, but it is often more reliable. The chapter should keep that tension alive without turning avoidance into cowardice or into a total denial of willpower.

Hard Test:
What does it mean to call someone disciplined if they win partly by encountering the cue less often?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- apparent self-control often reflects lower temptation exposure
- visible tempting cues reactivate bad habits
- cue removal can beat repeated resistance
- structural avoidance is not moral weakness
- agency remains even when the environment helps

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): remove distracting tabs and notifications from first-view work setup
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): snack habit stays active because the locker route passes the same vending cue
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): evening scrolling eases once the phone leaves the bedside table
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): calendar drift was not low discipline but repeated cue exposure
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choose a quieter library table away from tempting social cues
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): sweets stop dominating once they leave the visible kitchen path

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include object-level details such as browser tabs, a locker corner, a bedside table, a vending machine glow, a library aisle, or a kitchen shelf.

Preview Constraint:
The preview should point toward the next law by asking what happens after cues and temptations are set, but desire and attraction still have to be explained.
