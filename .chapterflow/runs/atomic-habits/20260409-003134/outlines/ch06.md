Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that environment design often determines which cues become visible enough to drive behavior before motivation has to do heavy lifting.

Opening Move:
Open on a person with sincere motivation whose habit still loses because the desired object or cue is buried, distant, or invisible in the room.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3 for environment shaping attention
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for visible and nearby cues
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for redesigning spaces instead of relying on willpower

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show motivation failing inside a badly arranged environment
- P2: explain that what is obvious in a room often gets acted on first
- P3: show how visible and nearby objects become stronger cues
- P4: connect cue placement to behavior design rather than decoration
- P5: show how environment redesign supports good habits and weakens bad ones
- P6: preserve the limit that environment helps but does not erase agency
- P7 if needed: bridge to self-control as reduced temptation exposure

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes inner intention from outer visibility. A desired action can be genuinely valued and still lose when its cue is buried while the competing cue is obvious and near.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Environment design changes what gets noticed first, but it should not be overstated into a claim that motivation is useless or that people are mechanically controlled by rooms.

Hard Test:
When does a space quietly steer behavior more strongly than a person's stated intention?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- visible surroundings shape cue exposure
- nearby objects become easier default actions
- environment design can support good habits without heroic effort
- hidden or distant cues weaken good intentions
- environment changes exposure, not human agency itself

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): move a project note and water bottle into first-view position on a desk
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): study habit failed because the needed notebook stayed buried in a backpack
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): guitar practice improves once the instrument leaves the closet and enters the living room
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): missed stretch break was less about discipline than about an invisible cue
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choose between a cluttered dorm desk and a prepared visible study setup
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): snacking changes once the fruit bowl is visible and the sweets leave the counter

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include object-level details such as a monitor edge sticky note, a backpack pocket, a guitar stand, a dorm desk lamp, a locker shelf, a fruit bowl, or a counter jar.

Preview Constraint:
The preview should ask what happens when the best version of self-control is not resisting temptation but seeing less of it.
