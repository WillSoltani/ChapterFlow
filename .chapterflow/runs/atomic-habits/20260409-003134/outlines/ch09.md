Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that habits become more attractive when they align with the people, norms, and groups around them.

Opening Move:
Open on someone who finds a behavior easier to repeat once it becomes normal inside a group they care about.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3 for belonging and social attraction
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for imitation of the close, the many, and the powerful
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for choosing a culture that makes the desired habit feel normal

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: widen attraction from private craving to social belonging
- P2: explain why people imitate group behavior
- P3: introduce the close, the many, and the powerful
- P4: show how normality changes what feels attractive
- P5: connect habit change to choosing peers and environments
- P6: preserve the limit that agency still exists
- P7 if needed: bridge into Chapter 10 diagnosis of bad habits

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes private attraction from social attraction. A behavior can feel desirable because it fits who is around us, not only because of the behavior itself.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Belonging makes habits easier, but choosing the wrong group can strengthen the wrong routine. The chapter should keep the tension between social pull and individual agency alive.

Hard Test:
What changes when the desired habit stops feeling exceptional and starts feeling normal inside the right group?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- belonging increases attraction
- we imitate the close, the many, and the powerful
- normality can make a habit feel easier to adopt
- selecting the right group changes what feels natural
- agency remains inside social influence

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): Mason joins a team routine where weekly review is standard
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): Anika realizes her club hallway routine keeps reinforcing the wrong norm
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): Jules makes exercise easier by training with friends who already normalize it
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): Troy sees the real change came from group expectation, not motivation alone
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): Selah chooses between a distracted review table and one where repetition is normal
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): Ibrahim changes reading consistency by joining a circle where reading is ordinary

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include object-level details such as a review table, team check-in, club hallway, gym class slot, shared tracker, or book circle notes.

Preview Constraint:
The preview should point toward Chapter 10 by asking how to diagnose the real cause of bad habits once attraction and norms are visible.
