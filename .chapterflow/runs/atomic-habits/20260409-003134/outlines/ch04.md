Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that invisible automatic behavior must first become noticeable before any cue redesign or first-law intervention can work.

Opening Move:
Open on a behavior that is already underway before the person fully notices it, then show why that invisibility is the real obstacle.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3 for automatic behavior hiding in plain sight
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for awareness as the condition for change
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for the first law beginning with visible cues

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: make an automatic behavior feel invisible until it is already happening
- P2: explain why repetition hides behavior inside familiarity
- P3: argue that unnoticed habits cannot be redesigned
- P4: keep awareness practical rather than spiritualized
- P5: tie the chapter back to cues and the first law
- P6: show how noticing changes diagnosis
- P7 if needed: bridge to deliberate cue design in Chapter 5

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes vague self-observation from concrete noticing of automatic cues and routines.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Automatic habits are hard to interrupt because the cue and first movement often become visible only after the routine has already started.

Hard Test:
How do you change a behavior that usually becomes noticeable only once you are already inside it?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- automatic behavior and invisibility
- awareness before redesign
- noticing cues as first-law groundwork
- awareness as diagnosis rather than self-judgment
- late noticing as the real obstacle

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): catch an autopilot pre-meeting routine before it runs
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): hallway distraction pattern noticed too late

Preview Constraint:
The preview should ask what to do once the cue is finally visible.
