Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that habits tend to follow the path of least resistance and that friction design changes which behaviors repeat most easily.

Opening Move:
Open on someone who keeps intending the better habit but repeatedly follows the easier nearby path instead.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for least resistance as a real behavioral tendency
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for reducing friction on the desired behavior
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for increasing friction on the unwanted behavior

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: shift from repeated reps to the path those reps naturally follow
- P2: explain why least effort is a default tendency
- P3: show how friction affects whether a good habit repeats
- P4: show how convenience and placement shape default behavior
- P5: show how adding barriers weakens a bad habit
- P6: preserve the limit that ease is a design principle, not a full substitute for discipline
- P7 if needed: bridge into Chapter 13 and environment reset logic

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes moral desire from friction path: people may want the better habit, yet still repeat the easier path if the environment keeps it simpler.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Ease can support growth, but the chapter must not become praise for comfort in every form. The real issue is which form of ease serves the desired behavior.

Hard Test:
What changes when the desired habit becomes the path with less resistance and the unwanted habit becomes the path with more resistance?
