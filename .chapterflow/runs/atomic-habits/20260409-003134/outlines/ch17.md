Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand how outside accountability and immediate consequence can strengthen follow-through.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between a private promise and a promise another person can see, then show why immediate social consequence changes the decision.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for accountability and follow-through
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for immediate consequence
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for partners, agreements, and contracts

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: shift from self-visible continuity into outside visibility
- P2: explain why accountability changes behavior
- P3: show why immediate consequence matters
- P4: show what another person or contract changes in weak moments
- P5: preserve the limit against humiliation or dependency
- P6: clarify that support plus consequence is the useful mix
- P7 if needed: bridge into Chapter 18

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes supportive accountability from shame and argues that follow-through improves when skipping the habit has a more immediate cost.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Outside accountability can strengthen discipline, but it should not become coercion, humiliation, or total dependence.

Hard Test:
What changes when failure stops being private and starts carrying an immediate consequence the person would rather avoid?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- accountability strengthens follow-through
- immediate consequence changes the choice
- outside visibility can protect a weak moment
- contracts or partners can raise the cost of skipping
- the chapter works best when accountability supports rather than shames
