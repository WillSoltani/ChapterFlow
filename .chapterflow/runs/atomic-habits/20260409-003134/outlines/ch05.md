Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that specific cues and habit stacking start new habits more reliably than vague intentions.

Opening Move:
Open on a person who sincerely plans a new habit but never gives it a clear starting condition.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3 for vague intentions versus specific cues
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for implementation intentions
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for habit stacking and anchor routines

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show a sincere plan failing because it has no clear start
- P2: explain why specificity matters at the cue level
- P3: define implementation intentions as explicit start conditions
- P4: show how implementation intentions reduce ambiguity
- P5: define habit stacking as attaching a new habit to an existing routine
- P6: preserve the limit that the anchor has to be stable enough to carry the new habit
- P7 if needed: bridge to broader environment design

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes vague good intentions from a habit that has a specific cue or a stable routine anchor.

Hard-Depth Tension:
A cue can look precise on paper and still fail in practice if the anchor routine is unstable or the trigger is not actually distinctive enough to fire consistently.

Hard Test:
What makes an existing routine stable enough to serve as a real anchor instead of just another vague hope?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- vague intentions fail at cue level
- implementation intentions create explicit starts
- habit stacking uses existing routines as anchors
- anchor stability matters
- cue design as ignition rather than full habit success

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): attach a short review habit to a known meeting close
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): after-school study plan failed because the start cue stayed fuzzy
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): reading habit improves once it stacks after making tea
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): missed admin habit was a cue problem, not an effort problem
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choose a stable hallway or desk anchor for stretching or prep
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): medication or journaling starts once it stacks after brushing teeth

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include object-level details such as a calendar pop-up, a mug by the kettle, a desk lamp, a gym badge swipe, a toothbrush cup, or a notebook left open.

Preview Constraint:
The preview should ask what happens when the wider environment either supports or undermines the cue.
