Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that environment setup can make good habits hard to ignore and bad habits hard to carry out.

Opening Move:
Open on someone whose room, desk, or routine now presents the good habit as the obvious next action and blocks the bad one more strongly than before.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for good habits becoming more inevitable through setup
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for availability and pre-staging the desired behavior
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for stronger barriers that make bad habits unusually difficult

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: shift from tiny first move to whole setup and availability
- P2: explain why environment can make behavior feel inevitable
- P3: show how pre-staging the good habit increases repetition odds
- P4: show how visibility and room design keep the good path obvious
- P5: show how stronger barriers make bad habits hard to perform
- P6: preserve the limit that inevitability changes odds, not perfection
- P7 if needed: bridge into Chapter 15 and satisfaction/reward logic

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes a merely easier habit from one that the environment keeps presenting so strongly that it becomes difficult to ignore.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Environment can do a lot of work, but the chapter must not claim total control. The real issue is dramatic odds-shifting, not perfection.

Hard Test:
What changes when the good habit is already waiting in the room and the bad habit is no longer readily available?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- good habits become more inevitable through setup
- availability and readiness shape repeated behavior
- stronger barriers can make bad habits feel almost impossible in the moment
- environment can reduce reliance on constant self-control
- inevitability changes odds without guaranteeing perfection
