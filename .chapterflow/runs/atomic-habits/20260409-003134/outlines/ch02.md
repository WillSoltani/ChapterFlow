Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that habits do not only produce results. They also produce evidence about identity, which is why identity-based change lasts longer than outcome-only motivation.

Opening Move:
Open on the friction of trying to act differently while still privately describing yourself as the old kind of person.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P2-P3 for outcomes, processes, and identity as layered change
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for identity-based habits as the chapter's main mechanism
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P7 for repeated action as evidence that can build or reinforce a self-concept

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: dramatize the mismatch between a new goal and an unchanged self-story
- P2: distinguish outcomes, processes, and identity cleanly
- P3: explain why most people stop at outcomes and why identity goes deeper
- P4: show how repeated action becomes evidence for a self-concept
- P5: argue that identity-based change lasts because it survives after the goal event
- P6: show how old labels can quietly defend bad habits
- P7: preserve the limit that identity is evidence through repetition, not a verdict from one action
- P8 if needed: bridge to the habit-loop mechanics in Chapter 3

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes changing what you get, changing what you do, and changing what you believe yourself to be.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Identity can stabilize change, but it can also trap you if you turn a temporary pattern or old label into a fixed story about who you are.

Hard Test:
How do you use identity as a guide for repeated action without mistaking a few actions, or a few failures, for a final verdict on who you are?

Takeaway Count Lock:

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- the outcomes / processes / identity stack
- identity-based habits versus outcome-only habits
- repeated action as evidence
- old labels reinforcing old behavior
- identity as direction rather than prison

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choose an identity standard over a short-term output excuse
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): lab performance explained by a self-story rather than by revisable practice
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): "not a morning person" identity challenged by a small repeated rep
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): documentation habit changes once the person acts like a reliable operator
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): debate-practice choice between protecting ego and collecting evidence
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): reading habit becomes easier when the person stops saying "I'm trying" and starts acting from identity

Scenario Vividness Targets:

Preview Constraint:
The preview should ask what kind of mechanism can repeatedly turn actions into identity evidence.
