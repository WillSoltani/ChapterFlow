Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that cues become harder to ignore when they are linked to craving, anticipation, and attractive association rather than visibility alone.

Opening Move:
Open on someone who can see the cue for a good habit but still does not feel pulled toward it until the routine is linked to something they genuinely want.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3 for craving as the motivational force behind habits
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for anticipation and attractive association
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for temptation bundling as a practical method

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: shift from cue visibility to attraction and desire
- P2: explain why craving gives cues motivational force
- P3: show how anticipation makes a habit feel appealing before action
- P4: introduce temptation bundling as the practical method
- P5: show how pairing want-to-do and need-to-do changes follow-through
- P6: preserve limits on overclaiming desire engineering
- P7 if needed: bridge into social shaping in Chapter 9

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes noticing a cue from wanting what the cue promises. Attraction, not visibility alone, creates the pull.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Habits become easier when they feel appealing, but attraction cannot be treated as a magical override. The chapter should hold the tension between designing desire and respecting the limits of motivational control.

Hard Test:
What changes when a cue is obvious but still not attractive enough to compete with the alternatives?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- craving provides the motivational pull behind habits
- anticipation can make a behavior attractive before action
- attraction can be shaped through association
- temptation bundling pairs a wanted behavior with a needed behavior
- attraction helps but does not replace structure

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): Emma pairs a report block with a favorite cafe playlist ritual
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): Diego sees that the cafeteria detour feels stronger because of the social reward attached to it
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): Lila makes exercise more attractive by saving a favorite podcast for walks
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): Carter's admin drift changes once the task is bundled with a wanted ritual
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): Hazel chooses between a sterile study block and one made attractive by a repeatable pre-routine
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): Ravi changes a reading habit by pairing it with a desired evening setup

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include object-level details such as earbuds, a cafe playlist, a cafeteria line, a study lounge table, a walking route, or a reading lamp.

Preview Constraint:
The preview should point toward Chapter 9 by asking how attraction is also shaped by the people and groups around us.
