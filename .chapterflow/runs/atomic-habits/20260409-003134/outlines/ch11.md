Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that habits improve through repeated action and that many people stall by waiting for polished performance before enough reps exist.

Opening Move:
Open on someone who keeps preparing, refining, or planning instead of doing enough real repetitions for the habit to improve.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3 for repetition over perfection
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for motion versus action and the need for reps
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for continued forward movement as the path to better execution

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: shift from diagnosis to real repetitions
- P2: explain why waiting for perfect execution delays improvement
- P3: distinguish motion from action
- P4: show how reps improve the habit over time
- P5: connect consistency to forward movement rather than polish
- P6: preserve the limit that quality still matters eventually
- P7 if needed: bridge into Chapter 12 and least effort

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes planning-like motion from actual repetitions that teach the habit.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Quality matters, but demanding quality too early can block practice. The chapter should keep the tension between craftsmanship and needless delay alive.

Hard Test:
What changes once the person stops trying to impress themselves with preparation and starts collecting real reps?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- repetition matters more than perfect starts
- motion is not the same as action
- reps teach the habit
- forward movement beats stalled polishing
- quality improves after enough action

- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): Micah learns the team improved because they shipped repetitions, not because they planned endlessly

Preview Constraint:
