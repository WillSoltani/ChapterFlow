## Chapter Order and Intents

| # | Title | One-Line Intent |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sort Out the Three Conversations | Convert vague "this went badly" into a precise three-part diagnosis the reader can apply immediately |
| 2 | Stop Arguing About Who's Right | Replace the message-delivery stance with a learning stance as the non-negotiable precondition for everything that follows |
| 3 | Don't Assume They Meant It | Disentangle intent from impact so the reader can take responsibility for impact without collapsing into defense |
| 4 | Abandon Blame | Replace blame with contribution-mapping to surface the system producing the problem rather than finding a villain |
| 5 | Have Your Feelings (Or They Will Have You) | Show that suppressing feelings doesn't eliminate them — it ventriloquizes them into the rest of the conversation |
| 6 | Ground Your Identity | Teach identity-quake preparation: build a self-image complex enough to absorb feedback without collapsing |
| 7 | What's Your Purpose? When to Raise It, When to Let Go | Screen purposes before having the conversation, and teach when letting go is the skilled move |
| 8 | Begin from the Third Story | Design the specific opener that invites joint inquiry without triggering defenses in the first thirty seconds |
| 9 | Listen from the Inside Out | Locate the listening problem inside the listener's head, not in their behavior |
| 10 | Speak for Yourself with Clarity and Power | Express the honest, owned version of experience — observation, response, story — without retreating under pressure |
| 11 | Problem-Solving: Take the Lead | Move from understanding and expression into joint inquiry for specific solutions |
| 12 | Putting It All Together | Integrate the full framework through a complete-conversation walkthrough, plus supplement extensions |

## Per-Chapter Dossier Notes

### Ch 1 — Sort Out the Three Conversations
- **Source richness:** High — the three-conversation model is the book's core claim, well-developed in source
- **Concept density:** 3 (three conversation types only; no skill moves)
- **Moral complexity flag:** None — this is purely diagnostic
- **Thin-chapter risk:** None
- **Premium routing:** YES — conceptually dense, foundational, must be S-tier for the rest of the book to work
- **Hard-depth minimum:** Identity conversation as the quietest and most reliably misread. Threshold question: surfacing it too bluntly — does naming help or entrench?
- **Callback opportunity:** Every subsequent chapter loops back to the three-conversation frame
- **Unsupported zones:** Specific resolution skills (ch8-11), contribution mapping (ch4), feelings moves (ch5)

### Ch 2 — Stop Arguing About Who's Right
- **Source richness:** High — message-delivery vs. learning stance is fully developed
- **Concept density:** 2 (two stances, one shift)
- **Moral complexity flag:** Mild — the learning stance can be misread as "give up your view"
- **Thin-chapter risk:** Low — the mechanism is abstract but has good exemplification
- **Hard-depth minimum:** Learning stance performed without being genuine — how the other person can tell, and what it produces
- **Callback:** Builds directly on Ch1's "what happened?" conversation layer

### Ch 3 — Don't Assume They Meant It
- **Source richness:** High — two failure modes well-defined
- **Concept density:** 3 (inventing intentions / good intentions / impact responsibility)
- **Moral complexity flag:** YES — taking impact responsibility without self-blame is genuinely hard and can misfire
- **Thin-chapter risk:** None
- **Hard-depth minimum:** What happens when the other person's intent genuinely WAS malicious — does the disentangle move still apply?
- **Callback:** Builds on Ch2 learning stance; feeds into Ch4 contribution

### Ch 4 — Abandon Blame
- **Source richness:** High — four contribution types named
- **Concept density:** 4 (blame vs. contribution, 4 contribution types)
- **Moral complexity flag:** YES — "naming your contribution" can be heard as self-blame performance
- **Thin-chapter risk:** None
- **Hard-depth minimum:** When contributions are genuinely unequal — contribution-mapping with a 90/10 split where naming 10% feels like blaming the victim
- **Callback:** Builds on Ch3 intent/impact; composition of avoiding-until-now contribution is a callback to Ch2

### Ch 5 — Have Your Feelings (Or They Will Have You)
- **Source richness:** High — five specific moves named
- **Concept density:** 4 (suppression failure, find/negotiate/don't-let-drive/share/acknowledge)
- **Moral complexity flag:** YES — "sharing feelings" in professional contexts has real costs that the book doesn't fully address
- **Thin-chapter risk:** None
- **Hard-depth minimum:** When the other person is not equipped or willing to receive your feelings — is sharing still the right move?
- **Callback:** Feelings layer from Ch1; suppression connects to identity conversation (Ch6)

### Ch 6 — Ground Your Identity
- **Source richness:** High — identity quake, three all-or-nothing beliefs, AND/BOTH fully developed
- **Concept density:** 3 (identity quake, all-or-nothing beliefs, AND/BOTH shift)
- **Moral complexity flag:** YES — identity grounding can become indifference to legitimate feedback
- **Thin-chapter risk:** None
- **Premium routing:** YES — hardest internal move in the book, premium treatment earns its place
- **Hard-depth minimum:** AND/BOTH as the move vs. AND/BOTH as emotional anesthesia — when does grounding suppress the real signal?
- **Callback:** Identity conversation layer from Ch1; intersection with feelings conversation (Ch5)

### Ch 7 — What's Your Purpose?
- **Source richness:** Medium — three legitimate purposes, three warning signs, when-to-let-go
- **Concept density:** 3 (legitimate purposes, warning signs, let-go decision)
- **Moral complexity flag:** YES — "when to let go" can be misread as cowardice or as silencing
- **Thin-chapter risk:** YES — source is relatively short; keep concept budget tight
- **Hard-depth minimum:** The cost of always raising — over-processing, relationship fatigue, using the framework as a weapon disguised as openness
- **Callback:** Connects all three conversations from Ch1 to purpose screening; transition chapter between internal and external skills

### Ch 8 — Begin from the Third Story
- **Source richness:** Medium — third-story principle and benefits clear, but specific mechanics are relatively spare
- **Concept density:** 2 (third-story vs. first-story opener, three benefits)
- **Moral complexity flag:** None
- **Thin-chapter risk:** YES — one main move; execution requires care to avoid padding
- **Hard-depth minimum:** What the third-story opener cannot fix — when both parties need the conversation to begin from their own story, or when the power gap makes "joint inquiry" feel condescending
- **Callback:** Builds on Ch7 purpose; third story requires the learning stance from Ch2

### Ch 9 — Listen from the Inside Out
- **Source richness:** High — internal voice problem, three listening moves
- **Concept density:** 3 (internal voice, quieting it, three external moves)
- **Moral complexity flag:** None
- **Thin-chapter risk:** None
- **Hard-depth minimum:** The paraphrasing move backfiring — when accurate reflection reads as sarcasm or as therapeutic performance
- **Callback:** Learning stance from Ch2; feelings acknowledgment connects to Ch5

### Ch 10 — Speak for Yourself
- **Source richness:** High — three-part form, say-hard-thing-first, don't-take-it-back
- **Concept density:** 3 (observation/response/story, honesty calibration, holding the point)
- **Moral complexity flag:** YES — not taking it back under pressure can be stubbornness
- **Thin-chapter risk:** None
- **Hard-depth minimum:** When offering your story as a story still triggers — the other person reads "I was telling a story that..." as accusation-in-disguise
- **Callback:** Builds on Ch9 listening; composition with third-story opener from Ch8

### Ch 11 — Problem-Solving: Take the Lead
- **Source richness:** High — four specific moves
- **Concept density:** 4 (joint inquiry, invent options, mutual standards, specific commitments)
- **Moral complexity flag:** None
- **Thin-chapter risk:** None
- **Hard-depth minimum:** When joint inquiry and specific commitments are used to extract a commitment from someone who has no real power to keep it
- **Callback:** Integrates all expression/listening from Ch9-10; problem-solving is distinct from the conversation that preceded it

### Ch 12 — Putting It All Together
- **Source richness:** High — integration walkthrough + supplement material
- **Concept density:** 5 (full composition, five supplement extensions folded in)
- **Moral complexity flag:** YES — "meta-openness" as the real skill is more demanding than any specific technique
- **Thin-chapter risk:** None
- **Premium routing:** YES — closing chapter, integration premium
- **Hard-depth minimum:** The framework failing — when the other person will not engage, when the power gap makes all of this academic, what "success" even means when you did everything right
- **Callback:** All chapters
