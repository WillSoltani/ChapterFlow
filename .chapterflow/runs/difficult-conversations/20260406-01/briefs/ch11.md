Core Claim:
Once both people have heard each other and each has spoken for themselves cleanly, the conversation can turn toward what to do. The skilled move is not to announce a solution or wait passively for one to emerge. It is to take the lead on problem-solving jointly. That means proposing a joint inquiry into solutions rather than a solution, inventing several options before judging any one, looking for a standard both people would accept in advance, building on the other person's proposals instead of attacking them, and ending with specific commitments rather than vague optimism. The chapter's central discipline is shared design. A plan imposed by one party usually gets surface agreement and quiet sabotage. A conversation that never shifts into design produces relief without change.

---

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the only chapter in the sequence that turns understanding into action. Ch8 opened the conversation, Ch9 made listening real, and Ch10 made honest expression possible. Ch11 asks what happens after all that work has been done well enough. Its risk is being mistaken for generic decision-making advice or a project-management module. That would flatten the chapter. The book's point is narrower: problem-solving inside a difficult conversation has its own failure modes. The first is drift: no one takes the lead and nothing changes. The second is unilateral control: one person prescribes the answer and calls that collaboration. The chapter's harder edge is about false commitments. Joint inquiry and specific next steps can become coercive when one person is pushed to promise something they do not actually control.

---

Author Logic Chain:
- First, the authors distinguish problem-solving from the earlier work of listening and expression.
- Then, they identify the two common failure modes: drift and unilateral prescription.
- Then, they introduce the main corrective: propose joint inquiry into solutions rather than a solution.
- Then, they specify four moves: invent options, look for mutual standards, build on each other's proposals, agree on what to do next specifically.
- Then, they clarify that specificity is not bureaucracy but the bridge between talk and action.
- Finally, they place a boundary around the chapter: not every difficult conversation is a problem-solving conversation, and false commitments are worse than no commitments.

Required Anchors:

Anchor 1 — Farah and Miles (work context):
Farah and Miles run a small business together. They have been arguing for weeks about whether to hire their first full-time employee. Farah is worried about payroll risk. Miles is worried about burnout, missed opportunities, and the cost of waiting longer. They have finally heard each other well enough that the argument is no longer about whether one of them is irresponsible. But hearing each other still has not produced a decision. The chapter's move enters here: the next step is not to vote on which fear wins. It is to invent several options and test them against standards both partners can live with.
- Concrete detail: The options on the table are not abstract. They include a part-time contractor, a full-time hire with a conservative compensation plan, and a deferred hire triggered by a specific revenue number.
- Friction: Their old pattern is to treat the first option named as the position to defend.
- Consequence: The moment they generate multiple options, the argument stops being "your way vs. my way" and starts becoming design.

Anchor 2 — Mira and Professor Rowan (school context):
Mira is a graduate teaching assistant trying to solve a recurring section-scheduling problem with Professor Rowan. The obvious failure would be to leave with a vague promise to "coordinate better next time." The subtler failure would be for Rowan to extract a specific commitment from Mira about class coverage that Mira does not actually have the authority to guarantee because the department keeps changing room assignments and enrollment caps at the last minute.
- Concrete detail: Rowan wants a tidy solution by the end of the meeting. Mira knows some of the moving parts are outside her control.
- Friction: Specific commitments usually help. Here they can become false promises.
- Consequence: The chapter's hard-depth question appears: a useful commitment has to track real agency, not just the senior person's desire for closure.

Specific Applications:
1. When the conversation is ready for design, say so explicitly: "I have one idea, but I do not want to turn the first idea into the only idea. What options do you see?"
2. Force option variety before judgment. Three imperfect options usually beat one defended option.
3. Ask what standard a reasonable outsider or newcomer would use. This moves the conversation off personality and onto criteria.
4. Treat the other person's proposal as material to improve rather than a target to defeat.
5. Convert agreement into observable next steps with dates, owners, and a follow-up point, but only where those commitments map to real control.

---

Common Misreadings:
1. Taking the lead means taking over. It does not. The lead is on process, not on outcome ownership.
2. Inventing options means brainstorming endlessly. It does not. The point is enough variety to prevent the first proposal from becoming a trench.
3. Mutual standards remove conflict automatically. They do not. They create a fairer basis for comparison.
4. Specific commitments are always better than ambiguity. Not if the commitment asks someone to promise what they do not control.

---

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter's main limit is timing. Problem-solving is not the goal of every difficult conversation. If the conversation is still trying to establish what happened, surface feelings, or hear each other accurately, reaching for solutions too early functions as escape. The harder limit is power and agency. Joint inquiry can become fake if one party lacks real power to help design the outcome, and specific next steps can become coercive if they require promises the less powerful party cannot actually keep. The chapter is strongest when it protects against false closure as carefully as it protects against drift.
