### 2. Anchor Use — 2/2
Naomi appears in paragraphs 1, 2, 3, 8, and 9 — she opens the chapter, reveals its stakes, and closes the diagnostic arc. Her anchor carries the chapter's thinking structurally: the reader sees her problem before they know the vocabulary for it, then sees it again through the three-conversation lens. Carmen/Miguel appears at the chapter's emotional center (P7) and extends the framework to a personal context that feels distinct from the professional anchor without repeating its lesson. Both anchors are load-bearing.

### 3. Analytical Value — 2/2
The chapter delivers a real perceptual tool, not advice. The core distinction — that the three conversations are not three parts of one conversation but three separate conversations with different subject matter, failure modes, and stakes — is non-generic. The specific insight that the identity conversation is the quietest and the one most likely to be doing the most damage is the chapter's sharpest edge, and it is delivered clearly without being resolved. The "they ventriloquize" formulation for suppressed feelings is a genuine insight, not a rephrasing of common knowledge.

### 4. Paragraph Motion — 2/2
Ten paragraphs, ten distinct jobs:
- P1: scene-setting hook (Naomi, specificity-first)
- P2: reveal the subtext (she's preparing for more than one conversation)
- P3: state the claim cleanly
- P4: the "what happened" conversation and its failure mode

- P6: the identity conversation and its failure mode (behavior stops making sense)

- P8: resolve Naomi arc through the three-conversation lens

No two paragraphs do the same job. The chapter has forward momentum.

### 5. Prose Quality — 2/2
Sharp and authored. The strongest sentences are not generic: "The feelings do not vanish. They ventriloquize." reads as discovered, not constructed. "She is not writing the wrong sentences. She is trying to carry too many conversations in one container." is vivid and precise — it earns the word "container" in a way that does not feel forced. The tone is warm and analytical without being either clinical or faux-folksy. The grade level for the canonical draft is approximately 10-11 (appropriate for medium depth as a baseline, with Easy mode simplifying and Hard mode deepening).

### 6. Hook and Bridge — 2/2
The opening pulls: Naomi at her desk, rewriting a sentence, does not know what is wrong with it. The reader wants to find out. The hook is a specific scene, not a thesis announcement. The ending ("What to do about the most common failure — walking into the facts argument while the identity conversation destroys the relationship underneath it — is the question the next chapter addresses") opens a real forward-facing question without summarizing Chapter 2. The bridge satisfies the outline's preview constraint.
