## Observations

**Weakest paragraph:** P6 (negotiating with the feeling) — the most abstract paragraph in the draft. The concept of examining the story to revise the feeling is sound but requires the reader to accept a premise (feelings are produced by stories) that some readers may resist. It works in context and does not repeat any other paragraph's job, but it is the paragraph most likely to produce pushback from readers who experience feelings as non-cognitive.

**Strongest sentence:** "The feelings conversation is already happening. The question is whether it is happening above or below ground."

**Contamination check:** CLEAN. "Suppression," "feelings-shaped accusation," and "negotiate with your feelings" are permitted vocabulary. No contamination from the banned list.

**Hard-depth note:** The professional-context complication is seeded throughout (Tara's professional context, Ben's managerial context) but not yet named as the hard-depth floor in the draft. The converter must develop this explicitly: when sharing feelings with the person they are about has asymmetric costs, the question shifts from whether to have the feelings conversation to who is the right audience for it. This is the hard-depth minimum per the brief and outline.

**The five moves:** All five are present and distinct. "Find, negotiate, don't let drive, share directly, acknowledge" — each gets its own paragraph or paragraph section. No move is repeated.
