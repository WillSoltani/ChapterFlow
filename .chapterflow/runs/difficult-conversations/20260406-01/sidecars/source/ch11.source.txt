Chapter 11 — Problem-Solving: Take the Lead
Source Reference (Paraphrased) — Difficult Conversations, Stone / Patton / Heen, 10th Anniversary Edition

---

SECTION 1: The Stage Shift

Once both people have heard each other and the feelings are in the room, some difficult conversations need to move into design. The question changes from "what is going on here?" and "what is true for each of us?" to "what do we do about it now?"

The chapter is clear that this is a stage shift, not the purpose of every difficult conversation. Some conversations are mainly for understanding, expression, or repair. Reaching for solutions too early can be another way of escaping the harder work of hearing and being heard.

---

SECTION 2: Two Failure Modes

The source names two common failures at the problem-solving stage.

1. Drift. No one takes the lead, the conversation trails off, and both people leave with the feeling that something important happened even though nothing actually changed.
2. Unilateral prescription. One person, often the one who raised the issue, prescribes the answer. The other person agrees on the surface because resisting feels costly, then quietly undermines the plan later because they did not help design it.

The chapter's correction is not stronger control. It is better process.

---

SECTION 3: Joint Inquiry into Solutions

The main move is to propose a joint inquiry into solutions rather than a solution. The lead is on process. A skilled opener at this stage sounds like: here is one thing I am thinking might help, what do you see that I might be missing, and what would you propose?

That language matters because it invites co-design. The other person's proposal is not a threat to the first proposal. It is part of how the outcome becomes implementable.

---

SECTION 4: Invent Options

The source warns against evaluating the first option immediately. The first option on the table is almost never the best one, but once it is judged too quickly the conversation collapses into defending or attacking that option.

The correction is to generate several options first. The modern business example in the source makes this concrete: partners debating a first hire should generate options such as a contractor, a conservative full-time hire, or a deferred hire tied to a revenue trigger before deciding which path is strongest.

---

SECTION 5: Mutual Standards and Building on Proposals

The chapter then moves the conversation off "your way vs. my way." Instead of comparing preferences directly, look for a standard both people would regard as fair in advance. What would a reasonable policy look like? What would we both want a newcomer to this situation to do?

This creates a criterion outside either person's ego. The chapter pairs that move with another: treat the other person's proposal as raw material to improve. Start with what works in it, adjust what does not, and keep building rather than switching into attack mode.

---

SECTION 6: Specific Next Steps

The conversation has not become action until there is a specific next step. The source is explicit here: "we'll figure it out" is too vague. Better is something observable with owners and time: I will do X by Thursday, you will do Y by the same time, and we will check in on Friday.

Specificity is not bureaucracy. It is the bridge between insight and behavior.

---

SECTION 7: Hard Case — False Commitments

The hard case for this run should focus on agency. Specific commitments help only when they match what the person actually controls. Joint inquiry can become fake if one party is pushed to agree to outcomes they have no power to guarantee.

This matters especially across power gaps. A junior person may agree to an exact plan for the sake of closure even when the real variables are controlled by someone else. In that case, the specific commitment is not good problem-solving. It is pressured theater.

---

SECTION 8: Boundary Around the Move

Problem-solving is not the goal of every difficult conversation. Some conversations should end with better understanding and nothing else yet. The move into design belongs after enough listening and expression have happened, not in place of them.

---

SECTION 9: Bridge Forward

By this point the book has opened the conversation, listened through it, spoken from each side, and turned it toward action. The final move is integration: how all of that works in one full difficult conversation.
