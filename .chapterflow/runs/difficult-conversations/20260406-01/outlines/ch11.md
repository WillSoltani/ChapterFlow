Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader can recognize when a difficult conversation is ready to move from understanding into design, avoid the twin failures of drift and unilateral solution-pushing, use joint inquiry to generate several options, compare them against shared standards, and leave with specific next steps that match real agency.

Paragraph Job Map:

P1 — Hook: Farah and Miles have finally heard each other on hiring. The fight is quieter, but nothing is solved. They need a move that is neither more discussion nor a forced verdict.

P2 — Distinction: not every difficult conversation should become a problem-solving conversation, but once both sides have been heard and expressed, some conversations do need to shift into design. Name the stage change.

P3 — The two failures at this stage: drift (no one takes the lead; relief without change) and unilateral prescription (one person announces the plan; surface agreement, quiet sabotage later).

P4 — Core move: propose joint inquiry into solutions rather than a solution. The lead is on process, not on control. Show the invitation language.

P5 — Invent options before evaluation. Farah and Miles stop arguing hire vs. no hire and generate three concrete options. The key lesson: the first option named should not become the trench.

P6 — Mutual standards and specific next steps. Ask what a reasonable policy would look like and what standard they would both want a newcomer to use. Then land on observable next steps with owners and dates.

P7 — Build on proposals. A good response to the other person's idea starts with what is useful in it and then adjusts it, instead of rejecting it wholesale.

P8 — Hard-depth: false commitments under unequal control. Mira can commit to what she controls, not to room assignments or enrollment shifts the department controls. Specificity without agency becomes coercion.

P9 — Boundary: reaching for solutions too early can be another avoidance move. Problem-solving is not a substitute for understanding or expression.

P10 — Bridge to Ch12: the whole sequence now exists and needs to be integrated in one full conversation.

---

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Joint inquiry into solutions vs. either drift or unilateral solution-pushing. The mechanism is four-part: invent options, use mutual standards, build on proposals, agree on specifics that track real control.

---

Hard-Depth Tension:
Specific commitments are the mark of real problem-solving, but they become false closure when one party is pressed to promise what they do not control. Good structure can still become coercive if agency is misread.

---

Threshold Question:
Are you trying to solve the problem together, or are you trying to get the other person to bless a solution you already chose?
