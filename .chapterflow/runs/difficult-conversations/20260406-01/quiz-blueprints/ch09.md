Chapter: 9 — Listen from the Inside Out

Core concepts to test:
- The internal voice: what it is and its three specific tasks during a charged conversation (counter-arguing, judging the speaker, managing the listener's own identity stakes)
- Why the performance of listening — being quiet while someone talks — is not the same as genuine listening
- The inside-out move: noticing the internal voice and setting it aside deliberately (not suppressing it — suppression makes it louder)
- The three external moves and their sequencing: ask questions with genuine curiosity, paraphrase (proof of reception not agreement), acknowledge feelings (acknowledgment is not agreement)
- Paraphrasing as proof of reception: what it is, what it is not, and when it invites correction
- Paraphrasing backfire conditions: when accurate reflection reads as sarcasm, condescension, or therapeutic performance — the register, timing, and phrasing conditions that produce misfire
- (Hard) Acknowledging feelings: naming the emotion the other person seems to be carrying, even when they have not named it — and the distinction between acknowledgment and agreement

Questions at the analyze level should present realistic listening scenarios and ask readers to identify what is happening internally or predict which external move is appropriate given the context.
