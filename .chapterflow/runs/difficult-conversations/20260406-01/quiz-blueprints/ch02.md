Chapter: 2 — Stop Arguing About Who's Right

Core concepts to test:
- Message delivery stance: the default orientation where the goal is to get the other person to accept your version
- Learning stance: the alternative orientation where the goal is to understand what each person sees and what each is missing
- Why message delivery fails structurally (both parties push, neither can hear)
- What the learning stance actually requires: holding your view as one perspective, not the only truth
- The gap between performing openness and genuinely being in the learning stance

Bloom ceiling:
- Highest justified level: analyze / early evaluate
- The chapter installs a fundamental stance shift. Questions should test whether readers can recognize the stance in a scenario and apply the shift, not just define the terms.

Question plan:
- q01: Basic recognition — which stance is being described? (remember/understand)
- q02: Understanding the structural failure of message delivery (understand)
- q03: Application — identify the stance in a scenario (apply)
- q04: Named scenario (Reza/Yuki) — what would a learning stance look like here? (apply)
- q05: Distinction / trap — performing openness vs. genuine learning stance (analyze)
- q06: Limit — what the learning stance does not guarantee (understand/analyze)
- q07: Transfer — applying the stance shift to a personal context (apply/analyze)
- q08: Scenario judgment — what is wrong with the conversation described? (analyze)
- q09: Synthesis — why the stance shift is the precondition for later skills (evaluate)
- q10: Connection to Ch01 / threshold — what does the learning stance require of the identity conversation? (evaluate)

- Tempting wrong answer type 2: "agree with the other person" — frequent misread of the learning stance
- Tempting wrong answer type 3: "be honest about your position" — sounds like what the chapter teaches for expression (Ch10) but is not the learning stance
