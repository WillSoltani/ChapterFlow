Chapter: 11 — Problem-Solving: Take the Lead

Core concepts to test:
- The stage shift from understanding/expression into design
- The two failure modes at this stage: drift and unilateral prescription
- Joint inquiry into solutions rather than a solution
- Invent options before evaluating any one
- Look for mutual standards instead of arguing "your way vs. my way"
- Build on the other person's proposals instead of attacking them
- Agree on specific next steps
- Hard case: do not extract commitments broader than a person's real control

Bloom ceiling:
- Highest justified level: analyze
- No moral complexity flag on this chapter. The hard case is real but the learning demand is diagnostic: readers need to identify whether a proposed commitment tracks actual agency and whether a conversation has shifted into genuine joint inquiry or only the language of it.
