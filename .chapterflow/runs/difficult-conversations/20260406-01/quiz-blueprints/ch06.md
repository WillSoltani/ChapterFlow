Chapter: 6 — Ground Your Identity

Core concepts to test:
- Identity quake: what it is, what triggers it, what it does to the conversation
- All-or-nothing identity beliefs: the structural cause of identity quakes; why binary belief structures fail under feedback
- AND/BOTH shift: the specific move for each of the three beliefs; how it differs from the all-or-nothing version
- What AND/BOTH is NOT: not detachment, not self-compassion as therapy, not anesthesia
- Timing: grounding is done before the conversation, not during it
- (Hard) AND/BOTH as anesthesia: when the grounding move suppresses the real signal rather than opening to it — the chapter's moral complexity

Bloom ceiling:
- Highest justified level: evaluate
- The chapter's sharpest distinction — AND/BOTH as grounding vs. AND/BOTH as anesthesia — requires readers to evaluate a specific application of the move and determine whether it is functioning as intended. Questions at evaluate level should present realistic scenarios and ask the reader to diagnose whether AND/BOTH is genuinely grounding the person or functioning as a more sophisticated defense.

Distractor notes:
- For identity quake questions:
  - Tempting wrong 1: "a strong emotional reaction to being criticized" — too generic; identity quake is specifically about attention redirecting from the content of the conversation to self-defense, not merely emotional intensity
  - Tempting wrong 2: "a communication breakdown caused by different assumptions" — conflates with the contribution conversation or the what-happened conversation
  - Tempting wrong 3: "a feeling of anger or shame during a conflict" — feelings are the signal; identity quake is the structural mechanism underneath the feeling

- For AND/BOTH questions:
  - Tempting wrong 1: "accepting that you can't do anything right in order to be humble" — AND/BOTH is not self-deprecation or defeat; it holds both the positive and the complication simultaneously
  - Tempting wrong 2: "learning not to care what other people think" — AND/BOTH is not detachment; the grounded person still cares about competence, goodness, and love
  - Tempting wrong 3: "telling yourself mistakes don't matter so you don't feel bad" — this is anesthesia, the failure mode, not the move

- For anesthesia questions:
  - Tempting wrong 1: "using AND/BOTH incorrectly by adding too many clauses" — anesthesia is not a syntactic error; it is a functional one: the belief structure absorbs the feedback before it can produce change
  - Tempting wrong 2: "being too hard on yourself when receiving feedback" — this is the all-or-nothing failure mode, not anesthesia
  - Tempting wrong 3: "denying that a mistake happened" — denial is the all-or-nothing failure mode; anesthesia acknowledges the mistake in language while deflecting it in practice
