Chapter: 3 — Don't Assume They Meant It

Core concepts to test:
- Intent and impact as two separate things that can run in any combination
- Receiver-side failure: inventing the other person's intent (almost always worse than the real one)
- Deliverer-side failure: using "I didn't mean it" as if it resolved the impact (it does not)
- The disentangle move: acknowledge impact without using it to infer intent; name intent without using it to close the impact conversation
- Sequence matters: acknowledging impact before explaining intent changes what the other person can hear
- (Hard) The asymmetric case: what happens when the intent was genuinely harmful

Bloom ceiling:
- Highest justified level: analyze / evaluate (q09–q10 can reach synthesis)
- The chapter's hardest move — taking responsibility for impact without accepting blame for intent — requires holding two things simultaneously. Questions should test whether readers can distinguish acknowledgment from blame-acceptance.
