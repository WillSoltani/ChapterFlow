Chapter: 5 — Have Your Feelings (Or They Will Have You)

Core concepts to test:
- Suppression failure: suppressed feelings redirect into tone, avoidance, contempt, and sudden escalation rather than disappearing
- Finding the feeling: the specific feeling rather than the generic "fine" or "frustrated"
- Negotiating with the feeling: examining the story producing it to see if it is accurate
- Direct feelings statement vs. feelings-shaped accusation: "I am hurt" vs. "I feel like you don't care"
- Acknowledge before problem-solve: let the feeling be seen before trying to fix anything
- (Hard) Asymmetric cost of sharing: when sharing has real professional or relational costs, the question shifts from whether to have the feelings conversation to who is the right audience for it

Bloom ceiling:
- Highest justified level: analyze / evaluate (q09-q10 can reach synthesis)
- The chapter's sharpest distinction — feelings-shaped accusation vs. direct feelings statement — requires readers to hold a structural difference, not just a tone difference. Questions should test whether readers can identify this in specific examples.
