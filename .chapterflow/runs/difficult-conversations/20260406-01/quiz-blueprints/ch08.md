Chapter: 8 — Begin from the Third Story (Getting Started: Begin from the Third Story)

Core concepts to test:
- First-story opener: opening from your own perspective, treating your view as the baseline, which positions the listener as defendant before the conversation begins
- Third-story opener: describing the disagreement from the vantage of a neutral, thoughtful observer — someone who can see that two parties are working from different assumptions without yet knowing who is closer to right
- The three benefits of the third-story opener: (1) does not assume the speaker is already right, (2) invites joint inquiry rather than message delivery, (3) lowers identity stakes because neither party is positioned as the one who got it wrong
- Invitation vs. summons: the third-story opener closes with a genuine invitation to hear the other person's view; the first-story opener closes with a summons to account for yourself
- The learning stance as prerequisite: the third-story opener only works when the speaker genuinely holds the learning stance — performing it without meaning it is detectable and can be more manipulative than an honest first-story opener
- What the third-story opener cannot fix: situations requiring acknowledgment before inquiry; power gaps that make joint inquiry structurally inert

Distractor notes:
- For first-story vs. third-story questions:
  - Tempting wrong 1: "a third-story opener means being vague or indirect about what you want to talk about" — incorrect; the third-story opener names the disagreement clearly, just without assigning fault
  - Tempting wrong 2: "a first-story opener is only a problem when it sounds accusatory — a polite first-story opener works fine" — incorrect; the chapter's claim is structural, not tonal; a polite first-story opener still positions the listener as defendant
  - Tempting wrong 3: "the third-story opener means pretending you have no view" — incorrect; the speaker has a view, the opener holds it without leading with it

- For benefits questions:
  - Tempting wrong 1: "it ensures the other person will agree with you by the end" — the opener affects the start of the conversation, not the outcome
  - Tempting wrong 2: "it prevents the conversation from becoming emotional" — the chapter makes no such claim; the third-story opener lowers identity stakes, not emotional temperature generally
  - Tempting wrong 3: "it tells the other person that their version of events is correct" — incorrect; it names the disagreement without assigning correctness to either version

- For learning stance questions:
  - Tempting wrong 1: "the third-story opener works as a technique regardless of whether the speaker means it" — the chapter specifically says the other person can feel the difference; technique without stance can be worse than an honest first-story opener
  - Tempting wrong 2: "you need to be fully neutral to use the third-story opener" — incorrect; full neutrality is neither required nor possible; what is required is genuine openness to the other person's version being real

- For hard-depth questions:
  - Tempting wrong 1: "the third-story opener always works if it is phrased correctly" — the chapter names specific situations where it cannot fix the underlying dynamic
  - Tempting wrong 2: "the third-story opener fails when the speaker is too emotional" — the chapter does not make this claim; the identified limits are structural (acknowledgment-before-inquiry; power gap), not about the speaker's emotional state
