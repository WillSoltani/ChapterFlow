# Style Memory — Difficult Conversations v12 Run

## Voice
- Clear, confident, readable — sounds like a smart person explaining what they understand
- Specific over generic; concrete over abstract; insight over padding
- "You" used naturally, not everywhere
- Feels authored, not assembled
- Author name allowed sparingly in breakdowns only
- Tone: warm, academic, specifically humble and tentative (closer to thoughtful law professor than management consultant)

## Meta-Distance Rule (HARD)
- Zero "the chapter says/teaches/warns" in takeaways, moreDetails, quiz explanations, scenarios, prompts, cards
- Teach the idea directly; do not narrate chapter existence
- "Chapter N" only in rare structural recap/preview contexts

## Grade Bands
- Easy: grade 8-9 — orient, clarify, graspable. Short/medium sentences, concrete nouns, one frame, one vivid example early. No stacked abstractions.
- Medium: grade 10-11 — explain how and why, sharpen mechanism. Moderate abstraction, contrast, 1-2 live tensions.
- Hard: grade 12 — add threshold question, boundary condition, contradiction, or synthesis. Multi-part sentences, real conceptual tension.

## Hard Constraints (constraints.md)
- No em dashes
- No made-up quotes
- No filler neuroscience (mirror neurons, oxytocin, cortisol, dopamine, vagal tone, evolutionary psychology, millisecond timing)
- No pseudo-precision
- No generic recap language
- No contamination phrases leaking into reader-facing content
- No em dashes

## Banned Contamination Phrases (reader-facing)
"keep the prose narrow", "the source is short", "used lazily", "keep this question alive", "one source pressure", "tied to the live constraint", "threshold question", "reading calibration", "unsupported zones", "motif watchlist", "sourceAnchorPriority", "internal concept budget"

## Banned Words (reader-facing)
delve, crucial, landscape, realm, It's worth noting, In today's world, Furthermore, Moreover, In conclusion, at its core, the art of, navigating, harnessing, robust, synergy, paradigm shift, game-changer, facilitate, utilize, foster, embark on, a testament to, shed light on, This matters because, This is significant because, It is essential to

## Bad Patterns to Reject
- Writing that could fit any chapter in the book
- Tone variants that differ only by adjective swap
- Sentence skeletons: "X is not Y. It is Z.", "The real issue is...", "What matters is..."
- Hard depth = medium + more words (not acceptable)
- Opening with thesis instead of curiosity/scene/tension
- Pseudo-drafts headed by "## Easy / ## Medium / ## Hard"

## Gold Patterns
- Open on vivid specific anchor, not thesis announcement
- Keep one governing principle visible across whole chapter
- Depth differences are structural, not volumetric
- Tone differences are functional (gentle=lower resistance, direct=cleaner mechanism, competitive=sharper stakes)
- Examples are socially realistic with concrete names/rooms/stakes
- whatToDo gives a precise move, not vague advice
- whyItMatters explains downstream consequence, not just restates whatToDo
- Quiz tests judgment in situations, not memorization
- End paragraphs with consequence, not mush

## Read-Aloud Test
Pre-manufactured sentences → cut | Generic podcast summary → cut | Sentences smarter than brief → cut | Sounds like note to writer → cut immediately
