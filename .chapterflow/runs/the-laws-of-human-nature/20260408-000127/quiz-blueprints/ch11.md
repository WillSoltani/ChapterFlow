# Quiz blueprint

Chapter Number: 11
Chapter Title: Beware the Fragile Ego: The Law of Envy

Bloom Ceiling: evaluate-create on q09-q10
Concept Budget: 3 to 5 live concepts
Primary Tested Concepts:
- Envy is self-concealing by design. The emotion carries a confession of inferiority that the ego cannot tolerate, so it undergoes a perceptual conversion into moral judgment. The envious person genuinely believes they are being principled, not jealous.
- The proximity principle is the structural predictor of envy. It intensifies between peers and near-equals, not between people at different levels. The closer the starting positions, the more personal the gap feels when one person advances.
- The four-signal detection arrangement gives you a practical diagnostic. Microexpressions of displeasure, compliments with buried insults, post-success helpfulness, and process-focused criticism each represent envy wearing a different mask.
- The leveler is a specific personality type to identify early. They enforce equality not through inspiration but through suppression, framing any individual rise as a betrayal of the group. Their tools are guilt, moral framing, and quiet obstruction.
- Empathy is a detection instrument, not a resolution tool for envy. Using it to identify the signals protects you. Using it to absorb or manage someone else's envy exposes you to sustained emotional cost with little return.

Prediction Questions:
- Chapter 12 addresses grandiosity as the inward defense against the same inferiority that drives envy outward. Using the perceptual conversion model from this chapter, predict the specific way a fragile self-image inflates itself and what triggers would cause the inflation to collapse.
- Chapter 12 is the companion piece. Envy defends the fragile ego by directing hostility outward. Grandiosity defends it by directing inflation inward. The connection: both responses protect against the same comparison, and many people use both depending on context. Watch for the trigger that determines which defense activates.

Likely Misreadings:
- reducing the chapter to a slogan
- mistaking a single moment for the whole principle
- forgetting the stated boundary condition
