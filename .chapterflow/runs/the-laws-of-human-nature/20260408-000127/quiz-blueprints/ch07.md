# Quiz blueprint

Chapter Number: 7
Chapter Title: Elevate Your Perspective: The Law of Shortsightedness

Bloom Ceiling: evaluate-create on q09-q10
Concept Budget: 3 to 5 live concepts
Primary Tested Concepts:
- Emotional intensity is inversely correlated with temporal depth. The stronger the feeling driving your decision, the shorter the timeframe you are actually evaluating. Treat intense urgency as a signal to extend your analysis window, not shorten it.
- Consequential thinking has three tiers. First order: the obvious, direct result. Second order: who gains or loses from that result. Third order: what systemic changes follow from those gains and losses. Most decisions are made at tier one. Tier-three thinkers consistently outperform.
- Quick-fix addiction is a form of emotional accounting. You pay down the discomfort today by borrowing against your future position. The relief is real but the debt is hidden, and it compounds.
- Social consensus is not validation. Crowd behavior produces convergence of opinion without convergence of evidence. The fact that many people believe something tells you about social pressure, not about the truth of the belief.
- Failing to aggregate signals into trends is a specific form of shortsightedness. Individual events explained in isolation lose their predictive power. Connecting them into a trajectory reveals the direction of change before the change becomes obvious.

Prediction Questions:
- Chapter 8 applies temporal depth to the domain of influence. Using the orientation from this chapter, predict the specific process by which long-term thinkers build compounding influence that reactive thinkers cannot replicate.
- Chapter 8 converts temporal depth into influence architecture. The person who holds a wider timeline can construct influence strategies that compound across cycles, remaining invisible to participants who evaluate only short-term outcomes. The connection from this chapter is direct: the same skill that prevents you from falling into bubbles also allows you to build positions that short-term thinkers cannot detect or disrupt.

Question Map:
- q01: What happened to Isaac Newton during the South Sea Bubble that illustrates the danger of short-term thinking?

- q03: How does Greene distinguish between first-order, second-order, and third-order thinking?
- q04: Tariq, a marketing director, is losing market share to a competitor. His instinct is to slash prices immediately to win back customers. Before acting, he maps the consequence chain. First order: lower prices attract some buyers back. Second order: the competitor matches the price cut. Third order: both companies are now earning less with no change in relative market position. What has Tariq's analysis revealed?
- q05: Gabrielle, a product manager, notices that three different enterprise clients have raised the same integration complaint over the past quarter. Each time, her team treated the complaint as an isolated issue and patched it individually. What sign of shortsightedness is Gabrielle's team displaying?
- q06: Wei, a graduate student, is deciding between accepting a well-paying corporate job and pursuing a research fellowship that pays much less but aligns with her long-term career goals. All her classmates are taking corporate offers, and the social pressure feels overwhelming. Which combination of shortsightedness markers is Wei most at risk of falling into?
- q07: Scipio Africanus defeated Hannibal not by fighting him directly in Italy but by attacking Carthage, forcing Hannibal to abandon his campaign and return home. What level of consequential thinking did Scipio use that the other Roman generals missed?
- q08: A founder is considering whether to take venture capital funding that would accelerate growth but require giving up board control. She maps three orders of consequence before deciding. This practice of extending the analysis beyond the immediate result represents which core habit from this chapter?
- q09: Greene argues that intelligence did not save Newton from the South Sea Bubble. If raw analytical power cannot prevent shortsightedness, what does this imply about the true nature of the skill Greene is teaching in this chapter?
- q10: A historian studying financial bubbles across centuries notices the same pattern: crowds driven by reactive emotion, quick-fix greed, trend blindness, and tribal reinforcement. The South Sea Bubble of 1720 and the Dutch tulip mania of the 1630s followed nearly identical playbooks despite occurring in different countries and eras. How does recognizing this recurring cycle across generations connect to the generational relationships examined in Chapter 18?
