# Quiz blueprint

Chapter Number: 16
Chapter Title: Make Them Want to Follow You: The Law of Fickleness

Bloom Ceiling: evaluate-create on q09-q10
Concept Budget: 3 to 5 live concepts
Primary Tested Concepts:
- Positional authority produces compliance but not commitment. The gap between the two is the gap between claimed authority and demonstrated authority. Closing it requires visible proof, not verbal assertion.
- The four-pillar authority model: demonstrate competence, create shared vision, show empathy, maintain word-action alignment. These four cover the full spectrum of what followers evaluate when deciding whether to commit.
- Authority decays without active maintenance. Each decision, conversation, and public action either deposits trust or withdraws it. Leaders who stop making deposits discover that the balance reached zero long before the visible collapse.
- The five authority styles are tactical modes, not personality types. Each style is optimal for specific conditions and suboptimal for others. The Adaptive leader who can shift between styles based on the situation has the broadest effectiveness.
- The Chapter 15 to Chapter 16 progression is sequential: understand group conformity pressure first, then learn to redirect it. Resistance without understanding produces isolation. Redirection with understanding produces leadership.

Prediction Questions:
- Chapter 17 addresses aggression triggered by earned authority. Using the four-pillar model, predict which pillar is most likely to provoke hostility, and from whom. Then predict what defense the other pillars provide.
- Chapter 17 addresses the direct consequence of earned authority: targeted aggression from people who feel diminished by your rise. The four pillars you built here are what paint the target. Chapter 17 provides the defensive lens for managing the hostility that competent leadership inevitably generates.

Likely Misreadings:
- reducing the chapter to a slogan
- mistaking a single moment for the whole principle
- forgetting the stated boundary condition
