# Quiz blueprint

Chapter Number: 6
Chapter Title: Become an Elusive Object of Desire: The Law of Covetousness

Bloom Ceiling: evaluate-create on q09-q10
Concept Budget: 3 to 5 live concepts
Primary Tested Concepts:
- Desire is powered by incompleteness. Full access shuts down the brain's anticipation circuit. Partial access keeps it firing. The dopamine setup responds to the possibility of reward, not the reward itself, and full access eliminates the possibility.
- Availability and perceived value are inversely correlated. This is not cynicism; it is supply economics applied to attention. Every instant response trains the recipient to treat your time as free. Occasional delay trains them to value the response when it arrives.
- Mystery works because it outsources the work of fascination to the other person's imagination. When you reveal everything, the audience has nothing to construct. When you leave gaps, they fill those gaps with projections that are almost always more flattering than the truth.
- The devaluation of the present and idealization of the absent is a default cognitive setting. It is not a sign of a bad relationship or a flawed character. It is the brain's tendency to allocate attention to unresolved possibilities rather than confirmed realities.
- Desire management is not about withdrawal for its own sake. It is about oscillation. Pure withdrawal creates abandonment. Pure presence creates boredom. The skill is calibrating the rhythm so that each return feels like a small gift rather than an obligation.

Prediction Questions:
- Chapter 7 addresses the tendency to sacrifice long-term value for short-term novelty. Using the desire gap architecture from this chapter, predict why Greene will argue that the covetous instinct, left unchecked, destroys more than it creates.
- Chapter 7 examines the failure mode of covetousness. The same brain circuitry that makes scarcity attractive in others makes you vulnerable to chasing phantom opportunities while neglecting real value. The connection is direct: the desire gap works on you exactly the way it works on the people around you.

Likely Misreadings:

- mistaking a single moment for the whole principle
- forgetting the stated boundary condition
