# Quiz blueprint

Chapter Number: 15
Chapter Title: Resist the Downward Pull of the Group: The Law of Conformity

Bloom Ceiling: evaluate-create on q09-q10
Concept Budget: 3 to 5 live concepts
Primary Tested Concepts:
- Social proof is a shortcut that trades accuracy for speed. The brain interprets group agreement as evidence of correctness, which is efficient in stable contexts and dangerous in volatile ones where the group may be converging on a shared error.
- Emotions propagate through groups at a speed that outpaces critical thought. By the time you notice you are anxious, angry, or excited, the feeling has already been installed by the people around you. Your rational mind then constructs a justification for the emotion rather than questioning its source.
- Polarization is a ratchet, not a pendulum. Group discussion does not moderate individual opinions. It pushes them further from center because members gain social status by escalating their position, and each escalation resets the baseline for what counts as normal.
- Collective action suppresses individual moral braking. When responsibility is distributed across a group, the personal cost of participation drops below the threshold required to trigger internal resistance. People do things in groups that they would refuse to do if the accountability rested solely on them.
- The three responses to group pressure have asymmetric costs. Conformity costs your independent judgment. Rebellion costs your position within the group. Navigation costs effort and self-observation, but preserves both your thinking and your standing.

Prediction Questions:
- Chapter 16 addresses how to direct group energy rather than just resist it. Using the four-force model from this chapter, predict what Greene will identify as the key variable that separates leaders who shape groups from leaders who are shaped by them.
- Chapter 16 inverts the framework. The same social proof, emotional contagion, polarization, and diffusion of responsibility that pull individuals down also create channels through which a skilled leader can direct group energy upward. The recognition skills from this chapter become the operational foundation for the influence skills in the next.

Likely Misreadings:
- reducing the chapter to a slogan
- mistaking a single moment for the whole principle
- forgetting the stated boundary condition
