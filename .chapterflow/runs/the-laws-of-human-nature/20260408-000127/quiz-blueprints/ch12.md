# Quiz blueprint

Chapter Number: 12
Chapter Title: Know Your Limits: The Law of Grandiosity

Bloom Ceiling: evaluate-create on q09-q10
Concept Budget: 3 to 5 live concepts
Primary Tested Concepts:
- Wins inflate self-image on an automatic schedule. Each success adds confidence that is attributed to general ability rather than specific circumstances. This inflation is cumulative and invisible, producing a self-model that grows increasingly detached from evidence.
- Information filtration is incremental and self-reinforcing. Each small choice to favor an agreeable advisor or sideline a critical one narrows the data stream feeding your decisions. The bubble does not announce itself. It forms through the accumulation of small preferences for comfort over accuracy.
- The variable that separates lasting ambition from self-destructive ambition is verification. Practical grandiosity includes a feedback loop that tests ambition against evidence. Fantasist grandiosity runs on conviction alone. The difference shows up not in the size of the goal but in whether reality testing accompanies it.
- Productive pessimism is a structured pre-mortem for your confidence. Before acting on a high-conviction decision, systematically identify the assumptions you are making, the evidence against them, and the cost of being wrong. This is not doubt. Every assumption gets stress-tested the same way you would stress-test a contract before signing.
- Envy and grandiosity are mirror distortions of the same vulnerability. A fragile self-image, when confronted with others' success, produces envy. The same fragile self-image, fed by personal success, produces grandiosity. Both represent failures of accurate self-assessment driven by emotional processing rather than evidence.

Prediction Questions:
- Chapter 13 examines rigid identity as a downstream consequence of grandiosity. Using the four-stage degradation model from this chapter, predict the specific way that an inflated self-image transitions into an inflexible identity and why that inflexibility becomes dangerous.
- Chapter 13 extends this chapter's analysis into identity rigidity. The inflated self-model, left unchecked, transitions from a confident interpretation to a fixed truth that resists updating. The operational question: at what point does your self-concept stop being a tool for handling reality and start being a constraint that prevents you from seeing it?

Likely Misreadings:
- reducing the chapter to a slogan
- mistaking a single moment for the whole principle
- forgetting the stated boundary condition
