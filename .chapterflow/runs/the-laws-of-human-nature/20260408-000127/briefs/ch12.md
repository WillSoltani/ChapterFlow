Chapter Title: Know Your Limits: The Law of Grandiosity

Core Claim:
Attribution stripping is the core cognitive process behind grandiosity. The brain removes contextual factors from successful outcomes and assigns credit to personal identity. Over repeated wins, this produces a self-model that significantly overestimates general competence because the situational contributors have been setupatically erased. The warning signs are causally linked, not independent. Each marker creates the precondition for the next. Treating them as a checklist misses the escalation logic. Catching the drift at stage one prevents stages two through four from engaging. Bubble formation is an emergent property of selection preferences, not a personality defect. The leader who builds a bubble is not choosing delusion. They are making marginal personnel and attention decisions that compound into an information environment with dramatically reduced corrective input.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
**Wins inflate self-image on an automatic schedule.** Each success adds confidence that is attributed to general ability rather than specific circumstances. This inflation is cumulative and invisible, producing a self-model that grows increasingly detached from evidence.

- The variable that separates lasting ambition from self-destructive ambition is verification. Practical grandiosity includes a feedback loop that tests ambition against evidence. Fantasist grandiosity runs on conviction alone. The difference shows up not in the size of the goal but in whether reality testing accompanies it.

Counterarguments or Limits:

The grandiosity from this chapter becomes the inflexibility of Chapter 13 when the person stops treating their self-model as an evolving assessment and starts treating it as a fixed truth. The rigidity makes adaptation impossible precisely when circumstances demand it.
