Core Claim:
Social proof is a cognitive shortcut that substitutes group behavior for individual analysis. The brain processes the quantity of agreement as a proxy for the quality of evidence, which makes it efficient in stable environments and exploitable in volatile ones. Emotional contagion operates on a faster channel than rational evaluation. Mirror neuron activation and unconscious mimicry synchronize your emotional state with the group before the prefrontal cortex can engage. The rational mind then rationalizes the imported emotion rather than questioning it. Polarization is driven by intra-group status competition. Members gain social standing by escalating their commitment beyond the current baseline, which pushes the baseline further from center with each cycle. Moderation earns no status within a group that is already leaning.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
**Social proof is a shortcut that trades accuracy for speed.** The brain interprets group agreement as evidence of correctness, which is efficient in stable contexts and dangerous in volatile ones where the group may be converging on a shared error.
