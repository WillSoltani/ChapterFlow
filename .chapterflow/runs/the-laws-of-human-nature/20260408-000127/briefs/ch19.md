Core Claim:
Death denial is culturally engineered, not individually chosen. Modern institutions have relocated death from the home to the hospital, creating a perceptual gap where time feels infinite and urgency feels optional. The four axes of the sublime perspective form an integrated system. Pettiness dissolution, urgency activation, gratitude generation, and purpose sharpening are not separate benefits. They are connected outputs of a single perceptual shift. O'Connor's career is a controlled experiment in mortality-driven productivity. Same talent, same training, different time horizon. The visible deadline produced work that an invisible one never would have.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
**Death denial is not a belief system. It is a built-in feature of modern life.** Hospitals, euphemisms, and cultural avoidance have separated most people from direct mortality contact, and the psychological costs compound silently.
