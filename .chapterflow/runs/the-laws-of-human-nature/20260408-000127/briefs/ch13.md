Core Claim:
Behavioral constraints installed during early development feel innate because they were formed during high neuroplasticity and zero critical capacity. They are learned, not fixed, which means they are modifiable through the same process that installed them: deliberate practice with new reinforcement trends. Suppression routes traits through unconscious channels where they express without modulation or control. The distorted expression is invariably more disruptive than the original trait because it cannot be calibrated, timed, or directed intentionally. Response diversity beats response magnitude. Caterina Sforza defeated opponents with more resources by maintaining a behavioral range wider than their predictive models. Integration of multiple capabilities produces an advantage that depth in a single capability cannot match in complex environments.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
**Your default behavioral range was selected for you, not by you.** Social environment determined which traits were reinforced and which were suppressed. Recognizing this as conditioning rather than identity is the first step toward expanding your options.

- Integration beats specialization in complex environments. Caterina Sforza's advantage was not superiority in any single trait. It was the breadth of her repertoire. She could deploy force or diplomacy as the situation demanded, which made her unpredictable to opponents who expected one or the other.
