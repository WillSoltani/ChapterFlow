Chapter Title: See Through People's Masks: The Law of Role-playing
Position In Book: package chapter 4 of 19

Core Claim:
Social masks evolved as survival tools. Modulating self-presentation to fit group expectations increased survival odds in ancestral environments. The mask is not a cultural artifact. The behavior is encoded at the level of neural architecture, not learned from social convention. Microexpressions originate subcortically and fire before conscious editing is possible. The 40-millisecond window between genuine reaction and social mask is your most reliable data source. Vocal indicators, pitch, pace, and prosodic irregularity, provide a second involuntary channel. They are especially useful when the face is partially obscured or when someone has trained their facial expressions.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
**Social masks are standard operating equipment.** Everyone curates their presentation. Treating this as abnormal or morally suspect prevents you from reading people accurately.
