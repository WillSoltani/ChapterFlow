Chapter Title: Become an Elusive Object of Desire: The Law of Covetousness
Position In Book: package chapter 6 of 19

Core Claim:
Dopamine is an anticipation signal, not a satisfaction signal. It peaks during the gap between wanting and obtaining, then declines upon confirmation. This neurological fact means desire is built-inly tied to incompleteness, not fulfillment. Incomplete experiences maintain active cognitive processing while completed ones are archived. The Zeigarnik effect explains why an interrupted conversation generates more post-interaction thought than one that reached a natural conclusion. Unresolved social encounters are open loops the brain keeps trying to close. Mystery is maintained through omission, not fabrication. The distinction matters ethically and practically. You do not need to create a false persona. You need to stop volunteering every piece of information before anyone asks for it. The gaps that other people fill with their imagination are almost always more flattering than anything you could narrate.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
**Desire is powered by incompleteness.** Full access shuts down the brain's anticipation circuit. Partial access keeps it firing. The dopamine setup responds to the possibility of reward, not the reward itself, and full access eliminates the possibility.
