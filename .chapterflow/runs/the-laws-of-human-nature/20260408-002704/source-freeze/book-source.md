## Official Description Summary

Across the frozen official materials, Greene frames the book as a practical guide to decoding motives, recognizing self-deception, mastering one's own emotions, building empathy, seeing through masks, resisting conformity, and developing purpose. The book is built around a sequence of "laws" about recurring human tendencies rather than around a single narrative argument.

## Early Baseline Chapter Support

### Chapter 1: Introduction

- safe claim range:
  - human nature is easiest to misread when we trust our first interpretation too quickly
  - the reader has to include personal blind spots in the analysis
  - the method should sharpen perception without degrading into paranoia

### Chapter 2: The Law of Irrationality

- frozen support identifies:
  - Pericles and Athens as the load-bearing historical anchor
  - the Athenians' decline after Pericles's death as a warning about emotional contagion and short-term reaction
  - financial bubbles as recurring examples of collective irrationality
  - Milton Erickson as a counter-model for disciplined observation and emotional control

### Chapter 3: The Law of Narcissism

- frozen support identifies:
  - healthy versus deep narcissism
  - Joseph Stalin and Leo Tolstoy as destructive forms
  - Shackleton as a healthier model tempered by empathy
