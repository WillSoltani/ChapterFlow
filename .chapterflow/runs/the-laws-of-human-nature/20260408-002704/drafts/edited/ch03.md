Stalin built power by controlling the emotional field around him. Greene uses that fact to sharpen the law of narcissism early: the deepest narcissist does not merely like admiration. He needs other people to function as mirrors, props, and targets. Once that need rules the social field, criticism feels like injury, disagreement feels like betrayal, and control starts looking necessary.

The chapter matters because Greene refuses the easy version of the term. Narcissism is not reserved for a few theatrical villains. Everyone begins with strong self-love. We all monitor status, replay slights, and drift toward interpretations that keep the self at the center. The real distinction is depth. Ordinary narcissism can still absorb other minds. Deep narcissism keeps collapsing them back into its own need for reassurance, superiority, or protection.

Tolstoy and Sonya bring that mechanism into intimate life. In a relationship ruled by self-reference, the point is no longer to understand or repair. The point becomes winning the emotional case. Each person listens mainly for injury, not for information. That is why narcissism is so corrosive. It turns conversation into mirror maintenance and leaves reciprocity too thin to survive pressure.

Greene's corrective is empathy, but he defines it as disciplined outward attention rather than softness. You suspend the automatic assumption that the scene is about your wound, your image, or your entitlement. You study the other person's interior weather. What do they want, fear, hide, or defend? That act does not erase your interests. It simply stops your interests from colonizing the whole interpretation.

Shackleton shows what healthier self-regard looks like. He was not meek, selfless, or vague about authority. He had presence and conviction, yet he kept the morale and fear of the crew in view. His self-belief remained answerable to reality and to other people. That is the chapter's deeper reversal. A person can have a strong self without needing to feed on constant confirmation.

The hard edge is that empathy still needs boundaries. It is not your job to heal every manipulator through understanding, and it is not a command to become less distinct. The gain is accuracy. When you stop forcing every scene to reinforce the self, you begin reading people more cleanly. That progress creates the next challenge, because people still hide behind surfaces, roles, and studied appearances. You need outward attention first, then sharper observation.
