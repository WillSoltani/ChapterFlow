Coco Chanel matters to Greene because she understood that desire weakens when possession feels too easy. She built allure through style, independence, restraint, and a sense that access to her world could not be fully bought or fully explained on demand. The lesson is larger than fashion. Human beings often want most intensely what leaves room for fantasy.

That is the core of covetousness. People do not desire only what is useful. They desire what feels scarce, elevated, partially withheld, or just beyond comfortable ownership. Once certainty rises and mystery collapses, the charge often weakens. The object may be the same, but the mind stops inflating it with projection.

Oversupply therefore has a cost. When you are constantly available, constantly explaining, constantly proving, or constantly offering more access than the moment has earned, you can flatten your value. Greene is not arguing for deception. He is arguing for pace, selectivity, and enough distance for imagination to stay alive. Desire often feeds on what it cannot fully inventory.

The chapter also highlights transgression. What feels slightly forbidden, exclusive, or status-lifting acquires extra pull because people imagine not just the object itself but the identity change attached to getting it. Wanting becomes tangled with prestige, fantasy, and the thrill of crossing a line into a more charged zone.

But the chapter refuses cheap tactics. Real allure is not random unavailability. It is self-possession. You carry standards, substance, and an independent center of gravity, so access to you or your work cannot be endlessly extracted. Hollow withholding fails because scarcity without value eventually looks like emptiness wearing a costume.

This law is also a warning about your own mind. If your desire spikes mainly when access becomes uncertain, scarcity may be inflating your judgment. The thing can start looking more valuable precisely when you are seeing it less clearly. That is why chapter 6 naturally leads into chapter 7. Once desire is activated, perspective gets easier to lose.
