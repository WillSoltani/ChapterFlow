Milton Erickson recovered from paralysis by doing something most healthy adults forget to do. He watched. Because he could not move much, he learned to notice the contradiction between what people said and what their bodies, timing, and tone leaked anyway. Greene uses him to open the law of role-playing because social life is full of managed surfaces and incomplete disclosures.

The chapter refuses the childish version of the point. Masks are not rare signs of villainy. People learn early that direct expression can be punished, ignored, or misunderstood, so they start shaping a social face. They smile, soften, flatter, perform competence, and hide envy or fear. That does not mean every performance is fake. It means words alone are never the whole read.

Leakage matters because the body and the sequence of behavior keep revealing pressures the surface cannot perfectly contain. The pause before agreement, the bright words with tightened posture, the warmth that disappears when attention shifts elsewhere, the pattern that changes under stress. Greene wants the reader to notice clusters, not magical single tells.

That warning matters because observation can be ruined by projection. Othello matters here as a model of what happens when jealousy starts reading the world before the signals are weighed cleanly. The reader who wants certainty too fast will start seeing proof everywhere and reality nowhere. Good observation needs calm, context, and revision.

Greene also adds a second demand: since everyone is judged by appearances, you have to play your own role well. Presenting yourself clearly, confidently, and with some drama is not automatically fraud. It becomes manipulative only when the performance is meant to replace substance instead of expressing it more effectively.

The chapter therefore ends with a limit. Signals reveal current states and tensions, but they do not by themselves settle character. A tight smile might show resentment today and exhaustion tomorrow. To judge character, you need repeated behavior over time. That is the next step after mask-reading: moving from momentary leakage to durable pattern.
