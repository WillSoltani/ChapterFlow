Greene closes with Flannery O'Connor because mortality can sharpen life instead of shrinking it. O'Connor lived with a clearer sense of fragility than most people allow themselves, and that awareness did not make her trivial or numb. It made her serious.

That is the law of death denial. Human beings know they will die, yet most of the time they keep that fact emotionally distant. They live as if time were broader, safer, and more renewable than it is. Greene's point is not that people should become gloomy. It is that denial quietly feeds vanity, petty conflict, drift, and false importance.

This is why mortality matters to the whole book. When people imagine endless time, they protect ego more easily, waste attention on small slights, postpone what matters, and treat other people as scenery in their private dramas. Death denial keeps self-deception comfortable.

Flannery O'Connor matters because she anchors a different response. Greene wants the reader to see that awareness of finitude can produce proportion. What is urgent becomes clearer. What is trivial loses some of its spell. Empathy can deepen because everyone around you is living under the same limit.

The practical signs of denial are ordinary. A workplace argument expands as if reputational irritation were the center of existence. A personal goal is delayed year after year because tomorrow feels endless. A school rivalry becomes theatrical because no one is remembering how small the performance will look against a finite life.

Greene's answer is steady meditation on mortality, not performance about mortality. The task is to remember finitude often enough that priorities reorder themselves. That memory can make gratitude less sentimental, ambition less vain, and connection less disposable.

That placement after generational myopia matters. Chapter 18 widened the lens to historical mood and timing. Now Greene narrows it to the one limit every generation shares. The closing loop is severe and clarifying: self-knowledge becomes harder to fake when time is no longer treated as endless.
