Howard Hughes could look like proof that daring, intelligence, and glamour were enough to make a person formidable. Greene uses him for the opposite reason. Hughes's talent and legend drew people in, but the deeper pattern showed rigidity, compulsion, suspicion, and decline. The point is not to gossip about a famous eccentric. It is to show how easily people confuse surface brilliance with strong character.

Character, in Greene's framing, is a durable pattern. It is the emotional and behavioral structure that keeps repeating when novelty fades, pressure rises, and self-presentation becomes harder to maintain. Charm can be improvised. Character has to survive boredom, frustration, delay, criticism, and success. That is why the reader is asked to look at repeated conduct, not isolated moments.

Compulsion matters because people keep returning to the same inner grooves. One person blames, another avoids, another overcontrols, another needs admiration before they can function. These patterns become most visible when plans fail or when life stops cooperating. Small behaviors matter here: how someone treats support staff, how they handle a missed deadline, how they talk about their own mistakes, how quickly they learn after friction. Greene wants the reader to study the pattern before investing trust.

Strong character therefore looks less glamorous than many people expect. It appears in steadiness, work ethic, adaptability, honesty about limits, and the ability to absorb frustration without immediately turning it into blame or collapse. Weak character often hides behind talent, intensity, wounded stories, or recurring promises. The problem is not that weak-character people never do anything impressive. The problem is that the underlying pattern makes them expensive to trust.

This is where many readers lose discipline. They start grading apologies instead of behavior. A moving admission, a convincing explanation, or a dramatic vow to change can temporarily soften judgment. Greene's point is harsher and more useful: if the pattern remains, the character evidence remains. Compassion does not require strategic blindness. You can understand why someone is difficult without pretending the difficulty has disappeared.

The chapter finally turns the standard inward. The reader is not only judging others. Character strengthens through repeated contact with reality: finishing work, enduring frustration, telling the truth faster, adapting instead of defending, and choosing long-term credibility over short-term emotional relief. That makes this chapter a bridge. If chapter 4 taught how to read the present moment, chapter 5 teaches how to judge what keeps returning over time.
