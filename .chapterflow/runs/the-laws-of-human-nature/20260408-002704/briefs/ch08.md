Chapter Number: 8
Chapter Title: Soften People's Resistance by Confirming Their Self-Opinion: The Law of Defensiveness
Position In Book: seventh law chapter, chapter 8 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that people resist influence less because they love truth and more because they are defending a self-opinion they need to preserve. To move them, the reader must lower that ego alarm by respecting how the person sees themselves and by letting change feel self-directed rather than humiliating.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's ego-resistance chapter. It shifts from correcting the reader's own time horizon to handling other people's self-protective reactions without triggering open opposition.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses Lyndon B. Johnson to show influence built on reading what other people needed to feel about themselves.
Then the author defines self-opinion and explains why direct correction, moral superiority, and public pressure trigger defensiveness.
Then the author concludes that effective influence works through affirmation, indirect pressure, face-saving, and guided discovery rather than blunt ego collision.

Required Anchors:
- Lyndon B. Johnson as the operational anchor for reading self-opinion and reducing resistance.
- self-opinion as the inner image people defend when they feel corrected or exposed.
- affirmation, face-saving, and indirect influence as the practical tools.

Specific Applications:

Common Misreadings:
- treating confirmation of self-opinion as empty flattery
- assuming influence means never confronting anything directly
- confusing face-saving with dishonesty or surrender

Counterarguments or Limits:
Some resistance is principled and some people are manipulative, but the chapter argues that unnecessary ego threat makes almost every correction harder. Respecting self-opinion should create better conditions for truth, not replace truth.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
Once the reader can create distance from their own emotional urgency, the next challenge is understanding why other people still resist change even when the facts are clear.
Next Chapter Bridge:
When self-opinion hardens inwardly, it becomes attitude. The next chapter turns from other people's resistance to the reader's own tendency to shape life through self-sabotaging attitudes.
