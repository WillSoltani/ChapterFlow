Chapter Title: Reconnect to the Masculine or Feminine Within You: The Law of Gender Rigidity
Position In Book: twelfth law chapter, chapter 13 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that people often overidentify with a narrow script of masculinity or femininity and repress the complementary traits that would make them more whole. That rigidity distorts behavior, weakens judgment, and encourages projection onto others.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the one-sided-identity chapter. It follows grandiosity by showing another way the self hardens into a caricature, this time through exaggerated gender coding rather than inflated success.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses Caterina Sforza to show a figure who embodied force and strategic toughness without fitting a narrow feminine script.
Then the author uses anima and animus to define the disowned complement inside the psyche and to explain why rigid self-identification breeds projection.
Then the author concludes that the reader must integrate rather than caricature masculine and feminine capacities, becoming more flexible, perceptive, and less reactive.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter is not denying sex differences or asking for personality sameness. It argues that overidentification with one side of the psyche narrows human range and often makes people hostile to what they secretly need.
