Chapter Title: Elevate Your Perspective: The Law of Shortsightedness
Position In Book: sixth law chapter, chapter 7 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that people slide into bad judgments when they let immediate emotion, crowd energy, and short-term relief dominate their time horizon. The reader must therefore create distance, widen perspective, and train themselves to see consequences over longer stretches of time.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's horizon-correction chapter. It moves from desire itself into the perspective damage that desire, panic, and immediate pressure can cause.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses the South Sea Bubble to show how crowds lose perspective when greed and urgency collapse the time horizon.
Then the author turns to Maria Mitchell as the counterexample of patient observation, distance, and disciplined perspective.
Then the author concludes that the reader must use delay, reframing, wider time scales, and strategic distance so immediate emotion does not dictate long-range choices.

Required Anchors:
- the South Sea Bubble as the warning about crowd heat, greed, and compressed time horizons.
- Maria Mitchell as the model of disciplined observation and wider perspective.
- distance, time scale, and second-order consequences as the chapter's practical tools.

Specific Applications:

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
Urgency is sometimes real, but the chapter argues that even under pressure the reader can widen the frame enough to avoid blind reaction. Perspective should sharpen action, not replace it.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
Once desire is activated, the next danger is that the reader starts seeing only the near-term object and loses the larger horizon.
Next Chapter Bridge:
With perspective restored, the next chapter turns to defensiveness and how self-opinion shapes resistance to influence.
