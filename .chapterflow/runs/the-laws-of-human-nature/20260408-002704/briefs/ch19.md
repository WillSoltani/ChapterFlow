Chapter Title: Meditate on Our Common Mortality: The Law of Death Denial
Position In Book: eighteenth law chapter, chapter 19 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that people push mortality out of awareness because it is frightening, but that death denial quietly feeds vanity, pettiness, aggression, and drift. Facing finitude more directly restores seriousness, empathy, gratitude, and clearer purpose.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the closing perspective chapter. It gathers the book's themes of self-blindness, aggression, conformity, and timing into a final claim that mortality awareness cuts through illusion and gives life proportion.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses Flannery O'Connor to show a figure whose awareness of fragility sharpened seriousness rather than collapsing into despair.
Then the author defines death denial as the habit of treating finitude as distant or unreal, emphasizing how that denial fuels vanity, trivial conflict, and avoidance.
Then the author concludes that the reader should meditate on mortality not morbidly but as a way to clarify priorities, deepen empathy, and live with more urgency and proportion.

Counterarguments or Limits:
Mortality awareness can turn sentimental or theatrical if it stays abstract. Greene's point is narrower: death becomes clarifying when it is faced steadily enough to reduce vanity and sharpen human connection rather than dramatize suffering.
