Chapter Title: Beware the Fragile Ego: The Law of Envy
Position In Book: tenth law chapter, chapter 11 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that envy is one of the most hidden human emotions because another person's success, beauty, talent, or ease can make the ego feel diminished. Since envy feels shameful, it often disguises itself as criticism, coolness, moral judgment, or poisonous praise.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the disguised-status-pain chapter. It takes the hidden emotional material from repression and isolates one feeling people almost never confess directly, then shows how it poisons relationships through comparison and indirect attack.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses Mary Shelley and Jane Williams to show envy appearing inside friendship, admiration, and comparison.
Then the author defines envy as ego pain triggered by another person's perceived advantage.
Then the author concludes that the reader must spot disguised envy in themselves and others before it turns into sabotage, poisonous praise, coldness, or secret pleasure at another's decline.

Required Anchors:
- Mary Shelley and Jane Williams as the central comparison pair.
- envy, comparison, poisonous praise, and status pain as the mechanism set.
- fragile ego as the condition that makes another person's gain feel like a personal loss.

Common Misreadings:

- assuming praise is always sincere when the tone is off

Counterarguments or Limits:
Not every criticism comes from envy, and not every comparison is toxic. The chapter's point is that fragile egos often disguise status pain instead of admitting it directly.
