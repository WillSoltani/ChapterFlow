Chapter Title: Determine the Strength of People's Character: The Law of Compulsive Behavior
Position In Book: fourth law chapter, chapter 5 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that the reliable way to judge people is not through charm, mood, or talent but through durable character patterns. Deep habits and compulsions keep repeating, especially under stress, frustration, power, and setback. The reader must therefore study how people work, adapt, apologize, recover, and treat others across time instead of trusting the polished surface.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's due-diligence chapter. It turns the observational gains of chapter 4 into longer-range judgment about trust, reliability, and inner strength.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author distinguishes momentary signals from deeper character by defining character as repeated emotional and behavioral pattern.
Then the author uses Howard Hughes to show how brilliance, glamour, and daring can hide compulsion, rigidity, and collapse in judgment.
Then the author concludes that the reader must judge people through consistency, relation to work, response to adversity, adaptability, and the gap between promises and repeated conduct.

Required Anchors:
- Howard Hughes as the warning that talent and glamour can conceal brittle character and escalating compulsion.
- repeated behavior under pressure as the chapter's main evidence standard.
- relation to work, frustration, power, and collaboration as practical character tests.

Specific Applications:
- deciding whether to hire, promote, or partner with a talented but unstable person

Common Misreadings:
- confusing charisma, intelligence, or talent with character
- freezing someone forever based on one bad moment
- using compassion as an excuse to ignore repeated harm

Counterarguments or Limits:
People can change, but change is slow and evidenced through new patterns rather than declarations. The chapter should avoid both cynicism and naive forgiveness.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
Reading masks helps with the immediate social read, but durable trust requires something deeper than mixed signals.
Next Chapter Bridge:
Once the reader can judge steadiness and weakness more clearly, the next problem is desire itself and how scarcity, mystery, and fantasy distort pursuit.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- The chapter should sharpen trust judgment without turning into fatalism.
- It should preserve room for growth while insisting that repeated pattern matters more than apology theater.

Moral Complexity:
High. The reader is asked to judge more soberly without denying compassion or the possibility of change.
