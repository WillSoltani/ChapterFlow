Chapter Title: See Through People's Masks: The Law of Role-playing
Position In Book: third law chapter, chapter 4 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that people naturally manage their surfaces because social life rewards controlled performance. Words alone therefore give an incomplete read. True feelings, motives, and tensions leak through posture, timing, expression, and contradiction. The practical demand is to become a calmer observer of clusters and patterns without slipping into theatrical certainty or paranoia.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's perceptual-method chapter. It moves from empathy to observation and teaches the reader how to notice what words conceal while also warning that projection can corrupt the read.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author normalizes masking as a human adaptation learned early in life.
Then the author uses Milton Erickson to show that contradiction between words and signals can reveal hidden attitudes more reliably than polished language alone.
Then the author concludes that the reader must study people in context, read clusters instead of single tells, and manage their own role deliberately.
