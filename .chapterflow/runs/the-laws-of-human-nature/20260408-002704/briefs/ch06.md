Chapter Title: Become an Elusive Object of Desire: The Law of Covetousness
Position In Book: fifth law chapter, chapter 6 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that people do not desire only what is useful or plainly available. Desire intensifies around what appears scarce, partially withheld, independent, slightly transgressive, or difficult to possess. The reader must understand how fantasy and absence distort value so they do not oversupply themselves or get captured by the wanting loop in others.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's desire-dynamics chapter. It moves from judging character into understanding why people still chase what stays partly out of reach.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses Coco Chanel to show how style, self-possession, and controlled distance can amplify desire.
Then the author argues that easy access reduces intensity while scarcity, strategic absence, and fantasy inflate wanting.
Then the author concludes that the reader should cultivate independence and selective pacing without becoming cruel, hollow, or transparently manipulative.

Counterarguments or Limits:
Scarcity without substance eventually collapses. The chapter should protect against hollow game-playing and stress that desire becomes durable only when the object still carries real value.
