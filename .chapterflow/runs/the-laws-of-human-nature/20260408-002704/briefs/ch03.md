Chapter Title: Transform Self-Love into Empathy: The Law of Narcissism
Position In Book: second law chapter, chapter 3 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that everyone begins with a strong investment in the self, but that investment becomes dangerous when it hardens into deep narcissism. Deep narcissists experience other people mainly as mirrors, stage props, or threats, so criticism wounds them, empathy thins out, and control becomes seductive. The chapter's practical answer is not self-hatred. It is trained outward attention. Empathy redirects energy away from constant self-reference and toward the actual interior lives of other people.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter clarifies that narcissism is not a rare villain trait. It is a baseline human condition with different depths and outcomes. That distinction prevents the book from turning into simple people-spotting and forces the reader back toward self-audit.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author normalizes ordinary narcissism as a universal starting point rooted in self-love and early attention needs.
Then the author distinguishes deep narcissists, narcissistic leaders, theatrical narcissists, and healthier forms of self-regard through historical examples.
Then the author concludes that empathy is the only durable corrective because it shifts attention outward without requiring self-erasure.

- Tolstoy and Sonya as a mutually damaging couple in which self-absorption corrodes intimacy.
- Shackleton as a model of healthy narcissism whose self-belief remains answerable to the morale and survival of others.
