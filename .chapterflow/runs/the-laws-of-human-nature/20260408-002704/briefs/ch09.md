Chapter Title: Change Your Circumstances by Changing Your Attitude: The Law of Self-Sabotage
Position In Book: eighth law chapter, chapter 9 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that people often recreate the very conditions they say they hate because their attitude filters what they notice, how they interpret events, and what they do next. A freer attitude can widen choice, while a resentful or fatalistic attitude quietly produces self-sabotage.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the inward-reframing chapter. It turns from reading other people's defensiveness to confronting the reader's own stance toward life and the repeated conditions that stance keeps reproducing.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses Anton Chekhov to show a person who resisted bitterness and widened his life through a different attitude.
Then the author defines self-sabotage as attitude turning into repeated behavior and repeated circumstance.
Then the author concludes that the reader must spot resentment, victim-posture, and narrowing interpretation before those habits keep remaking the same life.
