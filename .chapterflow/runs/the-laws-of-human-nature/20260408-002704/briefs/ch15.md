Chapter Title: Resist the Downward Pull of the Group: The Law of Conformity
Position In Book: fourteenth law chapter, chapter 15 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that groups exert a downward pull on judgment. Under pressure to belong, people absorb moods, imitate faction behavior, and surrender individual conscience more easily than they expect.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the group-pressure chapter. It follows aimlessness by showing what often fills the vacuum when inner direction is weak: the crowd, the faction, and the desire not to stand alone.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses Gao Jianhua, Gao Yuan, and the Cultural Revolution to show how collective fervor can engulf ordinary people and normalize moral collapse.
Then the author defines conformity as the pull of group mind: imitation, emotional contagion, and loyalty to faction over judgment.
Then the author concludes that the reader must create distance, watch for collective heat, and hold to a stronger individual line before the crowd rewrites reality.

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
Groups can support courage, learning, and coordinated action. Greene's target is narrower: the collective pull that weakens independent judgment, intensifies emotion, and makes people participate in things they would reject alone.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
Chapter 14 argued that purpose gives life a stronger internal direction. Chapter 15 shows why that matters: without direction and distance, the group can easily supply both mood and mission.
Next Chapter Bridge:
Once the reader sees how groups pull people downward, the next chapter studies leadership and fickleness, asking how influence can be won without becoming captive to unstable followership.
