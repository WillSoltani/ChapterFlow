Chapter Title: See the Hostility Behind the Friendly Facade: The Law of Aggression
Position In Book: sixteenth law chapter, chapter 17 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that aggression is a permanent part of human nature, but it often appears in disguised form because direct hostility is costly. Smiles, delays, moral language, friendliness, and politeness can all conceal drives toward control, punishment, humiliation, or sabotage.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the concealed-hostility chapter. It follows fickleness by showing what often sits beneath unstable followership: resentment that cannot safely go fully open, so it travels through indirect pressure, passive aggression, and covert control.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses John D. Rockefeller to show a figure whose power and control did not require theatrical rage to become formidable.
Then the author defines aggression as the drive to dominate, injure, punish, or control, emphasizing that modern social life often pushes the behavior into indirect forms such as passive aggression and strategic politeness.
Then the author concludes that the reader must stop reading friendliness as innocence by default, learn to spot pressure disguised as civility, and respond with distance, pattern recognition, and firmer boundaries.

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as permission for paranoia about everyone

- flattening passive aggression into mere awkwardness or poor communication

Counterarguments or Limits:
Not every conflict cue reveals deep hostility, and people can be blunt or irritated without being strategically aggressive. Greene's point is narrower: readers should stop assuming friendliness means harmlessness and start tracking repeated patterns of concealed pressure.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
Chapter 16 showed how unstable followers can hide resentment behind compliance and pleasantness. Chapter 17 studies that hidden pressure directly, asking how aggression survives when open attack is socially expensive.
Next Chapter Bridge:
Once the reader learns to read covert hostility at the personal level, the next chapter widens the frame to historical mood and timing, asking how generations misread the moment they are living through.
