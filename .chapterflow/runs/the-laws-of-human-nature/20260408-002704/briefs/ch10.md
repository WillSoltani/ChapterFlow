Chapter Title: Confront Your Dark Side: The Law of Repression
Position In Book: ninth law chapter, chapter 10 of 19

Core Claim:
Greene argues that people repress traits and impulses that conflict with the image they want to keep of themselves, but repression does not erase those forces. It pushes them into the shadow, where they leak out through projection, moral intensity, sudden eruptions, and self-sabotaging behavior.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the buried-material chapter. It goes below attitude and asks what the reader has pushed out of awareness, turning the law sequence from surface pattern into the hidden engine underneath it.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses Richard Nixon to show a public persona of control and discipline undermined by denied insecurity, hostility, and paranoia.
Then the author uses Carl Jung's shadow framework to define repression as disowning traits that still remain active.
Then the author concludes that the reader must stop projecting the shadow outward, notice leakage, and integrate darker traits consciously rather than pretending they do not exist.

Counterarguments or Limits:
Not every irritation is proof of repression, and the chapter does not glorify dark impulses. Integration means seeing what is there clearly and containing it more honestly, not indulging it.
