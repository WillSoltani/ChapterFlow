#chapter-outline-template.md
Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that repression hides unwanted traits rather than removing them, and that those traits leak outward through projection, overreaction, and distorted behavior.

Opening Move:
Open on Richard Nixon as a compelling image of a disciplined public persona undermined by a denied inner side.

Anchor Allocation:
- Richard Nixon used in P1 and P2
- Carl Jung used in P2 and P3
- shadow, repression, projection, and leakage used in P3 through P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with Nixon and the split between controlled image and darker leakage
- P2: define the shadow through Jung and explain repression as disowning, not erasing
- P3: show how projection and leakage work in ordinary behavior
- P4: explain the practical signs that the shadow is active
- P5: show integration as ownership plus restraint rather than indulgence
- P6: bridge to envy by naming it as one shadow feeling people hate admitting

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between integration and indulgence. The goal is to recognize the shadow clearly enough that it stops running behavior from below awareness.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must admit darker impulses without turning that honesty into self-loathing or moral permission.

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: repression as disowning, not erasing
- Takeaway 2: Richard Nixon as the split-self anchor
- Takeaway 3: Carl Jung and the shadow framework
- Takeaway 4: projection and leakage in ordinary life
- Takeaway 5: integration versus indulgence
- Takeaway 6: bridge from shadow to envy

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): spot projection inside moral overreaction to a coworker
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, personal): see how a partner accused the other of traits they would not own in themselves
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, school): show scapegoating and denied insecurity in a student group
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): reveal that a polished manager's control hid the aggression they blamed on others
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): choose between admitting a jealous impulse or disguising it as superior judgment
- Scenario 6 (before_after, school): show how naming competitiveness reduces passive sabotage

Next-Chapter Bridge:
Once the reader understands shadow and projection, the next chapter can isolate envy as a particularly hidden and socially disguised feeling.

Genericity Risks:
- becoming vague "embrace your dark side" advice
- using Jung as mystique instead of mechanism
- turning integration into indulgence

Forbidden Drift:
- pop spirituality
- therapy clichés with no concrete behavior
- permission talk disguised as honesty
