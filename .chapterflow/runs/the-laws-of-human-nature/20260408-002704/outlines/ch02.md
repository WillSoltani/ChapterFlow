#chapter-outline-template.md
Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that emotion quietly hijacks judgment, that this happens in crowds as well as in individuals, and that rationality must be built by creating distance from the heat of the moment.

Opening Move:
Open on Pericles urging restraint while Athens wants the emotionally satisfying response to Sparta.

Anchor Allocation:
- Pericles used in P1 and P2
- Athens after Pericles and collective ruin used in P2 and P4
- financial bubbles used in P4
- Milton Erickson or disciplined-observer counter-model used in P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with Pericles versus the city's appetite for reaction
- P2: show what Athens becomes after strategic discipline is lost
- P3: generalize the mechanism from history to personal life
- P4: show crowd-level irrationality through recurring bubbles and contagion
- P5: define rationality as distance, timing, and self-observation, not emotional numbness
- P6: bridge into narcissism by showing that self-love distorts the social field even when emotion cools

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The central distinction is between having emotions and being governed by them before conscious thought can inspect the scene.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Rationality cannot mean becoming emotionless. The deeper tension is that emotion is necessary data, but terrible master.

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: emotion arrives before reason
- Takeaway 2: rationalization after the fact
- Takeaway 3: Pericles versus crowd heat
- Takeaway 4: bubble and group contagion pattern
- Takeaway 5: practical distance and delay
- Takeaway 6: why rationality still leaves the next distortion of narcissism intact

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): delay a reactive email or meeting response
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, personal): reconstruct a fight and find the trigger that wrote the story
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, school): hear status threat inside a classroom disagreement
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predict intent under pressure, then discover the role of heat and timing
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): decide whether urgency is real or emotional contagion
- Scenario 6 (before_after, school): compare reactive versus delayed decision-making in a group setting

Next-Chapter Bridge:
Even after you cool the emotional surge, another distortion remains: the self-centered lens that makes empathy so hard.

Genericity Risks:
- turning the chapter into generic advice about staying calm
- overusing crowd examples without tying them back to personal judgment
- flattening Pericles into generic wise-leader language

Forbidden Drift:
- fake neuroscience or unsupported brain claims
- motivational bromides about emotional intelligence
- summary lines that treat irrationality as only anger rather than broader bias and rationalization
