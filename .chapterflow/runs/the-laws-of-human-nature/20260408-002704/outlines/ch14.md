#chapter-outline-template.md
Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand aimlessness as movement without organizing direction and purpose as the force that gathers energy into a steadier mission.

Opening Move:
Open on Martin Luther King Jr. as a figure whose gifts and urgency became powerful because they were organized by mission rather than left scattered.

Anchor Allocation:
- Martin Luther King Jr. used in P1 and P5
- aimlessness and purpose used in P2 through P6
- false purpose, drift, and direction used in P3 through P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with Martin Luther King Jr. and the contrast between talent alone and talent organized by mission
- P2: define aimlessness as drift, reactive motion, and life without an organizing direction
- P3: distinguish real purpose from false purpose, prestige pursuit, and borrowed ambition
- P4: show practical signs such as fragmentation, boredom, distraction, and dependence on external cues
- P5: explain how purpose organizes discipline, sacrifice, and sustained attention
- P6: bridge to conformity by showing how a purposeless self becomes easy for group pressure to steer

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between movement and direction. Purpose is not constant excitement or applause. It is the organizing mission that gives meaning to effort and filters distraction.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must avoid both destiny mysticism and productivity cliché, preserving purpose as a disciplined direction rather than a mood, brand, or instant revelation.

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: aimlessness as drift without organizing direction
- Takeaway 2: Martin Luther King Jr. as the anchor case
- Takeaway 3: real purpose versus false purpose
- Takeaway 4: practical signs of scattered energy and borrowed goals
- Takeaway 5: discipline, sacrifice, and sustained attention under mission
- Takeaway 6: bridge from purpose to resistance against group pull

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choose between reactive opportunity-chasing and a role that fits a deeper direction
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, personal): review boredom that came from living inside borrowed goals
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, school): show students confusing prestige with purpose
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): reveal that a busy high-performer is drifting because no mission organizes the work
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): choose between an admired path and a truer calling that requires discipline
- Scenario 6 (before_after, school): show how clear direction changes study, effort, and resistance to distraction

Next-Chapter Bridge:
Once the self has direction, the next danger is the downward pull of the group. Purpose has to become strong enough to resist collective drift.

Genericity Risks:
- drifting into generic inspiration language
- flattening purpose into passion or motivation
- using Martin Luther King Jr. only as reverence instead of as a direction anchor

Forbidden Drift:
- destiny slogans
- hustle rhetoric
- vague "follow your dreams" advice
