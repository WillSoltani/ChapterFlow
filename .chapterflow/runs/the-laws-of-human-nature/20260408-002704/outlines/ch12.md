Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand grandiosity as success-fed self-inflation that forgets luck, rejects limits, and invites overreach.

Opening Move:
Open on Michael Eisner as a convincing example of early success expanding into the belief that his instincts no longer needed real correction.

Anchor Allocation:
- Michael Eisner used in P1 and P2
- grandiosity and success delusion used in P2 and P3
- luck, limits, feedback, and overreach used in P3 through P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with Eisner's rise and the seductive expansion of self-trust after visible success
- P2: define grandiosity as confidence inflated past reality, with success misread as proof of special infallibility
- P3: explain why success is dangerous because praise narrows feedback and luck gets rewritten as pure genius
- P4: show practical signs such as impatience with correction, contempt for limits, and overextension
- P5: explain the antidote through reality testing, dissent, and memory of dependence on others
- P6: bridge to gender rigidity by showing how inflated identities harden into one-sided self-constructions

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between real confidence and grandiosity. Confidence stays in contact with limits and feedback. Grandiosity treats success as permission to stop listening.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must preserve ambition without romanticizing self-inflation, and must admit how success can damage self-knowledge precisely when things seem to be working.

Takeaway Count Lock:

- Hard: 6

- Takeaway 1: grandiosity as success-fed self-inflation
- Takeaway 2: Michael Eisner as the anchor case
- Takeaway 3: luck forgotten and rewritten as personal genius
- Takeaway 4: feedback collapse and overreach
- Takeaway 5: limits, dissent, and reality testing as antidotes

- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): choose between protecting a glowing self-image and facing ordinary limits
- Scenario 6 (before_after, school): show improvement once a high-performing student rebuilds a feedback loop
