#chapter-outline-template.md
Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that the first story the mind tells about other people is usually too fast, too flattering to the self, and too fragile to trust without pattern-based revision.

Opening Move:
Open on the same tense room producing several contradictory explanations before anyone has real evidence.

Anchor Allocation:
- official relationship-reading frame used in P2 and P5
- self-blindness and projection frame used in P3 and P4
- anti-paranoia boundary and bridge to emotion used in P5 and P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook the reader with the speed of post-conflict interpretation
- P2: define the book's problem as motive-reading inside human relationships
- P3: show that the observer's ego corrupts the read before it feels settled
- P4: explain the working method: compare story to sequence, words to repeated behavior
- P5: draw the boundary against paranoia and moral grandstanding
- P6: reward the reader with a sharper lens, then open the next question of emotion

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is not gullibility versus cynicism. It is interpretation governed by pattern versus interpretation governed by projection.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The same method that can sharpen perception can also become a vanity tool or paranoia machine if the reader applies it only outward.

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: first-interpretation danger
- Takeaway 2: the observer's own incentives
- Takeaway 3: pattern over declaration
- Takeaway 4: anti-paranoia boundary
- Takeaway 5: why chapter 2 on emotion has to come next

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): hold an ambiguous signal open long enough to compare it against pattern
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): reconstruct sequence after humiliation before assigning motive
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): separate behavior from assumed intent in a live conversation
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, school): predict motive too early and learn what missing context changes
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): distinguish painful familiarity from real evidence of a pattern
- Scenario 6 (before_after, work): demote first impression from verdict to clue

Next-Chapter Bridge:
If the first story arrives this quickly, what power is moving that fast before reason can intervene?
