Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand aggression as a constant human force that often hides behind friendliness, delay, guilt, or passive aggression rather than open attack.

Opening Move:
Open on John D. Rockefeller so the law begins with controlled, indirect power rather than cartoon rage.

Anchor Allocation:
- John D. Rockefeller used in P1, P3, and P5
- aggression, passive aggression, control, and hostility used in P2 through P6
- disguised resentment, obstruction, and baiting used in P3 through P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with Rockefeller as controlled aggression rather than theatrical anger
- P2: define aggression as the urge to dominate, punish, or control, often hidden by social polish
- P3: explain why modern aggression often becomes indirect through passive aggression, delays, and sabotage
- P4: show practical signs such as baiting, moralized pressure, strategic silence, and obstruction
- P5: explain how to respond with observation, boundaries, and reduced naivete
- P6: bridge from fickleness into aggression and from aggression into the wider generational lens of chapter 18

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between isolated irritation and repeated concealed hostility. The chapter is about patterns of pressure, punishment, or control hidden beneath socially acceptable behavior.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must avoid both paranoia and naivete. The chapter has to make covert aggression visible without turning every awkward interaction into proof of malice.
