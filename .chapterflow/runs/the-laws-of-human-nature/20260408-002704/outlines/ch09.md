#chapter-outline-template.md
Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that attitude is not a private mood but a force that shapes perception, action, and repeated circumstance.

Opening Move:
Open on Anton Chekhov as a vivid counterexample to bitterness and self-limiting interpretation.

Anchor Allocation:
- Anton Chekhov used in P1 and P2
- attitude and interpretation used in P2 and P3
- resentment, reframing, and freedom used in P4 and P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with Chekhov and the refusal to let hardship become a permanent inner posture
- P2: define self-sabotage as attitude becoming repeated pattern
- P3: explain how resentment and fatalistic interpretation narrow choice
- P4: show practical reframing without denial or fantasy
- P5: apply the law to work, personal life, and school
- P6: bridge to repression by showing how unexamined attitude hides deeper material

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between honest reframing and denial. Attitude changes what the reader notices and does next, but it does not magically erase reality.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must own their part in repeated patterns without using the law to blame themselves for everything.

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: attitude as a life-shaping lens
- Takeaway 2: Anton Chekhov as the anchor
- Takeaway 3: resentment and victim-posture as self-sabotage accelerants
- Takeaway 4: reframing and freedom
- Takeaway 5: repeated work, personal, and school patterns
- Takeaway 6: bridge from attitude to repression

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): stop reading one setback as proof of permanent defeat
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, personal): see how resentment recreated the same fight
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, school): compare fatalism with more active reframing
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): show how a cynical attitude quietly creates the expected bad result
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): choose between a grievance identity and a freer response
- Scenario 6 (before_after, school): show performance change after widening the interpretive lens

Next-Chapter Bridge:
Once repeated patterns are seen as partly attitude-driven, the next question is what deeper disowned material has been pushed down and is still leaking into behavior.

Genericity Risks:
- collapsing into motivation clichés
- sounding like blame rather than agency
- forgetting Chekhov and the resentment mechanism

Forbidden Drift:
- positivity slogans
- total personal-responsibility absolutism
- denial presented as reframing
