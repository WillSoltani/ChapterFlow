Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that people defend their self-opinion before they examine new information, and that influence improves when the reader preserves dignity while guiding change.

Opening Move:
Open on Lyndon B. Johnson as a vivid operator who understood that people can be moved more effectively through ego management than through frontal correction.

Anchor Allocation:
- Lyndon B. Johnson used in P1 and P2
- self-opinion and ego threat used in P2 and P3
- affirmation, face-saving, and indirect influence used in P4 and P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with Johnson reading what people needed to feel about themselves
- P2: define defensiveness as protection of self-opinion rather than rational debate
- P3: explain why direct correction, humiliation, and superiority trigger resistance
- P4: show practical tools of affirmation, indirect suggestion, and face-saving
- P5: apply the law to work, personal, and school feedback situations
- P6: bridge to self-sabotage by showing how defended self-opinion turns into hardened attitude

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's key distinction is between honest confirmation of a person's dignity and manipulative flattery. The mechanism is that ego threat closes people, while respected autonomy keeps them open longer.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must learn to lower resistance without becoming fake, submissive, or morally foggy.

- Hard: 6

- Takeaway 1: defensiveness as self-opinion protection
- Takeaway 2: Lyndon B. Johnson and operational influence
- Takeaway 3: ego threat, humiliation, and resistance
- Takeaway 4: affirmation, autonomy, and face-saving
- Takeaway 5: work, personal, and school feedback dynamics
- Takeaway 6: the bridge from defended self-opinion to self-sabotaging attitude

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): correct a teammate without attacking competence
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, personal): see how moral superiority made honest feedback fail
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, school): revise criticism so a peer can keep dignity and still improve
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): compare public pressure with private, face-saving influence
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): decide whether to force a confession or open a safer route to honesty
- Scenario 6 (before_after, school): show how feedback improves after starting from a student's self-image and agency

Next-Chapter Bridge:
If defended self-opinion keeps shaping what a person can accept, the next problem is how that inward attitude begins creating the very circumstances the person claims to hate.
