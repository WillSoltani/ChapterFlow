#chapter-outline-template.md
Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand fickleness as the instability of followership and see why authority weakens when leaders chase affection, overexpose themselves, or make their favor too predictable.

Opening Move:
Open on Queen Elizabeth I with William Cecil and Mary Queen of Scots in the frame so the law begins with a concrete contrast between preserved authority and authority made vulnerable.

Anchor Allocation:
- Queen Elizabeth I and William Cecil used in P1, P3, and P5
- Mary Queen of Scots used in P1 and P4 as the contrast anchor
- fickleness, authority, and leadership used in P2 through P6
- distance, favor, appearances, and overfamiliarity used in P3 through P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with Queen Elizabeth I, William Cecil, and Mary Queen of Scots as the authority contrast
- P2: define fickleness as unstable followership rather than simple disloyalty
- P3: explain why authority requires distance, appearances, and symbolic weight
- P4: show practical failure patterns such as overfamiliarity, visible neediness, and favoritism
- P5: explain how steadiness, controlled access, and fewer emotional bids preserve leadership
- P6: bridge from conformity into fickleness and from fickleness into the aggression hidden behind polite surfaces

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between authority and approval-seeking. A leader can be respected without being constantly liked, and the effort to force visible liking often weakens the authority people claim to want.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must avoid both naive authenticity language and cynical stagecraft. The chapter has to hold together real substance, symbolic distance, and the instability of public feeling without reducing leadership to manipulation alone.

- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): choose between holding a boundary and buying temporary approval
