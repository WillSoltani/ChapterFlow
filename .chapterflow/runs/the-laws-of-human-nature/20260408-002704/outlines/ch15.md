#chapter-outline-template.md
Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand conformity as the group's downward pull on judgment and see why distance and inner direction are necessary to resist faction heat.

Opening Move:
Open on Gao Jianhua, Gao Yuan, and the Cultural Revolution as a concrete demonstration of how collective fervor can engulf ordinary people.

Anchor Allocation:
- Gao Jianhua / Gao Yuan and Cultural Revolution used in P1 and P2
- conformity, group mind, and faction used in P2 through P6
- distance, judgment, and belonging pressure used in P4 through P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with Gao Jianhua, Gao Yuan, and the Cultural Revolution as the cost of collective heat
- P2: define conformity as group mind and downward pull rather than simple agreement
- P3: explain faction, emotional contagion, and moral outsourcing
- P4: show practical signs such as imitation, fear of dissent, and outsourced judgment
- P5: explain why distance, purpose, and independent judgment interrupt the group pull
- P6: bridge to fickleness by showing that groups are unstable and leaders must understand that volatility

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between healthy cooperation and group mind. Cooperation keeps judgment alive. Conformity asks the individual to borrow conviction from the crowd.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must avoid both naive collectivism and simplistic anti-group individualism, preserving the real danger of faction heat without treating every shared project as corruption.

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: conformity as downward group pull
- Takeaway 2: Gao Jianhua / Gao Yuan and Cultural Revolution as anchor set
- Takeaway 3: group mind, faction, and emotional contagion
- Takeaway 4: practical signs of moral outsourcing and fear of dissent
- Takeaway 5: distance and inner direction as resistance
- Takeaway 6: bridge from conformity to the instability of followership in fickleness

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choose whether to join a team pile-on because dissent feels risky
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, personal): review how a friend group shifted one person's standards
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, school): show faction loyalty overtaking judgment in a student bloc
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): reveal that moral certainty was borrowed from the team mood
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): choose between belonging and saying no to the group line
- Scenario 6 (before_after, school): show how distance and purpose reduce faction capture

Next-Chapter Bridge:
Once the reader sees how unstable and contagious group feeling can be, the next question becomes leadership: how do you work with collective moods without becoming hostage to them?
