## Chapter order and one-line intents
1. **Introduction** — the book opens by arguing that people are easier to misread than they appear because motives hide beneath stories, self-love distorts self-knowledge, and social life rewards masks.
2. **Master Your Emotional Self: The Law of Irrationality** — the first law argues that emotion directs judgment long before reason starts narrating what happened, which means self-command begins by learning to see biases, inflaming factors, and the delayed arrival of the rational self.
3. **Transform Self-Love into Empathy: The Law of Narcissism** — this law treats narcissism as a spectrum, arguing that nearly everyone begins from self-absorption and that the real developmental move is not to abolish self-love but to convert some of its energy into empathy.
4. **See Through People's Masks: The Law of Role-playing** — this law argues that social life runs on performance, so insight comes from seeing the masks people need, the second language beneath their words, and the gap between polished presentation and repeated conduct.
5. **Determine the Strength of People's Character: The Law of Compulsive Behavior** — this law argues that character is best read through recurring compulsions and repeated patterns, not through the image people project when things are going well.
6. **Become an Elusive Object of Desire: The Law of Covetousness** — this law argues that desire intensifies around distance, scarcity, projection, and rivalry, which means people often covet what stays partly withheld more than what is immediately available.
7. **Elevate Your Perspective: The Law of Shortsightedness** — this law argues that people repeatedly trade long-term position for emotional immediacy, which makes perspective itself a strategic asset.
8. **Soften People's Resistance by Confirming Their Self-Opinion: The Law of Defensiveness** — this law argues that direct attacks on identity produce resistance faster than truth can land, so influence works best when it confirms a person's self-opinion before trying to move behavior or belief.
9. **Change Your Circumstances by Changing Your Attitude: The Law of Self-Sabotage** — this law argues that attitude filters what people notice, attempt, and persist with, so a constricted attitude quietly sabotages options that an expansive attitude can still use.
10. **Confront Your Dark Side: The Law of Repression** — this law argues that people repress traits they dislike or fear in themselves, but those traits do not disappear. They leak out through projection, overreaction, moralizing, and contradiction.
11. **Beware the Fragile Ego: The Law of Envy** — this law argues that envy is one of the most disguised and corrosive human emotions because it converts another person's success into a wound to the self, especially among peers and near-equals.
12. **Know Your Limits: The Law of Grandiosity** — this law argues that success, praise, and insulation from contradiction can swell people into grandiosity, causing them to confuse self-image with scale, momentum with invulnerability, and desire with reality.
13. **Reconnect to the Masculine or Feminine Within You: The Law of Gender Rigidity** — this law argues that rigid gender performance narrows people by forcing assertive and receptive capacities into hostile camps instead of letting a fuller range of human traits become available.
14. **Advance with a Sense of Purpose: The Law of Aimlessness** — this law argues that energy without direction gets scattered into imitation, distraction, and false missions, while purpose concentrates effort across time.
15. **Resist the Downward Pull of the Group: The Law of Conformity** — this law argues that groups bend perception, intensify emotional contagion, and punish deviation, which means reality often needs an organized counterweight inside collective life.
16. **Make Them Want to Follow You: The Law of Fickleness** — this law argues that people are fickle in what they demand from leaders, so authority has to be established through steadiness, fairness, anti-neediness, and inner authority rather than entitlement.
17. **See the Hostility Behind the Friendly Façade: The Law of Aggression** — this law argues that aggression often hides behind friendliness, delay, ambiguity, moral pose, or passive resistance, which makes sophisticated aggression more dangerous than open conflict.
18. **Seize the Historical Moment: The Law of Generational Myopia** — this law argues that every generation mistakes its emotional weather for timeless reality, while the larger strategic advantage belongs to people who can read the spirit of the age without becoming trapped by it.
19. **Meditate on Our Common Mortality: The Law of Death Denial** — the final law argues that denying death inflates vanity, triviality, and fear, while sustained awareness of mortality clarifies proportion, gratitude, urgency, and what deserves real investment.

## Thin-chapter risks
- Ch13 is the source-thinnest law in the frozen bundle. Keep it integrated and careful instead of inflated.
- Ch19 is richly framed but easy to sentimentalize. Keep it hard, practical, and concrete.
