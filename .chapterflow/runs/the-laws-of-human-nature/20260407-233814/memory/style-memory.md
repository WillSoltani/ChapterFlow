# Style Memory — The Laws of Human Nature

Operating cheat sheet for writer, editor, and converter. Distilled from the v14 pack style files plus this run's source limits.

## Voice
Concrete, observant, psychologically sharp, and high-agency. Explain mechanisms beneath behavior without sounding clinical, mystical, or sermonizing. Use modern rooms, teams, classrooms, families, and social scenes instead of pretending to summarize unseen historical case studies.

## Book-specific posture
- This book is about hidden motives, distorted self-perception, masks, status, resentment, conformity, authority, and mortality.
- Keep the movement from self-diagnosis to social diagnosis visible.
- Favor mechanisms, patterns, and limits over moral performance.
- Treat every law as a disciplined lens, not as a license for cynicism or manipulation theater.

## Non-negotiables
- Use only claims supported by the brief and source sidecar.
- No em dashes.
- No fake page references, hidden anecdotes, or invisible quotes.
- No raw source splice into reader-facing prose.
- No contamination phrases, validator language, or prompt leakage.

## Banned phrasing
- Avoid thesis-first openings such as "In this chapter" or "The author argues."
- Avoid generic self-help filler like "unlock your potential," "level up," and "be the best version of yourself."
- Avoid treating readers as saints diagnosing monsters; the book always turns the lens back onto the self.

## Depth contract
- Easy: one clean frame, one live contrast, one practical implication.
- Medium: mechanism plus application, with one boundary, distortion, or cost.
- Hard: preserve the unresolved danger, moral ambiguity, or strategic limit.
