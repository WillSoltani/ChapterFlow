Core Claim:
First principles thinking means breaking a problem down until you reach the underlying truths that still hold after assumptions, convention, and analogy are stripped away. The model matters because people usually reason from what already exists, which produces incremental variation rather than genuine redesign. By separating the irreducible pieces from inherited framing, a thinker can rebuild from firmer ground and see possibilities that analogy hides.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is specifically about disassembly and rebuild, not about general skepticism. The Lego-house image, the contrast between analogy and first principles, the Five Whys, and Socratic questioning all point to one mechanism: progress becomes non-incremental when you stop treating current form as given.
