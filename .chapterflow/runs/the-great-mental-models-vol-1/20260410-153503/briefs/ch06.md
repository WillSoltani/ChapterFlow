Core Claim:
Necessity and sufficiency is the distinction between what an outcome requires and what is enough to produce it. The chapter matters because people often confuse a required condition with a complete explanation, or mistake one successful route for the only route. The safe downstream frame is practical: ask whether a factor must be present, whether it is enough by itself, or whether it is neither.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is specifically about condition logic, not about generic careful thinking. Its distinguishing move is to separate "must be there" from "will get you there." That distinction sharpens diagnosis after a plan fails, keeps people from over-crediting one cause, and improves the handoff from thought experiments to cleaner causal reasoning.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author introduces the difference between required conditions and enough conditions.
Then the author shows why confusion between the two creates reasoning errors.
Then the author turns the distinction into a practical check on explanations, plans, and predictions.
