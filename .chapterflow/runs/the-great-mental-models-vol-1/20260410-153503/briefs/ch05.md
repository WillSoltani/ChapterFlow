Core Claim:
Thought experiments are structured mental simulations that let a thinker explore implications, hidden assumptions, and consequences before reality imposes the full cost of testing. The model matters because many important questions are too expensive, impossible, or premature to test physically at first. By simplifying a situation and varying conditions in the mind, a person can expose what their theory really implies and where their understanding breaks.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is specifically about disciplined simulation, not daydreaming. Einstein's beam-of-light example, Mach's low-cost experimentation framing, and the trolley problem's moral pressure all point to one mechanism: a thought experiment works when it creates a constrained imagined world that reveals what a claim would force or forbid.

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
Thought experiments simplify aggressively and therefore can miss messy real-world variables. The chapter should keep them as preparation and clarification tools, not as universal substitutes for evidence.
