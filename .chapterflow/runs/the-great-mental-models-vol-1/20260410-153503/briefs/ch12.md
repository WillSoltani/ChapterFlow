Core Claim:
Hanlon's razor is the discipline of not rushing to explain bad outcomes by malice when ignorance, carelessness, confusion, or ordinary error may already explain enough. The model matters because motive inflation can worsen judgment and relationships.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about explanatory restraint with motive, not naive trust and not blanket excuse-making. Its edge is that a darker story should not be added when a simpler non-malevolent one already accounts for the outcome.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows how people often interpret harm through intention too quickly.
Then the author frames Hanlon's razor as checking whether ignorance or error may already explain the result.
Then the author shows how this restraint can improve judgment without denying that malice sometimes exists.
