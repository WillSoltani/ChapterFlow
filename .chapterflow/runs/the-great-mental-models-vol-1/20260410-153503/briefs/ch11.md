Core Claim:
Occam's razor is the discipline of preferring the explanation, structure, or path that is sufficient without adding unnecessary complexity. The model matters because people often mistake extra complication for depth when a simpler account may already do the needed work.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about parsimony, not about saying the simplest answer is always true. Its edge is disciplined sufficiency: use enough explanation to account for the situation, but do not add moving parts that have not earned their place.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows how complexity can be added too quickly.
Then the author frames Occam's razor as preferring the simpler adequate explanation.
Then the author shows how unnecessary complexity can waste effort and distort judgment.
