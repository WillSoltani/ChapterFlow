Core Claim:
Probabilistic thinking is the discipline of estimating likelihoods instead of pretending to certainty. The model matters because the future rarely arrives as one guaranteed line. Better decisions come from comparing possibilities, assigning rough confidence, and updating as new evidence appears rather than clinging to one absolute prediction.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is specifically about reasoning under uncertainty, not about generic open-mindedness. Its mechanism is to replace yes-or-no certainty with weighted possibilities. Bayesian thinking, fat-tailed curves, and asymmetries anchor the chapter's distinctive edge: not all uncertainty is shaped the same way, and not all mistakes cost the same.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows why certainty language misreads uncertain decisions.
Then the author frames probabilistic thinking as estimating and updating likelihoods.
Then the author shows why asymmetry and fat tails matter when the downside or upside is uneven.
