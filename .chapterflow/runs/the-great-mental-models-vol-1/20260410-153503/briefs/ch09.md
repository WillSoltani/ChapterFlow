Core Claim:
Causation versus correlation is the distinction between two things moving together and one thing actually producing the other. The chapter matters because people are easily seduced by patterns, especially when the pattern is vivid or repeated. The safe downstream frame is practical: a pattern may be real without being causal.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is specifically about association versus mechanism, not about generic skepticism. Its distinctive move is to keep the thinker from upgrading co-movement into explanation too quickly. The model sharpens diagnosis by asking whether there is a real causal link, a shared driver, a coincidence, or a misleading pattern.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows why patterns are tempting.
Then the author separates co-movement from true cause.
Then the author turns that distinction into a practical check on explanations, claims, and observed trends.
