Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand the difference between a condition an outcome requires and a condition that is enough to produce it.

Opening Move:
Open with a familiar reasoning error where someone mistakes a prerequisite for a guarantee.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1 and P2 for required versus enough distinction
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3 and P4 for misdiagnosis when the two are confused
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5 and P6 for practical transfer across plans, credentials, and habits

- P1: tension

- P5: anchor

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is that a necessary condition must be present but may not be enough, while a sufficient condition is enough but may not be the only possible route.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep the tension that people overclaim in both directions: they treat prerequisites as guarantees and successful routes as exclusive explanations.

Threshold Question:
Is this factor something the outcome cannot happen without, or is it something that by itself can make the outcome happen?
