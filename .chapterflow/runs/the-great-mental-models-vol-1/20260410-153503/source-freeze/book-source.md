## Frozen support notes by chapter

### Chapter 1: The Map Is Not the Territory

Official support is strong.

- The official Farnam Street excerpt explains that maps are reductions and cannot capture all of reality.
- The excerpt uses Newton and Einstein as a model-updating example.
- It stresses three practical rules: reality is the ultimate update, consider the cartographer, and maps can influence territories.
- The excerpt uses Jane Jacobs and city planning as a warning about forcing reality to fit an abstract model.
- The official mental-models page adds a modern practical frame: resumes, experts, and mapmaker choice.

### Chapter 2: Circle of Competence

Official support is moderate.

- The official mental-models page defines the circle as the domain where judgment is reliable.
- The key emphasis is boundary awareness, not circle size.
- The main practical warning is that moving outside the circle creates avoidable error.

### Chapter 3: Falsifiability

Official support is thinner in the frozen bundle.

- Treated as a supporting idea in the TOC.
- Later chapter work must stay narrow and avoid overclaiming unless stronger official support is frozen.

### Chapter 4: First Principles Thinking

Official support is moderate.

- The official mental-models page defines it as breaking problems down to fundamental truths.
- It is framed as hard, status-quo-challenging work rather than incremental improvement.

### Chapter 5: Thought Experiment

Official support is strong enough.

- The official thought-experiment article frames thought experiments as a way to test implications, expose hidden assumptions, and explore impossible or simplified scenarios.

### Chapter 6: Necessity and Sufficiency

Official support is thin in the frozen bundle and must stay narrow.

### Chapter 7: Second-Order Thinking

Official support is strong enough.

- The official article frames the model around consequence chains and the question "And then what?"

### Chapter 8: Probabilistic Thinking

Official support is strong enough.

- The official article frames the model as estimating likelihoods rather than pretending to certainty.
- It specifically points to Bayesian thinking, fat-tailed curves, and asymmetries.

### Chapter 9: Causation vs. Correlation

Official support is thin in the frozen bundle and must stay narrow.

### Chapter 10: Inversion

Official support is strong enough.

- The official article frames inversion as solving problems by addressing them backward and by avoiding stupidity rather than chasing brilliance directly.

### Chapter 11: Occam's Razor

Official support is moderate through the official mental-models page and TOC identity, but not yet rich in frozen excerpts.

### Chapter 12: Hanlon's Razor

Official support is moderate through TOC identity and general model references, but not yet rich in frozen excerpts.
