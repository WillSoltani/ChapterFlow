## Narrow factual support

- Thought experiments are structured mental simulations used to explore implications and outcomes.
- They are valuable because they let thinkers explore impossible, costly, or not-yet-testable situations.
- They simplify reality to expose core principles, hidden assumptions, and unintended consequences.
- The article gives historical anchors: Zeno, Descartes, Galileo, Laplace, Einstein, Plato, Wittgenstein, and the trolley problem lineage.
- Ernst Mach is used to frame thought experiments as low-cost experimentation that often precedes physical experimentation.
- Thought experiments matter in science, philosophy, ethics, and everyday planning.
- The chapter should keep the method practical: use imagination under structure, not free-floating fantasy.
