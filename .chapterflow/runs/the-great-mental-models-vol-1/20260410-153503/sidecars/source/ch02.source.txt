Title: Circle of Competence

## Narrow factual support

- The model is about knowing where you have earned knowledge and where you do not.
- The size of the circle is less important than knowing the perimeter.
- Inside the circle, you have an advantage. As you approach the perimeter, the advantage shrinks. Outside it, the advantage transfers to others.
- A chess grandmaster example is used to show that excellence in one domain does not generalize automatically.
- Rose Blumkin is used as an anchor for focused competence: she understood furniture, cash, and real estate, but not stocks.
- Charlie Munger is used as the chapter's practical voice: play where you have an edge; avoid games where others clearly understand more than you do.
- Confidence does not fall as fast as competence when people move beyond the perimeter.
- Expanding the circle is allowed, but pretending the edge is elsewhere than it is becomes dangerous.
