## Narrow factual support

- Karl Popper treats falsifiability as a criterion separating scientific theories from pseudoscientific ones.
- A theory counts as scientific when a possible observation or experiment could show it false.
- Confirmations are easy to find if you go looking for confirmations.
- Risky predictions matter more than easy confirmations.
- A strong theory forbids certain things from happening; the more it forbids, the more exposed it is to refutation.
- A theory that can explain every outcome after the fact is not stronger for that flexibility; it becomes harder to test.
- Confirmation bias pushes people to seek support instead of disproof.
- Wason's work is used to show that people naturally confirm instead of trying to falsify their ideas.
