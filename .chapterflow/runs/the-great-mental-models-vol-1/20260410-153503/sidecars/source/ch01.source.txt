# Chapter 1 Source Sidecar

Title: The Map Is Not the Territory
Primary frozen sources:
- https://fs.blog/map-and-territory/
- https://fs.blog/mental-models/

## Narrow factual support

- The chapter's core claim is that maps, models, and representations are reductions of reality rather than reality itself.
- The official excerpt uses financial statements, policy documents, manuals, and performance reviews as examples of useful but incomplete maps.
- Jeff Bezos is used as a concrete anchor: customer complaint anecdotes conflicted with the reported call-wait metric, and a live phone call showed the metric was missing the territory.
- Newtonian physics and Einstein's special relativity are used to show that an older map can remain useful while still being incomplete or superseded in some domains.
- The excerpt explicitly says good users of maps should know where the map works and where it does not.
- The chapter names three practical principles:
  1. Reality is the ultimate update.
  2. Consider the cartographer.
  3. Maps can influence territories.
- Jane Jacobs is the key anchor for the third principle. Her city-planning critique is used to show how people try to force living cities to fit abstract plans.
- The mental-models reference page adds a hiring example: a resume can look excellent on paper while the person cannot actually do the job.
- The same page also reinforces the rule to choose cartographers carefully when using maps made by other people.
