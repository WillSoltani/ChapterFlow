# Chapter 22 — Canonical Draft

The easier story is already sitting there before we choose it. That is the safest way into `At the Center of All Our Problems Is Us`. Chapter 21 argued that abstraction keeps winning when leaders let numbers outrank people. Chapter 22 most likely asks the uncomfortable question that follows. Why do we keep helping that happen even while knowing the abstraction is thinner than reality? The title gives a severe answer. At the center of the problem is not only the system out there. It is also us inside it.

That only works if the chapter keeps its balance. It is not saying structure, incentives, and pressure stop mattering. It is saying we are not innocent bystanders inside them. We often prefer the cleaner score, the faster win, the more flattering explanation, the safer silence, or the more comfortable story. Then we rationalize that preference as necessity, efficiency, realism, or just how the world works. The system may shape the choice, but we still keep making it.

That is why Part 7 matters. `A Society of Addicts` most safely reads here as a chapter about repetition and appetite, not as a clinical claim. The chapter is pointing toward the way people keep returning to what gives quick relief even when it weakens something more durable. In this book's terms, that can mean preferring self-protection over trust, convenience over candor, or simplification over the messier truth about people and responsibility.

The trust framing gives the lesson its stakes. Healthy workplaces foster trust and cooperation because people feel trusted and valued inside a Circle of Safety. But those conditions are not only threatened from above. They are also eroded from within when we choose the easier excuse, hide behind the metric, protect ourselves first, or reward the short-term move that makes the room less honest. Cynicism and self-interest do not spread only because leaders fail. They spread because ordinary people adapt to, excuse, and sometimes quietly prefer the same logic.

That is why the chapter is sharper than ordinary systems critique. It says explanation can become cover if we use the system to explain away every choice we still control. The hard claim is not that everyone has equal power. It is that participation matters. We may not have built the whole machine, but we help keep it running when we reward the same appetites that make it profitable, comfortable, or easy to endure.

The chapter only stays honest if it keeps that limit clear. This is not blame-the-victim rhetoric and not contempt for people under pressure. Systems are real, and incentives matter. But `At the Center of All Our Problems Is Us` says repair cannot begin if we locate all corruption outside ourselves. We have to see the habits, comforts, and rationalizations by which we keep feeding what we also claim to hate. And once that is true, the next chapter almost writes itself. What happens when winning faster becomes acceptable at any expense?
