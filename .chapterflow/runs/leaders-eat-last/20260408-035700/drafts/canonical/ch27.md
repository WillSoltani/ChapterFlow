# Chapter 27 — Canonical Draft

The problem is already hurting people, but everyone in the room is waiting for someone higher up to notice. That is the right way into `We Need More Leaders`. After `Shared Struggle`, the final chapter most likely asks what follows if leadership is proven by accepting burden instead of escaping it. The answer is not merely that we need better people at the top. It is that too much responsibility for human consequences has been outsourced upward.

That is why the title matters. `We Need More Leaders` does not have to mean more titles, more managers, or more charismatic vision. It works more safely as a claim about shortage. Institutions become less human when only a few people think protection, trust, and moral responsibility belong to them. The rest of the group starts treating harm as someone else's department.

The book's earlier logic gives the chapter its mechanism. Healthy groups foster trust and cooperation because people feel trusted and valued inside a Circle of Safety. Those conditions do not survive on formal authority alone. They also depend on people inside the system choosing to notice human cost, speak up before damage spreads, and protect others even when no title forces them to care.

That does not erase hierarchy. Formal leaders still matter because power, policy, and protection are not imaginary. But the chapter's harder point is that formal leaders cannot humanize an institution by themselves if everyone else behaves like a spectator. Waiting for rescue from above is one more form of abstraction. It lets people enjoy the system while disowning responsibility for what the system does to human beings.

That is why the bridge from `Shared Struggle` matters. If real leadership shares burden rather than floating above it, then the final shortage is not simply better executives. It is more people at every level willing to carry responsibility for the humans near them. Leadership becomes less a rank than a practice of refusing to let cost disappear into someone else's life.

The close should stay live, not inspirational. `We Need More Leaders` leaves the reader with a harder question than ambition. Where are you still waiting for authority to protect people in a place where you already have enough influence to make the space more human? The book ends by widening responsibility, not by flattering the reader.
