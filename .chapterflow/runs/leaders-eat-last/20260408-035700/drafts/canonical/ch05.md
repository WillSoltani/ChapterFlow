The goal has already been met, but nobody in the room knows how to stop. That is the tension inside a title like "When Enough Is Enough." It suggests that the danger is not effort by itself. The danger is the moment when more keeps pulling even after the original need has been answered, and the push starts to feel wiser than proportion.

That is why the chapter matters as the opening of Part 2, "Powerful Forces." The book seems to be widening the frame. The earlier chapters asked what safety, dignity, belonging, and skepticism look like in leadership. This chapter appears to ask what larger force makes people lose sight of sufficiency in the first place. "Enough" is not a sleepy word here. It is a judgment word. It marks the point where pursuit should still be guided by purpose instead of by appetite alone.

The official book description helps keep that reading narrow. The book keeps warning about self-interest, cynicism, and fragmentation as the alternatives to trust and cooperation. Chapter 5 likely belongs on that fault line. Once enough goes out of view, self-interest gets easier to justify because "more" can keep sounding responsible long after it has started narrowing perspective.

The later structure of the book quietly supports that reading. Frozen chapter titles like "Destructive abundance," "At any expense," and "A society of addicts" suggest that excess and appetite remain live concerns later on. That does not prove the details of Chapter 5, but it does support the idea that this chapter is opening a longer argument about what happens when accumulation, escalation, or restless pursuit stop answering a real need.

The boundary matters. The chapter is not strongest if it sounds anti-growth or anti-ambition. Some situations really do call for more effort, more discipline, more reach, or more resources. The sharper question is when pursuit slips its purpose and becomes distorting. That is when "enough" stops sounding like weakness and starts sounding like clarity.

That is why the chapter feels like a pivot. Chapter 4 asked whether practical objections were real or lazy. Chapter 5 asks what force keeps practical people chasing more even after the point of sufficiency should have been visible. The next chapter title suggests the book is about to give that force a more specific name.
