# Chapter 27 — Edited Draft

The problem is already hurting people, but everyone in the room is waiting for someone higher up to notice. That is the safest way into `We Need More Leaders`. After `Shared Struggle`, the final chapter most likely asks what follows if leadership is proven by accepting burden instead of escaping it. The answer is not simply that we need better people at the top. It is that too much responsibility for human consequences has been outsourced upward.

That is why the title matters. `We Need More Leaders` should not be read as a call for more titles, more charisma, or more ambition. It works more safely as a claim about shortage. Institutions become less human when only a few people think protection, trust, and moral responsibility belong to them. Everyone else starts treating harm as someone else's job.

The book's earlier trust logic gives the chapter its mechanism. Healthy groups foster trust and cooperation because people feel trusted and valued inside a Circle of Safety. Those conditions do not survive on formal authority alone. They also depend on people inside the system choosing to notice human cost, protect others locally, and act before damage spreads.

That does not erase hierarchy. Formal leaders still matter because power, policy, and protection are not imaginary. But the chapter's harder point is that formal leaders cannot humanize an institution by themselves if everyone else behaves like a spectator. Waiting for rescue from above is one more form of abstraction. It lets people benefit from the system while disowning responsibility for what the system does to human beings.

That is why the bridge from `Shared Struggle` matters. If real leadership shares burden rather than floating above it, then the final shortage is not merely better executives. It is more people at every level willing to carry visible responsibility for the humans near them. Leadership becomes less a rank than a practice of refusing to let cost disappear into someone else's life.

The close should stay live, not motivational. `We Need More Leaders` leaves the reader with a harder question than ambition. Where are you still waiting for authority to protect people in a place where you already have enough influence to make the space more human? The book ends by widening responsibility, not by flattering the reader.
