# Chapter 23 — Edited Draft

The result looks clean because the bill has been moved. That is the safest way into `At Any Expense`. Chapter 22 argued that people help sustain broken patterns when they keep choosing quick relief and easy justification. Chapter 23 most likely asks what those choices become once success itself starts outranking the people who bear its cost. The answer is severe. A win gets morally thinner when the chooser feels the benefit while someone else absorbs the strain, silence, risk, or cleanup.

That only works if the chapter keeps its boundary. This is not a complaint about ambition, growth, or effort itself. It is a warning about detached success. `At Any Expense` most safely means that some results stop being clean when the price is merely hidden, delayed, or assigned to another person. A gain can still look impressive from one angle while being corrosive from another.

The earlier abstraction sequence explains the mechanism. Distance makes cost easier to discount. Numbers can make damage look tidy. Scale can make the harmed person feel remote enough to ignore. When the person carrying the burden becomes less visible than the result, it becomes easier to call the exchange necessary, efficient, or just how the world works.

The trust framing gives the chapter its stakes. Healthy groups foster trust and cooperation because people feel trusted and valued inside a Circle of Safety. But those conditions weaken when people learn that visible success matters more than who gets exhausted, exposed, or quietly sacrificed beneath it. That is how self-interest hardens. It is not only that people chase reward. It is that they become willing to let someone else absorb the human bill.

That is why the chapter feels like an escalation of Part 7. Appetite first taught us to prefer the easier reward. This chapter shows the social cost of that preference. Once the room starts treating hidden human expense as acceptable collateral, cynicism becomes reasonable and trust becomes thinner. A person no longer has to be openly despised to be used. They only have to become less visible than the result.

The chapter stays honest only if it preserves one final limit. Not every hard tradeoff is immoral, and not every burden means exploitation. But `At Any Expense` says a result should stop feeling innocent when its success depends on someone else's uncounted loss. Once that is true, the next chapter can ask what happens to people raised inside systems where abstraction and distance are already normal.
