# Chapter 6 Outline — E.D.S.O.

## Shape
- P1: Scene hook. The board is green, the team is wired, and still the room feels thin. Use that to frame the chapter as naming a force underneath behavior.
- P2: Decode the acronym from the frozen FAQ. State the narrowest safe read: endorphins, dopamine, serotonin, oxytocin.
- P3: Explain the useful distinction. Some rewards can be triggered alone; others depend on other people and shared safety.
- P4: Apply that distinction to leadership. A culture can accidentally overfeed the first set and undernourish the second.
- P5: Turn it into a management diagnostic. What does your environment make easy to chase, and what does it make hard to sustain?
- P6: Close with restraint and bridge. This is an environment-design chapter, not a chemistry lecture, and the next chapter likely isolates the remaining fifth force.

## Key tensions
- Busy versus healthy
- reward hit versus trust formation
- individual chase versus reciprocal support
- visible productivity versus invisible social strength
- chapter specificity versus thin-source restraint

## Must-keep language moves
- Open with a room-level problem, not “this chapter says.”
- Use the FAQ anchors to justify acronym expansion once, then move back to concrete leadership consequences.
- Tie the chapter back to Chapter 5 by showing how “more” can feel smart when the wrong rewards dominate.
- Keep the practical lesson focused on systems, incentives, and environment design.

## Avoid
- chemical-by-chemical mini encyclopedia
- pop-science confidence
- turning serotonin into status theory or oxytocin into sentimentality unless the draft can keep it fully supported
- naming cortisol unless the bridge absolutely requires it
