# Chapter 11 Outline — The Boom Before the Bust

Chapter Promise:
The reader should leave understanding that present dysfunction may have been prepared during an earlier period that looked successful, because systems that scale output can hide their human costs while growth is still strong.

Opening Move:
Open inside a system that looks like it is working too well to question, then ask what that visible success might be covering up.

Anchor Allocation:
- title / part placement used in P1 and P6
- factory-bell / machine routine used in P2
- linear hierarchies and recognition-from-above language used in P3
- designed-versus-current conditions language used in P4 and P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live success scene and the core question: what if the boom trained the bust?
- P2: use the factory-bell image to show how one kind of system can feel normal while shaping behavior mechanically
- P3: connect to linear hierarchy and recognition-from-above incentives
- P4: explain how those success-era structures can later misfit trust, cooperation, and human functioning
- P5: practical reading: success can hide imbalance because growth covers the cost
- P6: hard edge: not all booms are fake and not all busts are monocausal; bridge to the generation/institution question

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Differentiate visible success from healthy design.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve that earlier systems may have solved real problems and delivered real benefits, while arguing that those same systems could harden into unhealthy norms once circumstances changed.

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: boom before bust as delayed-causation frame
- Takeaway 2: earlier systems can look healthy while hiding human cost
- Takeaway 3: hierarchy and reward logic shape behavior
- Takeaway 4: success can mask mismatch with trust and human design
- Takeaway 5: hard limit against simple anti-growth storytelling

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): teaches not to confuse fast growth with healthy culture
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): teaches how a high-performing team can hide brittle process
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): teaches family success habits that later become strain
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): teaches how a metric boom can forecast later trust collapse
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): teaches whether tradition that once worked still fits current people
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): teaches seeing today's conflict as yesterday's helpful rule carried too far

Next-Chapter Bridge:
If these conditions hardened over time, what kinds of people and expectations grew up inside them?

Genericity Risks:
- generic “growth has downsides” language
- fake economic history
- simplistic anti-hierarchy slogans

Forbidden Drift:
- macroeconomic certainty unsupported by frozen sources
- culture-war framing
- consultant talk about change management

Fresh Ticket Notes:
- Keep the causal chain small and legible.
- Use “boom” as a masking condition, not as a dataset.
