# Chapter 12 Outline — The Boomers All Grown Up

Chapter Promise:
The reader should leave understanding that many workplace conflicts described as generational may really be conflicts between inherited leadership norms and changed conditions.

Opening Move:
Open with a room frustrated by younger workers or changing expectations, then ask whether the conflict is really about age or about adults carrying forward old rules.

Anchor Allocation:
- title / part placement used in P1 and P6
- Chapter 11 carryover used in P2
- linear hierarchies / recognition-from-above used in P3
- millennials appendix signal plus trust/cooperation language used in P4 and P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live generational-friction scene and chapter question
- P2: move from boom-era systems to the adults shaped by them
- P3: show how hierarchy, approval, and machine logic become leadership expectations
- P4: use the revised-edition millennials signal to show the conflict persists as a leadership problem
- P5: distinguish institutional inheritance from lazy cohort stereotypes
- P6: hard edge: every generation is mixed, but institutions still get shaped by those who rise inside them; bridge to abstraction

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Differentiate generation as carrier of norms from generation as personality stereotype.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve that inherited boom-era norms may contain real strengths like discipline or clarity, while arguing that the same norms can later misfit trust, flexibility, and changed expectations.

- Takeaway 2: adults carry systems forward

- Takeaway 5: limit against generational caricature

Scenario Lesson Map:

- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): teaches when tradition reproduces old authority habits
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): teaches how family or business rules get passed forward as adulthood scripts

Next-Chapter Bridge:
