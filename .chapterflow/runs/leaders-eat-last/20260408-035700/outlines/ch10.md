# Chapter 10 Outline — Snowmobile in the Desert

Chapter Promise:
The reader should leave understanding that some failures are best explained by environmental misfit before personal defect, and that leaders must diagnose terrain before judging people.

Opening Move:
Open with a capable person failing in a setting that clearly frustrates their strengths, then ask whether the problem is talent or terrain.

Anchor Allocation:
- title metaphor used in P1 and P2
- designed-to-function disconnect used in P3
- healthy and safe environment / conditions language used in P4 and P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live misfit scene and chapter question
- P2: unpack the snowmobile metaphor
- P3: connect to designed-versus-current conditions
- P4: translate into leadership and environment design
- P5: practical implication: role, incentive, and trust misfit
- P6: hard edge: keep responsibility in frame while refusing lazy blame; bridge to the historical question

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Differentiate structural misfit from personal defect.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve that some failures are personal, but leaders fail when they make that the first explanation instead of checking the terrain.

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: the title metaphor as diagnostic lens
- Takeaway 2: designed-versus-current conditions
- Takeaway 3: leadership responsibility for environment
- Takeaway 4: misfit versus defect in performance judgment
- Takeaway 5: hard limit against pure system-blame

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): teaches checking environment before blaming a struggling employee
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): teaches how a bad process can make a strong student look weak
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): teaches fit mismatch inside family expectations
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): teaches incentive design before talent judgment
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): teaches when redesign beats removal
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): teaches how changing terrain can change behavior without changing identity

Next-Chapter Bridge:
If the terrain itself is the problem, how did the modern terrain get built this way?

Genericity Risks:
- generic “right role” advice
- anti-accountability drift
- turning the metaphor into a total explanation for all failure

Forbidden Drift:
- hiring-manager cliches
- pseudo-scientific personality language
- blame-the-system slogans with no limits

Fresh Ticket Notes:
- Keep the metaphor tight and useful.
- The bridge should set up Chapter 11 as a historical build-up question.
