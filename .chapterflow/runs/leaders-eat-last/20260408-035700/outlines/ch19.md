# Chapter 19 Outline — Leadership Lesson 3: Integrity Matters

Chapter Promise:
The reader should leave understanding that integrity is whether standards, words, and actions stay coherent when pressure makes compromise attractive.

Opening Move:
Open with a leader facing a costly choice where everyone can see whether the stated standard will actually hold.

Anchor Allocation:
- title / part placement used in P1 and P6
- Chapter 18 bridge used in P1 and P2
- trust / Circle-of-Safety framing used in P3 and P4
- cynicism / self-interest framing used in P4 and P5
- earlier protection logic used in P2 and P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live pressure test and chapter question
- P2: define integrity as coherence under pressure rather than image
- P3: connect integrity to trust, safety, and credible standards
- P4: show how broken integrity teaches cynicism faster than words can repair
- P5: explain cost, tradeoff, and why integrity matters operationally
- P6: hard edge: integrity is not perfection, but standards that collapse when costly cannot anchor culture; bridge to friendship

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Differentiate integrity from reputation or private sincerity. The mechanism is coherence: people trust leadership when declared standards remain operative at the moment they become expensive.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve that integrity is tested by cost and pressure, not by easy agreement. Hard mode only works if it argues that integrity does not mean flawlessness, but it does mean the standard cannot become optional precisely when it matters most.

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: title as non-optional leadership condition
- Takeaway 2: integrity as coherence under pressure
- Takeaway 3: trust depends on standards remaining real when costly
- Takeaway 4: hypocrisy teaches cynicism and self-protection
- Takeaway 5: next bridge to friendship because integrity alone is principled but still relationally tested

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): teaches how one costly choice teaches whether the standard is real
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): teaches how broken integrity changed trust in a group
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): teaches integrity as alignment between promise and action in a close relationship
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): teaches how small tolerated hypocrisy forecasts bigger cultural damage
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): teaches when enforcing a standard selectively destroys its credibility
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): teaches how repaired coherence changes what people believe about the relationship or group

Next-Chapter Bridge:
If integrity keeps standards real, what kind of human bond helps people hold those standards together in serious institutions?

Genericity Risks:
- sermonizing about morals
- treating integrity as image or branding
- describing honesty without cost or tradeoff

Forbidden Drift:
- invented scandals
- fake-deep virtue language
- treating one error as identical to total corruption

Fresh Ticket Notes:
- Keep integrity operational, costly, and pressure-tested.
- The chapter is about coherence others can rely on, not private purity.
