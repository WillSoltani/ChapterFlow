# Chapter Outline

Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that trouble begins when people or systems lose the ability to recognize sufficiency and keep treating "more" as wisdom after it has started distorting judgment.

Opening Move:
Open on a moment when the original goal has already been met but the push keeps going anyway, and the room no longer knows how to stop without feeling weak.

Anchor Allocation:
- Anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for the title and Part 2 "Powerful Forces" framing.
- Anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3 for the official self-interest versus trust/cooperation contrast.
- Anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P5 for the later TOC echoes around destructive abundance, at any expense, and addiction as thematic corroboration.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Hook with the moment after sufficiency has already been crossed but pursuit keeps accelerating.
- P2: Define the title's threshold question and explain why "enough" is not laziness but proportion.
- P3: Connect the chapter to the broader danger of self-interest, cynicism, and narrowed judgment.
- P4: Use the "Powerful Forces" frame to show that appetite drift can become systemic, not merely personal.
- P5: Add the boundary: the chapter is not anti-ambition, but anti-proportion-loss.
- P6: Close by pointing toward the more specific force implied by the next chapter title, "E.D.S.O."

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is that once sufficiency disappears from view, "more" can keep commanding behavior even after it stops serving the original need. The distinction is between healthy pursuit and appetite that has slipped its purpose.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard mode must preserve the contradiction that growth, speed, and ambition can be necessary and admirable right up until they become distorting. The chapter fails if it turns into anti-effort moralizing or if it refuses to say that unchecked pursuit can become corrosive.

Scenario Lesson Map:

- Scenario 2 (postmortem, work): teaches how an output chase kept going after it stopped improving results

- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, school): teaches that one sufficiency rule can later improve quality by stopping needless accumulation
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): teaches how family provision can become control or exhaustion when enough is never named
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): teaches how one threshold check changes a household from restless accumulation to clearer priorities
