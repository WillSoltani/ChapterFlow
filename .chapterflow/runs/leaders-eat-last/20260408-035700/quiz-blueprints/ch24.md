# Chapter 24 Quiz Blueprint — The Abstract Generation

Chapter Core:
This chapter most safely argues that a generation can be formed under more mediated and abstract conditions, shaping expectations about trust, belonging, and authority. The hard limit is that the chapter diagnoses formation under systems, not moral defect in a cohort.

Question Targets:
1. Define the chapter's generational-formation claim safely.
2. Distinguish formation from stereotype.
3. Explain the bridge from Chapter 23 to Chapter 24.
4. Apply the idea that systems shape expectations, not just behavior.
5. Connect abstraction to thinner trust or mediated belonging.
6. Recognize why contempt or mockery would miss the chapter's point.
7. Preserve the limit that leaders must respond concretely rather than nostalgically.
8. Distinguish system-shaped expectation from personal weakness in a scenario.
9. Apply the lesson to school or personal contexts.
10. Predict why `Step 12` follows next.

Distractor Rules:
- Wrong choices should stereotype a generation or deny that systems shape people.
- Wrong choices can overstate specific technology or clinical claims.
- Avoid culture-war slogans.

Transfer Scenarios:
- a team misreading mediated habits as character failure
- a classroom rewarding shallow visible metrics over durable trust
- a family learning to replace abstract approval with concrete attention

Difficulty Spread:
- easy: title meaning, bridge, stereotype limit
- medium: formation mechanism, trust cost, applied scenario
- hard: leadership response, system-shaped expectation, Chapter 25 prediction
