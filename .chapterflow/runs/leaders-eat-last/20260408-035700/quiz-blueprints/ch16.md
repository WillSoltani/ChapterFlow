# Chapter 16 Quiz Blueprint — Imbalance

Chapter Core:
This chapter most safely argues that organizations become distorted when what is easy to measure, control, or scale keeps winning at the expense of the human conditions the system still depends on. The hard limit is that the chapter is not anti-efficiency and not offering a mystical theory of balance. It names a concrete asymmetry.

Question Targets:
1. Define the safest meaning of `Imbalance`.
2. Explain the bridge from Chapter 15 to Chapter 16.
3. Distinguish imbalance from generic dissatisfaction.
4. Apply the measurable-side versus human-side frame.
5. Connect imbalance to trust versus cynicism.
6. Use the designed-versus-current-conditions anchor correctly.
7. Preserve the limit that efficiency itself is not the enemy.
8. Recognize crowding-out in a decision or system.
9. Apply the chapter to a rule-versus-judgment case.
10. Predict why the next chapter would move toward culture.

Distractor Rules:
- Wrong choices should leave imbalance undefined or purely emotional.
- Wrong choices can overcorrect into saying all measurement or order is harmful.
- Avoid invented science, formulas, or named business examples.

Transfer Scenarios:
- high-control systems that weaken trust
- school processes that stay orderly while contribution quality worsens
- personal situations where management logic crowds out care

Difficulty Spread:
- easy: title, bridge, and concrete definition
- medium: apply crowding-out logic
- hard: test limits, trust implications, and culture bridge
