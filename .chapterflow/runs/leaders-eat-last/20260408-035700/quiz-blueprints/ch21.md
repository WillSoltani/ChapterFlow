# Chapter 21 Quiz Blueprint — Lead the People, Not the Numbers

Chapter Core:
This chapter most safely argues that numbers are tools for seeing, not the objects of leadership. The hard limit is that the lesson is anti-abstraction, not anti-measurement.

Question Targets:
1. Define the chapter's ordering claim safely.
2. Distinguish signals from source reality.
3. Explain the bridge from Chapter 20 to Chapter 21.
4. Apply the idea that numbers can hide human strain.
5. Connect people-first leadership to trust and candor.
6. Connect numbers-first leadership to cynicism and self-protection.
7. Preserve the limit that numbers still matter as tools.
8. Recognize the substitution error in a concrete scenario.
9. Apply the lesson to school or personal settings.
10. Predict why Chapter 22 turns the critique inward.

Distractor Rules:
- Wrong choices should either demonize all measurement or defend metrics as self-sufficient reality.
- Wrong choices can reduce the lesson to generic empathy instead of ordering.
- Avoid invented case studies or specific business examples not frozen in the run.

Transfer Scenarios:
- dashboards improving while local truth gets quieter
- school teams chasing a score while members withdraw
- parents or coaches reducing a person to grades, stats, or outputs

Difficulty Spread:
- easy: definition, bridge, signal-versus-source
- medium: trust, candor, substitution error, concrete application
- hard: abstraction preference, limits of measurement, Chapter 22 prediction
