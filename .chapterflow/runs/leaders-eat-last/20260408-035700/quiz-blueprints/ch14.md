# Chapter 14 Quiz Blueprint — Modern Abstraction

Chapter Core:
This chapter most safely argues that modern organizational conditions make abstraction easier to scale and normalize. The hard limit is that the chapter is not anti-modern or anti-metric. It says current scale, summary dependence, and system distance create extra pressure for leaders to compensate if they want to preserve trust and human judgment.

Question Targets:
1. Define the safest meaning of `Modern Abstraction`.
2. Explain the bridge from Chapter 13 to Chapter 14.
3. Use the designed-versus-current-conditions anchor correctly.
4. Distinguish modern amplification from a blanket attack on modernity.
5. Apply the chapter to scale, summaries, or metric-dependent systems.
6. Explain why trust weakens when people are known mainly through modern system signals.
7. Preserve the limit that coordination tools can be useful.
8. Recognize when current conditions create distance by default.
9. Apply the chapter to a fairness-at-scale dilemma.
10. Predict why the next chapter would move toward managing abstraction.

Distractor Rules:
- Wrong choices should reduce the chapter to anti-technology or anti-data rhetoric.
- Wrong choices can overcorrect into saying modern conditions change nothing.
- Avoid invented tools, apps, or workplace systems not present in frozen support.

Transfer Scenarios:
- large-team decisions made through summaries
- school coordination systems that scale but flatten people
- everyday life where convenience and speed reduce human contact

Difficulty Spread:
- easy: title, sequence, and anchor use
- medium: apply the modern-amplifier mechanism
- hard: test limits, trust consequences, and next-chapter prediction
