# Chapter 11 Quiz Blueprint — The Boom Before the Bust

Chapter Core:
This chapter most safely argues that the present mismatch in leadership and work conditions may have been prepared during an earlier period that looked successful. Growth can hide unhealthy assumptions because success reduces pressure to question the system producing it.

Question Targets:
1. Define the safest meaning of “boom before bust.”
2. Explain why visible success is not the same as healthy design.
3. Use the factory-bell / machine routine anchor correctly.
4. Use the linear-hierarchy / recognition-from-above anchor correctly.
5. Connect earlier work logic to later trust and collaboration problems.
6. Apply the chapter to a fast-growing team or institution.
7. Distinguish delayed cost from immediate failure.
8. Preserve the limit that not all growth is fake or harmful.
9. Recognize when a system is hiding cost because outcomes still look good.
10. Predict why the next chapter would turn to generational or institutional inheritance.

Distractor Rules:
- Wrong choices should confuse visible success with healthy structure.
- Wrong choices can overcorrect into “all hierarchy is bad” or “all growth is corrupt.”
- Avoid unsupported economic trivia or dates.

Transfer Scenarios:
- a team praised for speed while trust erodes
- a school tradition that once worked but now distorts participation
- a family rule that made sense in one season and later creates resentment

Difficulty Spread:
- easy: define the causal frame and anchors
- medium: apply the frame to concrete systems
- hard: test the boundary conditions and next-chapter bridge
