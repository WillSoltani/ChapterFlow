# Chapter 25 Quiz Blueprint — Step 12

Chapter Core:
This chapter most safely argues that leadership repair requires admission, process, accountable practice, and mutual help rather than one-time insight or willpower. The hard limit is that the recovery frame stays respectful and non-clinical.

Question Targets:
1. Define the chapter's recovery-process claim safely.
2. Distinguish admission and practice from one-time resolve.
3. Explain the bridge from Chapter 24 to Chapter 25.
4. Apply the difference between insight and retraining.
5. Connect recovery logic to trust rebuilding.
6. Recognize why mutual help matters.
7. Preserve the limit that the chapter is not making clinical claims.
8. Distinguish support from indulgence in a scenario.
9. Apply the lesson to school or personal settings.
10. Predict why `Shared Struggle` follows next.

Distractor Rules:
- Wrong choices should reduce change to private willpower or a single apology.
- Wrong choices can drift into unsupported clinical claims.
- Avoid recovery clichés without mechanism.

Transfer Scenarios:
- repeating a known bad leadership habit despite insight
- a group that names a pattern but does not practice differently
- a home routine that makes healthier behavior more durable

Difficulty Spread:
- easy: title meaning, bridge, process claim
- medium: admission, accountability, applied scenario
- hard: support-versus-indulgence, non-clinical limit, Chapter 26 prediction
