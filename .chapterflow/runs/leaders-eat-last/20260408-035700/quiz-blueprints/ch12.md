# Chapter 12 Quiz Blueprint — The Boomers All Grown Up

Chapter Core:
This chapter most safely argues that workplace generational friction is not mainly about fixed personality traits by age cohort. It is about adults shaped by earlier boom-era systems carrying inherited norms about hierarchy, reward, and seriousness into institutions that now fit people less well.

Question Targets:
1. Define the safest meaning of the chapter title.
2. Explain the bridge from Chapter 11 to Chapter 12.
3. Use linear hierarchy / recognition-from-above correctly.
4. Use the millennials appendix signal correctly and narrowly.
5. Distinguish stereotype from institutional diagnosis.
6. Apply the chapter to leadership and retention conflict.
7. Explain why inherited norms can feel like common sense.
8. Preserve the limit that no generation is uniform.
9. Recognize the strengths that can coexist with later misfit.
10. Predict why the next chapter would move toward abstraction.

Distractor Rules:
- Wrong choices should reduce the chapter to boomer-versus-millennial stereotypes.
- Wrong choices can overcorrect into “generation never matters at all.”
- Avoid unsupported historical facts, dates, or cohort psychology.

Transfer Scenarios:
- retention problems interpreted as entitlement
- seniority norms passed forward in clubs or schools
- family arguments about work ethic that are really about inherited expectations

Difficulty Spread:
- easy: title, bridge, and anchor use
- medium: apply the institutional-inheritance frame
- hard: test limits, mixed strengths, and next-chapter implications
