# Chapter 23 Quiz Blueprint — At Any Expense

Chapter Core:
This chapter most safely argues that a short-term win becomes dangerous when its human cost is hidden, delayed, or shifted onto other people. The hard limit is that the chapter criticizes detached success, not ambition itself.

Question Targets:
1. Define the chapter's core cost-shifting claim safely.
2. Distinguish `At Any Expense` from generic anti-ambition rhetoric.
3. Explain the bridge from Chapter 22 to Chapter 23.
4. Apply the idea of a visible win with an invisible bill.
5. Connect abstraction to moral distance.
6. Recognize trust erosion caused by success pursued at others' expense.
7. Preserve the limit that not every tradeoff is corrupt.
8. Distinguish hard necessity from expendable-person reasoning.
9. Apply the lesson to school or personal settings.
10. Predict why `The Abstract Generation` follows next.

Distractor Rules:
- Wrong choices should either claim that all success is bad or deny that hidden human cost matters.
- Wrong choices can collapse every tradeoff into abuse.
- Avoid ideological slogans or named public examples.

Transfer Scenarios:
- a clean metric that depends on burnout elsewhere
- a group success that hides unequal burden
- a family convenience that quietly dumps labor on one person

Difficulty Spread:
- easy: definition, bridge, title meaning
- medium: hidden bill, trust cost, applied scenario
- hard: not-every-tradeoff limit, expendable-person logic, Chapter 24 prediction
