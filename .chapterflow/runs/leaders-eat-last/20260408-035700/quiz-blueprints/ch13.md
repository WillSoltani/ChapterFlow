# Chapter 13 Quiz Blueprint — Abstraction Kills

Chapter Core:
This chapter most safely argues that harmful leadership becomes easier when people are treated mainly as categories, units, or distant outputs instead of as concrete humans with consequences attached to decisions. The hard limit is that abstraction can be a useful tool for coordination, but it becomes dangerous when it replaces encounter, judgment, and responsibility.

Question Targets:
1. Define the safest meaning of `Abstraction Kills`.
2. Explain the bridge from Chapter 12 to Chapter 13.
3. Use the factory / machine / hierarchy anchors correctly.
4. Connect abstraction to trust versus cynicism and self-interest.
5. Distinguish useful measurement from dehumanizing distance.
6. Apply the chapter to leadership decisions made through categories only.
7. Explain why upward recognition can increase abstraction.
8. Preserve the limit that the chapter is not anti-data.
9. Recognize when a process sounds fair but still loses the person.
10. Predict why the next chapter would move to `Modern Abstraction`.

Distractor Rules:
- Wrong choices should reduce the chapter to a blanket attack on numbers, planning, or scale.
- Wrong choices can overcorrect into saying abstraction is never a problem.
- Avoid invented technology claims or unsupported modern-work specifics.

Transfer Scenarios:
- headcount-style staffing discussion
- school postmortem driven by scores while missing contribution
- family or friend conflict where someone is treated as a role more than a person

Difficulty Spread:
- easy: title, sequence, and anchor use
- medium: apply the human-distance mechanism
- hard: test limits, trust implications, and next-chapter prediction
