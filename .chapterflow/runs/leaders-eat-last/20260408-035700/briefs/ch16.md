# Chapter 16 Brief — Imbalance

Book: Leaders Eat Last
Author: Simon Sinek
Publication Year: 2017
Book ID: leaders-eat-last

Chapter Number: 16
Chapter Title: Imbalance
Position In Book: Part 5 closing diagnosis chapter after modern abstraction and its management burden

Core Claim:
This chapter most safely argues that when abstraction and system distance are left insufficiently managed, organizations begin over-serving what is easy to measure, scale, or control while under-serving the human conditions they still rely on. `Imbalance` should therefore read as a distortion chapter, not as a vague complaint that things feel off. The narrow claim is that one side of organizational life starts winning too consistently: process over people, summary over judgment, efficiency over trust, or measurable output over the human conditions that make durable performance possible.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The chapter is not mainly about distance itself or how to manage it. It names the pattern that develops when distance keeps winning. Its distinct move is to treat imbalance as a lopsided system condition, where one set of priorities becomes too strong and crowds out what the organization still needs but sees less clearly.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses the title to name a distortion rather than a single event.
Then the author likely builds from the previous chapters: abstraction and modern conditions privilege what travels cleanly through systems, and leaders do not always compensate enough.
Then the author connects that drift to the book's wider trust-versus-cynicism framing and to the disconnect between human design and current conditions.
Then the author concludes that the issue is not efficiency by itself but an asymmetry where measurable order grows stronger while human trust, judgment, or protection grows thinner.
