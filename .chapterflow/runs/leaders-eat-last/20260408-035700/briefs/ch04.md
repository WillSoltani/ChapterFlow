Core Claim:
This chapter argues that the first objection to safety-first leadership often arrives sounding practical while quietly assuming that fear, cynicism, or self-protection are unavoidable under pressure. The title itself signals the move: "Yeah, but..." is the reflexive resistance that meets protection, dignity, and belonging whenever they start sounding costly. The chapter's likely job is not to deny pressure, standards, or tradeoffs, but to challenge the lazy conclusion that pressure makes humane leadership unrealistic.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is not a generic "handle objections" chapter and not a debate-club chapter about winning arguments. It is the book's realism filter. The distinctive move is separating serious constraints from excuse language that uses deadlines, competition, or toughness to keep unsafe norms in place. If it turns into broad inspirational rebuttal, it fails.

First the chapter inherits the resistance generated by Chapters 1 through 3: top cover, humane management, and belonging all sound attractive until someone asks what happens under real pressure.
Then the title gives that resistance a shape: "Yeah, but..." signals the familiar practical objection that tries to flatten the argument before it changes behavior.

Then the chapter likely clarifies the real boundary: pressure is real, but pressure does not automatically justify unsafe leadership habits.
Then it distinguishes useful objection from lazy objection. Good objections force precision about standards and tradeoffs; bad objections use realism as cover for fear-based management.
