# Chapter 23 Brief — At Any Expense

Book: Leaders Eat Last
Author: Simon Sinek
Publication Year: 2017
Book ID: leaders-eat-last

Chapter Number: 23
Chapter Title: At Any Expense
Position In Book: Part 7 escalation after the inward-accountability turn, moving from participation in distortion to the willingness to accept gains whose cost falls on other people

Core Claim:
This chapter most safely argues that the broken pattern becomes more dangerous when speed, status, growth, or visible success are treated as acceptable no matter who absorbs the cost. `At Any Expense` should therefore read as a cost-shifting chapter, not as a generic attack on ambition or achievement. The narrow claim is that once people normalize quick relief and self-protective rationalization, they become more willing to tolerate wins whose human bill is delayed, hidden, or assigned to someone else. The sharper version is that the problem is not wanting success. It is treating success as clean even when somebody else is paying for it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The chapter is not merely repeating that people participate in bad systems. Its distinct move is to show the moral direction of that participation: the fast win often depends on displaced harm. If Chapter 22 says we help sustain the pattern, Chapter 23 says the pattern hardens when we accept outcomes that look beneficial to us while degrading trust, fairness, or safety for others.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses the title to intensify the Part 7 critique from appetite to willingness to incur hidden cost.
Then the chapter likely shows that status, growth, or short-term performance can look attractive because the pain is pushed away from the chooser.
Then it argues that distance and abstraction make that trade easier to justify because the harmed person becomes less visible.
Then the author concludes that any system rewarding success without human accountability will teach people to treat others as acceptable collateral.
