# Chapter 22 Brief — At the Center of All Our Problems Is Us

Book: Leaders Eat Last
Author: Simon Sinek
Publication Year: 2017
Book ID: leaders-eat-last

Chapter Number: 22
Chapter Title: At the Center of All Our Problems Is Us
Position In Book: Part 7 turn inward after the anti-abstraction lessons, shifting from system critique to human participation in the system

Core Claim:
This chapter most safely argues that the broken system persists not only because the structure is flawed but because human beings keep feeding it. `At the Center of All Our Problems Is Us` should therefore read as an accountability chapter, not as a self-hatred chapter. The narrow claim is that our appetites for convenience, status, simplification, short-term relief, and self-protection make us vulnerable to preferring the thinner abstraction even when it costs trust and durability. The sharper version is that the system is real, but it keeps winning partly because we keep rewarding what feels easier in the short run.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The chapter is not a generic "look in the mirror" sermon and not an excuse for leadership to avoid structural responsibility. Its distinct move is to say that the system's distortions are sustained by ordinary human preferences and rationalizations. If Chapter 21 says abstraction beats people when numbers outrank reality, Chapter 22 asks why we keep helping that happen.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses the title to turn the critique inward and remove the comfort of a purely external enemy.
Then the chapter likely builds from Part 6: abstraction, imbalance, culture, leader signals, integrity strain, friendship, and numbers-first logic all described a broken pattern.
Then it argues that human appetites or habits keep choosing the shorter, easier, or more flattering option that sustains that pattern.
Then the author concludes that repair requires more than blaming the system because we are often participating in it, benefiting from it, or excusing it.
