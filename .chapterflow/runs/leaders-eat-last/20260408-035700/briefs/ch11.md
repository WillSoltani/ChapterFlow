# Chapter 11 Brief — The Boom Before the Bust

Book: Leaders Eat Last
Author: Simon Sinek
Publication Year: 2017
Book ID: leaders-eat-last

Chapter Number: 11
Chapter Title: The Boom Before the Bust
Position In Book: Part 4 opener, immediately after the environment-fit chapter

Core Claim:
This chapter most safely argues that the later imbalance the book is diagnosing did not appear from nowhere. Earlier success conditions likely created habits, structures, and incentives that looked productive during a boom and only later revealed their human costs. The chapter should therefore read as a causal bridge: some systems seemed to work while growth was masking what they were training people to normalize.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The chapter is not mainly about an individual's fit inside a bad environment. It steps back and asks how the environment itself got built. Its distinct move is to treat success as part of the problem: what looked like proof of health during the boom may have contained the logic of the later bust.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author names a temporal pattern: boom before bust.
Then the author suggests that periods of visible success can hide structural imbalance.
Then the author links that imbalance to the kinds of work conditions already visible in the frozen material: linear hierarchy, machine-paced routine, reward-from-above logic, and human systems designed around output more than trust.
Then the author concludes that present dysfunction may be the delayed consequence of earlier norms that once looked efficient.
