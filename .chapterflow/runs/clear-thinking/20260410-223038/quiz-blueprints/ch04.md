Quiz Blueprint — Chapter 4

Core checks:
- define the social default as conformity pressure rather than generic friendliness
- distinguish approval reward from truth-seeking
- identify the cost of borrowed judgment in ordinary groups
- preserve the cooperation-versus-conformity distinction
- bridge correctly from ego to social pressure and from social pressure to inertia

Difficulty split:
- 3 easy recall / understanding questions
- 4 medium apply / analyze questions
- 3 hard analyze / evaluate questions

Question targets:
1. definition of the social default
2. official support line about conformity before truth
3. why group approval feels compelling
4. fast-consensus meeting scenario
5. school-group confidence scenario
6. personal friend-group scenario
7. difference between cooperation and conformity
8. why silent agreement can still be a decision failure
9. threshold test: division of labor versus outsourced judgment
10. bridge distinction between social pressure and inertia

Choice-shape rules:
- 3 choices per question
- only one answer should preserve the chapter's group-pressure mechanism
- wrong answers should fail by confusing social pressure with ego defense, emotion, or healthy collaboration

Explanation requirements:
- easy explanations should restate belonging pressure plainly
- medium explanations should tie the scenario back to borrowed judgment or approval reward
- hard explanations should preserve the connection-without-obedience tension
