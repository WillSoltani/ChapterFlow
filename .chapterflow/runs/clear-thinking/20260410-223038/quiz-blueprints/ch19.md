Quiz Blueprint — Chapter 19

Core checks:
- define criteria, tradeoffs, information quality, and judgment operationally
- distinguish preference from disciplined evaluation
- preserve why evidence quality matters more than evidence volume
- reject hidden-cost blindness and seductive favorites
- keep the bridge into action after evaluation

Difficulty split:
- 3 easy recall / understanding questions
- 4 medium apply / analyze questions
- 3 hard analyze / evaluate questions

Question targets:
1. definition of criteria
2. why tradeoffs matter
3. what information quality means
4. work scenario about convenience beating fit
5. dialogue about evidence quality versus preference
6. school scenario comparing candidates against standards
7. hidden costs
8. why more data is not always better
9. how evaluation reduces seduction without removing uncertainty
10. threshold test: when options are ready for action

Choice-shape rules:
- 3 choices per question
- only one answer should preserve the chapter's actual mechanism
- wrong answers should fail by endorsing vibes, fake objectivity, or data overload

Explanation requirements:
- easy explanations should restate criteria, tradeoffs, information quality, or judgment plainly
- medium explanations should tie a scenario back to stronger comparison
- hard explanations should preserve the rigor-versus-uncertainty tension
