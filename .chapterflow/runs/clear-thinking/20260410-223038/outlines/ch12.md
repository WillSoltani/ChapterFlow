Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will understand why rules and standards protect clear thinking by reducing avoidable improvisation and drift.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between wanting to do the right thing and having a standard that keeps the right thing from being renegotiated away.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 move from visible action to rules that preserve it
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3 standards and self-command as the chapter lane
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4 improvisational failure and decision protection
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P5 standards without rigidity
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P6 bridge into exemplars and practice

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: position standards as the protection layer for repeated good action
- P2: show how improvisation reopens old failures
- P3: define standards as pre-decided rules or thresholds that protect judgment
- P4: show practical value: lower drift, less renegotiation, fewer predictable misses
- P5: preserve the tension that standards are not blind rigidity
- P6: bridge into exemplars and practice

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Standards are not generic discipline slogans. The mechanism is decision protection: rules set in clearer moments reduce avoidable improvisation when pressure rises later.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must not imply that every good judgment can be scripted in advance. Hard should ask how standards can protect decisions without replacing judgment entirely.

Threshold Test:
What keeps your better standard from being renegotiated away when the moment gets busy, social, or uncomfortable?
