Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will understand how belonging pressure and group norms can substitute for independent thought without looking dramatic in the moment.

Opening Move:
Open on a room where agreement is gathering speed and dissent is becoming socially expensive, then show how group momentum quietly takes over judgment.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 social named as a default and tied to conformity before truth
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3 ordinary belonging pressure in meetings, teams, and groups
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4 local safety reward versus loss of independent judgment
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P5 distinction between cooperation and conformity
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P6 bridge to inertia and the comfort of the familiar

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define the social default through belonging pressure and borrowed judgment
- P2: explain why group approval feels urgent enough to distort thought
- P3: show ordinary scenes where agreement replaces examination
- P4: make the cost practical: silence, weak dissent, and bad group decisions
- P5: preserve the tension that groups matter, but truth cannot be outsourced to them
- P6: bridge from crowd pressure to the familiar-pattern pull of inertia

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Social pressure is not the same as cooperation. The mechanism is that approval and belonging become rewards strong enough to replace independent judgment.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must not imply that all agreement is weakness. Hard should ask how someone can remain loyal and collaborative without surrendering truth-testing.

Threshold Test:
When is following the group's lead a legitimate division of labor, and when is it just conformity hiding inside comfort?
