Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will understand why clearer thinking depends on accurate self-calibration about strengths, weaknesses, edge, and limits.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between confidence and calibration, then show how misreading yourself can quietly distort decisions even when intentions are good.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: position self-knowledge as the strength that makes accountability more accurate
- P2: explain how self-misread distorts judgment through overreach, blind spots, or false confidence
- P3: define self-knowledge as calibration about strengths, weaknesses, edge, and limits
- P4: show practical value: better positioning, support, and lower blind-spot tax
- P5: preserve the tension that honesty about limits is not weakness or self-diminishment
- P6: bridge into self-control, where known limits meet fear, desire, and impulse

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Self-knowledge is not generic introspection. The mechanism is calibration: accurate self-reading changes where you should trust yourself, slow down, or add support.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must not imply that knowing your limits means distrusting yourself in general. Hard should ask how someone can trust real strengths while still building around known weak spots.

Threshold Test:
When is confidence helping a decision, and when is it only hiding that you have misread your own edge?
