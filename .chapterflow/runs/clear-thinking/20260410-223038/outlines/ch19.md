Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will understand why better decisions require criteria, honest tradeoffs, and trustworthy information before commitment.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between liking an option and judging it well against alternatives.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 move from open search into comparative judgment
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P3 criteria and tradeoffs
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 information quality versus information quantity
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P4-P5 why attractive options can hide weak fit or hidden costs
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P5 boundary against false objectivity or formalism drift
- anchor 6 used in paragraph(s): P6 bridge into action and commitment

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: position evaluation as the next need after broad option search
- P2: show why comparison needs criteria and tradeoffs
- P3: define information quality and evidence relevance
- P4: show how weak evidence or hidden costs distort favorites
- P5: preserve the tension against mechanical scoring or fake certainty
- P6: bridge into doing the chosen thing

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Evaluating options is not simply picking the one you like most. The mechanism is comparing serious candidates against criteria, tradeoffs, and evidence quality before narrowing.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must not imply that judgment becomes perfectly objective or that more data automatically means better evaluation. Hard should ask how disciplined comparison can reduce seduction without pretending uncertainty disappears.

Threshold Test:
Are you comparing real alternatives against clear standards and decent evidence, or are you still letting convenience or attraction decide for you?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3

- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: criteria keep preference from pretending to be judgment
- Takeaway 2: tradeoffs reveal what each option costs
- Takeaway 3: information quality matters more than sheer volume
- Takeaway 4: favorites can hide weak evidence or hidden costs
- Takeaway 5: disciplined judgment still leaves uncertainty
