Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will understand how self-image and status defense can quietly overpower judgment even after the initial emotion cools.

Opening Move:
Open on a moment of correction or disagreement that becomes a status threat, then show how the reader's need to protect image starts rewriting the purpose of the moment.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 ego named as a default linked to hierarchy and self-preservation
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3 status threat turns correction into self-defense
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4 image protection blocks learning and worsens decisions
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P5 space between stimulus and response as the practical interruption
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P6 bridge to social pressure and belonging

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define the ego default through identity defense rather than cartoon arrogance
- P2: connect status threat to hierarchy, territory, and self-preservation
- P3: show how criticism, disagreement, or embarrassment becomes a self-protection problem
- P4: explain the cost in lost learning, weak revision, and damaged trust
- P5: show the practical need to create space before self-image answers first
- P6: bridge from private identity defense to public conformity pressure

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Ego is not confidence. The mechanism is that a threat to self-image makes being right, admired, or uncorrected feel more important than seeing clearly.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must not imply that dignity comes from self-erasure. Hard should ask how a person can keep self-respect while still letting truth outrank image.

Threshold Test:
When is resistance to criticism protecting a real standard, and when is it only protecting identity from correction?
