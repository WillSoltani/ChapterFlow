Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will understand why better decisions begin with clearer problem framing, more accurate obstacle identification, and cleaner ownership.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between reacting to the most visible symptom and defining the actual problem that needs to be solved.

- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P3 symptom versus obstacle
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 responsibility and ownership
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P4-P5 root cause versus surface irritation
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P5 boundary against overanalysis
- anchor 6 used in paragraph(s): P6 bridge into solution search

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: position problem definition as the next decision-process need
- P2: show why shallow framing wastes action
- P3: define obstacle and ownership
- P4: show why root cause matters before solution search
- P5: preserve the tension against blame language or endless diagnosis
- P6: bridge into exploring possible solutions

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Defining the problem is not brainstorming a fix. The mechanism is clarifying what is actually wrong, what obstacle matters most, and what part of it is owned before solutions start competing.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must not imply that root cause is always perfectly knowable or that more analysis is always wiser. Hard should ask how clarity can improve action without freezing it.

Threshold Test:
What exactly is the problem, what obstacle is truly in the way, and what part of it is yours to own or change?
