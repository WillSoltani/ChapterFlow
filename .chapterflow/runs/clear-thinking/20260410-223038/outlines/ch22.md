Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will understand why outcomes only improve future judgment when they are examined through feedback, postmortem thinking, and reflection.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between merely having an experience and actually learning from it.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 move from protected action into learning from outcomes
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P3 reflection and postmortem
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 feedback and process legibility
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P4-P5 improvement and future judgment
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P5 boundary against rumination or false certainty
- anchor 6 used in paragraph(s): P6 bridge into the late-book value shift

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: position learning as the next need after action and protection
- P2: show why experience alone is too noisy to teach clearly
- P3: define feedback, postmortem, and reflection operationally
- P4: show how lessons become future improvement
- P5: preserve the tension against rumination, self-blame, or overreading outcomes
- P6: bridge into asking what outcomes are worth wanting

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Learning from your decisions is not merely feeling bad or good about the result. The mechanism is making the process and outcome legible enough that future judgment can improve.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must not imply that outcomes always reveal a clean lesson or that process and result line up perfectly. Hard should ask how reflection can improve judgment while accounting for luck and noise.

Threshold Test:
Have you made the process and outcome legible enough to carry a real lesson forward, or are you still reacting to the result at face value?

- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: experience alone does not guarantee learning
- Takeaway 2: reflection makes outcomes legible
- Takeaway 3: postmortem thinking separates process from result
- Takeaway 4: feedback must be used without turning into self-attack
- Takeaway 5: improvement still has to account for luck and noise
