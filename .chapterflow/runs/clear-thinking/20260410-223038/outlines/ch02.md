Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will understand why emotional intensity can erase judgment so quickly and how to notice the cost before acting.

Opening Move:
Open on the contrast between disciplined patience and impulsive emotional action through the *Godfather* anchor, then widen into ordinary hot-state mistakes.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 Sonny versus Vito as the frame for emotional speed and discipline
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3 emotions pushing immediate action that rarely serves the person
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4 Steve regret story and irreversibility
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P5 vulnerability conditions that strengthen emotional hijack
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P6 bridge to safeguards

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: open with vivid emotional recklessness versus patient restraint
- P2: explain what emotional possession does to judgment
- P3: widen to anger, fear, outrage, and urgency in ordinary life
- P4: make the cost irreversible, not just embarrassing
- P5: identify conditions that make emotional hijack more likely
- P6: bridge from diagnosis to the need for safeguards

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Emotion is not the same as information. The mechanism is that hot feeling compresses time and pushes action before goals and facts can stabilize the moment.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must not imply that all strong feeling is noise. Hard should ask how to respect feeling as data while refusing to let hot emotion govern action.

Threshold Test:
When does an emotion deserve to slow you down and inform judgment, and when is it simply trying to take command before judgment has a chance?

- Takeaway 1: emotional possession erases access to reasoning tools
- Takeaway 2: local relief can create irreversible regret
- Takeaway 3: fear and urgency can hijack judgment as much as anger
- Takeaway 4: hot-state vulnerability conditions must be treated as decision risks
- Takeaway 5: feeling can contain signal without earning control
