Core Claim:
Default to clarity is the turning chapter where Parrish moves from naming how defaults hijack judgment to showing that better defaults can be built deliberately. The frozen bundle supports this chapter more strongly than Chapter 5 because the official Farnam Street materials emphasize creating space between stimulus and response, turning desired behaviors into default behaviors, and widening options through better positioning. The chapter should feel like a controlled pivot, not a vague motivational reset.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 5 was about the drag of passive familiarity. Chapter 6 is about replacing passive defaults with deliberate clarity-supporting ones. Its texture should feel constructive, practical, and systems-minded rather than merely diagnostic.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author closes the four-default diagnosis by showing that people do not need to stay trapped inside automatic reactions.
Then he reframes the task: clearer thinking requires better positions, more space, and more deliberate defaults.
Then he shows that the answer is not heroic willpower in the moment, but designing patterns that make clearer action more likely.
Then he ties that move to the strengths and safeguard chapters ahead.
Then he positions the rest of the book as implementation rather than more diagnosis.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Default to clarity: building conditions where better judgment becomes easier to access consistently.
Positioning: arranging life, environment, and options so the right move is easier to see and take.
Deliberate default: a pattern or rule installed on purpose rather than inherited passively.
Environment leverage: shaping surroundings so willpower does not have to do all the work in real time.

Specific Applications:
- designing routines that create thinking space before important decisions
