Core Claim:
Setting standards matters because good judgment degrades when every moment has to be renegotiated from scratch. The frozen support here is medium-high and the heading-index lane is clear: rules, standards, decision quality, and self-command. The chapter should therefore stay practical. It is not about rigid moralizing. It is about building standards that protect action from mood, drift, and improvisational failure.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 11 asked whether the strengths show up in visible action. Chapter 12 asks what keeps that action stable over time. Its texture should feel rule-shaping, preventive, and protective rather than punitive or bureaucratic.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Standards: pre-decided rules or thresholds that protect judgment from improvisation.
Decision protection: using rules to preserve quality before pressure can renegotiate the moment.
Improvisational failure: the errors that return when every choice must be rebuilt from mood or convenience.
Self-command: the ability to live under standards you judged to be wiser in clearer moments.

Specific Applications:
- setting rules before a predictable weak moment arrives
- defining thresholds that reduce avoidable error
- using standards to reduce unnecessary decision fatigue
- protecting good action from social or emotional drift
