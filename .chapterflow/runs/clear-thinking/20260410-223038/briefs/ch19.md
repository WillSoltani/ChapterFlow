Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Criteria: the standards by which alternatives should be judged.
Tradeoffs: what each option gives up in order to gain something else.
Information quality: how trustworthy, complete, or decision-relevant the supporting evidence actually is.
Judgment: the act of comparing alternatives with enough discipline that fit beats mere attraction.

Specific Applications:
- comparing two or three serious candidates against clear standards
- noticing when a favorite option is winning on vibe rather than evidence
- testing whether the information behind an option is solid or thin
- identifying hidden costs that only appear when tradeoffs are faced directly

Common Misreadings:
- making evaluation sound like spreadsheet worship
- confusing stronger preference with stronger evidence
- treating more information as better even when it is noisy or low quality

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that every decision needs a heavy formal rubric or that comparison can remove judgment. Keep it narrower and more practical: evaluation matters because options need clear standards, honest tradeoffs, and evidence that deserves trust.
