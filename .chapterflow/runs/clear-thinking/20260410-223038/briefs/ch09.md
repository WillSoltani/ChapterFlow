Core Claim:
Self-control protects decision quality when pressure makes a person want relief faster than truth. The frozen support here is medium but coherent: the heading-index lane centers discipline, fear, desire, and emotional regulation. The chapter should therefore stay practical and narrow. Self-control is not generic toughness or personality branding. It is the strength that keeps a person aligned with what they already know once impulse, mood, and urgency try to redraw the map.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 8 was about reading strengths and limits accurately. Chapter 9 is about obeying that reading under pressure. Its texture should feel steady, restraining, and pressure-aware rather than punitive or macho.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Self-control: the ability to keep action aligned with a truer map when pressure pushes for relief or reaction.
Pressure override: the moment fear, desire, anger, or urgency tries to displace better judgment.
Restraint window: the space in which a person can keep from acting on the first impulse.
Decision hold: preserving standards long enough for clear thinking to stay in charge.

Specific Applications:
- pausing before a fearful message or retaliatory reply
- holding to a slower process instead of grabbing quick relief
- staying with a known limit instead of pretending pressure changed it
- refusing to let urgency erase prior standards
