Core Claim:
Self-knowledge improves judgment because a person makes better choices when they know their real edge, weakness, and limit. The frozen support is narrower here than for Chapters 6 and 7, so the chapter should stay close to the heading-index lane: self-knowledge is not generic introspection. It is calibration about where you are strong, exposed, overconfident, or in need of support.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 7 was about taking ownership. Chapter 8 is about knowing what exactly you are owning: your real capacities, blind spots, exposures, and limits. Its texture should feel observant, precise, and calibrating rather than confessional or therapeutic.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that ownership without accurate self-reading can still misfire.
Then he argues that clear thinking depends on knowing strengths, weaknesses, edge, and limits.
Then he shows how self-misread leads to overreach, blind spots, and misplaced confidence.
Then he reframes self-knowledge as calibration for better positioning, support, and decision quality.
Then he points toward self-control, where accurate self-reading has to survive desire, fear, and impulse.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Self-knowledge: accurate reading of your strengths, weaknesses, edge, and limits as they affect decisions.
Calibration: matching self-belief to actual capacity and exposure.
Blind-spot tax: the cost of not seeing where you are weak or unreliable.
Support fit: arranging roles, rules, or help around what you should not trust yourself to do alone.

Specific Applications:
- noticing where confidence outruns competence
- identifying recurring weak spots before important decisions
- arranging support or friction around known limits
- trusting strengths without pretending they cover every condition

Common Misreadings:

- making it sound like insecurity or self-doubt
- confusing honesty about weakness with loss of confidence

Counterarguments or Limits:
