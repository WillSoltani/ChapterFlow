Core Claim:
Learning from your decisions matters because outcomes do not automatically teach; experience only improves judgment when reflection, feedback, and postmortem thinking make the result legible. The frozen support here is medium and the heading-index lane is clear: feedback, postmortem, reflection, and improvement. The chapter should therefore stay practical. It is not about self-criticism or vague growth talk. It is about extracting usable lessons from decisions after reality has answered back.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 21 protected action against uncertainty. Chapter 22 asks what to do once the results arrive. Its texture should feel reflective, exact, and improvement-oriented rather than emotional or indulgent.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Feedback: the information reality provides after a decision is carried out.
Postmortem: a structured look back at what happened and why.
Reflection: the work of making the decision, process, and outcome more legible.
Improvement: carrying the lesson forward into better future judgment.

Specific Applications:
- reviewing what a decision produced instead of only how it felt
- distinguishing luck from judgment quality
- using postmortem thinking to make outcomes legible
- converting feedback into a change for future action

Common Misreadings:
