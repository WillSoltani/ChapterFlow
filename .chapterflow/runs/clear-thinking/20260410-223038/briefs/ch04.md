The social default distorts judgment when belonging, approval, and group norms become more important than truth. What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 3 is about protecting self-image. Chapter 4 is about protecting membership, approval, and standing inside a group. The chapter should feel collective, imitative, and norm-sensitive rather than purely emotional or purely status-defensive.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Social default: conforming to group norms, momentum, or approval pressure instead of thinking independently.
Belonging pressure: the felt cost of standing apart from the group.
Borrowed judgment: accepting a group's conclusion without doing the thinking yourself.
Conformity reward: the short-term safety or comfort that comes from staying aligned with the crowd.

Specific Applications:
- agreeing in meetings because dissent feels socially expensive
- copying a group's urgency without checking whether it is justified
- softening honest feedback to preserve approval
- following a friend group, team, or class norm without testing it

Common Misreadings:
- turning the chapter into anti-community individualism
- collapsing social pressure into ego or emotion without a group mechanism
- treating all cooperation as weak thinking
