Core Claim:
Self-accountability is the first practical strength because it interrupts autopilot at the level of ownership. The frozen official support is strong enough to keep the chapter narrow and clear: accountability is not mainly self-punishment or blame absorption. It is the move of owning choice, contribution, and responsibility quickly enough that clearer action becomes possible.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 6 was about designing better defaults. Chapter 7 is about who takes responsibility for installing and maintaining them. Its texture should feel candid, grounded, and corrective rather than scolding or performatively tough.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author makes accountability the first strength because design needs an owner.
Then he shows that autopilot survives when people explain, excuse, or externalize too quickly.
Then he reframes accountability as ownership of choice, contribution, and response rather than shame.
Then he shows how ownership widens options because it restores agency faster than blame does.
Then he points forward to self-knowledge, where ownership becomes more precise by understanding actual edge and exposure.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Self-accountability: owning choice, contribution, and response quickly enough to improve judgment.
Ownership turn: shifting from blame-first explanation to responsibility-first examination.
Mirror test: asking what in this outcome belongs to me before diagnosing everyone else.
Agency recovery: the way accountability restores options by focusing attention on controllable contribution.

Specific Applications:
- owning a preventable part of a bad meeting instead of only blaming the room
- admitting a missed prep step before criticizing a result
- asking what part of a conflict or failure belonged to your contribution
- using responsibility to regain agency rather than to perform guilt

Common Misreadings:
- turning accountability into self-blame or humiliation
- making the chapter sound like accepting fault for everything
- flattening ownership into corporate slogan language

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not say that every bad outcome is equally your fault. Keep it narrower and more useful: accountability means identifying your real contribution and your next responsible move. Do not erase structural constraints, but do not let them become the first refuge either.
