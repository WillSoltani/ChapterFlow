Core Claim:
Dickens's Hidden Lesson matters because better decisions are not enough if the reader is still aiming at shallow or mistaken outcomes. The frozen support here is medium-thin and the heading-index lane is clear: what matters, hidden costs, miswanting, and fulfillment. The chapter should therefore stay narrower and more concrete. It is not about literary analysis for its own sake. It is about how people can pursue visibly impressive things while missing what actually contributes to a meaningful life.

Chapter 22 improved the reader's ability to learn from decisions. Chapter 23 shifts the book from process quality to value quality. Its texture should feel revealing, morally serious, and quietly destabilizing rather than abstract or preachy.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Miswanting: pursuing something that seems desirable but does not deliver what matters most.
Hidden costs: the losses or tradeoffs attached to an apparently attractive goal.
Fulfillment: a deeper sense of meaning or rightness that may differ from status or display.
What matters: the outcomes, relationships, and uses of life that hold up better under honest reflection.

Specific Applications:

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that ambition is bad or that there is one universal formula for a meaningful life. Keep it narrower and more practical: the danger is not wanting anything large, but wanting something whose hidden costs quietly hollow out what matters. Do not invent literary theory, Victorian-analysis frameworks, or generic anti-materialist sermons absent from the frozen bundle.
