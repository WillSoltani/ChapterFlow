Core Claim:
Protecting yourself with safeguards matters because seeing a weakness is not enough if the same pressure can still reach it without resistance. The frozen support here is medium-high and the heading-index lane is clear: rules, guardrails, premortem, and friction. The chapter should therefore stay practical. It is not about fearfulness or bureaucratic overbuilding. It is about placing structure around predictable exposure before the live moment gets a chance to renegotiate the wiser move.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 14 was diagnostic. Chapter 15 is protective. Its texture should feel preventive, structural, and realistic rather than anxious or controlling.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author moves from knowing exposure to protecting it.
Then he shows why insight alone is weak when pressure arrives with no built-in resistance.
Then he reframes safeguards as decision support rather than mistrust of the self.
Then he shows how friction, guardrails, and premortem thinking reduce avoidable failure.
Then he points toward mistakes, where even good safeguards cannot eliminate every miss and the reader must learn how to recover well.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Safeguard: a protective structure that reduces the chance that known weakness will retake control.
Guardrail: a boundary, rule, or design constraint that blocks an avoidable bad path.
Premortem: a way of asking how a foreseeable failure would happen before it happens.
Friction: deliberate resistance that slows a weak impulse long enough for judgment to return.

Specific Applications:
- adding a pause, checklist, or second review to a known weak decision point
- designing friction around spending, replying, scheduling, or handoff errors
- using a premortem to notice how a plan could fail before it becomes expensive
- choosing simpler guardrails instead of relying on last-minute willpower

Common Misreadings:
- making safeguards sound fearful or rigid
- treating friction like punishment instead of protection
- turning premortem thinking into catastrophizing

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that every risk can be engineered away or that a person should live under endless restrictions. Keep it narrower and more practical: safeguards matter most where exposure is already known and repeated failure is foreseeable. Do not invent formal risk systems, management acronyms, or compliance frameworks absent from the frozen bundle.
