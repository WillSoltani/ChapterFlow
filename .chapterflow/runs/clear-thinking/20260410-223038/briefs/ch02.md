Core Claim:
The emotion default pushes people to act for immediate discharge rather than long-term outcome. Parrish uses Sonny Corleone as the cleanest anchor: quick temper and emotional reaction create mistakes that look obvious only afterward. The chapter's practical lesson is not to suppress all feeling. It is to recognize that anger, fear, outrage, and other hot emotions can erase judgment before facts and goals have time to matter.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 1 diagnosed the general problem of missing the thinking moment. Chapter 2 names the first and fastest default that often steals it. This chapter is about emotional possession, not social pressure, ego defense in general, or habit. Its texture should stay bodily, immediate, and consequence-driven.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author contrasts patient discipline with impulsive emotional action through *The Godfather* frame.
Then he generalizes the mechanism: anger, fear, outrage, and similar feelings push people toward action that rarely serves them.
Then he shows the cost through regret and irreversible consequences, including the Steve story where a final conversation cannot be repaired.
Then he widens the frame by naming conditions that make the emotion default stronger: fatigue, hunger, stress, distraction, rush, and unfamiliar settings.
Then he points forward to safeguards that can prevent hot emotional states from governing action.

Required Anchors:
- Sonny Corleone as the visible model of emotional speed becoming self-destruction, contrasted with Vito Corleone's patience and discipline.
- The line of reasoning that emotional response often makes later mistakes obvious in hindsight because the person was "possessed by the moment."
- The Steve tragedy note from the official excerpt: one final emotional conversation with parents, followed by a fatal car crash on their way home, making the reaction permanently unrepairable.
- The exposure list: sleep deprivation, hunger, fatigue, stress from feeling rushed, distraction, and unfamiliar environments as conditions that leave people more vulnerable to the emotion default.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Emotion default: responding to feelings rather than reasons and facts.
Possessed by the moment: a state where feeling has already overrun judgment.
Hot-state vulnerability: conditions that make emotional hijack more likely.
Safeguards: later protective rules or structures that keep emotion from governing action.

Specific Applications:
- catching anger before a reply, text, or meeting escalation becomes irreversible
- noticing fear-driven urgency before cutting thinking short
- recognizing that exhaustion, hunger, stress, and unfamiliarity make emotional judgment less trustworthy

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as a demand to feel nothing
- turning the lesson into generic stoicism without consequence and body cost
- treating emotion as only anger when fear and urgency matter just as much here

Counterarguments or Limits:
The frozen support is strongest on emotional hijack, regret, and vulnerability conditions. Do not expand this into a full theory of emotional intelligence, trauma, or neuroscience. The chapter should stay narrow: feelings can outrun judgment, and the reader needs to see the cost before later safeguards arrive.
