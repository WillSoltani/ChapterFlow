Core Claim:
Exemplars and practice matter because a standard can be intellectually clear and still remain behaviorally weak until it is reinforced by good models and repeated rehearsal. The frozen support here is medium and the heading-index lane is clear: role models, practice, calibration, and measurement. The chapter should therefore stay practical. It is not about admiration alone. It is about how standards become more reliable when the reader sees them embodied and then rehearses them deliberately.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Exemplar: a model that shows what stronger judgment looks like in lived form rather than in slogans.
Practice: repeated rehearsal that helps a person use a standard under ordinary pressure.
Calibration: adjusting your sense of good judgment by exposure to better examples and repeated attempts.
Measurement: simple feedback that shows whether practice is making the standard more usable.

Specific Applications:
- choosing a role model for a specific judgment skill instead of admiring greatness in the abstract
- rehearsing a better response before the next predictable pressure moment
- using light measurement to see whether a standard is becoming easier to keep
- separating genuine calibration from performance vanity

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:

Keep it narrower and more practical: exemplars help readers see the right line, and practice helps them use it more reliably.
