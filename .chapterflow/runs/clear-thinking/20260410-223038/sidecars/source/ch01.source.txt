   - The official excerpt references an "angry CEO" as the vivid example of circumstances thinking for someone before they realize it.

   - The official excerpt supports a cluster of ordinary scenes:
     - lashing out when slighted
     - assuming malice when cut off
     - getting frustrated by delay
     - escalating passive-aggression
     - hoarding information to gain advantage
   - The chapter's practical point is that these reactions consume time and energy that could have gone toward desired outcomes.

4. Biology is the hidden driver.
   - The official excerpt supports the claim that biology, not deliberate reasoning, often drives reactive behavior.
   - Territory, hierarchy, and self-preservation are the supported explanatory lenses.
   - The source supports the idea that these tendencies once helped survival but now often create downstream problems in modern settings.

5. The four defaults are the closing map for the chapter.
   - emotion default: feelings over reasons and facts
   - ego default: reacting to threats to self-worth or rank
   - social default: conforming to group norms
   - inertia default: preferring the familiar and resisting change

6. Official book-level payoff.
   - Farnam Street and PRH summary copy support the larger promise that clearer ordinary moments improve later position and options.
