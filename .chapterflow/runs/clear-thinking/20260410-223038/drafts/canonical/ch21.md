Taking action matters, but action that only works in the ideal case remains fragile. A person can make a sound decision, execute it decisively, and still get broken by ordinary uncertainty if the plan leaves no room for error, delay, or pressure. That is why Margin of Safety follows next. Parrish uses this chapter to move from action into protection. Clear thinking improves when the reader stops treating buffers, slack, and reserves as waste and starts seeing them as the practical room that keeps reality from breaking the plan too cheaply.

The chapter should stay practical rather than sounding like technical risk language. The heading index gives it a narrow lane: slack, buffer, uncertainty, and resilience. That means margin of safety here is not about fearfulness or excessive caution. It is about recognizing that the world is noisy and that tight plans fail when they depend on everything going right. A schedule with no room, a budget with no reserve, or a system run at the edge may look efficient until ordinary variance turns that neatness into fragility.

That is why slack matters. People often treat extra time, space, or capacity as waste because it is not being consumed in the ideal case. The chapter pushes back on that instinct. Slack is what lets a plan absorb delay without collapse. Buffer is what keeps one mistake from becoming a cascade. Parrish's point is not that every plan should become padded and timid. It is that action becomes stronger when it is built to survive ordinary deviation instead of only perfect execution.

Resilience matters for the same reason. A strong plan is not one that looks elegant when the conditions are friendly. It is one that can keep functioning when reality stops being cooperative. The chapter should keep that pressure visible. If efficiency removes every reserve, then efficiency may only be another name for fragility. The better question is not whether the plan looks lean at rest, but whether it can keep going once uncertainty arrives.

The chapter still needs a boundary against overprotection. Margin of safety should not become an excuse to avoid commitment, inflate every reserve, or hide timid thinking behind prudent language. Parrish is arguing for enough room, not endless room. The goal is not to eliminate uncertainty. It is to keep uncertainty from breaking the decision before it has a fair chance to work.

The bridge into learning follows naturally from there. Once action has been protected well enough to survive reality, the next question is what the outcome should teach. Margin of Safety protects the move. Learn from Your Decisions asks how the result becomes better future judgment.
