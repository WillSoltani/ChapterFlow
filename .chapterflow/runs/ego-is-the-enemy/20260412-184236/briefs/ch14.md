Core Claim:
The chapter argues that when ego takes over success, three linked distortions start feeding each other: entitlement, the urge to overcontrol, and growing paranoia. They are signs that the self is trying to secure what success can never make permanently safe.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter names a cluster of success-stage ego defenses rather than one isolated habit. Its distinct move is to show how entitlement, control, and paranoia reinforce each other once importance and realism have been lost.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter follows value drift into a more defensive posture.
Then it shows how success can make the self feel owed, overresponsible, and suspicious at the same time.
Then it treats that cluster as an ego pattern that corrodes judgment and stability.
