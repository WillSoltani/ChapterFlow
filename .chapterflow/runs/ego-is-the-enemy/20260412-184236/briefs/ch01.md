Core Claim:
The opening chapter argues that ego often appears first as appetite for recognition rather than as obvious arrogance. Talking about plans, identity, and future greatness can produce a counterfeit sense of progress before anything has been built. The chapter therefore treats silence and private labor as strategic protection, not as deprivation.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is not merely about bragging less. Its distinct move is that talk itself becomes the trap because it feeds ambition with attention before work has earned substance. That makes the chapter impossible to confuse with later material about success or failure. It is about the earliest leak in ambition.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter identifies the temptation to announce, explain, and narrate your ambitions too early.
Then it shows that this talk gives ego a reward that should have belonged to the work.
Then it concludes that quiet effort is stronger because it preserves hunger, focus, and honesty.
