Ambition usually wants to be seen moving in a straight line. It wants ownership, visible importance, and fast credit. That is why ego resists any role that looks secondary. The chapter pushes against that reflex. Sometimes the stronger move is not to stand in the center of the frame but to widen the frame so the real work can advance.

That is the discipline behind the canvas strategy. Instead of asking how to be noticed first, the person asks how to make the room, the process, or the project stronger. This does not erase ambition. It redirects ambition into support that compounds. A person can organize the hidden dependencies, remove friction, prepare the conditions, and make other people's strengths easier to use. Those moves may look smaller from the outside. They can be far more effective.

Ego dislikes this because support often delays visible reward. When you do the setup work well, someone else may look smooth in public. When you solve the hidden blockage, the team may only notice that the result arrived on time. The chapter keeps that short-term frustration in view. If your identity depends on immediate recognition, you will keep abandoning the positions where real leverage is quietly built.

The point is not passivity. The chapter is not advising a person to disappear, surrender judgment, or become easy to use. It is describing a strategic way to move. Useful service creates trust, access, information, and freedom of action later. You are not stepping back because your ambition got smaller. You are stepping sideways so the work can move and so your ambition is attached to something more durable than applause.

This matters because ego keeps confusing direct credit with actual influence. Direct credit is emotionally satisfying, but it can be narrow and fragile. The person who creates the conditions for good work often ends up understanding the whole system better than the person chasing the microphone. That understanding becomes a deeper kind of power. It is less theatrical, less immediate, and often more transferable.

So the chapter asks for a different test. Do not ask only whether you were seen. Ask whether the work gained room, coherence, momentum, or trust because you were there. That shift keeps ambition sharp without making it noisy. It also prepares the next move. Once you can help the work advance without demanding spotlight every time, you are closer to the restraint that later chapters will require.
