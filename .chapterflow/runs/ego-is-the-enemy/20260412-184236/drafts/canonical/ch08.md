Early recognition can feel like proof that the struggle is over. A little praise arrives, one result goes well, or the room starts treating you differently, and ego begins building identity around the signal. That is the danger the chapter names. The problem is not that progress happened. The problem is that pride arrives before the underlying development has become stable.

When that happens, growth gets harder. A person who has started thinking of the early signal as proof of who they are will begin protecting that identity. Correction feels more threatening. Repetition feels less attractive. The ordinary work of getting better starts to feel beneath the image the person has already accepted. Early pride therefore slows the very process it is celebrating.

That is why the chapter is not attacking confidence itself. Confidence can be useful when it remains subordinate to continued work. The warning is about inflation. Identity starts rising faster than substance. The outside recognition may be real, but it is still too early to let the self harden around it.

This can feel especially tempting because early pride is emotionally bright. It offers a quick story: you have arrived, you are different now, you are no longer just becoming. The chapter keeps that temptation visible. Giving it up can feel like surrendering the most exciting version of yourself, the version that wants to believe the first applause already means permanent status.

But the chapter insists that development needs a slower rhythm than ego wants. Better to keep the signal small inside your identity and keep the work large inside your days. Better to treat early praise as information, not as a finished self-definition. That posture keeps the person open to more learning instead of prematurely defending a version of the self that has not yet earned its solidity.

So the chapter turns ambition back toward humility without turning it into denial. You do not have to pretend the result meant nothing. You do have to refuse the temptation to let one early result define who you are. That refusal leads directly into the next chapter's answer. If early pride is dangerous because it makes the self act finished too soon, then the remedy is continued labor, repetition, and work.
