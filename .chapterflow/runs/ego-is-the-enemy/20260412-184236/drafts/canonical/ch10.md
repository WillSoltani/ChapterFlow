Aspiration can create a dangerous illusion of completion. A person may think that because some early ego traps were exposed, the danger has been handled. The chapter refuses that relief. The same enemy does not disappear when the stage changes. It follows ambition forward.

That is the point of this interstitial. The lessons of the first section are not a past chapter in the person's life. They are a warning being carried into the next environment. Talk, pride, self-story, and premature certainty may have looked one way during aspiration. Success will give them brighter materials and quieter disguises.

This matters because success can feel safer than it really is. Recognition can make ego harder to spot precisely because the room starts rewarding the self more openly. A person can mistake progress for purification and believe that the earlier struggle has already made them immune. The chapter says that is another form of danger.

So the transition does not announce a new enemy. It announces continuity. Ego remains the recurring threat for everything that comes next. The next section will not ask whether ego still matters. It will ask how ego changes shape when the person has more proof, more standing, and more reasons to believe flattering stories.

That is why the handoff leads directly into studenthood again. If success can harden into certainty, then the protection cannot be relief. It has to be continued humility. Aspire ends with work. Success begins with the reminder that the same enemy is still waiting.
