Passion feels like seriousness because it is vivid. It speaks loudly, moves quickly, and gives a person the impression that strong emotion must mean strong commitment. The chapter pushes directly against that impression. Ego loves passion for the same reason it loves any dramatic surface: passion can make you feel intense, chosen, and alive before the harder question is answered. Can you keep working when the feeling changes?

That is the problem with ego-heavy intensity. It is often mood-dependent. A person surges into the work, speaks in absolute terms, frames the effort as destiny, and mistakes emotional heat for durable strength. The performance can look convincing. It can even persuade the person living inside it. But if the work relies on that high temperature, then the work has been tied to something unstable.

Discipline is the chapter's answer because discipline survives mood. It is steadier, less flattering, and less theatrical. That makes it easier for ego to underrate. Yet discipline is what keeps ambition alive once excitement fades, once obstacles become repetitive, and once the work stops offering dramatic proof that you are special for attempting it.

This is why the chapter is not anti-caring. It is anti-self-dramatization. Passion becomes dangerous when a person begins loving the feeling of intensity more than the standard the work requires. Then the emotional experience starts acting like evidence. The person sounds committed, feels committed, and may be admired for commitment, even while the actual pace of the work depends too heavily on whether the mood is currently burning.

The attraction remains important. Passion looks noble. It looks pure. It can also make a person socially impressive because intensity is visible in a way steadiness is not. The chapter keeps that temptation on the table so the correction does not feel fake. Ego prefers dramatic force because dramatic force is easier to display than controlled repetition.

So the chapter chooses the less glamorous loyalty. Work governed by discipline may look cooler from the outside, but it is more reliable when the task gets boring, humiliating, or slow. That reliability is what the previous chapter's studenthood needed all along. Once ambition is teachable, it still has to keep moving after the emotional surge dies. The next chapter can therefore shift from inner energy to tactical humility. After ego loses passion theater, it has one fewer costume and one more reason to work indirectly instead of loudly.
