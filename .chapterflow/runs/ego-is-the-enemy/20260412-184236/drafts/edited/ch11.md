Success can make learning feel optional. Praise, wins, and stronger standing tempt a person to believe that correction belongs to an earlier stage. This chapter rejects that feeling. Success does not cancel studenthood. It makes studenthood more necessary.

The temptation is understandable. During aspiration, many people treat being a student as temporary. They imagine that once the work is rewarded, uncertainty can relax. Success gives that hope more evidence because recognition starts confirming the identity the self wants to defend.

That is where ego becomes dangerous. Ego wants wins to become proof that correction is now beneath you or only for other people. The chapter pushes against that false finish. When success turns learning into a formality, the person becomes harder to correct and slower to notice drift.

Studenthood here is not beginner weakness. It is discipline under flattering conditions. It means treating praise as a harder test of humility, not as graduation from it. The person who stays a student after success keeps room for revision, instruction, and discomfort when certainty would be easier to perform.

That placement matters. The interstitial already warned that the same enemy enters success. This chapter gives the first answer: stay teachable. Once that answer is active, the next danger is easier to recognize, because ego will start building a flattering story around the proof it has just received.
