# Make Time Skeleton

## Metadata

- Title: `Make Time`
- Subtitle: `How to Focus on What Matters Every Day`
- Authors: `Jake Knapp`, `John Zeratsky`
- Edition lock: Crown Currency hardcover, `2018-09-25`, ISBN `9780525572428`
- Chapter packaging: 5 high-level units

## Chapter Order

1. `Introduction + How Make Time Works`
   - intent: explain why modern defaults capture the day and introduce the four-part framework
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: low
   - thin-chapter risk: low
2. `Highlight`
   - intent: show how to choose and protect one daily center of gravity
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: low
   - thin-chapter risk: low
3. `Laser`
   - intent: show how attention is defended against frictionless distraction
   - source richness: medium-high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: medium
   - thin-chapter risk: low
4. `Energize`
   - intent: connect focus to movement, food, sleep, and bodily energy instead of pure discipline
   - source richness: high
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: low
   - thin-chapter risk: medium
5. `Reflect`
   - intent: close the loop with experimentation, daily logging, and iteration
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium-high
   - moral complexity: low
   - thin-chapter risk: medium

## Rotation Plan

- Example formats: use all six canonical formats per chapter.
- Work / school / personal balance: 2 each per chapter.
- School settings rotate between high school, college, study group, lab, club, and team project.
- Callback plan:
  - Ch2 calls back to defaults from Ch1.
  - Ch3 treats Laser as protection for Ch2's Highlight.
  - Ch4 explains why Laser fails when the body is depleted.
  - Ch5 turns the whole book into a repeatable daily experiment.

## Motifs And Risks

- motif: small design beats heroic willpower
- motif: humane flexibility beats brittle optimization
- motif: the day needs a chosen center
- risk: making the book sound like generic productivity advice
- risk: overstating tactic-level support where the frozen bundle is thin
- risk: repeating the same anti-distraction lesson across Highlight and Laser
