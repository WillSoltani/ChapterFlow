The triad still organizes that first read. Presence changes whether someone seems fully there or partly elsewhere. Power changes whether they seem consequential or easy to ignore. Warmth changes whether the room experiences them as safe to approach or costly to trust. Different first impressions are really different early balances of presence, power, and warmth. A person who arrives composed but cold gets one read. A person who arrives warm but uncertain gets another. A person whose signals are steadier across the three often gets a read that gives later words a better chance to land cleanly.

The harder problem is that people often try to control first impressions too aggressively and end up thinning them out. If someone is busy managing every gesture, smile, or item of clothing, the room may feel the management before it feels the person. First impressions improve when outer signals are aligned, not when they are overproduced. The practical question is not how to force a perfect read. It is how to make the opening cues less contradictory so the room is more likely to read the right balance of presence, power, and warmth from the start.
