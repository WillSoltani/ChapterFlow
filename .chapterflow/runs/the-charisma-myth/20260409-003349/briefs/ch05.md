Core Claim:
This chapter argues that charisma becomes more stable when the reader prepares internal state before the room starts reading them. If internal state shows up outwardly, then charisma cannot be built only from surface technique. The chapter's job is to make state work practical without turning it into pseudo-science or therapy language.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the pre-room preparation chapter. Chapter 4 focused on in-the-moment repair after a slip. Chapter 5 must move earlier and show why stronger attention, steadiness, and goodwill need to be cultivated before the interaction begins.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter explains why internal state leaks into body language, timing, and social read.
Then it argues that technique layered over the wrong state remains fragile.
Then it gives the reader a narrow, practical way to prepare more useful mental states before pressure begins.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not promise that one exercise creates charisma on command. State preparation is partial, repeatable, and useful because it changes what is more likely to leak outward. Keep the chapter practical and behavioral. Do not drift into clinical advice, meditation doctrine, or absolute claims about confidence.
