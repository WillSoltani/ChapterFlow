Core Claim:
This chapter argues that charisma often breaks for understandable reasons. Presence, power, and warmth do not vanish randomly. They are disrupted by internal obstacles such as anxiety, distraction, self-consciousness, and defensive inwardness. The chapter's job is to make those disruptions readable so weak rooms stop feeling mysterious.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the instability chapter. Chapter 2 named the triad. Chapter 3 explains why the triad becomes unreliable under pressure and why a person who understands the model can still lose the room in real time.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter reminds the reader that presence, power, and warmth are fragile under pressure.
Then it shows how internal state leaks outward and distorts each signal differently.
Then it turns weak charisma from a trait verdict into an obstacle diagnosis.

Specific Applications:

Common Misreadings:
- treating obstacles as proof of permanent low charisma
- using one vague word like nerves instead of differentiating the actual blocker
- reaching for outward tricks before naming the internal disruption
- turning the chapter into pop psychology or pseudo-neuroscience

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not promise that every bad interaction has one clean cause. Obstacles can stack. The practical gain is not perfect certainty. It is better diagnosis. Keep the chapter diagnostic, not therapeutic, and do not steal Chapter 4's repair work or Chapter 5's deeper state work.
