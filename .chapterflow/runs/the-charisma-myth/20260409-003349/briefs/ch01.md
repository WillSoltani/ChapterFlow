Core Claim:
The opening chapter argues that charisma is not a mystical gift reserved for a few rare personalities. It is a set of learnable behaviors and internal states that change how other people feel in your presence. The chapter's main job is to break the myth that charisma is innate without overpromising instant transformation. It should make the reader believe charisma can be practiced, observed, and improved in concrete ways.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the myth-breaking opener. Its distinct job is not to teach the full triad yet, but to clear away the false binary of "born with it or not" so the rest of the book has permission to become practical.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter names the common belief that charisma is an inborn trait associated with famous people.
Then it counters that belief by framing charisma as a trainable set of behaviors and states rather than fixed essence.
Then it shows why this matters: if charisma is trainable, the reader can stop admiring it from a distance and start working on it directly.

Required Anchors:
- the familiar public myth built around figures like Bill Clinton, Steve Jobs, and Oprah as examples people treat as naturally charismatic
- Marilyn Monroe's reported ability to "turn on" charisma in public as support for charisma behaving more like a switchable social effect than a fixed trait
- the official "dial" framing from the lawful source bundle: charisma can be raised or lowered through specific internal states and external behaviors

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
The frozen bundle supports the learnable-charisma claim, but not a full ledger of experiments or detailed scientific mechanisms. Keep the proof narrow and practical. Preserve a live limit: if charisma is trainable, training still has to feel human rather than staged, and some contexts reward different expressions of charisma.
