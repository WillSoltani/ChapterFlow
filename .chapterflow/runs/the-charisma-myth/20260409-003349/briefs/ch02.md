Core Claim:
This chapter argues that charisma is not one mysterious quality. It is built from three readable ingredients: presence, power, and warmth. Presence makes people feel you are actually with them. Power makes them feel you can affect outcomes. Warmth makes them feel your strength will not be used against them. The chapter's job is to define those ingredients clearly enough that later chapters can work on them one by one.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's defining triad chapter. Its distinct job is to replace vague charisma talk with a three-part model and to show why imbalance matters just as much as presence of the parts themselves.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter names the three ingredients behind charisma.
Then it defines presence, power, and warmth in practical social terms.
Then it shows that charisma depends on how the three signals combine, not on one of them alone.

Required Anchors:
- the official `3 keys to charisma` framing: presence, power, and warmth

- power as perceived ability to affect outcomes and warmth as perceived goodwill

Specific Applications:
- noticing why someone can seem impressive but cold
- understanding why a friendly person may still fail to influence a room
- preparing for meetings, seminars, or personal conversations by asking which ingredient is missing

Common Misreadings:
- treating the triad as a checklist instead of an interaction
- collapsing warmth into niceness
- collapsing power into aggression or rank alone
- ignoring presence because it feels less dramatic than power

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter gives a useful model, but the right balance changes by context. Too much visible power without warmth can trigger distance. Warmth without enough power can lower influence. Presence is necessary because without it the other two often fail to register. Keep the chapter practical and avoid claiming a universal formula.
