Chapter Promise:
This chapter will make the reader see charisma as a readable combination of presence, power, and warmth instead of one vague trait word.

Opening Move:
Open on the familiar puzzle of why someone can feel impressive but cold, or kind but forgettable, and use that tension to introduce the triad.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: surface the puzzle that charisma cannot be one thing if different people unsettle or attract us in different ways
- P2: introduce the triad and define presence as full attention that people can feel
- P3: define power as perceived capacity, status, or force that creates credibility
- P4: define warmth as visible goodwill that makes power easier to trust
- P5: show how imbalance among the three changes the whole read
- P6: preserve the live question of missing-signal diagnosis and bridge to obstacles

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is combinational. Charisma is not identical with confidence, kindness, or status alone. It is the read created when presence, power, and warmth land together or land unevenly.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep the diagnostic problem visible: when a person feels off, is the missing ingredient presence, power, warmth, or the balance between them?

Threshold Question:
Which missing ingredient changes the whole room's interpretation when the other two seem present?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: charisma is a triad, not one trait
- Takeaway 2: presence is full attention and cannot be faked for long
- Takeaway 3: power creates credibility and consequence
- Takeaway 4: warmth creates trust and safety
- Takeaway 5: imbalance changes the whole read

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dialogue, work): teaches diagnosing why a strong manager feels powerful but distant
- Scenario 2 (decision_point, school): teaches how divided attention weakens presence in a seminar or club setting
- Scenario 3 (inner_monologue, personal): teaches the private read of warmth without enough power
- Scenario 4 (dilemma, work): teaches balancing authority and goodwill in feedback
- Scenario 5 (vignette, school): teaches how presence changes the social feel of a room before words
- Scenario 6 (contrast, personal): teaches how the same friendliness lands differently when power is present or absent

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include one concrete detail: conference-room speakerphone light, seminar notebooks spread open, half-finished tea mug, club election ballots, text-message typing dots, hallway echo before a conversation.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask why these ingredients disappear or distort under stress. It must not explain the obstacle chapter in full.

Genericity Risks:
- turning the chapter into bland leadership advice
- treating the three ingredients as obvious and leaving them undefined
- making the chapter sound like a personality test

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: confidence equals charisma
- filler move 2: warmth means being nice all the time
- invented authority move 1: unsupported body-language percentages or brain claims
- brief-language leak 1: repeating triad labels without giving them social consequence
