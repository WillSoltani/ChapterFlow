Chapter Promise:
This chapter will explain why people who understand presence, power, and warmth can still lose those signals under pressure.

Opening Move:
Open on the frustration of knowing the triad and still feeling it fall apart in a live room because anxiety, distraction, or self-consciousness gets there first.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P5
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: carry forward the Chapter 2 question by showing that the triad often breaks in real time rather than in theory
- P2: diagnose obstacles to presence as inward attention, anxiety, distraction, and split focus
- P3: diagnose obstacles to power as doubt, hesitation, shrinking, and over-apology
- P4: diagnose obstacles to warmth as defensiveness, self-protection, and social bracing
- P5: show that obstacles stack and get misread as personality or fate if they are not differentiated
- P6: leave the reader with a better question about obstacle diagnosis and bridge into repair

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is distortion under pressure. Internal obstacles change what the room reads before the person has time to explain themselves.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep this question live: which obstacle is actually distorting the room first, and how does that obstacle change presence, power, or warmth?

Threshold Question:
What inner obstacle is distorting the room before you reach for more technique?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: weak charisma under pressure is often obstacle-driven rather than trait-driven
- Takeaway 2: distraction and anxiety fracture presence
- Takeaway 3: doubt and shrinking weaken power
- Takeaway 4: defensiveness cools warmth
- Takeaway 5: stacked obstacles must be diagnosed before repair can work cleanly

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dilemma, work): teaches choosing between bluffing through anxiety or naming the obstacle before a client call
- Scenario 2 (vignette, school): teaches how a distracted graduate seminar student loses presence before speaking
- Scenario 3 (decision_point, personal): teaches noticing self-consciousness before a difficult personal conversation
- Scenario 4 (contrast, work): teaches the difference between confident authority and self-doubting drift in feedback
- Scenario 5 (dialogue, school): teaches how defensiveness cools warmth in a lab or seminar disagreement
- Scenario 6 (inner_monologue, personal): teaches how multiple obstacles stack before a social gathering

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include one concrete detail: laptop tabs flickering in a seminar, dry mouth before a client call, scholarship folder edge bending in a hand, hallway fluorescent hum, half-zipped backpack, unread message banner lighting a screen.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask how the reader can interrupt the obstacle once it is visible. It must not pre-explain Chapter 4's repair sequence.

Genericity Risks:
- naming stress in general without showing what it does to a specific signal
- collapsing all obstacles into one bland nerves paragraph
- turning the chapter into motivational reassurance instead of diagnosis

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: people just need confidence
- filler move 2: charisma failure is always a personality flaw
- invented authority move 1: unsupported brain-region or chemical explanations
- brief-language leak 1: calling every obstacle anxiety when the social effect is different
