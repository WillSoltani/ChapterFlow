Chapter Promise:
This chapter will show the reader how to interrupt an obstacle in time and restore enough presence, power, or warmth for the room to change.

Opening Move:
Open on the relief that a charisma lapse does not have to become destiny if the reader can notice the obstacle early and reset the right signal.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P5
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: reject the idea that once the obstacle starts, the room is already lost
- P2: show that repair begins with noticing and interrupting the internal loop rather than pretending it is not happening
- P3: explain repair for presence through outward attention and re-entry into the interaction
- P4: explain repair for power and warmth through steadiness, clear consequence, and restored goodwill
- P5: show why partial recovery still matters even when the reader does not feel perfectly calm
- P6: leave the reader with a forward-looking question about building stronger states before the moment and bridge to Chapter 5

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is practical interruption. Repair works by changing what the room is reading soon enough to keep a temporary obstacle from becoming the whole interaction.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep this question alive: how do you recover under pressure without becoming visibly theatrical or fake?

Threshold Question:
What reset would restore the missing signal soon enough to change the room's read?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: a charisma lapse is not final if the obstacle is noticed early
- Takeaway 2: presence recovers when attention returns outward
- Takeaway 3: power recovers when steadiness and consequence return
- Takeaway 4: warmth recovers when goodwill becomes visible again
- Takeaway 5: imperfect repair still changes the social read and prepares later mental-state work

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (inner_monologue, work): teaches a private reset before a difficult meeting begins badly
- Scenario 2 (contrast, school): teaches two versions of a scholarship interview waiting-room recovery
- Scenario 3 (vignette, personal): teaches a small reset before an emotionally loaded conversation at home
- Scenario 4 (decision_point, work): teaches choosing whether to pause, re-center, and re-enter after a shaky opening
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): teaches repairing warmth and steadiness after a tense admissions or seminar exchange
- Scenario 6 (dialogue, personal): teaches helping someone else recover presence without smothering them

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include one concrete detail: scholarship folder on a lap, reception clock ticking, glass of water warming in a hand, elevator reflection, admissions name badge, chair legs scraping before a panel starts.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask how to build better mental states before the obstacle appears. It must not pre-explain Chapter 5's full state-creation chapter.

Genericity Risks:
- drifting into soft reassurance without operational moves
- promising perfect calm as the condition for repair
- making the chapter sound like generic confidence advice

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: just be yourself and relax
- filler move 2: repair means pretending the obstacle is gone
- invented authority move 1: unsupported breathing science or performance hacks
- brief-language leak 1: turning every reset into vague positivity instead of signal repair
