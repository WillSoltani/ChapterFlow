Chapter Promise:
This chapter will make the reader stop treating charisma as a gift some people simply have and start seeing it as a trainable social effect.

Opening Move:
Open on the instant assumption that some people were "just born with it" and make that belief feel familiar before breaking it.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: surface the familiar myth that charisma belongs to rare public figures
- P2: use Bill Clinton / Steve Jobs / Oprah as the stereotype people point to when they call charisma innate
- P3: use Marilyn Monroe's reported ability to switch charisma on and off to crack the fixed-trait story
- P4: use the "dial" framing to argue that charisma behaves like something that can be raised or lowered
- P5: explain why this changes the reader's stance from admiration-at-distance to practice-in-context
- P6: preserve the limit that trainable does not mean fake, then bridge to the question of components

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is not confidence versus insecurity. It is fixed-trait myth versus trainable social behavior. The chapter matters because that distinction determines whether the reader treats charisma as destiny or practice.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the tension between trainability and authenticity. Hard depth should ask how deliberate charisma practice avoids turning into obvious self-performance.

Threshold Question:
If charisma can be learned, what exactly are people responding to when they say someone "has it"?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: charisma is not an inborn trait
- Takeaway 2: public myths distort how people read charismatic figures
- Takeaway 3: charisma can be switched up or down by behavior and state
- Takeaway 4: trainability changes what the reader should do next
- Takeaway 5: deliberate practice must still feel human

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): teaches replacing "I’m just not charismatic" with trainable behavior before a meeting
- Scenario 2 (dialogue, school): teaches reframing charisma as practice in a professor or student-leader conversation
- Scenario 3 (dilemma, personal): teaches why waiting to feel naturally magnetic keeps someone passive
- Scenario 4 (inner_monologue, work): teaches how the fixed-trait myth sabotages preparation before a presentation
- Scenario 5 (contrast, school): teaches the difference between idolizing a charismatic classmate and noticing repeatable behaviors
- Scenario 6 (vignette, personal): teaches how a small shift in attention and state changes a social read

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include one concrete environmental detail: laptop camera light, seminar table with water bottles, elevator mirror before a meeting, noisy apartment entryway, student club sign-in table.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask which ingredients create charisma if it is not magic. It must not explain the full triad.

Genericity Risks:
- drifting into generic confidence coaching
- using public figures as empty celebrity decoration
- promising full transformation without practice or limits

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: "believe in yourself" as the mechanism
- filler move 2: charisma as vague energy
- invented authority move 1: unsupported lab detail or brain jargon
- brief-language leak 1: repeating "trainable social effect" mechanically
