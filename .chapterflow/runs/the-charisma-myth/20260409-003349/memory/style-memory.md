# Style Memory — compact

Compiled from the v13 style benchmark files and frozen for this run.

## Voice
- Sharp human explainer with taste. Authored, not assembled.
- Specific over generic. Concrete over abstract. Insight over padding.
- Use "you" naturally, not constantly.
- Zero reader-facing "this chapter says/teaches" language.
- Author name only sparingly in breakdowns.

## Grade bands
- Easy: grade 8-9. Accessible doorway, one vivid example early, short-to-medium sentences.
- Medium: grade 10-11. Mechanism plus application, moderate abstraction, one or two live tensions.
- Hard: grade 12. Boundary condition, contradiction, failure mode, or synthesis. Never medium-plus-adjectives.

## Hard constraints
- No invented facts, quotes, studies, mechanisms, anecdotes, or chapter-specific subframeworks.
- No em dashes. No made-up quotes.
- No pseudo-science filler or prestige language unless the brief explicitly supports it.
- No contamination phrases or planner-language leakage.
- First sentence must create curiosity, not thesis.
- Every paragraph must do a distinct job.
- No generic self-help cadence, corporate tone, or podcast-summary mush.

## Tone-object contract
- Gentle: lowers resistance and feels warm without going soft.
- Direct: names the mechanism and decision line cleanly.
- Competitive: sharpens stakes and consequence.
- Tone variants must differ in function, not intensity only.
- Plain-string scenarios or near-identical tone variants are auto-fails.

## Gold-pattern reminders
- Anchors must be load-bearing, not decorative.
- Examples must feel socially real: work, school, personal, concrete details.
- `whatToDo` gives a precise move the reader can picture.
- `whyItMatters` pays off the scenario instead of restating it.
- Quiz questions should test judgment with tempting distractors.

## Read-aloud test
- If it sounds pre-manufactured, cut it.
- If it sounds smarter than the brief, cut it.
- If it sounds like a note to the writer, cut it.

## The Charisma Myth guardrails
- Keep the prose practical, behavioral, and interpersonal rather than mystical.
- Do not flatten charisma into extroversion, charm, or attractiveness.
- Treat presence, power, and warmth as distinct levers, not one blended vibe word.
- Avoid unsupported neuroscience language even though the book gestures at science.
- Do not mix the older 2012 ending chapter (`The Charismatic Life`) into this current-edition run.
- Later applied chapters should stay specific to first impressions, listening, body language, digital settings, presentations, difficult interactions, and crisis moments.
