#chapter-outline-template.md
Chapter Promise:
Show how long-term relationships are maintained through reciprocity, repair, and repeated attention rather than assumed momentum.

Opening Move:
Open by distinguishing getting close from keeping closeness alive over time.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2, P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3, P4

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: move from closeness into maintenance
- P2: explain why reciprocity still matters over time
- P3: explain repair after strain or neglect
- P4: warn that bonds decay when maintenance is assumed away
- P5: connect durability to repeated small actions rather than dramatic rescue
- P6: bridge into relationship risk and deception themes

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between a relationship that feels established and one that is still being actively maintained.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard mode must preserve that strong bonds still weaken if reciprocity or repair disappears.

Threshold Question:
When does a relationship feel durable because it is being maintained, and when does it only feel durable because decay has not shown up yet?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: closeness must be maintained
- Takeaway 2: reciprocity remains a condition of durability
- Takeaway 3: repair matters after strain
- Takeaway 4: neglect weakens long-term bonds
- Takeaway 5: maintenance sets up the book's risk chapter

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choose maintenance over assumption after a good early bond
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): analyze how a relationship thinned after neglect
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): compare defensiveness with repair-minded response
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predict which team bond lasts
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): decide whether to address distance before it hardens
- Scenario 6 (before_after, school): compare passive drift with active maintenance

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what can go wrong inside relationships even after a bond has formed.

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: “real relationships should be effortless”
- filler move 2: unsupported counseling framework
- invented authority move 1: detailed marital research claims
