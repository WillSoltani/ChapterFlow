#chapter-outline-template.md
Chapter Promise:
Show that friendship and attraction grow through identifiable conditions rather than pure chemistry, and that those conditions can be adjusted deliberately.

Opening Move:
Open on the familiar mystery of why some relationships deepen while others stay thin, then reveal that Schafer treats friendship as a formula with four variables.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2, P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4, P5

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Turn "chemistry" into a practical question about what makes relationships grow.
- P2: Introduce the four-part friendship formula.
- P3: Explain proximity, frequency, and duration without letting them blur together.
- P4: Show why intensity is different and why it acts like glue.
- P5: Show how the formula can diagnose weak relationships or let unwanted ones cool gradually.
- P6: Close on the formula's limit and bridge into the first-impression signals of Chapter 2.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between contact and closeness. More contact does not automatically create closeness unless the interaction is nonthreatening and emotionally meaningful enough to produce intensity.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the uncomfortable fact that the same variables can deepen bad influence as well as healthy closeness, and that the formula sets conditions but cannot manufacture sincerity.

Threshold Question:
If the conditions for friendship can be engineered, where is the line between relationship skill and manipulation?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: relationships are shaped by conditions, not luck alone
- Takeaway 2: proximity works only when the setting feels safe
- Takeaway 3: frequency and duration are related but not identical
- Takeaway 4: intensity is the force multiplier and emotional glue
- Takeaway 5: the formula is a diagnostic tool, not a guarantee of genuine trust

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choose how to increase rapport with a new colleague without crowding them
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): analyze why a study-group bond stayed weak despite frequent meetings
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): notice that a long friendship feels stale because intensity dropped
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predict whether a remote working relationship will strengthen or fade
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): decide how to distance yourself from a draining person without a dramatic break
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): compare a couple's high-duration low-intensity routine with a more intentional version

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, specify one sensory or situational detail to include.
- office kitchen with half-finished coffee
- seminar room under fluorescent lights
- apartment couch with the television on
- text thread that grows shorter over weeks

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask how people make themselves readable and safe before a relationship has enough time or intensity to build.

Genericity Risks:
- turning the formula into bland social advice
- collapsing proximity, frequency, and duration into one fuzzy idea
- treating intensity as romance only

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: generic "quality over quantity" platitudes
- filler move 2: vague advice to "be authentic" without tying it to the formula
- invented authority move 1: unsupported brain chemistry or body-language science
- brief-language leak 1: formulaic restatement of "unsupported zones" or "concept budget"
