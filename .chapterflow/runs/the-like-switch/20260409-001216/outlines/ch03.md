Chapter Promise:
Show that people like those who make them feel good about themselves because attention directed outward creates positive emotional residue.

Opening Move:
Open on the common mistake of trying too hard to be impressive when the real driver of liking is how the other person feels around you.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2, P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3, P4

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Contrast self-display with making the other person feel good.
- P2: State the golden rule and explain the basic mechanism.
- P3: Show how ego hijacks attention and weakens likability.
- P4: Explain why outward focus creates repeat contact and stronger liking.
- P5: Draw the sincerity limit and warn against fake flattery.
- P6: Bridge into broader attraction forces for Chapter 4.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between impressing people and improving their emotional experience of themselves in your presence.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard mode must preserve that the rule fails when it becomes obvious flattery, manipulation, or self-erasure without judgment.

Threshold Question:
How do you make someone feel good about themselves without turning your attention into performance?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: liking follows emotional residue, not only visible charm
- Takeaway 2: the ego keeps attention trapped on the self
- Takeaway 3: real attention is more persuasive than self-display
- Takeaway 4: fake praise breaks the rule instead of fulfilling it
- Takeaway 5: the rule helps deepen existing bonds as well as start new ones
