# Source Sidecar

Chapter: ch03 — The Golden Rule of Friendship
Richness: medium
Source IDs: src-02, src-03
Canonical edition: Touchstone / Simon & Schuster, 2015, ISBN 9781476754482
Quote posture: paraphrase_first_with_one_verified_line

Core claim:
- If you want people to like you, make them feel good about themselves.

Distinct mechanism:
- Liking grows when attention is directed outward and the other person leaves the interaction feeling better about who they are.

Required anchors:
- The golden rule itself.
- Most people fail because their ego keeps attention on themselves.
- People return to those who leave them with positive feelings about themselves.

Specific applications:
- New conversations.
- Existing relationships.
- Work, school, and personal interactions where self-display competes with attention.

Common misreadings:
- Flattery equals the rule.
- The rule means agreeing with everything.
- The rule excuses manipulative attention.

Hard-edge requirement:
- Preserve the limit that the rule fails when attention turns into obvious performance or self-erasure.

Threshold question:
- How do you focus on someone else sincerely enough to help them feel good without making the interaction feel staged?
