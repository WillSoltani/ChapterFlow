# Source Sidecar

Chapter: ch01 — The Friendship Formula
Richness: medium
Source IDs: src-02, src-04, src-05
Canonical edition: Touchstone / Simon & Schuster, 2015, ISBN 9781476754482
Quote posture: paraphrase_first

Core claim:
- Friendship can be understood as a function of proximity, frequency, duration, and intensity rather than as pure chance.

Distinct mechanism:
- The chapter gives the reader a four-part model for diagnosing, strengthening, or cooling a relationship.

Required anchors:
- The formula itself combines proximity, frequency, duration, and intensity.
- Proximity works only in a nonthreatening setting.
- Intensity is expressed through verbal and nonverbal behaviors and acts as the glue of closeness.

Specific applications:
- Evaluate why a relationship feels weak.
- Strengthen a relationship by increasing one or more variables consciously.
- Let an unwanted relationship fade gradually by reducing the variables over time.

Common misreadings:
- More contact always means better relationship.
- Intensity is only romantic.
- Duration is harmless regardless of the quality of influence.

Hard-edge requirement:
- Keep the limit visible: the formula can shape conditions, but it cannot guarantee trust or ethical intent.

Threshold question:
- If these conditions can be adjusted deliberately, when does good relationship skill become manipulation?
