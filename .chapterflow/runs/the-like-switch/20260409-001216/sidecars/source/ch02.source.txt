Chapter: ch02 — Getting Noticed Before a Word Is Spoken
Richness: medium
Source IDs: src-02, src-04, src-05
Canonical edition: Touchstone / Simon & Schuster, 2015, ISBN 9781476754482
Quote posture: paraphrase_first

Core claim:
- First impressions form quickly, and low-threat nonverbal cues help another person read you as approachable before conversation has much chance to help.

Distinct mechanism:
- Friend signals such as eyebrow flash, head tilt, smile, and open posture reduce perceived threat and create permission for contact.

Required anchors:
- Eyebrow flash is a rapid nonthreat signal.
- Head tilt exposes vulnerability and implies trust.
- Smile and open signals improve approachability, but do not create lasting trust on their own.

Specific applications:
- Open meetings and introductions better.
- Avoid accidentally projecting coldness or threat.
- Recover from a poor first impression by adjusting style early.

Common misreadings:
- First impressions do not matter much.
- One cue guarantees instant rapport.
- Friend signals can replace later listening and substance.

Hard-edge requirement:
- Keep the limit visible: first impressions are sticky, but friend signals only open the door; they do not finish the relationship.

Threshold question:
- How much of a first impression can you shape deliberately before the effort starts to feel like a mask?
