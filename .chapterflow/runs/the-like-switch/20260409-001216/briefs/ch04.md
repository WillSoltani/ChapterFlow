Core Claim:
Attraction grows through recurring supportive forces rather than through mystery alone. This chapter broadens from the golden rule into the larger conditions that increase liking: repeated contact, reciprocity, self-disclosure, and social support that confirms who people feel themselves to be. The safest framing is that attraction compounds when contact feels rewarding, reciprocal, and identity-supporting.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is broader and more synthetic than Chapter 3. It is not just about the immediate emotional rule of attention. Its distinct job is to connect exposure, reciprocity, disclosure, and support into a wider account of why some people become more attractive over time.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter expands beyond initial liking into the forces that deepen attraction.
Then it shows that regular contact, reciprocity, and appropriate self-disclosure increase closeness.
Then it warns that attraction weakens when support, reciprocity, or calibration is missing.

Required Anchors:
- Repeated contact increases the chance of attraction.
- Reciprocity helps acquaintanceship become friendship.
- Self-disclosure and support deepen the bond when calibrated.
