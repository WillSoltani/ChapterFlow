Core Claim:
Relationships grow in predictable ways rather than by magic. Schafer frames friendship as a controllable mix of proximity, frequency, duration, and intensity. The chapter's job is to show that likeability can be improved by regulating those conditions rather than waiting for chemistry to appear on its own.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's first real mechanism chapter. It is not yet about a specific conversational trick or first-impression move. Its distinct job is to turn friendship into a workable operating model with four interacting levers and to show that weakening or strengthening a relationship can be done by changing those levers.

Author Logic Chain:
First Schafer argues that relationships follow a pattern instead of random luck.
Then he defines the four variables: proximity, frequency, duration, and intensity.
Then he shows how the same model can diagnose weak relationships, strengthen promising ones, or let unwanted relationships fade gradually.

Required Anchors:
- The formula itself: proximity x (frequency + duration) x intensity.
- Proximity only helps in a nonthreatening environment.
- Intensity functions as the emotional glue that makes the other variables matter.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Friendship Formula: a four-part model for evaluating or shaping relationships.
- Proximity: shared space and exposure over time in a nonthreatening setting.
- Frequency and duration: how often and how long contact happens.
- Intensity: the degree to which interactions meet psychological or physical needs through behavior.

Specific Applications:
- Diagnose why a relationship feels stalled.
- Strengthen a promising relationship by adjusting one or more of the four levers.
- Let an unwanted relationship cool without abrupt confrontation.

Common Misreadings:
- Treating the formula like a manipulation trick instead of a relationship diagnostic.
- Assuming frequency alone can replace intensity.
- Treating proximity as mere physical closeness even when the setting feels threatening.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should acknowledge that the formula is a simplification and does not guarantee genuine trust or ethical intent. It should also note that duration can deepen both good and bad influence.
