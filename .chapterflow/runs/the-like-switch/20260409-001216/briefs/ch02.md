Core Claim:
People read safety, warmth, and approachability before a real conversation begins. Schafer's chapter narrows to nonverbal "friend signals" and first-impression dynamics that make others less defensive and more open to contact. The point is not flashy charisma; it is sending low-threat signals quickly enough that an interaction can start well.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Unlike Chapter 1, this chapter is about the opening seconds rather than whole relationships. Its distinct mechanism is that small, readable cues such as eyebrow flash, head tilt, smile, and open posture shape whether another person sees you as safe or threatening before your words can do much work.

Author Logic Chain:
First Schafer establishes that people form impressions fast and keep them.
Then he identifies friend signals as rapid, low-threat nonverbal cues.
Then he shows that the real job of these cues is to lower threat and make conversation possible, not to fake depth.

Required Anchors:
- Eyebrow flash as a rapid nonthreat signal.
- Head tilt as a vulnerable, trusting display.
- Smile and related open signals as fast rapport builders.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Friend signals: eyebrow flash, head tilt, smile, and related open cues.
- First impression: a filter through which future words and actions get judged.
- Low-threat signaling: behavior that tells the other person you are safe to engage.

Specific Applications:
- Improve the opening seconds of a meeting, introduction, or negotiation.
- Avoid accidentally signaling threat, arrogance, or coldness.
- Recover from a potentially negative first impression by adjusting communication style quickly.

Common Misreadings:
- Assuming first impressions are trivial because words matter more.
- Treating the signals as a manipulative hack rather than a doorway to real rapport.
- Confusing warmth with dominance or oversharing.

Counterarguments or Limits:
