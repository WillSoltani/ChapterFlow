Core Claim:
If you want people to like you, make them feel good about themselves. Schafer treats this as the central friendship rule because people gravitate toward those who leave them feeling seen, comfortable, and more positive about who they are. The mechanism is not flattery alone. It is overriding your own ego long enough to focus real attention on the other person.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter shifts from approachability to emotional payoff. Chapter 2 opened the interaction; Chapter 3 explains what makes the interaction rewarding enough for the other person to want more of it. Its distinct mechanism is that liking grows when attention is directed outward rather than inward.

Author Logic Chain:
First Schafer states the golden rule clearly: people like those who make them feel good about themselves.
Then he argues that most people fail here because their ego keeps attention on their own wants and needs.
Then he shows that outward focus creates the positive emotional residue that keeps people coming back.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Golden Rule of Friendship: making others feel good about themselves.
- Ego override: redirecting attention away from yourself and toward the other person.

Specific Applications:
- Keep attention on the other person during early conversations.
- Make questions, listening, and responses serve their experience rather than your self-display.
- Use the rule to deepen existing relationships, not only to start new ones.

Common Misreadings:
- Treating the rule as shallow flattery.
- Assuming making people feel good means agreeing with everything they say.
- Using attention as a manipulation script instead of genuine focus.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter must admit that praise without sincerity feels fake and that self-erasure is not the goal. The rule works best when attention is real, responsive, and proportionate.
