Chapter 6 takes the relationship one step deeper. Good conversation helps people feel heard, but closeness requires more than a well-run exchange. Schafer's next move is to explain how shared ground, reciprocal disclosure, and growing trust turn rapport into something more personal.

Common ground matters because similarity lowers friction. When people discover that they share interests, experiences, values, or circumstances, the interaction gets easier to continue. The point is not that the similarity has to be dramatic. Even modest overlap can create the sense that two people are not starting from entirely separate worlds. That early alignment helps rapport feel less accidental.

The chapter then moves into self-disclosure. Closeness grows when people reveal more of themselves over time, but Schafer's framing is narrower than a simple command to be vulnerable. Disclosure helps when it is reciprocal and paced to the relationship. If one person keeps opening while the other mostly receives, the bond can start feeling unbalanced instead of intimate. The disclosure has to be met.

That is where trust calibration becomes important. A relationship can be promising and still not be ready to hold everything. Too little openness keeps the connection shallow, but too much too early can create pressure, embarrassment, or the sense that the closeness is being forced. The chapter's logic is that timing matters just as much as honesty. What deepens trust in one stage can strain it in another.

This is also why common ground has a limit. Real similarity can help people move toward each other faster, but fake similarity works against trust once it is felt as performance. You do not build closeness by inventing alignment that is not there. The chapter stays strongest when common ground is treated as discovery rather than as manipulation.

Put together, the chapter gives a practical model of deepening friendship. Find real overlap. Let disclosure grow in both directions. Keep the pace matched to the amount of trust already earned. Closeness is not just the presence of openness. It is openness that the relationship can actually carry without tipping into imbalance.

That makes Chapter 6 the right bridge into long-term relationship maintenance. Once a bond has real trust and personal knowledge inside it, the next problem is not how to start closeness but how to preserve it. Chapter 7 can then move from building connection into sustaining, repairing, and protecting it over time.
