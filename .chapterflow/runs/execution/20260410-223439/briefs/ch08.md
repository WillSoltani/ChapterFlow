Core Claim:
Strategy review is not a ceremonial presentation. It is a live management practice that pressures the plan through competition knowledge, organizational capability, strategic focus, idea quality, linkage to people and operations, and follow-through. Its pressure point is that review becomes useful only when leaders treat it as a disciplined conversation strong enough to expose weakness and force action.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 7 defined what a real strategy has to answer. Chapter 8 defines how leaders review that strategy without letting the meeting become theater. Its distinct job is to shift from strategy content into review-process discipline.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author frames strategy review through the questions leaders must raise.
Then the author tests competitive knowledge and organizational capability.
Then the author asks whether the plan is scattered or focused and whether the right ideas are being chosen.
Then the author forces linkage to people and operations and closes on following through after the review.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- review discipline: using live questions to test strategy rather than merely hear it described.
- presentation theater: a meeting that protects the plan from real pressure.
- focus test: asking whether the plan is sharply chosen or scattered across too many ideas.
- review-to-follow-through link: the requirement that the conversation changes what happens next.

Specific Applications:
- checking whether a strategy meeting is actually testing assumptions or merely displaying slides
- evaluating whether leaders know the competition and capability deeply enough to judge the plan
- identifying whether follow-through is built into the review or postponed indefinitely

Common Misreadings:
- strategy review mainly means hearing an update presentation
- focus and idea choice are aesthetic questions rather than execution questions
- people and operations can be checked later after the strategy meeting
- follow-through is separate from the quality of the review itself

Counterarguments or Limits:
The frozen bundle confirms the chapter frame and its main review questions, but it does not license invented meeting transcripts, boardroom scenes, or company-review anecdotes. Keep the chapter on review mechanics, question quality, and follow-through rather than on unsupported storytelling.
