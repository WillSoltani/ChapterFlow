Core Claim:
Execution becomes durable only when leader behavior turns into culture. This chapter operationalizes culture through rewards, social software, robust dialogue, and the behaviors leaders exhibit and tolerate. Its pressure point is that culture is not a values poster or an atmosphere. Culture is the repeated lesson about what gets respect, consequence, and attention inside the organization.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 3 named the leader behaviors that support execution. Chapter 4 shows how those behaviors become shared norms instead of staying trapped in one leader's personal style. It is a culture chapter, but not in an abstract morale sense. Its distinct mechanism is operationalization.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author reframes culture as something leaders make concrete.
Then the author links rewards and performance so standards are visible.
Then the author uses social software and robust dialogue to explain how truth and coordination move through the organization.
Then the author closes on a hard mirror: leaders get the behavior they exhibit and tolerate.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- operationalized culture: culture expressed through repeated management signals rather than slogans.
- reward signal: the visible link between performance and status, trust, or consequence.
- social software: the human operating pattern through which dialogue, truth, and coordination move.
- tolerance effect: the way leader inaction quietly defines the real standard.

Specific Applications:
- checking whether rewards, meetings, and consequences reinforce the stated standard
- diagnosing whether dialogue is robust enough to surface truth before drift hardens
- spotting where tolerated sloppiness is rewriting the culture more strongly than formal values

Common Misreadings:
- culture is mainly mood or inspiration
- strong culture means friendliness or harmony
- dialogue matters only for communication style, not execution quality
- tolerance is passive and therefore culturally neutral
