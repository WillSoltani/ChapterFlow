Core Claim:
The operations process turns strategy and people decisions into coordinated execution through budgets, assumptions, synchronization, trade-offs, contingencies, quarterly reviews, and disciplined follow-through. Its pressure point is that plans become real only when leaders surface assumptions, make hard trade-offs, and align moving parts fast enough for the business to carry them.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 8 defined how strategy gets reviewed seriously. Chapter 9 defines how the business then synchronizes the plan in operating terms. Its distinct job is to turn sharper strategic understanding into budgets, timing, assumptions, contingencies, and living operating discipline.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- operating synchronization: aligning timing, resources, and responsibilities so strategy can move coherently.
- assumption discipline: forcing goals and plans to rest on explicit, realistic premises.
- trade-off work: choosing what matters most when constraints prevent doing everything at once.
- contingency readiness: planning for what happens if the plan meets disruption or misses.

Specific Applications:
- checking whether a plan has realistic assumptions under it
- identifying whether teams are synchronized or merely busy in parallel
- testing whether trade-offs and contingencies are being made explicitly or avoided

Common Misreadings:
- operations is only budget administration
- assumptions are background details rather than central operating risks
- good plans remove the need for trade-offs
- contingencies matter only after failure appears
