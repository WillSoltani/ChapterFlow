Core Claim:
Strategy is not an isolated planning document. It is an executable process that must stay linked to people and operations and must answer practical questions about environment, customers, competition, capability, milestones, short-term and long-term balance, critical issues, and sustainable profit logic. Its pressure point is that strategy gets weak when leaders admire broad direction but refuse to specify the hows.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 6 made talent review answer the business. Chapter 7 makes strategy answer reality with the same discipline. Its distinct job is to shift from people-process realism into strategy-process realism without dissolving into generic planning language.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author stresses the importance of the hows.
Then the author identifies the building blocks of a strategy and how the strategic plan gets built.
Then the author asks who builds the plan and what questions a real strategic plan must answer.
Then the author pressures the plan through environment, customers, competition, execution capability, milestones, time balance, critical issues, and sustainable economics.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- executable strategy: a plan that answers how the business will win with actual capability and coordinated follow-through.
- strategy realism: pressure-testing a plan against environment, competition, capability, and milestones.
- plan ownership: the question of who builds the strategy and therefore who has to carry it.
- sustainable profit logic: whether the business can make money on a continuing basis rather than by temporary hope.

Specific Applications:
- checking whether a strategic plan explains how growth will happen, not only that growth is desired
- testing whether the people and operational capability behind a strategy is real
- spotting when a strategy discussion ignores competition, milestones, or critical issues

Common Misreadings:
- strategy is mostly vision language and directional ambition
- the plan can be built apart from the people and operations that must carry it
- competition and environment analysis are optional background work
- long-term aspiration excuses weak short-term milestones

Counterarguments or Limits:
The frozen bundle confirms the chapter title, the link to people and operations, the importance of the hows, and the named strategic-plan questions. It does not license invented market case studies, fake boardroom scenes, or unsupported frameworks. Keep the chapter centered on executable strategy mechanics and realism tests rather than on consulting jargon.
