Core Claim:
Execution is the missing discipline between promises and results, and leaders fail when they treat it as a downstream detail instead of a core job. The chapter argues that execution is not separate from strategy, because plans without operational follow-through never become business reality. It also insists that execution has to live in culture, since organizations reproduce what leaders inspect, reward, and tolerate. The chapter's pressure point is simple: people often think a good strategy or a smart goal is enough, when the real break happens in the handoff from aspiration to disciplined action.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is not a general case for hard work or accountability. It is the book's diagnosis chapter about why organizations underperform even when plans sound intelligent. Its distinct mechanism is the gap between aspiration and result, and the chapter names execution as the missing link instead of blaming only strategy quality, talent, or market change.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that execution has become unavoidable as a business problem.
Then the author argues that execution is a discipline and a leader's job, not a support function.
Then the author shows that execution must live in culture, because culture determines whether plans are actually carried through.
Then the author closes on why smart people and companies still miss this gap.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter can be misread as if execution alone solves everything. Hard depth must preserve that execution still depends on sound priorities and honest diagnosis. The limit is that discipline cannot rescue fantasy goals, though it can expose them faster.
