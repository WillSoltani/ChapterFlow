Core Claim:
The right people in the right jobs is not an HR side task. It is core leadership work that cannot be delegated away without weakening execution. This chapter explains why leaders fail to put the right people in the right roles, names causes such as lack of knowledge, lack of courage, psychological comfort, and lack of personal commitment, and clarifies what kind of people leaders should look for. Its pressure point is that people decisions expose the leader's willingness to judge reality honestly.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 4 showed how culture becomes operational. Chapter 5 drives that logic into the most uncomfortable leader decision area: people placement. The chapter is not only about hiring preferences. Its distinct job is to show why staffing and role assignment are non-delegable execution decisions.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that leaders often fail to get the right people into the right jobs.
Then the author names why: lack of knowledge, lack of courage, psychological comfort, and weak commitment.
Then the author describes what kind of people leaders should look for.
Then the author closes on how to get the right people into the right jobs and why unvarnished truth is necessary.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- role-fit discipline: placing people based on what the work truly needs rather than what feels comfortable.
- comfort bias: the leader's tendency to protect ease, loyalty, or familiarity over reality-based placement.
- people judgment: the combination of knowledge, courage, and commitment needed for sound staffing choices.
- unvarnished truth: the refusal to soften the real fit problem.

Specific Applications:
- evaluating whether someone is in the wrong role even if they are likable or historically loyal
- distinguishing between development potential and role mismatch
- checking whether leaders are avoiding hard people decisions for comfort reasons
