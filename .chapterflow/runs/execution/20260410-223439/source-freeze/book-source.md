# Frozen Book Source Bundle

## Canonical Book
- Title: `Execution: The Discipline of Getting Things Done`
- Authors: Larry Bossidy, Ram Charan, with Charles Burck
- Locked edition: Crown Business, 1st ed., 2002
- Canonical ISBN: `9780609610572`

## What The Authorized Bundle Confirms
- The book argues that execution is the missing link between aspirations and results.
- The governing management triangle is `people`, `strategy`, and `operations`.
- Leadership is not treated as vision-only work; the leader is expected to stay deeply engaged in execution.
- The core structural route is:
  - why execution is needed
  - the building blocks of execution
  - the three core processes of execution

## Confirmed Chapter-Level Support

### Chapter 1
- Execution is treated as a discipline, not an afterthought.
- It is framed as a leader's job and as something that must live in culture.
- The chapter diagnoses why organizations miss execution even when plans look sound.

### Chapter 2
- The book contrasts companies that close the execution gap with companies that do not.
- Named organizational examples include EDS, Xerox, and Lucent in the secondary contents digest.
- The chapter is about the difference between strategic intent and actual delivery.

### Chapter 3
- The seven essential behaviors are:
  - know your people and your business
  - insist on realism
  - set clear goals and priorities
  - follow through
  - reward the doers
  - expand people's capabilities through coaching
  - know yourself
- Emotional fortitude is treated as part of the chapter's inner leadership standard.

### Chapter 4
- Culture change is operationalized through rewards, social software, robust dialogue, and leader behavior.
- The chapter treats culture as something leaders set by what they reward and tolerate.

### Chapter 5
- Hiring, placement, and appraisal are positioned as the leader's non-delegable work.
- The chapter asks what kind of people leaders should look for and how to place them well.
- Lack of courage and lack of personal commitment are named causes of poor people decisions in the secondary heading map.

### Chapter 6
- A robust people process evaluates individuals accurately and in depth.
- It identifies and develops leadership talent needed for strategy execution.
- It fills the leadership pipeline and supports succession.
- The CIO excerpt confirms dealing with nonperformers and linking HR to business results as chapter concerns.

### Chapter 7
- Strategy is linked explicitly to people and operations rather than treated as an isolated planning document.
- The chapter stresses the importance of the "hows."
- The strategy process asks whether the business understands the environment, customers, competition, capability to execute, milestones, and the balance between short and long term.

### Chapter 8
- Strategy review is framed as a live management practice, not a ceremonial presentation.
- Confirmed review questions include competitive knowledge, organizational capability, focus, idea choice, and linkage to people and operations.

### Chapter 9
- Operations links strategy and people through budgets, assumptions, synchronization, trade-offs, contingencies, and quarterly reviews.
- The chapter treats realistic assumptions as central to sound goals.

### Chapter 10
- The conclusion is a direct letter to a new leader.
- Frozen support is thinner than for the operational chapters, so keep this chapter concrete, advice-centered, and narrow.
