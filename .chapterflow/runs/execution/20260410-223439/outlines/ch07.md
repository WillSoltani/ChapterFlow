Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should be able to explain why strategy must answer practical execution questions about markets, capability, milestones, and sustainable economics rather than rest on broad ambition alone.

Opening Move:
Open on a plan that sounds exciting until someone asks how it will work, who will carry it, and what milestone would prove it is moving.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 strategy linked to people and operations, importance of the hows
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 building the strategic plan, who builds it, and core strategic-plan questions
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 environment, customers, competition, capability, milestones, time balance, critical issues, and sustainable profit logic

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: frame, why strategy fails when it stays abstract
- P2: mechanism, linkage with people and operations and the importance of the hows
- P3: building blocks of strategy and who builds the plan
- P4: strategic-plan questions as realism tests
- P5: environment, customers, competition, and execution capability
- P6: milestones, short-term and long-term balance, critical issues, and sustainable profit logic

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The strategy process is real only when it can explain how the business will win, who can carry the plan, what signals will show progress, and whether the economics can hold over time.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter has to preserve that strategy can become aspiration theater when leaders admire growth language, vision language, or direction language without forcing the plan through capability, milestones, competition, and economic sustainability.

Threshold Question:
What proves that a strategy is more than a polished direction statement?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: strategy must stay linked to people and operations
- Takeaway 2: the hows matter because direction alone is too weak
- Takeaway 3: a strategic plan must answer environment, customers, and competition
- Takeaway 4: capability and milestones test whether the plan can execute
- Takeaway 5: short-term and long-term balance plus sustainable profit logic prevent ambition theater

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dilemma, work): teaches how a promising growth plan fails when the how is missing
- Scenario 2 (audit, school): teaches how to pressure-test a plan against competition and capability
- Scenario 3 (decision_point, personal): teaches the difference between a goal and a strategy
- Scenario 4 (dialogue, work): teaches milestone and capability questions in real conversation
- Scenario 5 (before_after, school): teaches what sharp focus does to a scattered plan
- Scenario 6 (reframing, personal): teaches sustainable logic and long-short balance

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, specify one sensory or situational detail to include.
- scenario 1: slide deck with a blank how row
- scenario 2: whiteboard column for rival teams or rival publications
- scenario 4: milestone sheet marked beside a staffing list
- scenario 5: messy recruitment plan replaced by a narrowed calendar

Preview Constraint:
The preview must point toward Chapter 8's strategy review without sounding like the review is already happening here.

Genericity Risks:
- drifting into empty strategy slogans
- treating customers or competition as checklist decoration
- sounding like the strategy is detached from the people who must execute it

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: vision-language inflation without mechanism
- filler move 2: generic business-school planning jargon
- invented authority move 1: unsupported market anecdotes or company examples
- brief-language leak 1: internal support-strength notes
