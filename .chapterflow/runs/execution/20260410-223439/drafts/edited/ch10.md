If you are a new leader, do not expect the role to become real because people recognize your title. It becomes real when you accept that responsibility will keep asking more of you after the first energy of taking charge fades. Bossidy and Charan close the book by turning toward that reality. The job is not to sound visionary, calm, or impressive in the abstract. The job is to keep reality in view long enough to act on it.

Start there. Insist on realism before you reach for comfort. If people are weakly placed, say so clearly enough to deal with it. If the strategy is broad but soft on the hows, pressure it until it answers. If the operations of the business are busy but unsynchronized, do not mistake motion for execution. The leader's discipline begins in refusing the easier story when the harder truth is what the business needs.

That discipline also means refusing to split the work into separate virtues that never meet. People, strategy, and operations belong together. A leader who treats them as isolated topics will keep finding that one decision quietly cancels another. The right people in the wrong strategy will still fail. A sharp strategy carried by a weak operating process will still fail. A busy operating process aimed at the wrong priorities will still fail. Leadership becomes serious when you keep those links visible and keep forcing them back into the same conversation.

Do not think follow-through is a lower form of leadership than declaring direction. It is one of the clearest proofs that you mean what you say. Anyone can sound decisive at the beginning of a meeting, a quarter, or a role. The harder work is to keep asking what has actually moved, what has drifted, what assumptions have weakened, and what must be corrected now. That repetition is not beneath leadership. It is leadership.

You will be tempted to protect momentum by postponing hard questions, to protect harmony by softening weak truths, or to protect your own image by admiring plans that are not yet ready. Resist that temptation. Execution is not built on protected illusion. It is built on disciplined responsibility carried over time. That means choosing, correcting, following through, and doing it again after the novelty is gone.

So if you want a compact rule for the role, keep this one: stay close to reality, keep the links visible, and keep the work moving. Hold people, strategy, and operations together. Let discipline outlast mood. Let follow-through outlast declaration. If you do that, you will be leading in the sense this book means. If you do not, the title will not rescue you.
