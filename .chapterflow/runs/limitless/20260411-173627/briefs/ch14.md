Core Claim:
Speed reading should be treated as a method of reducing friction and improving reading efficiency rather than as blind skimming. This chapter should argue that faster reading depends on better attention, fewer regressions, and cleaner reading behavior that preserves comprehension.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the reading-efficiency chapter. It should feel distinct from Chapter 13 because it moves from retention into input speed, showing that faster reading comes from better process rather than from reckless haste.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from keeping information into taking information in more efficiently.
Then the author reframes slow reading as something often worsened by friction or inefficient habits rather than fixed limitation.
Then the author argues that reading speed can improve when attention is steadier and reading behavior is less cluttered or repetitive.

- faster reading as cleaner process rather than blind skimming

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not claim that every text should be read at maximum speed. The narrower claim is that reading speed can improve when the learner reduces friction and reads with more deliberate pacing while preserving comprehension fit.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 13 made retention more deliberate; this chapter asks how incoming material can be handled faster without losing depth.
