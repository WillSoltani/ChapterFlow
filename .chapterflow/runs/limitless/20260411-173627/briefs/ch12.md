Core Claim:
Attention alone is not enough if the learner does not know how to study effectively. This chapter should argue that studying is an active method for working material, not passive exposure to it. Its job is to make study feel deliberate, practical, and learnable without drifting into generic school-advice clichés.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the active-learning chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 11 because it moves from protecting attention into using that attention through concrete study behavior.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from defended attention into deliberate study behavior.
Then the author shows that passive contact is weaker than active processing.
Then the author concludes that better studying comes from method, not just more time near the material.

Required Anchors:
- study as active processing rather than passive exposure
- better learning coming from deliberate interaction with material
- study as the practical use of protected attention

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not claim there is one universal study move that solves every subject. The narrower claim is that studying becomes stronger when the learner actively works the material instead of only re-encountering it passively.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 11 protected attention; this chapter asks how to use that attention well enough for learning to deepen.
