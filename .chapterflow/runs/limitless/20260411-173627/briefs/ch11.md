Core Claim:
Learning quality depends on protecting attention from fragmentation. This chapter should argue that focus is not mere sternness or staring harder, but a selective-attention skill that decides what gets admitted into the mind and what gets filtered out. Its job is to make focus feel trainable and practical without drifting into generic anti-distraction preaching.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the attention-protection chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 10 because it moves from immersion as a valuable state into focus as the deliberate gate that protects that state and supports learning.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from absorbed attention into defended attention.
Then the author shows how distraction fragments learning quality.
Then the author concludes that focus is a practical gatekeeping skill that protects cognitive effort.

Specific Applications:

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not claim that focus means perfect control or zero distraction forever. The narrower claim is that better learning requires more deliberate control over what attention admits and sustains.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 10 showed the power of immersion; this chapter asks how to protect the attention that immersion and learning both depend on.
Next Chapter Bridge: once attention is protected, the next question is how to use that attention effectively through study method.
