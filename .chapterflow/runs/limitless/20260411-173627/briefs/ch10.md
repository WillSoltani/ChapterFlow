Core Claim:
Once action has begun, performance strengthens when attention, challenge, and engagement lock together more fully. This chapter should argue that flow is not random magic but a state of deep absorption that makes work feel cleaner, stronger, and more alive. Its job is to make flow feel practical without drifting into unsupported performance mysticism.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the immersion chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 9 because it moves from making action easy to begin into making action deep enough to absorb the learner.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from starting into sustained immersion.
Then the author shows that challenge and attention can combine into deeper engagement.
Then the author concludes that flow strengthens performance because the learner is fully inside the work instead of resisting entry.

Required Anchors:
- flow as deep engagement rather than vague hype
- challenge-skill balance supporting immersion
- flow as what can happen after action has already begun

Specific Applications:
- noticing when attention deepens because the task is demanding but workable
- setting up study or work conditions that allow fuller immersion
- distinguishing true engagement from frantic busyness

Common Misreadings:
- turning flow into mystical talent language
- claiming flow appears on demand without preparation
- confusing stimulation or panic with deep engagement

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not claim that flow can be forced at will or that every meaningful task will feel effortless. The narrower claim is that performance improves when attention is deeply engaged and the task difficulty is matched well enough to keep the learner absorbed.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 9 made action easier to begin; this chapter asks what happens when that begun action becomes immersive instead of shallow or scattered.
Next Chapter Bridge: once the learner sees the power of immersion, the next question is how to protect attention directly through focus.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:

- absorption versus frantic overactivation

Moral Complexity:
Low. The chapter should avoid implying that readers who do not feel flow are failing spiritually or intellectually.
