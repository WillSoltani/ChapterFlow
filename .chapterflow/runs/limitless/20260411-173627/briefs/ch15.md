Core Claim:
Thinking should be treated as the quality-control stage of learning rather than as a vague talent. This chapter should argue that better learning, memory, and reading matter because they improve how the learner interprets, judges, connects, and uses what enters the mind.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the judgment-and-use chapter. It should feel distinct from Chapter 14 because it shifts from intake efficiency into discernment, decision, and thought quality, closing the methods arc by asking what all the earlier gains are for.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from taking information in faster into what the mind does with that information.
Then the author reframes thinking as something affected by the quality of inputs and processing, not just raw intelligence.
Then the author argues that better thought comes from clearer interpretation, stronger connection, and more deliberate judgment.

Required Anchors:
- thinking as a quality-of-processing issue rather than a vague gift
- better thought coming from clearer interpretation, connection, and judgment
- thinking as the practical culmination of learning, memory, and reading methods

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not claim that good thinking eliminates uncertainty or guarantees perfect decisions. The narrower claim is that thought quality improves when the learner brings clearer inputs, stronger connections, and more deliberate judgment to what they know.
