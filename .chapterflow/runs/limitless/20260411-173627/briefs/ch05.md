Core Claim:
Readers do not approach learning as blank operators. They carry beliefs about intelligence, memory, talent, and identity that quietly shape what effort seems reasonable. This chapter should argue that belief systems can function like hidden ceilings long before reality has been honestly tested. Its job is to expose those ceilings without pretending that belief alone erases all constraints.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the belief-ceiling chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 4 because it moves from reading behavior at the page to the identity and assumption layer beneath that behavior.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from outer reading habits to the inner story the reader brings to learning.
Then the author shows how beliefs influence interpretation, effort, and persistence.
Then the author concludes that untested limiting beliefs can shrink performance before ability and method have had a fair chance.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not claim that beliefs are the only factor in performance. The narrower claim is that beliefs powerfully affect effort, interpretation, and persistence, which means they can become ceilings even when they are not fully true.
