Core Claim:
If modern life raises the stakes of learning, the next move is to stop underestimating the brain that has to meet those stakes. This chapter should challenge fixed-capacity assumptions and argue that current performance is not identical to final potential. Its job is to make the reader treat the brain as trainable in broad, defensible terms rather than as a static limit.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the capacity-reframe chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 2 because it moves from environmental pressure to the possibilities and constraints of the learner's own brain.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from urgency to the question of what the brain can actually do.
Then the author challenges fixed assumptions about mental capacity.
Then the author concludes that training, method, and a better frame of the brain reopen possibility.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that every person can do anything instantly or that biology does not matter. The stronger claim is that people often mistake current state for permanent ceiling and underuse trainable capacity.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 2 showed why learning now matters; this chapter shows why the reader should not start from a shrunken view of the brain.
Next Chapter Bridge: if the brain is more capable than assumed, the next question is how to use reading and memory more intentionally.
