Core Claim:
If the reader now grants that learning matters and that the brain is more trainable than a fixed-ceiling story allows, the next move is to read differently. This chapter should argue that passive exposure is a weak way to use a book. The chapter's job is to shift the reader into active reading and retention posture so the rest of the book is treated like training rather than inspiration theater.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the reading-posture chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 3 because it stops arguing about capacity in the abstract and starts changing how the reader interacts with pages, attention, and recall.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author converts trainable-capacity confidence into a practical demand: read with intention.
Then the author argues that intake without retention or use is shallow learning.
Then the author concludes that this book should be approached actively so later mindset, motivation, and methods chapters can compound.

Required Anchors:
- the shift from broad capacity to practical reading behavior
- reading as active engagement rather than passive exposure
- remembering and using ideas as the real test of reading value

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not promise that a single posture change makes every book memorable. The tighter claim is that active engagement, reflection, and review improve the odds that reading becomes usable knowledge rather than evaporating input.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 3 argued that the brain is more trainable than a fixed story admits; this chapter shows how to treat reading as part of that training.
