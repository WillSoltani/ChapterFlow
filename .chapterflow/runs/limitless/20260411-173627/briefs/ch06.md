Core Claim:
Belief systems stay powerful because they are carried by recurring false ideas about what learning is and who learning is for. This chapter should expose those lies as distortions that shrink effort, self-trust, and experimentation. Its job is not to finalize a frozen seven-item taxonomy unsupported by the source bundle, but to show how learning myths keep readers passive, intimidated, or dependent until those myths are challenged.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the myth-clearing chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 5 because it moves from the atmosphere of belief into the more specific false ideas that sustain that atmosphere.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author moves from hidden belief systems to the named myths that keep those systems alive.
Then the author shows how bad learning myths create passivity, intimidation, or dependence.
Then the author concludes that exposing those lies makes better effort, self-trust, and method use more rational.

Required Anchors:
- learning myths as sustaining mechanisms for limiting beliefs
- passivity, intimidation, and dependence as downstream effects
- myth repair as preparation for stronger motivation and methods

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not claim that every struggle comes from myth alone. The narrower claim is that bad learning myths distort interpretation and behavior, which makes stronger effort and better methods less likely to survive.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 5 exposed the belief atmosphere around learning; this chapter identifies the recurring lies that keep that atmosphere intact.
Next Chapter Bridge: once the lies are cleared, the book can move from false ceilings into the motivational drivers that sustain real effort.
