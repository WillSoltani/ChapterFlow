Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand thinking as the quality of how they interpret, connect, and use what they learn.

Opening Move:
Open on the mistake of treating more information as if it automatically produced better judgment.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for thinking as a quality-of-processing issue rather than a vague gift
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for better thought coming from clearer interpretation, connection, and judgment
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for thinking as the culmination of learning, memory, and reading methods

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: expose the more-information-equals-better-thinking trap
- P2: distinguish mental clutter from real thought quality
- P3: define thinking as interpretation, connection, and judgment
- P4: show how clearer processing improves decisions and use
- P5: guard against abstract genius talk or perfect-decision claims
- P6: bridge from speed reading into thinking and close the methods arc
- P7 if needed: preserve nuance around uncertainty and responsible judgment

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes collecting inputs from processing them well enough to produce clearer judgment.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must elevate thought quality without promising certainty, genius, or flawless decisions.

Threshold Question:
How do better learning inputs become better thinking rather than just more crowded mental shelves?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- more information versus better thinking
- interpretation and connection as thought work
- judgment as the use-stage of learning
- anti-genius-talk guardrail
- culmination of the methods arc

- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choose between collecting more material and actually thinking with what is already there
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): from information hoarding to clearer interpretation and use
