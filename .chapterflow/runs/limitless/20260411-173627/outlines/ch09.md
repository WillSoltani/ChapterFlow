Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that action becomes more likely when the first move is made behaviorally small and simple enough to begin.

Opening Move:
Open on the mistake of treating a stalled start as proof that the learner does not care enough.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for small simple steps as a motivation component
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for reducing resistance by shrinking the start
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for beginning action before confidence or intensity fully arrives

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: expose the oversized-start trap
- P2: show how high entry cost creates avoidable resistance
- P3: define small simple steps as a start-cost intervention
- P4: guard against trivializing the work or confusing small with meaningless
- P5: connect easier starts to repetition and momentum
- P6: bridge into flow by showing how beginning enables deeper engagement later
- P7 if needed: preserve nuance around ambition and difficulty

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes shrinking the first move from shrinking the whole goal and treats simplicity as a way to lower initiation cost.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must honor the power of small starts without turning them into permanent avoidance of difficulty.

Threshold Question:
How do you make the beginning smaller without making your standard for real work permanently small?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- start cost as a real motivational barrier
- smallness as a behavioral entry tool
- resistance reduction versus ambition reduction
- repetition and momentum after the first step
- bridge from starting easier into later flow
