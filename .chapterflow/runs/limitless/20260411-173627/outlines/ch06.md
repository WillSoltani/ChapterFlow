Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that false ideas about learning can preserve passivity, intimidation, and dependence until those ideas are named and challenged.

Opening Move:
Open on a learning rule that sounds familiar and reasonable but quietly keeps the learner passive.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for myths as sustaining mechanisms for limiting beliefs
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for passivity, intimidation, and dependence as downstream effects
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for myth repair as preparation for stronger motivation and method

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: expose the familiar authority of a learning myth
- P2: show how myths preserve passivity or intimidation
- P3: separate myth from tested learning reality
- P4: guard against cynicism toward all structure or expertise
- P5: connect myth repair to self-trust, effort, and method use
- P6: bridge into the motivation section
- P7 if needed: preserve nuance around legitimate guidance and real difficulty

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes bad learning myths from tested learning reality and from useful structure.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must reject false rules without collapsing into anti-method cynicism.

Threshold Question:
How do you reject bad learning myths without becoming cynical about all structure, expertise, or method?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- myths as hidden sustaining mechanisms
- passivity, intimidation, and dependence
- false rules versus tested reality
- myth repair and self-trust
- guardrail against cynicism

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, school): whether to obey a familiar myth about who counts as a good learner
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, work): identify a false learning rule that shaped dependence
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): challenge a myth that learning should feel easy if talent is real
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, school): myth repair increases effort before confidence fully stabilizes
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, work): cling to a passive rule or test a more active method
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): from inherited myth obedience to evidence-based learning trust

Scenario Vividness Targets:
Use objects like a study-hall planner, campus-cafe notebook, training checklist, or sticky note with a familiar learning rule.

Preview Constraint:
The preview should point toward motivation as the next major section, not summarize purpose, energy, and small simple steps in detail.

Genericity Risks:
- generic myth-busting voice
- making the chapter sound anti-expert
- flattening seven-lies framing into vague negativity

Forbidden Drift:
- exact unsupported seven-item taxonomy
- contempt for structure or teachers
- brief-language leak
- blanket cynicism about guidance
