Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that reading becomes far more useful when it is approached as active training aimed at retention and use rather than as passive page consumption.

Opening Move:
Open on the false comfort of finishing pages without being able to retrieve or use what was just read.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for the shift from capacity to reading behavior
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for active engagement versus passive exposure
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for retention, review, and bridge to the coming mindset section

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: challenge the assumption that finishing pages equals learning
- P2: show the cost of passive reading
- P3: introduce active reading posture as the stronger default
- P4: connect remembering to review and retrieval rather than page count
- P5: frame books as material to use, not merely complete
- P6: bridge from practical reading posture to the beliefs that shape learning behavior
- P7 if needed: keep the advice practical rather than faddish

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes reading exposure from usable retention.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Practical reading guidance must stay useful without overclaiming a universal formula.

Threshold Question:
What is the value of finishing a book if its ideas never become retrievable or usable?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- finishing pages versus learning
- active reading posture
- retention through review and recall
- application as the test of reading value
- preparation for deeper learning work

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, school): whether to keep highlighting passively or pause to restate an idea

- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, school): short review creates stronger retrieval than longer passive reading
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, work): rush through pages or stop to convert one idea into action
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): from passive completion language to active-use language
