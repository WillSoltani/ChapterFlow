Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that the book's promise begins in a real wound: the feeling of being mentally limited after injury, and the discovery that learning itself can become the lever that changes that story.

Opening Move:
Open on the injury aftermath or the feeling of sitting inside a "broken brain" identity, then widen into the contrast between that felt limit and the hunger for mental superpowers.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for childhood injury and lasting cognitive shame
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for identity trap and social labeling
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for possibility turn through learning and mental-superpower framing

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: immediate pressure and wounded self-story
- P2: show how the injury became a durable limit identity
- P3: explain why shame and labeling matter more than one bad day
- P4: widen from personal pain to the larger question of fixed versus trainable ability
- P5: introduce the first turn toward possibility through learning
- P6: show why learning how to learn is different from merely wishing to be smarter
- P7 if needed: bridge to the modern relevance question in Chapter 2

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes a felt limit from a fixed limit, and then distinguishes belief-level release from skill-level change.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hope matters, but hope without method can still leave a person trapped. Hard depth must keep the gap open between a new belief and a new capability.

Threshold Question:
What actually changes after you stop accepting the old limit but still have to build a different way of learning?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- injury as origin, not destiny
- self-story as a performance trap
- fascination with mental possibility
- learning as leverage skill
- belief shift versus method gap

- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): chase motivation talk or build a learning routine
