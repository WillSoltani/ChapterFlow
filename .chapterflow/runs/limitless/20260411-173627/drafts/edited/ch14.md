A lot of people hear speed reading and imagine a reckless trade: move your eyes faster, lose half the meaning, and call the result productivity. The chapter pushes against that false choice by treating speed reading as cleaner reading. It is not blind skimming. It is a way of reducing friction, unnecessary regressions, and cluttered reading habits so the learner can move through text with better pace while still preserving comprehension.

That matters because slow reading is not always deep reading. Sometimes it is friction-heavy reading. A learner may keep backtracking, rereading lines they already understood, or letting attention drift so often that the page gets processed in broken fragments. The chapter argues that this kind of drag can make reading slower without making it smarter.

This is why speed reading has to be treated as a process of cleaner intake. The learner has to do more than try to force speed. They have to read with steadier pacing, fewer unnecessary returns, and better attentional continuity so the eyes and mind stop wasting motion. The chapter does not reduce reading faster to one universal trick. Its stronger claim is that efficiency improves when friction is lowered and pacing becomes more deliberate.

The chapter also needs a guardrail. Speed reading should not become permission to skim carelessly or to outrun comprehension for the sake of bragging rights. The point is not to race the page. The point is to make reading more efficient than it would be if the learner kept feeding regressions, distraction, and stop-start pacing. Better speed is the result of cleaner reading behavior, not of pretending every text should be rushed.

That matters because memory by itself still leaves an open problem. Chapter 13 helped the learner keep material once it was encoded well. Chapter 14 asks how the learner can bring material in faster without damaging the understanding that memory still depends on later. Reading method decides whether increased pace stays useful or turns shallow.

This is why the chapter sits before thinking. Once the learner understands that speed can improve through cleaner pacing and lower friction, the next question becomes what to do mentally with that better intake. Memory preserved the material. Speed reading improves how it arrives. Thinking will ask how the learner judges, connects, and uses what was read.
