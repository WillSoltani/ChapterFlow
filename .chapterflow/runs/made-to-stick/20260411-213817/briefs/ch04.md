Core Claim:
Ideas stick more strongly when people have a reason to believe them. Credibility can come from external authority, internal vivid detail, anti-authorities, testable proof, or statistics translated to human scale. The chapter argues that raw claims rarely persuade on their own. Belief grows when evidence becomes concrete, checkable, and close enough to human judgment to matter.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is anchored by Barry Marshall and Robin Warren on ulcers, Pam Laffin as an anti-authority, human-scale principle examples, and the contrast between statistics that numb and evidence that lands. It is about why messages become believable, not just clear or vivid.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show that authority alone is only one route to belief.
Then they widen credibility into anti-authorities, vivid detail, and testable proof.
Then they show why statistics need translation into human scale before people can feel what the evidence means.

Required Anchors:
- Barry Marshall and Robin Warren ulcer claim: friction is disbelief around H. pylori, consequence is a vivid proof path that makes belief harder to dismiss
- Pam Laffin anti-authority example: friction is skepticism toward official warnings, consequence is trust through lived cost
- human-scale statistics example: friction is numbness to large numbers, consequence is evidence translated into something people can actually feel

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

- internal credibility: vivid detail and checkable structure inside the message itself
- human-scale principle: large statistics become persuasive when translated to lived scale

Specific Applications:
- turning a claim into something a skeptical audience can test or picture
- using the right witness for a message instead of defaulting to status
- translating big numbers into a human-scale comparison that actually lands

Common Misreadings:
- credibility means borrowing a prestigious source and stopping there
- more numbers automatically means more belief
- vividness alone makes a claim trustworthy

Counterarguments or Limits:
- anti-authority can manipulate if the story outruns the evidence
- vivid detail can fake confidence if it is not actually probative
- human-scale translations can oversimplify if they distort what the data means
