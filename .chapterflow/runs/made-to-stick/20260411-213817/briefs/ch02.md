Core Claim:
Attention starts when a message breaks a pattern, but attention lasts only when the surprise opens a meaningful question. Unexpectedness is not gimmickry. It works when the shock is tied to the core and becomes understandable after a beat of rethinking. The chapter argues that curiosity grows from a knowledge gap, yet that gap works only when the audience has enough context to notice what is missing.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is anchored by pattern breaks, the dangerous line between surprise and gimmick, Nora Ephron's buried-lead lesson, George Loewenstein's gap theory, and high-ambition surprises like Sony's pocket radio and JFK's moonshot. It is about how to get and keep attention without becoming random.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show that surprise grabs attention by breaking an expected pattern.
Then they show that surprise must be post-predictable and tied to the core or it decays into gimmickry.
Then they widen into curiosity gaps and context-building to explain how attention can be sustained instead of merely stolen.

- curiosity gap: attention lasts when people can feel a missing answer

Specific Applications:
