Core Claim:
Ideas move people when they make a stake feel personal enough to matter. Emotion in this chapter is not sentiment for its own sake. It is the shift from abstract concern to felt significance through identity, self-interest, or one concrete person. The chapter argues that people care more readily when the message gives them a reason to feel the consequence rather than merely understand it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is anchored by the contrast between statistics and one person, analytical thinking reducing giving, identity-based motivation, and Floyd Lee's morale-and-dignity example. It is about why people care enough to act, not just why they understand or believe.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show that abstract suffering at scale often fails to move people.
Then they show how one person, identity, or direct stake can make the issue feel immediate.
Then they widen emotion beyond pity into self-interest, identity, and meaning.

Required Anchors:
- one-person-versus-many contrast: friction is large-scale suffering that stays abstract, consequence is stronger response to one vivid human stake
- analytical-thinking reducing giving: friction is detached calculation, consequence is weaker emotional movement
- Floyd Lee / dining in Iraq: friction is hardship and distance, consequence is morale and dignity made emotionally meaningful

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

Specific Applications:
- replacing abstract stakes with one human consequence the audience can feel
- connecting a message to identity without turning it manipulative
- framing effort around dignity, meaning, or purpose rather than only material reward
