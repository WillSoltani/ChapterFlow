Core Claim:
Ideas stick when they are forced down to a core that guides decisions without dragging every detail along. Simplicity here does not mean dumbing down. It means ruthless prioritization around the single thing the audience has to understand and act on. The chapter argues that a compact core helps people remember, choose, and improvise under pressure better than overloaded explanation does. It also warns that compactness works best when it hooks into something already familiar, such as a proverb, schema, or vivid shorthand.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is impossible to confuse with another one because it is built around Commander's Intent, Southwest Airlines as "the low-fare airline," Hoover Adams's local-news obsession at the Daily Record, and the difference between core simplicity and reductionist oversimplifying. The chapter is about forced prioritization under pressure, not about catchy wording alone.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors establish that plans and messages fail when they carry too much detail to survive real-world use.
Then they show how a compact core, such as Commander's Intent or Southwest's low-fare positioning, helps people make local decisions without waiting for full instructions.
Then they widen the idea through proverbs, schemas, and compact analogies to show that simple ideas can still carry depth when the core is sharp and memorable.
