# Book Skeleton

## Metadata

- bookId: made-to-stick
- title: Made to Stick
- authors: Chip Heath; Dan Heath
- edition lock: 2007 Random House Publishing Group trade edition family
- numbered chapter range: 1-6
- continuity context: introduction plus epilogue
- global mechanism: SUCCESs explains why ideas survive, spread, and drive action

## Chapter Order

1. Simple
Intent: teach forced prioritization and core-message compression without flattening meaning.
Source richness: high
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity: low

2. Unexpected
Intent: show that surprise only works when it opens a coherent curiosity gap tied to the core idea.
Source richness: high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: medium because attention tactics can slide into gimmickry

3. Concrete
Intent: argue that ideas stick when people can picture, handle, or mentally simulate them.
Source richness: high
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: low

4. Credible
Intent: explain how messages earn belief through proof, vivid detail, trusted witnesses, and human-scale evidence.
Source richness: high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: medium because credibility tactics can be abused

5. Emotional
Intent: show how ideas move people when they make one person, identity, or stake feel immediate.
Source richness: medium-high
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: medium because emotional leverage can manipulate as well as motivate

6. Stories
Intent: show how narrative carries embedded lessons and action cues farther than explanation alone.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity: medium because stories can transmit falsehoods as well as truth

## School-Setting Plan

- ch01: overloaded student choosing what matters
- ch02: teacher using surprise without gimmickry
- ch03: instructor making an abstract idea concrete
- ch04: student persuading with proof rather than status
- ch05: class appeal that makes one human stake visible
- ch06: story used to transmit a principle through a school community

## Vocabulary Watchlist

- avoid abstract communication jargon: leverage, framework, landscape, paradigm
- avoid generic self-help fillers: mindset, consistency, accountability, motivation boost
- protect the book's actual terms when supported: SUCCESs, Commander's Intent, Curse of Knowledge, gap theory, Velcro Theory of Memory, human-scale principle
