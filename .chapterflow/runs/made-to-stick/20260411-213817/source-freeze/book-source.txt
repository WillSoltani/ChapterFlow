Chapter 1 supported anchors and claims
- Commander's Intent: military plans improve when the core objective is clear enough to guide action under changing conditions.
- Southwest Airlines: "the low-fare airline" is used as a compact strategic core that helps employees resolve local tradeoffs.
- Daily Record / Hoover Adams: local-news obsession is compressed into a memorable rule focused on names and local relevance.
- Decision paralysis research is used to support forced prioritization and simplicity.
- Proverbs and schema use support the claim that compact, familiar structures make ideas easier to remember and apply.
- Palm Pilot and high-concept Hollywood pitches illustrate compact framing that is clear without carrying every detail.

Chapter 2 supported anchors and claims
- Surprise gets attention; curiosity keeps it.
- Pattern breaks matter only when they serve the core message rather than gimmickry.
- Commander-style hyperbole at the Daily Record shows how unexpected wording can remove ambiguity.
- Gap theory of curiosity explains why knowledge gaps keep people engaged.
- Sony's pocket radio and JFK's moonshot illustrate unexpected but coherent ambition.

Chapter 3 supported anchors and claims
- Aesop's sour-grapes fable shows how concrete imagery preserves abstract lessons.
- Nature Conservancy had to translate abstract acreage talk into more graspable landscape framing.
- Concrete, hands-on accounting instruction helped novices understand and retain the subject.
- Jane Elliott's brown-eyes/blue-eyes exercise made prejudice concrete rather than merely discussed.
- Velcro Theory of Memory: ideas stick better when they have more hooks.

Chapter 4 supported anchors and claims
- Barry Marshall and Robin Warren made an ulcer claim stick by creating vivid proof around H. pylori.
- Credibility can come from anti-authorities, not just official experts; Pam Laffin is a key example.
- Internal credibility can be built with vivid details and wisely scaled statistics.
- The human-scale principle matters because raw numbers often fail to move people until translated into lived scale.

Chapter 5 supported anchors and claims

- Analytical priming can suppress generosity compared with emotionally vivid framing.
- Self-interest is one route to care, but identity and ideals can matter too.
- Floyd Lee's "Dining in Iraq" example shows that dignity, morale, and meaning can motivate beyond comfort alone.

Chapter 6 supported anchors and claims

Epilogue supported claims
