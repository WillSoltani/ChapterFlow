It is tempting to read a leap to greatness as the finish line. A company breaks through, the evidence becomes visible, the story becomes impressive, and the work can start sounding complete. Collins refuses that ending. Good to great is a real achievement, but the last chapter treats it as a stage rather than as permanent safety.

That shift matters because success can make leaders less disciplined in exactly the areas that created the success. Once the flywheel is visibly turning, people can start telling a flattering story about inevitability. The right people begin to look optional because the system appears strong. The brutal facts start feeling less urgent because the results are encouraging. The hedgehog gets treated as proven enough to loosen. What created greatness can then begin to erode under the glow of the achievement it created.

The chapter therefore works as a closing integration of the entire book. Disciplined people, disciplined thought, and disciplined action do not matter only during the climb. They matter just as much once the climb begins to work. The earlier disciplines are not launch tools that can be retired after the leap. They are the conditions under which a leap can become durable.

That is why the flywheel still matters at the end. Momentum does not turn into endurance by magic. It turns into endurance when the institution keeps protecting the same logic that created the momentum. If leaders keep reality visible, keep the concept clear, keep stop-doing discipline alive, and keep technology in its proper role, then the wheel can continue to strengthen instead of becoming a monument to one strong period.

The risk is complacency disguised as celebration. People can begin to think the institution is exceptional in a way that frees it from the disciplines that made it exceptional. Standards soften. Small drifts get excused. The culture starts living off the story of prior greatness rather than the present practice of it. Collins closes by resisting that romance. Greatness that lasts requires continued discipline, not a more polished memory.

That makes the last chapter morally useful. It asks readers to hold achievement and vulnerability together. The leap matters. The results matter. But success is not permission to stop telling the truth, stop narrowing, or stop guarding the wheel. It is the moment when those responsibilities become harder because pride has more material to work with.

The closing move toward enduring institutionhood therefore stays narrow and demanding. Collins is not handing out guarantees. He is showing that lasting strength depends on whether the organization can keep earning its own story. The same disciplines that created the leap now have to survive success itself.

That is a fitting place for the book to end. Greatness is not reduced by saying it still requires maintenance. It becomes more serious. A company that has made the leap now faces the harder question of whether it can keep becoming the kind of institution whose strength is not merely dramatic, but durable.
