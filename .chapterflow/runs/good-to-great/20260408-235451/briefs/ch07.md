Core Claim:
This chapter argues that technology does not create greatness by itself. Collins presents technology as an accelerator of momentum that already exists because disciplined people are working inside a clear concept. The chapter must show that great companies do not become anti-technology. They become disciplined about adopting technology that fits the hedgehog and refusing technology theater that promises transformation without foundational strength.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It rejects the idea that breakthrough comes from a tool, platform, or trend rather than from disciplined alignment that technology can later amplify.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author rejects technology as a root cause of greatness.
Then the author shows that strong companies use technology selectively when it fits the hedgehog.
Then the author contrasts disciplined adoption with reactive technology frenzy.

Required Anchors:
- technology as accelerator rather than primary cause
- fit with the hedgehog concept
- discipline against fear-driven or fashion-driven technology adoption

Specific Applications:
- evaluate whether a tool strengthens the existing concept
- avoid reactive adoption driven by fear or prestige
- treat technology as amplifier of momentum, not substitute for strategy

Common Misreadings:
- reading the chapter as anti-technology
- assuming waiting on technology is the main reason companies lag

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter weakens if it becomes a blanket warning against innovation. It also weakens if tools are treated as neutral add-ons with no need for concept fit. Keep the distinction clear: Collins is against tool worship, not against serious adoption.
