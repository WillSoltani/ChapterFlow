Core Claim:
The chapter argues that the leap begins with a paradoxical kind of leader who combines personal humility with fierce professional will. Collins presents Level 5 leadership as institution-first leadership rather than celebrity leadership. The chapter's force comes from contrast: the leaders who built durable greatness were often quieter, less self-promoting, and more obsessed with the institution than with personal fame. Its job is to show that the human engine behind the leap is disciplined ego, not louder charisma.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's leadership reversal. Instead of glorifying larger-than-life executives, it makes humility-plus-will the decisive pattern.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author defines Level 5 as humility plus professional will.
Then the author contrasts that profile with ego-driven or celebrity leadership.
Then the author shows that Level 5 leaders credit others, take blame themselves, and build something that can outlast them.

Required Anchors:
- Level 5 as the paradoxical blend of humility and will

Specific Applications:

Common Misreadings:
- treating humility as softness or passivity
- treating charisma as proof of greatness

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter risks becoming personality worship if readers forget that Level 5 is valuable because it serves the institution. It also needs nuance around whether people can grow toward the profile rather than being born finished.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
If greatness is a real and durable leap, the next question is what kind of leader can begin it without substituting spectacle for substance.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
