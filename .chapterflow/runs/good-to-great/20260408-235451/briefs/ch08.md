Core Claim:
This chapter argues that breakthrough is rarely a single dramatic event. Collins presents greatness as the result of cumulative momentum built through many consistent pushes in the same direction. The flywheel names that compounding force. The doom loop names the opposite pattern: lurching from one program, restructuring, or dramatic gesture to another because leaders want instant transformation. The chapter must show that momentum looks gradual from the inside until it becomes undeniable.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It explains where visible breakthrough actually comes from and attacks the craving for one decisive miracle move.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author rejects the myth of one dramatic breakthrough.
Then the author defines the flywheel as cumulative force from disciplined consistency.
Then the author contrasts it with the doom loop of reactive shifts and resets.

Required Anchors:
- breakthrough as cumulative momentum rather than one event
- the flywheel as many consistent pushes in one direction
- the doom loop as reactive lurching, restructuring, and loss of momentum

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter weakens if it turns into vague persistence talk. Keep the momentum tied to aligned pushes built from earlier chapters. It also weakens if the doom loop is reduced to change alone; the issue is reactive, incoherent change that keeps resetting force.
