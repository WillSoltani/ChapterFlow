Core Claim:
The chapter argues that greatness requires a simple, disciplined strategic concept built from the intersection of three circles: what you can be best in the world at, what drives your economic engine, and what you are deeply passionate about. Collins presents the hedgehog concept as a clarity tool, not a slogan. Its job is to show that strategic power comes from narrowing honestly, not from trying to be everything at once. The chapter must keep the three-circle discipline concrete and demanding.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's central strategic focus chapter and the place where disciplined clarity defeats sprawling ambition.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author rejects fox-like complexity and scattered initiatives.
Then the author defines the three circles and their intersection.
Then the author shows that the hedgehog requires patient clarity and disciplined noes.
