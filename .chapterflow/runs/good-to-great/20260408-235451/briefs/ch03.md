Core Claim:
This chapter argues that great leaders get the right people on the bus and the wrong people off before locking the destination. Collins treats talent quality and seat fit as the first strategic act, not as a follow-up to strategy. The chapter's edge is that adaptability under uncertainty matters more than a polished plan built with the wrong team. Its job is to show that people quality is not support work around strategy. It is the precondition.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It reverses the normal order of strategy thinking by putting people before direction.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author rejects the sequence of vision first and staffing later.
Then the author shows why the right people create adaptability under uncertainty.
Then the author distinguishes rigorous people decisions from ruthless behavior.

Required Anchors:
- right people on the bus before deciding exactly where to drive it
- wrong people off the bus and right people in the right seats
- rigorous, not ruthless, people discipline

Specific Applications:

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter like generic hiring advice
- assuming the point is only to remove weak performers faster

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter can turn simplistic if readers forget that seat fit matters as much as raw talent and that rigor is not the same thing as coldness.
