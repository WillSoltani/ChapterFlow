Core Claim:
This chapter argues that disciplined people who understand a clear concept make bureaucracy less necessary, not more. Collins presents a culture of discipline as freedom within a framework, where self-disciplined people act consistently with the hedgehog without needing constant external control. The chapter must show that discipline is not synonymous with rigid rules or authoritarian management. It is aligned self-control around a clear concept.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It transforms discipline from control imposed from above into coherent freedom anchored to the hedgehog.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author defines discipline as consistent action inside a clear framework.
Then the author contrasts disciplined freedom with bureaucratic control.
Then the author shows that a culture of discipline depends on disciplined people and disciplined thought first.

Required Anchors:
- freedom within a framework
- disciplined people reduce the need for excessive hierarchy and bureaucracy
- consistency with the hedgehog rather than scattered action

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter can turn brittle if readers keep the control language and lose the disciplined-people premise. It can also turn vague if the framework is not tied back to the hedgehog.
