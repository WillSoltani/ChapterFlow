Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand why great organizations confront reality directly while keeping faith in eventual success.

Opening Move:
Open on the human urge to soften bad news, then bring in the discipline of hearing what is true before it becomes catastrophic.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for brutal facts
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for climate where truth is heard
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for the Stockdale paradox and the bridge to strategic clarity

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show why people hide or soften bad news
- P2: insist that good decisions start with reality
- P3: explain question-led leadership and debate
- P4: show autopsies without blame and red-flag mechanisms
- P5: define the Stockdale paradox
- P6: bridge from reality discipline to strategic clarity

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes disciplined candor from demoralizing harshness.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the dual demand of realism plus faith. Remove either side and the concept breaks.

Threshold Question:
How do leaders keep brutal facts from hardening into cynicism or panic?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- reality before decision
- climate where truth is heard
- questions not answers first
- autopsies without blame and red flags
- Stockdale paradox

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): manager decides whether to surface a worsening metric
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): lab team reviews a failed experiment without blame
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): family discusses hard finances without denial
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): leader suppresses bad news and pays later
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): student president chooses debate over forced consensus
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): coach builds a red-flag habit that prevents silent drift

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include dashboard numbers, wet lab notebooks, meeting silence, red cards, or kitchen-table bills.

Preview Constraint:
The preview should ask what simple idea an organization can commit to once reality is finally visible.
