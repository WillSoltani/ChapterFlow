Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand why comfortable success can block greatness and why Collins treats the leap as a researched sequence rather than a lucky breakthrough.

Opening Move:
Open on an organization that is doing well enough to stop asking harder questions, then snap into the idea that "good" can be the trap.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for good-as-ceiling
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for the research design and comparison companies
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for sustained outperformance and the emerging flywheel pattern

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show why "good enough" reduces urgency
- P2: sharpen the central claim that good can block great
- P3: explain that Collins studied a large set of companies over years instead of offering anecdotes
- P4: define the good-to-great set versus the comparison set
- P5: show that the leap meant sustained results, not a short spike
- P6: bridge toward the cumulative pattern and the concepts that follow

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes episodic success from a durable leap produced by a sequence of reinforcing disciplines.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must feel the difference between dramatic-looking change and the slower, harder process that actually builds greatness.

Threshold Question:
How do you tell whether a bold move is real momentum or just a flashy interruption with no staying power?
