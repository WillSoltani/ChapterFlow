# Book Skeleton

## Metadata
- Book: Good to Great
- Author: Jim Collins
- Locked edition: 2001 English trade family
- Wave size: 2 by default
- Residual final wave note: chapter 9 remains after the paired waves because the chapter count is odd

## Rotation Plan For Examples
- Use the canonical six-format rotation once per chapter: decision_point, postmortem, dialogue, predict_reveal, dilemma, before_after.
- Keep the 2 work / 2 school / 2 personal split in each chapter package.
- Rotate ending types across broader_principle, self_directed_question, surprising_implication, cross_domain, common_trap, perspective_reframe.

## School-Setting Plan
- Use business-school classrooms, student startup teams, debate teams, lab groups, campus newspapers, and project presentations.
- Avoid turning every school example into generic studying harder advice.

## Vocabulary Watchlist
- Keep Level 5, right people on the bus, brutal facts, Stockdale paradox, hedgehog, culture of discipline, technology accelerator, flywheel, and doom loop precise.
- Avoid startup-bro hype, generic hustle talk, and pseudo-strategy jargon.

## Motif / Callback Opportunities
- Chapter 1's study discipline should frame every later concept as part of a researched sequence rather than isolated tips.
- Chapter 2's humility-plus-will logic should echo in the people decisions of Chapter 3.
- Chapter 4's brutal-facts discipline should feed directly into the clarity demanded by Chapter 5.
- Chapters 5 through 8 should feel like a cumulative operating system, not a list of disconnected frameworks.
- Chapter 9 should close the loop by connecting momentum-building to institutional endurance.

## Chapter Plan
### ch01 Good Is the Enemy of Great
- Intent: Establish the research frame and the claim that settled competence often blocks breakthrough performance.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: low
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Avoid making the opening read like a generic summary of the whole book.
- Wave routing: baseline wave

### ch02 Level 5 Leadership
- Intent: Show that the transition begins with leaders who combine fierce institutional will with personal humility.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Needs distance from hero-leader cliches while keeping the humility-will paradox sharp.
- Wave routing: baseline wave

### ch03 First Who... Then What
- Intent: Argue that getting the right people in the right seats precedes strategy because adaptability matters more than a polished plan.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Avoid collapsing into generic hiring advice.
- Wave routing: wave 2

### ch04 Confront the Brutal Facts
- Intent: Show that great organizations build truth-telling systems without surrendering confidence.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Keep the Stockdale paradox central instead of drifting into vague realism talk.
- Wave routing: wave 2

### ch05 The Hedgehog Concept
- Intent: Explain how strategic greatness depends on disciplined clarity around the three circles.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Needs specificity so the three circles do not become a generic Venn-diagram slogan.
- Wave routing: wave 3

### ch06 A Culture of Discipline
- Intent: Show how freedom, accountability, and adherence to the hedgehog combine into disciplined execution.
- Source richness: medium-high
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Must distinguish discipline from bureaucracy and from tyrannical control.
- Wave routing: wave 3

### ch07 Technology Accelerators
- Intent: Reframe technology as an amplifier of an existing concept, not the source of greatness.
- Source richness: medium
- Concept density: medium
- Moral complexity: low
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Avoid generic anti-tech contrarianism or shallow innovation talk.
- Wave routing: wave 4

### ch08 The Flywheel and the Doom Loop
- Intent: Make the cumulative-momentum logic vivid and show why dramatic resets usually destroy compounding progress.
- Source richness: high
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Needs clean contrast between visible breakthrough and invisible buildup.
- Wave routing: wave 4

### ch09 From Good to Great to Built to Last
- Intent: Connect the book's transition logic to the longer problem of institutional endurance.
- Source richness: medium
- Concept density: high
- Moral complexity: medium
- Thin-chapter risk notes: Must stay concrete enough to avoid sounding like an abstract epilogue.
- Wave routing: final residual wave
