Core Claim:
Chapter 7 argues that once attention is concentrated, the presenter has to make the value feel scarce instead of needy. Klaff positions prize as the move that reverses the usual seller-buyer energy so the audience experiences the opportunity as something it may need to win rather than as something the presenter is begging to place.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter has to keep strategic distance clear without turning it into manipulation theater. It should show that value perception changes when the presenter stops chasing approval and starts treating fit, selectivity, and standards as real parts of the interaction.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author treats prize as the value-positioning move after intrigue has made the room alert.
Then the author explains that neediness lowers status and makes the offer feel common.
Then the author shows how selective posture and standards can make value feel more scarce and credible.
Then the author implies that hookpoint lands harder once the audience already feels the opportunity has to be won.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- prize: positioning the opportunity so the audience feels it is valuable and not automatically available
- scarcity: the felt sense that the offer cannot simply be claimed through neediness or pressure
- neediness leak: behavior that tells the room the presenter wants the deal more than the room wants the opportunity

Specific Applications:
- holding investor interest without acting like any check is automatically worth taking
- showing judges that a recommendation has standards and tradeoffs rather than pure compliance
- making a personal opportunity or request feel like a serious fit question instead of a plea

Supported Concrete Details:
- prize follows intrigue as the next move in the practical sequence
- scarcity and selective standards change how the audience perceives value
- neediness often appears through overchasing, overqualifying, or approval-seeking behavior

Common Misreadings:
- treating prize as fake aloofness or manipulative social posture
- assuming scarcity alone can replace a real opportunity
- confusing selective value with disrespect toward the audience

Counterarguments or Limits:
Prize can strengthen value perception, but if it becomes empty posturing or contempt for fit, it weakens trust and turns strategic distance into performance. The prose should keep that limit visible so the chapter stays about credible value rather than theater.
