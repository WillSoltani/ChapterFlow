Core Claim:
Chapter 9 argues that the goal of a pitch is not endless interest but a real decision. Klaff positions the decision move as the moment when the presenter converts attention and desire into commitment without slipping into neediness, overexplaining, or approval-seeking behavior.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter has to show that asking for movement is part of frame control, not a desperate closing trick. It should keep decision tied to timing, scarcity, and status rather than generic sales-closing formulas.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author treats decision as the next move after hookpoint has made the offer vivid.
Then the author explains that many presenters lose force by treating interest as enough.
Then the author shows that begging, over-answering, or chasing certainty weakens the decision moment.
Then the author implies that the right move is to define the next step cleanly while preserving status and momentum.

Required Anchors:
- getting a decision as the next STRONG move after hookpoint
- the idea that interest must become a real decision or the pitch has not done its work
- the warning that neediness, overexplaining, or approval-seeking weakens the close

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- decision discipline: designing the pitch so it ends in a concrete move instead of indefinite interest
- needy close: asking for approval in a way that lowers status and invites delay
- commitment path: the specific next action the room can take once the offer is vivid enough

Specific Applications:
- ending an investor pitch with a defined diligence step instead of fishing for vague enthusiasm
- asking a classroom panel for adoption of one recommendation rather than more discussion
- moving a personal opportunity from warm talk to a real yes-or-no choice

Supported Concrete Details:
- decision follows hookpoint as the next move in the practical sequence
- interest without commitment often turns into delay, politeness, or loss of momentum
- weak closes often show up as begging for approval, answering every objection before it appears, or leaving the next step undefined

Common Misreadings:
- treating decision as a hard-sell script instead of a status-aware final move
- assuming more explanation always increases the chance of a yes
- confusing audience warmth with actual commitment

Counterarguments or Limits:
Pushing for a decision too early can feel coercive or premature. The prose should keep that limit visible so the chapter stays about disciplined timing and concrete movement rather than pressure for its own sake.
