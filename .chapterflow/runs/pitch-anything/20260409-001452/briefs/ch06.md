Core Claim:
Chapter 6 argues that once the room can follow the story, the presenter has to keep attention alive by controlling intrigue and novelty. Klaff positions intrigue as the disciplined release of surprise, tension, and unanswered significance so the audience stays mentally active instead of settling into passive comprehension.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is the first place where the method leans hard on attention economics. It should make novelty feel purposeful rather than flashy, showing that the room cares more when it senses there is still something consequential to discover.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author treats intrigue as the move that keeps attention from flattening once story has created sequence.
Then the author explains that surprise and novelty matter because the audience filters for what feels different or unresolved.
Then the author shows how overexplaining too early or removing all tension kills interest.
Then the author implies that prize, hookpoint, and decision work better when curiosity and consequence remain alive.

Required Anchors:
- revealing intrigue as the next move in the STRONG method
- the idea that novelty and surprise help keep the audience mentally active
- the warning that confusion, hype, or total explanation too early can flatten attention instead of heightening it

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- intrigue: the controlled sense that something important is still unfolding
- novelty: the difference or surprise signal that keeps attention from going flat
- tension release timing: deciding what to explain now versus what to hold a moment longer

Specific Applications:
- previewing the unexpected customer behavior that makes a startup opportunity hard to ignore
- keeping a case-room audience alert by delaying the decisive reveal until the problem is clear
- making a personal proposal more compelling by surfacing the surprising implication before the conclusion

Supported Concrete Details:
- intrigue follows story as the next move in the practical sequence
- novelty helps attention stay active when the room senses difference, consequence, or unresolved meaning
- weak intrigue use often appears as bland full explanation, empty hype, or confusing mystery with no payoff

Common Misreadings:
- treating intrigue as manipulation through vagueness
- assuming novelty means theatrics instead of meaningful difference
- stretching suspense so long that the audience loses trust

Counterarguments or Limits:
Intrigue can sharpen attention, but if it becomes gimmickry or needless obscurity, it wastes trust and blurs the offer. The prose should keep the limit visible so the chapter stays about disciplined curiosity rather than drama for its own sake.
