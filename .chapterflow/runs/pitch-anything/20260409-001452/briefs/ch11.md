Core Claim:
Chapter 11 argues that the real benefit of the method is not one successful pitch but a durable change in capability. Klaff positions mastery as the point where the reader can reliably command the room without slipping back into neediness, overexplaining, or low-status pitching habits.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter has to land identity shift without turning into empty self-congratulation. It should tie confidence to disciplined sequence control, frame awareness, and anti-neediness rather than to hype about natural charisma.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author treats the earlier method as trainable capability rather than a one-time trick.
Then the author explains that readers who can run the sequence repeatedly change how they enter and control high-stakes rooms.
Then the author shows that mastery still depends on discipline, not ego inflation or theatrical confidence.
Then the author implies that real advantage comes from repeatable command under pressure.

Supported Concrete Details:

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as permission for arrogance
- assuming mastery means no longer needing discipline or preparation
- ending on self-congratulation detached from the actual method

Counterarguments or Limits:
Readers can overread a mastery ending as proof that technique guarantees domination in every room. The prose should keep the claim bounded: the method raises control and clarity, but it still requires judgment, adaptation, and continued discipline.
