Chapter Promise:
Show that prize makes value feel scarce and credible by reducing neediness and reversing the usual seller-buyer dynamic.

Opening Move:
Open inside a pitch where the presenter starts chasing approval and any deal looks acceptable, so value leaks out before the concept is named.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> prize as the next STRONG move after intrigue
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4 -> scarcity, selective value, and the reversal of needy pitch energy
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-6 -> neediness leaks lower status and weaken value, while hookpoint depends on prize

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: scene of value dropping because the presenter acts as though the audience is doing them a favor
- P2: define prize as value positioning through selective standards and scarce opportunity
- P3: explain why neediness lowers status and makes the offer feel common
- P4: show how false aloofness or manipulative posture fails
- P5: preserve the limit against fake scarcity without substance
- P6: bridge to hookpoint as the concentrated peak once value feels scarce

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Prize is not arrogance. It is the discipline of presenting the opportunity as something with standards, fit, and scarcity rather than as something the presenter is desperate to place anywhere.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Strategic distance can strengthen value, but pushed too far it can become performance that hides weak fit or weak substance instead of clarifying real scarcity.

Threshold Question:
When does strategic distance make the opportunity easier to judge as valuable, and when does it harden into performance that hides weak fit or weak substance?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: prize follows intrigue because attention is now ready for value positioning
- Takeaway 2: scarcity and selectivity change how the audience feels the offer
- Takeaway 3: neediness leaks lower status and cheapen value
- Takeaway 4: false aloofness or fake scarcity destroys trust
- Takeaway 5: hookpoint depends on value already feeling scarce and worth winning

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): deciding whether to chase any investor check or signal real selectivity
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): seeing how a team made its recommendation feel common by begging for approval
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): noticing how a request changes once it becomes a fit discussion instead of a plea
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): forecasting what happens when a founder stops overqualifying and holds standards
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing between approval-seeking explanation and confident selectivity
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing needy asking with value-positioned asking

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like a founder rushing to say any terms work, judges sensing defensiveness, a room leaning in once standards appear, or a personal ask changing once fit replaces pleading.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must imply that once value feels scarce, the pitch needs one concentrated peak where that value hits hardest.

Genericity Risks:
- turning prize into generic confidence talk
- endorsing false scarcity without substance
- losing the chapter's connection to status and neediness

Forbidden Drift:
- no manipulative social-game doctrine
- no contempt for fit, standards, or real audience questions
- no brief-language leakage into reader-facing prose
