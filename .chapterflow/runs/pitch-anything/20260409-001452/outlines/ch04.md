Chapter Promise:
Show that frame control determines how the room interprets the pitch and why losing frame early makes every later move weaker.

Opening Move:
Open inside a room where the presenter starts reacting to the other side's assumptions before establishing any footing, so frame loss is visible before the term is defined.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> live room as competing interpretation rather than neutral exchange
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4 -> frame control shapes status and momentum
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-6 -> reactive answering and neediness as frame surrender, plus the dependency of later STRONG moves

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: scene of a room being defined against the presenter before the main value is stable
- P2: define frame as the meaning-container of the interaction
- P3: explain why the dominant frame shapes status and how later facts are heard
- P4: show how overanswering, fast reacting, or approval-seeking give frame away
- P5: preserve the limit against ego theater or fake alpha behavior
- P6: bridge to story as the next move inside a held frame

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Frame control is not domination for its own sake. It is the discipline of preventing the conversation from being defined in a way that strips the offer of meaning or status before it is understood.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Holding frame can clarify the interaction, but pushed too far it can become a vanity performance that protects the presenter from feedback rather than protecting the offer from distortion.

Threshold Question:
When does holding the frame create real clarity for the room, and when does it harden into ego performance that protects the presenter more than it helps the audience judge the offer well?
