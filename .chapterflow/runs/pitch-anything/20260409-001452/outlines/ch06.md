Chapter Promise:
Show that intrigue and novelty keep the room mentally active by holding back just enough resolution for curiosity and consequence to stay alive.

Opening Move:
Open inside a pitch where the presenter explains everything too early and the room's attention goes flat, so the need for intrigue is visible before the term is defined.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> intrigue as the next STRONG move after story
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4 -> novelty and surprise keep attention active when meaning is still unfolding
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-6 -> confusion, hype, or premature full explanation kill curiosity, and later moves need live attention

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: scene of a room going passive after the presenter removes all tension too quickly
- P2: define intrigue as controlled unresolved significance rather than empty mystery
- P3: explain why novelty and difference keep the audience from mentally checking out
- P4: show how full explanation too early or hype without payoff breaks attention
- P5: preserve the limit against gimmickry, vagueness, or manipulative suspense
- P6: bridge to prize as the next move once curiosity and consequence are alive

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Intrigue is not withholding for the sake of ego. It is the timing discipline that lets the audience feel there is still meaningful difference to discover.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Intrigue can sharpen attention and understanding, but pushed too far it becomes a fog machine that protects the presenter by delaying clarity.

Threshold Question:
When does intrigue sharpen the audience's understanding by making them lean in, and when does it become a manipulative fog that delays clarity for the presenter's benefit?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: intrigue follows story in STRONG because attention still needs fuel
- Takeaway 2: novelty and surprise help the audience keep scanning for meaning
- Takeaway 3: unresolved significance can be useful when it stays purposeful
- Takeaway 4: confusion, hype, or total explanation too early flatten attention
- Takeaway 5: intrigue should heighten clarity, not postpone it indefinitely

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): deciding whether to reveal the decisive customer behavior immediately or set up the contrast first
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): seeing how a team lost interest by giving away the conclusion before the problem felt consequential
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): noticing how a surprising implication keeps a difficult conversation more engaged than a blunt dump of conclusions
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): forecasting what happens when a founder holds one crucial reveal until the audience feels the stakes
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing between safe full explanation and a sharper sequence that preserves curiosity
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing a flat answer with one that lets the consequence land a beat later

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like a judge looking up only when a missing piece appears, a team losing the room after showing the answer too early, a founder pausing on a surprising metric, or a listener leaning in when the implication lands one step later.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must imply that once attention is alive, the next move is to turn that attention into value and scarcity through prize.
