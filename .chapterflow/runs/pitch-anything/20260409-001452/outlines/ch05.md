Chapter Promise:
Show that story is the next efficient move after frame because it carries meaning, sequence, and stakes faster than explanation alone.

Opening Move:
Open inside a pitch where the presenter starts listing facts and credentials until the room looks informed but not moved, so the need for story is visible before the term is defined.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> story as the next STRONG move after frame
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4 -> story transfers meaning and stakes faster than exhaustive explanation
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-6 -> rambling backstory weakens attention and status, and intrigue depends on a clean sequence

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: scene of a pitch drowning in facts before the room understands why they matter
- P2: define story as sequence carrying meaning through the frame
- P3: explain why people follow movement, cause, and consequence faster than disconnected detail
- P4: show how backstory dumps and chronology without purpose burn attention
- P5: preserve the limit against manipulative or indulgent storytelling
- P6: bridge to intrigue as the next move that keeps the story charged

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Story is not decorative narrative. It is the disciplined sequence that lets the room understand what changed, why it matters, and where the presenter is taking the audience next.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Story can clarify the opportunity quickly, but pushed too far it becomes a self-protective performance that keeps the presenter talking without increasing understanding.

Threshold Question:
When does a pitch story clarify the opportunity for the room, and when does it become a self-protective performance that spends time without increasing understanding?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: story is the next move after frame in STRONG
- Takeaway 2: sequence helps the room follow meaning faster than piled facts
- Takeaway 3: stakes rise when cause and consequence are visible
- Takeaway 4: rambling exposition lowers attention and status
- Takeaway 5: story should clarify, not manipulate or replace substance

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): deciding whether to keep listing startup facts or tell the customer sequence cleanly
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): seeing how a team lost judges by explaining the recommendation before telling the situation that made it necessary
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): noticing how a promotion ask changes once the turning-point story replaces a credential dump
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): forecasting what happens when a founder compresses the backstory into a short, pointed narrative
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing between full chronology and the few events that actually move the recommendation
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing a scattered explanation with a story that makes the stakes legible

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like an investor glancing at the deck while facts pile up, a judge stopping note-taking, a manager leaning forward when the turning point appears, or a room waking up once cause and consequence become visible.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must imply that once story has created movement, intrigue becomes the next way to keep the room leaning forward.

Genericity Risks:
- turning story into generic advice about being relatable
- treating chronology as enough without consequence
- forgetting that the chapter is about pitch movement, not memoir

Forbidden Drift:
- no screenplay jargon or hero myth templates
- no evidence-free celebration of emotion
