Chapter Promise:
Show why good ideas still lose when the pitch method is wrong, and why a trainable, status-aware, attention-aware method is worth learning before the mechanics arrive.

Opening Move:
Open on the familiar humiliation of bringing more slides, more data, and more preparation into a room that still slips away within minutes.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 2-3 -> Klaff's more-than-$400-million credibility claim
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 1-4 -> method matters more than effort
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-6 -> the book's promise across investor, client, and negotiation contexts

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: picture the familiar failure of the overprepared pitch
- P2: argue that the mistake is methodological, not moral or motivational
- P3: establish why Klaff's claimed results make the promise worth hearing
- P4: explain the real target: attention, status, and decision pressure rather than pure logic
- P5: widen the applicability beyond venture deals
- P6: preserve the limit that method cannot save a weak offer and bridge to the brain-model chapter

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter is not saying substance does not matter. It is saying that strong substance still fails when it is delivered through a method that ignores how audiences actually receive a pitch.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Better pitch method can clarify a real opportunity, but technique becomes dangerous or hollow when it is used to make a weak offer feel stronger than it is.

Threshold Question:
When does better pitch method genuinely clarify a strong opportunity, and when is a presenter using technique to hide the fact that the underlying offer is still weak?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: effort is not the same thing as method
- Takeaway 2: pitch reception is shaped by attention and status, not logic alone
- Takeaway 3: the book's method is trainable and transferable
- Takeaway 4: applicability across deals, clients, and negotiations
- Takeaway 5: method has a credibility limit when the offer itself is weak

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choosing whether to add more slides or change the pitch approach
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): a failed case-competition pitch that had research but lost the room

- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): forecasting what happens when a founder leads with data instead of control of the room
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): deciding how to pitch a student startup to skeptical judges
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing a needy ask with a more controlled, method-based approach
