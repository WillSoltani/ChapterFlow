Chapter Promise:
Show that a pitch is not finished when the room is interested. It is finished when the room takes a real next step without the presenter becoming needy.

Opening Move:
Open inside a pitch where the room looks impressed, but the presenter leaves with compliments instead of commitment because no decision was defined.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> decision as the next STRONG move after hookpoint
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4 -> interest must become commitment or momentum leaks away
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-6 -> needy closing behavior weakens status and invites delay

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: scene where audience warmth is mistaken for progress
- P2: define decision as the move from vivid interest to concrete commitment
- P3: explain why undefined endings dissolve momentum
- P4: show how neediness and overexplaining weaken the close
- P5: preserve the limit against premature pressure or forced certainty
- P6: bridge to the full live sequence in the next chapter

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Getting a decision is not the same as pushing harder. It is the discipline of ending the pitch with a clear commitment path while keeping status, scarcity, and timing intact.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Decision pressure can clarify reality, but too much pressure too early reveals neediness and damages trust.

Threshold Question:
When does pressing for a decision clarify the real state of the room, and when does it become premature pressure that reveals neediness instead of control?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: decision follows hookpoint because vivid value still needs a concrete move
- Takeaway 2: interest alone is not progress if the next step stays undefined
- Takeaway 3: needy closes weaken status by asking for reassurance instead of commitment
- Takeaway 4: overexplaining can dilute the force of the decision moment
- Takeaway 5: the best close defines the next step without pushing before the room is ready

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choosing one concrete diligence ask instead of fishing for enthusiasm
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): realizing a strong committee presentation failed because no recommendation was actually requested
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): noticing the difference between warm interest and a real yes
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): forecasting what happens when a founder stops filling silence and lets the decision land
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing between one clear ask and a vague request for feedback
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing an approval-seeking ending with a calm commitment path

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like a partner meeting ending in “keep me posted,” a scholarship panel waiting for a clear ask, a founder resisting the urge to add one more slide, or a personal conversation turning once the next step becomes explicit.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must imply that the next chapter shows what the full pitch looks like when all the earlier moves, including decision, work together in one sequence.

Genericity Risks:
- drifting into generic closing-technique advice
- confusing pressure with control
- praising audience enthusiasm as if it were commitment

Forbidden Drift:
- no boilerplate sales-closing tactics
- no pressure doctrine detached from timing and status
- no brief-language leakage into reader-facing prose
