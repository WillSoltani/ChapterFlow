# Chapter 5: Managing Others

Managing others is not mainly about pushing harder. Duhigg frames this chapter around solving a kidnapping with lean and agile thinking and a culture of trust. That combination matters because leadership gets tested most when information is incomplete, conditions shift fast, and people have to keep reporting reality instead of protecting themselves from the boss.

Lean and agile thinking matter here because uncertain work cannot rely only on brittle plans handed down from above. It needs shorter loops of action, feedback, and adjustment. A manager has to keep learning from what the team sees in real time and keep the work movable enough to respond. Leadership becomes less about command alone and more about designing a system where useful information can travel fast enough to improve execution.

Trust is the social condition that makes those loops work. If people fear punishment for bad news, uncertainty, or correction, then the manager gets a cleaner story and a weaker grasp of reality. A team can look orderly while the most useful signals are being edited out. In that kind of system, control creates silence, and silence slows adaptation.

The chapter therefore pushes toward a harder definition of strong management. A strong leader does not simply tighten oversight. They build conditions where people can report change, revise what is not working, and stay coordinated without freezing up. Standards still matter, but standards become more effective when the room does not punish the signals that help the work improve.

The limit is important. Trust does not mean softness, and agile thinking does not mean chaos. Trust matters because it improves execution, and iterative structure matters because it keeps plans learnable instead of rigid.

The bridge into the next chapter is clean. Once leaders create better signal flow and adaptation, the next problem is how decisions themselves get made under uncertainty. Teams can report reality faster, but they still need a way to revise beliefs and choose well when certainty never fully arrives.
