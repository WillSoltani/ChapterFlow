Core Claim:
Productive teams depend less on stacking brilliant individuals than on building interaction norms that let people speak, notice trouble, and recover together. The frozen source bundle ties this chapter to Google's team study and to Saturday Night Live. The chapter therefore needs to treat psychological safety as a practical group condition, not a warm feeling. Teams become stronger when members can contribute without paying a hidden social penalty for speaking up.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about group interaction quality, especially psychological safety, rather than about motivation or talent alone. The Google study and Saturday Night Live pairing makes the chapter specifically about team process and room conditions.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that Google studied what makes teams effective.
Then the author shows that the answer turned on how people interact rather than who is on the roster.
Then the author extends that principle to creative teams like Saturday Night Live, where conversational safety and candor affect output.

Required Anchors:
- Google data scientists run a four-year study on team effectiveness; the friction is expected talent-based answers fail, the consequence is interaction norms matter more.
- Psychological safety becomes the governing condition; the friction is people hold back when the room punishes voice, the consequence is weaker collective performance.
- Saturday Night Live serves as a second anchor showing that even highly creative, high-pressure teams depend on how the group handles contribution.
