Core Claim:
Motivation is not just wanting something harder. Duhigg opens by showing that action can collapse even when intelligence, memory, and ordinary mood look intact. The chapter frames motivation as tied to a felt sense of agency and control rather than raw energy alone. The practical implication is that people become more self-propelling when situations are structured so choice, ownership, and action become easier to initiate.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is not a generic productivity opener. It is built around the puzzle of Robert Philippe's sudden apathy, the striatum and action-initiation question, and the subtitle-level linkage between boot camp, nursing home rebellions, and locus of control.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes a motivation puzzle through Robert Philippe's abrupt passivity after the South America trip.
Then the author shows that the problem is not simple sadness or low intelligence, because Dr. Richard Strub finds intact cognition but damaged action-initiation.
Then the author widens the frame toward agency, boot-camp redesign, and locus of control as the pattern that makes action more available.

- Robert Philippe returns from La Paz with sudden apathy despite a prior identity built on hustle and initiative; the friction is loss of drive, the consequence is business and daily life stall.
