Core Claim:
Dick Smith's chapter argues that outsider rationality can survive inside family-control realities if continuity is governed by disciplined capital allocation rather than by sentiment or insulation. The edge is not rebellious disruption. It is optimization under ownership constraints that could easily soften the scorecard.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter shifts from Stiritz's public-company toughness to a more politically and emotionally protected structure. Smith matters because the book must show that owner logic can work even when family control changes incentives, expectations, and the social cost of hard decisions.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author frames General Cinema as a family-linked company where continuity and control shape the decision environment.
Then the author positions Smith as balancing the advantages and traps of family influence.
Then the author shows that rational capital allocation can optimize rather than merely preserve the enterprise.
Finally the author warns that continuity becomes dangerous when it turns into shelter for inertia or softness.
