Core Claim:
Becker closes by asking what kind of heroic individual can face finitude without collapsing into cheap illusion, private grandiosity, or destructive dependence. This chapter matters because it gathers the whole book's argument about death, heroism, transference, failed salvation projects, and mental illness into a final religious-psychological question about mature courage.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the closing synthesis chapter. It must not become generic self-help, anti-religious polemic, or a simple endorsement of comforting belief. The key move is that heroism remains necessary, but the best form must be more reality-facing, less grandiose, and less dependent on totalizing illusion.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author asks what sort of heroism could survive the book's destruction of cheap immortality projects.
Then the author argues that a viable heroic individual must face creatureliness, dependence, and death without denial-driven inflation.
Then the author brings psychology and religion together around humility, courage, and openness to transcendence beyond private grandiosity.
Then the author concludes that mature heroism is neither illusion-free certainty nor narcissistic self-expansion, but a more truthful stance toward finitude and being.
