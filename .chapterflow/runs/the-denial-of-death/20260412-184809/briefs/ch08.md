Core Claim:
Becker turns to Otto Rank to show that romantic love, creativity, and religion are attempts to solve the hunger for transcendence, but each can fail when asked to bear too much of the human need for meaning and self-completion. This chapter matters because it takes the dependency logic of Chapter 7 and tests broader escape routes beyond one person: lovers, art, and spiritual aspiration can become hero systems too.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the Rank synthesis chapter on love, creativity, and transcendence. It must not become a generic praise of romance or artistic freedom. The key move is that these are serious but unstable answers to the human need for heroic significance.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author turns from dependency on persons to Rank's broader account of human transcendence hunger.
Then the author shows how love, creativity, and religion can function as attempts to solve self-burden and mortality pressure.
Then the author explains why these answers can break down when they are asked to provide total salvation.
Then the author concludes that psychoanalysis closes itself too soon when it stops before this wider existential and spiritual terrain.

Counterarguments or Limits:

Next Chapter Bridge:
These partial answers prepare Becker's later argument that modern psychoanalysis still lacks a convincing world-picture strong enough to heal failed heroism.
