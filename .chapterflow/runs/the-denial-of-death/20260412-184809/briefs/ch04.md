Core Claim:
Becker argues that character is not just personality style. It is a durable defensive arrangement, a vital lie that allows a person to function without being crushed by creatureliness and death terror. This chapter matters because it converts the earlier structural analysis into a stable psychological form. Character becomes the answer the self builds to stay coherent, worthy, and protected.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the character-as-defense chapter. It must not drift back into general mortality explanation or forward into Kierkegaard and Freud disputes. The key move is that character is a necessary falsehood that supports living while narrowing reality.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that people need stable character structures to bear life.
Then the author shows how those structures simplify reality and defend against terror.
Then the author explains why a vital lie is both necessary and costly.
Then the author concludes that character protects life by shrinking what the person can honestly face.

Required Anchors:
- character as a defense structure rather than neutral personality
