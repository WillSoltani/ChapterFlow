Core Claim:
Becker revisits Freud by treating Freud himself as a case of character and heroic defense. Freud's insight into repression and human conflict remains central, but Becker argues that Freud's own character also narrowed what he could affirm or see. This chapter matters because it turns method back onto the founder: genius and limitation emerge from the same defended structure.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the Freud-as-case-study chapter. It must neither become biography gossip nor a wholesale dismissal of Freud. The chapter's force lies in holding admiration and criticism together: Freud's severity, courage, and reductionism belong to one character structure.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author acknowledges Freud's massive psychological insight.
Then the author asks what Freud's own character made possible and what it blocked.
Then the author shows how defended style can shape theory as well as personal life.
Then the author concludes that Freud's greatness and his limitations are bound together.
