Core Claim:
Becker argues that the present outcome of psychoanalysis remains inadequate because neurosis is not only a technical symptom problem but a form of failed heroism, and modernity lacks a convincing world-picture strong enough to heal that failure. This chapter matters because it gathers the book's earlier arguments and judges psychoanalysis as too thin when it offers adjustment without a larger framework of meaning.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the failed-heroism and psychoanalytic inadequacy chapter. It must not become a generic anti-therapy argument. The key move is that psychoanalysis can expose defenses yet still fail to provide a world-picture proportionate to the scale of the human rescue problem.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author reviews what psychoanalysis can genuinely reveal about repression, conflict, and defense.
Then the author argues that neurosis is better understood as failed heroism rather than as isolated symptom trouble.
Then the author shows why modern psychoanalysis lacks a convincing world-picture strong enough to answer that failure.
Then the author concludes that understanding pathology without a larger frame leaves the deepest human problem unresolved.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- failed heroism: breakdown that comes when the self cannot sustain a workable meaning system
- thin cure: relief or insight that does not reach the larger existential burden
- world-picture: a structure of meaning strong enough to hold life, worth, and mortality
- psychoanalytic insufficiency: diagnostic truth without adequate existential answer

Specific Applications:
- reading recurring dysfunction as collapse in meaning and hero-system support
- understanding why insight into one's patterns may still leave life directionless
- seeing why adjustment alone may feel emotionally thin

Common Misreadings:
- claiming Becker thinks psychoanalysis is useless
- reducing failed heroism to ordinary low self-esteem

Counterarguments or Limits:
Keep the chapter focused on inadequacy of scope, not total dismissal. Becker is not denying psychoanalytic insight. He is saying that without a stronger world-picture it cannot complete the work it begins.
