Chapter Promise:
The reader will understand why Becker treats fear of death as a constant but mostly hidden pressure that development and culture struggle to contain.

Opening Move:
Open with the unnerving claim that death fear is not rare morbidity but background pressure that most people survive by refusing to hold directly.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 competing views on death fear
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 childhood helplessness and immature ego
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 adult coping and managed dependence

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with the mismatch between how little people speak of death and how much it governs them
- P2: set up the two views and Becker's siding with universality
- P3: explain why terror has to be repressed or translated
- P4: make childhood helplessness concrete
- P5: show how fear, wishes, dependence, and lack of control get braided together
- P6: connect those early conditions to adult coping styles
- P7 if needed: bridge to the bodily-symbolic paradox of Chapter 3

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Death fear is not constant conscious panic. It is a universal pressure transformed into indirect defenses, routines, dependencies, and symbolic control.

Hard-Depth Tension:
If good adaptation mostly means learning how not to feel death terror directly, how much of ordinary maturity is concealment?

Threshold Question:
When does a defense against helplessness keep a person alive, and when does it quietly become a smaller prison?
