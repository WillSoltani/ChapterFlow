Chapter Promise:
The reader will understand character as a necessary defensive structure, Becker's vital lie, rather than as neutral personality alone.

Opening Move:
Open with someone whose stable self-image protects them and traps them at the same time.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 character as defense
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 vital lie as selective reality
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 costs of rigidity and narrowed truth

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with the relief of a fixed self
- P2: define character as defense structure
- P3: explain the vital lie and why it is necessary
- P4: show how character narrows reality into something livable
- P5: show the cost in rigidity and defended identity
- P6: question whether loosening character is survivable
- P7 if needed: bridge to Kierkegaard and inward struggle

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Character is not just habit. It is an organized answer to terror that keeps the person coherent by filtering reality.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How much truth can a person bear without losing the lie that keeps daily life stable?

Threshold Question:
What would remain of the self if its most necessary simplifications started to loosen?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: character as defense
- Takeaway 2: vital lie as necessary simplification
- Takeaway 3: selective reality and rigidity
- Takeaway 4: cost of defended identity
- Takeaway 5: danger of loosening the lie too quickly
