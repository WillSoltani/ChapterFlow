Core Claim:
Chapter 3 deepens the intuition-first argument by turning it into a control problem. The reflective, verbal part of the mind is not usually steering with clean authority; it is often riding a larger system of intuition, momentum, and habit. The chapter's force comes from showing why explanation is easier than control.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the chapter that converts the intuition-first thesis into the rider-elephant problem of self-command.

Author Logic Chain:

Then he uses the rider-elephant frame to show why control is difficult.
Then he turns that difficulty into a live question about self-regulation and persuasion.
