Core Claim:
Chapter 1 reopens morality as a design question instead of assuming that people arrive at right and wrong through careful reasoning. Haidt puts rationalist and blank-slate accounts under pressure by moving through developmental psychology, cross-cultural moral boundaries, and cases of moral dumbfounding. The chapter's real turn is that people often feel moral certainty before they can explain it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It breaks the book open by attacking the prestige of moral reasoning itself rather than beginning with politics, religion, or moral foundations.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author sets up the old debate between innate moral structure and learned moral content.
Then he revisits Piaget, Kohlberg, Turiel, and cross-cultural work to show that morality is not just a single rational ladder.
Then he lands on moral dumbfounding as evidence that judgment often arrives ahead of articulated reasons.

Required Anchors:
- Piaget and Kohlberg as the reasoning-heavy developmental tradition
- Turiel and cross-cultural contrast as pressure on a single narrow moral domain
- moral dumbfounding as the chapter's cleanest demonstration that judgment outruns explanation

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter destabilizes rationalism, but it does not yet offer the book's full replacement model. It opens the case for intuitionism; it does not complete it.
