Core Claim:
Chapter 4 moves the intuition-first model into public life by showing that reasoning often works as persuasion, coalition-building, and self-explanation in front of other people. The point is not only that people defend prior judgments. It is that they do so in ways shaped by audience, alliance, and social payoff. The chapter makes reasoning look public, strategic, and group-aware.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is where Part I turns from internal sequence into public persuasion and political or social explanation.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author moves from internal judgment into outward argument.
Then he shows that reasoning helps recruit allies and justify positions socially.
Then he frames public argument as group-aware and strategically selective rather than purely truth-seeking.
