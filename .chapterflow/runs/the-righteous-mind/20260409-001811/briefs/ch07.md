Core Claim:
Chapter 7 connects the widened moral framework to politics by arguing that different political intuitions often arise from different moral emphases rather than from one side having no morality at all.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the first explicit political application chapter. The job is to turn the moral-foundations map into a way of reading ideological disagreement without flattening either side into stupidity or bad faith.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author carries Chapter 6's multiple-foundations framework into political conflict.
Then he argues that different camps respond more strongly to different moral concerns.
Then he uses that difference to make political disagreement more intelligible before the asymmetry claim of Chapter 8.

Required Anchors:
- political disagreement as different moral emphasis rather than simple moral absence

Specific Applications:
- noticing that two political arguments may defend different moral goods
- seeing how a policy dispute can activate different moral priorities in different listeners
- re-reading partisan conflict as a clash of salience profiles before judging motives

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as proof that all political positions are equally wise
- assuming one side simply lacks morality
- importing Chapter 8's asymmetry too early

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should preserve that explaining moral differences is not the same as endorsing them. The point is to make political intuitions legible before later evaluative claims intensify.
