Core Claim:
Chapter 9 argues that human beings are not only self-interested but also groupish, and that morality has to be understood through the tension between selfishness and group-binding tendencies.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the pivot from political foundations into the social and evolutionary logic of group life. The job is to reopen morality at the level of groups rather than isolated individuals.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author asks why humans are so groupish in addition to being self-interested.
Then he uses multilevel selection to frame the tension between selfishness and group binding.
Then he prepares the later chapters on the hive switch and religion as group technologies.

Required Anchors:

Specific Applications:
- noticing why people cooperate intensely inside groups yet compete across them

Counterarguments or Limits:
