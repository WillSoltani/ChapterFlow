Chapter Promise:
You will leave Chapter 2 seeing why moral reasoning so often behaves less like a judge and more like a defense lawyer.

Opening Move:
Open on the familiar experience of hearing a person explain a decision that already feels settled before the explanation begins.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P2 for Hume and the older intuitionist line
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P1 and P3 for the intuitive-dog inversion
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P6 for post-hoc justification and persuasion

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with explanation arriving after commitment
- P2: bring in Hume and the anti-rationalist line
- P3: explain what the intuitive-dog image overturns
- P4: show why reasoning often looks like defense rather than discovery
- P5: explain the social use of reasoning in persuasion and reputation
- P6: preserve a narrow role for reasoning without putting it back on the throne
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 3's rider-elephant depth

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish reasoning used to discover from reasoning used to justify and persuade.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The deeper question is when servant-reason can still improve judgment instead of merely polishing it.

Threshold Question:
If the rider usually serves the elephant, what makes genuine self-correction possible at all?
