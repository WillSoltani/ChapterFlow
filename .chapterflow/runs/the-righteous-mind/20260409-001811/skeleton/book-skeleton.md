# Book Skeleton

## Metadata
- Title: The Righteous Mind
- Subtitle: Why Good People Are Divided by Politics and Religion
- Author: Jonathan Haidt
- Locked edition family: Pantheon hardcover / Vintage paperback text family
- Source basis: official introduction, official table of contents, official endnotes, official Chapter 9 preview, narrow secondary support

## Chapter Order And Intent
1. `Where Does Morality Come From?`
Intent: break the assumption that morality is mainly reasoned out and reopen the question through developmental psychology, anthropology, and moral dumbfounding.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: medium

2. `The Intuitive Dog and Its Rational Tail`
Intent: establish the first principle directly by arguing that intuition leads and reasoning usually follows in service of justification and persuasion.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: medium

3. `Elephants Rule`
Intent: deepen the rider-elephant model and show why direct argument so often fails to move people.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity: low

4. `Vote for Me (Here's Why)`
Intent: move from individual reasoning to social and political persuasion, with attention to self-interest, hypocrisy, and signaling.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity: medium

5. `Beyond WEIRD Morality`
Intent: widen morality beyond Western individualist assumptions using cross-cultural contrast.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: high

6. `Taste Buds of the Righteous Mind`
Intent: introduce the multiple moral foundations as a better map than harm-and-fairness reductionism.
Source richness: medium-high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: high

7. `The Moral Foundations of Politics`
Intent: connect differing foundation profiles to political intuitions.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: high

8. `The Conservative Advantage`
Intent: explain why conservatives often communicate across more moral registers than liberals.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: high

9. `Why Are We So Groupish?`
Intent: reopen morality through multilevel selection and the tension between selfishness and groupishness.
Source richness: high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: high

10. `The Hive Switch`
Intent: explain the conditions under which people shift from self to group-minded coordination.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity: high

11. `Religion Is a Team Sport`
Intent: reinterpret religion as a group-binding technology rather than only a truth-claim system.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity: high

12. `Can't We All Disagree More Constructively?`
Intent: end by translating the framework into more honest, less self-righteous political disagreement.
Source richness: medium-high
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity: high

## Rotation Plan For Examples
- Work / school / personal remain the default 2/2/2 split for each chapter.
- Early chapters should privilege conversation, judgment, and disagreement scenarios rather than generic productivity scenes.
- Political chapters need concrete but non-cartoon partisan situations.
