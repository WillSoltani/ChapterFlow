Frozen-source basis:
- Official table of contents confirms Chapter 1 title and placement.
- Official introduction frames Part I around the principle that intuitions come first and strategic reasoning comes second.
- Official endnotes for Chapter 1 support the chapter's active debate with empiricism, rationalism, Piaget, Kohlberg, Turiel, Shweder, and moral dumbfounding.

Supportable chapter-local points:
- Haidt opens by asking where morality comes from rather than assuming that people reason it out cleanly.
- The chapter engages the older contrast between nativism and blank-slate empiricism.
- Piaget and Kohlberg matter because they treated moral development as a reasoning-heavy achievement.
- Turiel matters because he distinguished moral rules from social conventions.
- Cross-cultural work matters because not every society draws the moral boundary in the same place.
- Haidt's phrase "moral dumbfounding" marks cases where people react strongly yet struggle to justify the reaction.
- The chapter sets up the turn toward intuitionism and away from a purely rationalist account.
