Ana keeps promising herself a full evening cleanup ritual and keeps missing it. The usual diagnosis is motivation. Chapter 3 says the cleaner diagnosis is often ability. BJ Fogg argues that many behaviors do not happen because they ask too much of the moment. The plan may look reasonable from far away and still be too heavy at 9:20 p.m. after a tired day.

That is why this chapter treats ease with more respect than people expect. Fogg is not saying easy is noble for its own sake. He is saying easy changes what can actually happen. A behavior that takes one minute, one breath, one page, one tooth, or one sentence can survive moods and schedules that would crush the grander version. Once the step becomes light enough, the habit gets a chance to exist in real life instead of only in planning language.

The chapter therefore reframes smallness as strategy. The tiny version is not an insult to the goal. It is the doorway to repetition. If you want to become someone who flosses regularly, flossing one tooth is not trivial. It is the smallest version that still points in the right direction. If you want to write daily, one sentence may serve you better than an elegant hour that collapses every third day. Ability is about whether the action fits the moment, not whether the aspiration sounds impressive.

Fogg also keeps the chapter from shrinking into anti-effort dogma. Making a habit easier is not the same as never asking more of yourself. It means removing the needless friction that keeps the behavior from getting started. The lighter first move makes consistency possible. Growth can come later, but later only matters if there is something stable enough to grow.

That is the chapter's real argument. Hardness is often confused with seriousness. Chapter 3 says seriousness is better measured by whether the habit actually happens. A heavy routine that keeps failing may flatter the ego more than a tiny one, but the tiny one is the one that leaves evidence in the day. Ease is not weakness here. It is design that respects how behavior actually gets done.
