Core Claim:
The second control trap must show that not every move toward more control is wise just because control is attractive. Where the first trap focused on outside resistance, this chapter should focus on a different danger: a control move can still be poor, premature, or unstable even without reading resistance badly. The chapter should keep the warning narrow and strategic, setting up the need for criteria about when autonomy is truly supportable.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the companion trap chapter that balances the first trap. It prevents Rule 3 from becoming a simplistic defense of all autonomy-seeking.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author distinguishes this second trap from simple stakeholder resistance.
Then the author shows that some control moves are weak because the underlying support is not strong enough.
Then the author concludes that pursuing autonomy requires standards for timing and sustainability, not just desire.

Required Anchors:
- the second control trap as a different failure mode from outside pushback
- control moves that are still premature or unstable
- the need to judge autonomy by support and sustainability
