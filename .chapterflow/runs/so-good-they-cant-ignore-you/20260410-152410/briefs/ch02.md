Core Claim:
This chapter moves from myth-breaking to evidence. Newport argues that preexisting occupational passion is uncommon, which makes it a poor default planning tool. He uses Roadtrip Nation stories to show that compelling careers often begin in messy, indirect ways, and he uses a student-passion statistic to show that even when people report passions, those passions rarely map cleanly onto work.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the chapter that replaces iconic biography with frequency evidence. Instead of arguing about one famous person, it asks how often a stable, career-relevant passion is actually present before the work begins.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author looks at Roadtrip Nation interviews and notices that fulfilling careers usually have winding origins.
Then the author adds evidence that most reported passions are hobbies rather than viable work directions.
Then the author concludes that preexisting passion is too rare to function as a general career rule.

Required Anchors:
- Roadtrip Nation as a broad archive of fulfilling but nonlinear career stories.
- Ira Glass as an example of someone who was not immediately good and did not begin with polished certainty.
- The Canadian-student finding that only a tiny minority named passions tied directly to work or education.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Career passion rarity plus how it works: even when people feel strong interests, those interests usually do not arrive as career-ready directions.

Specific Applications:
- reducing panic about not yet having one career-defining passion
- favoring exploration that yields evidence over identity declarations
- distinguishing hobbies from realistic work paths without belittling the hobby

Common Misreadings:
- rare means impossible
- if a passion starts as a hobby it has no value at all

Counterarguments or Limits:
Some people do begin with unusually clear callings. The chapter should admit the exception while arguing it is too uncommon to govern advice for everyone else.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
Chapter 1 weakened the Jobs myth as proof.
Next Chapter Bridge:
If passion-first planning is both rare and unstable, the next step is to show how it can also make people less happy.
