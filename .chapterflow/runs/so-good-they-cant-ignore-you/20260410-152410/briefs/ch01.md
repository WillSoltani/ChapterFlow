Core Claim:
The chapter opens by attacking the modern career myth that people discover a preexisting passion and then build a good work life by obeying it. Steve Jobs is useful here not because he proves the rule, but because his story has been simplified into a cultural slogan. Newport's point is narrower and sharper: Jobs' public advice made the passion hypothesis sound obvious, while his actual path was more exploratory, contingent, and skill-driven than the slogan suggests. The chapter is an argument about bad evidence, not a full theory of what replaces passion.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the book's myth-breaking cold open. It uses Jobs as an iconic but misleading mascot for career passion, then turns that example into a methodological warning about taking a polished success story as a universal operating rule.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author names the modern passion script through Steve Jobs.
Then the author shows that Jobs' own path was messier than the script suggests.
Then the author concludes that the passion hypothesis starts from weak evidence and needs to be challenged before the rest of the book can proceed.

Required Anchors:
- Steve Jobs' Stanford commencement advice telling graduates to find work they love, and the way that advice became a cultural career slogan.
- Jobs' nonlinear path through dropping out, sampling calligraphy, building Apple, being fired, and returning, which resists a tidy preexisting-passion story.
- The gap between an inspiring origin story and a trustworthy career rule.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Passion hypothesis plus how it works: the belief that occupational happiness comes from first identifying a preexisting passion and then matching work to it.

Specific Applications:
- evaluating career advice that comes wrapped in a heroic founder story
- resisting early panic that you have not yet found the one right calling
- treating compelling work as something built, not simply uncovered

Common Misreadings:
- rejecting the passion hypothesis means feelings never matter
- Steve Jobs is being dismissed rather than used as a caution against bad inference

Counterarguments or Limits:
Jobs was obviously energized by some of what he built, so the chapter cannot pretend interest is irrelevant. The chapter should admit that interest matters while insisting that retrospective stories are weak career instructions.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
This is the opener.
Next Chapter Bridge:
If Jobs is weak proof for passion-first careers, the next question is whether preexisting passions are common enough to anchor a career strategy at all.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:

Moral Complexity:
Keep admiration for Jobs separate from blind imitation. The chapter is about strategic reasoning, not icon demolition.
