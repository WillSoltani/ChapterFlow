Core Claim:
This chapter names the central currency of Rule 2: career capital. Newport argues that compelling work is usually purchased with rare and valuable skills, not simply wished into existence. The craftsman mindset matters because it builds that currency. Without it, people tend to demand autonomy, mission, or ideal conditions before they have earned the leverage required to get them.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the chapter that turns the craftsman mindset into a concrete mechanism. It explains why better work later depends on capital accumulated now.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author defines career capital as rare and valuable skill.
Then the author shows why desirable work traits must usually be bought with that capital.
Then the author concludes that wanting rewards before capital exists leads to weak strategy.

Required Anchors:
- Newport's explicit framing of career capital as the currency of compelling work.
- The contrast between capital builders and people who want rewards too early.
- The idea that autonomy, creativity, and mission are purchased, not merely selected.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

Specific Applications:

- resisting early demands for autonomy without skill backing

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
Some environments may consume effort without converting it into rare and valuable skill. The chapter should keep the distinction between real capital-building and empty grind.
