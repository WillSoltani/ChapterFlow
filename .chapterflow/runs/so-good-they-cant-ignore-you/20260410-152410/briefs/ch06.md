Core Claim:
After defining career capital, this chapter asks who actually builds it. Newport's answer is behavioral rather than mystical. Career capitalists treat work as a place to accumulate rare and valuable skill before making large demands for autonomy, mission, or ideal conditions. They are strategic about sequencing. They do not confuse wanting a better life with having already built the leverage to negotiate for it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter personifies the concept from Chapter 5. It profiles the kind of posture that turns career capital from an idea into a repeatable approach.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author distinguishes capital-builders from early demanders.
Then the author shows that patience and disciplined craft produce leverage more reliably than immediate lifestyle demands.
Then the author concludes that the right question is not how quickly to extract rewards but how deliberately to accumulate something rare and valuable.

Required Anchors:
- Newport's presentation of career capitalists as people who build rare and valuable skills patiently.
- The contrast between strategic builders and people who demand autonomy or mission before leverage exists.
- The idea that compelling work follows from sustained capital accumulation rather than from early insistence on ideal conditions.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Career capitalist plus how it works: a person who organizes early work around building scarce, exchangeable value before trying to spend it on better conditions.

Specific Applications:
- distinguishing leverage-building from premature reward-seeking
- evaluating whether current effort is producing portable value
- choosing patience when patience is compounding instead of merely delaying

Common Misreadings:
- career capitalist means cold opportunist
- it means suppressing all desire until some distant future

Counterarguments or Limits:
Do not imply that all patience is wise. The chapter should preserve that the patience only makes sense where skill and output are actually compounding.
