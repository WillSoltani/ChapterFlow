Chapter Promise:
The reader will understand why a move toward more control can trigger resistance and why that resistance may partly reflect the value they have built.

Opening Move:
Open on the surprise that a reasonable freedom move can face pushback precisely because it threatens others' access to value.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define the first control trap
- P2: explain why resistance appears
- P3: connect resistance to leverage and value
- P4: distinguish pushback from automatic proof of rightness
- P5: show how to read resistance more intelligently
- P6: bridge to the second control trap

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between resistance as evidence that value is at stake and resistance as automatic proof that the control move is strategically correct.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard must preserve that pushback can signal leverage while still leaving room for poor timing or weak judgment.

Threshold Question:
When should resistance be read as evidence of value rather than as evidence that the move itself is wrong?
