Heading: The Passion of Steve Jobs

Frozen source support:
- Google Books / Google Play establish Rule 1 as "Don't follow your passion" and frame the book as a challenge to passion-first career advice.
- Stanford official transcript freeze establishes that Steve Jobs publicly urged graduates to find work they love and described a nonlinear path that included dropping out, taking calligraphy, founding Apple, being fired, and later returning.
- Secondary summary support establishes that Newport uses Jobs as the emblem of the passion hypothesis while arguing that compelling careers usually do not begin with a clear preexisting passion.

Usable chapter facts:
- Jobs' commencement advice became influential career folklore.
- Jobs' own path was nonlinear and cannot be reduced to a clean one-passion-from-the-start arc.
- Newport uses Jobs to show how a persuasive story can outrun the evidence it supposedly provides.
- The chapter opens Rule 1; it weakens the passion hypothesis but does not yet present the full replacement system.

Support limits:
- No detailed psychological claims about Jobs' motives.
- No invented chronology beyond the narrow frozen milestones.
- Use paraphrase-first language.
