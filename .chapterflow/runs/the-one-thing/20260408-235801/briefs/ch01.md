Core Claim:
Chapter 1 introduces the book's governing idea: extraordinary results come from identifying the most important task or concern and focusing on it hard enough that other work becomes easier or unnecessary. The chapter is not yet about process discipline. It is about the leverage logic behind choosing one focal priority instead of spreading effort broadly. Keller uses this opening to frame focus as the difference between variable effort and disproportionate results.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the conceptual key chapter. It names the book's central question and frames narrow focus as leverage rather than as minimalism, efficiency theater, or generic motivation.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author introduces the "one thing" idea as the simple but hard secret behind meaningful results.
Then the author ties uneven outcomes to uneven focus, showing why concentration matters more than sheer activity.
Then the author establishes the book's operating standard: find the focal task that makes other work easier or unnecessary.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

- leverage logic: one well-chosen action changes the value of later actions
- extraordinary results: outcomes driven by concentration rather than scattered effort

Specific Applications:

- narrowing a student's study block to the exam topic that unlocks later understanding
- deciding which personal commitment creates the strongest downstream relief or progress

Counterarguments or Limits:
Chapter 1 names the principle, but it does not yet solve the operational problem of how to sequence work, build a habit, or protect the chosen priority from interruptions and competing obligations.
