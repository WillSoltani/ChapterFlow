Core Claim:
Chapter 7 argues that willpower is not permanently available on command. The lie is assuming that disciplined effort can be summoned at full strength whenever convenient. The chapter pushes the reader to respect timing, energy, and cognitive freshness by placing important work where real strength is most available.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter should feel like a correction to heroic self-control mythology. It does not erase discipline. It narrows it by saying the right routine still has to be paired with realistic respect for when mental force is strongest.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author names endless willpower as a lie rather than as a virtue ideal.
Then the author argues that important work should not be scheduled as if inner fuel is equally available at all moments.
Then the author makes the practical demand: protect high-value tasks when strength is available and stop assuming discipline can rescue every badly timed decision.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- willpower lie: the belief that focused effort can be summoned at full strength whenever desired
- timing advantage: placing important work when cognitive force is strongest
- bad timing tax: the avoidable cost of scheduling meaningful work after strength has already been spent down

Specific Applications:
- doing the hardest project work during the most mentally fresh part of the day instead of after a day of reactive tasks
- placing demanding study or writing earlier rather than assuming it can be rescued late at night
- handling key personal decisions before fatigue and scattered obligations degrade clarity

Common Misreadings:
- turning the chapter into unsupported neuroscience theater
- treating temporary low energy as a moral excuse for never doing hard things
- acting as if discipline and scheduling no longer matter because willpower fluctuates

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not become surrender to moods. The point is not that hard work only happens when a person feels inspired. The point is that serious effort should respect predictable strength windows instead of pretending they do not matter.
