Core Claim:
Chapter 6 argues that extraordinary results do not require being perfectly disciplined in every area of life. The lie is the fantasy of total self-control. What matters is narrower, repeated discipline directed toward the behavior or routine that supports the One Thing.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter should feel like a relief from moral perfectionism without becoming soft. The reader should come away seeing that success depends less on being disciplined about everything and more on being disciplined long enough, and narrowly enough, for the right pattern to hold.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author names the disciplined-life fantasy as a lie.
Then the author argues that broad self-control across everything is not the real engine of results.
Then the author makes the practical demand: choose the behavior that most supports the One Thing and apply repeatable discipline there instead of trying to become universally perfect.

Required Anchors:

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- discipline myth: the belief that high performance requires complete self-control across all domains
- selective consistency: repeatable discipline applied to one leveraged behavior
- support routine: a simple structure that protects the One Thing from drift

Specific Applications:
- protecting one daily deep-work start time instead of trying to optimize every hour of the day
- building a stable study routine around the hardest course instead of redesigning an entire life in one sweep
- using one consistent household reset or planning ritual to reduce chaos around a key responsibility

Common Misreadings:
- turning the chapter into permission for sloppy living everywhere else
- importing later willpower ideas before the book gets there
- confusing harsh self-punishment with useful consistency

Counterarguments or Limits:
Selective discipline does not mean randomness everywhere else. The chapter should keep enough seriousness to show that chosen structure still matters. The limit is that the structure must remain tied to the One Thing rather than becoming a perfectionist identity project.
