Core Claim:
Chapter 9 should argue that people often shrink ambition because large goals feel uncomfortable, unrealistic, or destabilizing. The lie is not size itself but the reflex to treat bigness as inherently irresponsible or dangerous.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter should feel like a defense of legitimate scale after the earlier lies about equality, multitasking, discipline, willpower, and balance. It should attack smallness as a comfort strategy rather than praising reckless fantasy.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author names "big is bad" as another false belief.
Then the author argues that people often prefer smaller aims because they preserve comfort, balance, and social plausibility.
Then the author pushes the reader toward respecting bigger targets while keeping them connected to sequence and reality rather than fantasy.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

- legitimate scale: allowing a goal to stay large without turning it into fantasy

Specific Applications:
- resisting the habit of shrinking a work goal just because it feels exposed or difficult
- noticing when a student narrows the target to avoid the strain of a bigger standard
- seeing how people sometimes prefer smaller personal aims because they disturb life less

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not glorify fantasy or ego. The point is that big is not automatically bad, not that every large aim is wise. The chapter should preserve the distinction between legitimate scale and empty exaggeration.
