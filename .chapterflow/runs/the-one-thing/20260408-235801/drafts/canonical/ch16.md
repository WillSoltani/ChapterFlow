Chapter 16 follows productivity with a harder question: what keeps focused execution from thinning out the moment pressure rises? Keller answers with commitment. The chapter does not treat commitment as emotional heat, inspirational language, or brute stubbornness. It treats commitment as the disciplined protection of the path already chosen.

That move matters because productivity can still remain fragile. A person can execute well for a day or two and still lose the one thing when interruptions, competing demands, and mood swings start pressing back. Chapter 16 argues that productive focus has to be held in place. Commitment is what keeps the chosen path from being admired in theory and abandoned in practice.

This is why the chapter must be read as a protection chapter, not another intensity chapter. Keller is not asking for louder effort. He is asking for disciplined follow-through that keeps the right claim intact over time. Commitment means that the reader keeps protecting the structure, time, and attention that the one thing requires.

The limit is important. Commitment can be misunderstood as raw force, but the chapter is not praising blind stubbornness. Protective commitment still has to serve the chosen path well. It is not devotion to strain for its own sake. It is the discipline that keeps focus durable without turning seriousness into theater.

The applications make the argument concrete. A team may choose the right initiative and still let roadmap reviews, side requests, and polite dilution hollow it out unless commitment protects the lane. A student may name the right study path and still lose it during exam week unless the reading block plan is actively guarded. A person at home may value a personal change and still treat it as mood-based unless a real commitment keeps the path intact when the day gets crowded.

That is why Chapter 16 stands between productivity and the coming threats to it. Chapter 15 showed what focused execution looks like. Chapter 16 says that focused execution lasts only when the reader is committed enough to protect it. The chapter leaves the reader asking what, exactly, is most likely to steal that committed focus once the path has finally been held in place.
