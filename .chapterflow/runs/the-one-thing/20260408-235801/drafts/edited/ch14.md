A larger purpose is still too broad if it never decides what comes first. That is the turn Chapter 14 makes. After Chapter 13 connects focus to purpose, Keller now asks how that broader direction should begin governing actual choices. The chapter's answer is priority. Purpose becomes practical when it starts selecting what deserves first claim on effort.

That shift matters because purpose can otherwise stay noble and vague. A person can name a bigger reason, care deeply about it, and still let too many worthy options remain equally active. Chapter 14 pushes against that drift. Priority is the discipline that turns larger direction into order. It forces sequence where broad intention alone would still leave a crowd.

This is why the chapter is not just another plea to organize better. Keller is not talking about cosmetic sorting. He is talking about real narrowing. Priority means that one thing receives first force because it best serves the larger purpose. What comes first is no longer decided by noise, visibility, or politeness toward every reasonable option. It is decided by what the larger aim now requires.

The chapter also keeps a necessary limit in place. Priority fails if it is only a label applied to many active goals at once. Calling several items priority does not create order. It protects confusion with better language. Chapter 14 wants narrowing, not a more respectable pile. The point is not to admire a ranked list. The point is to let one sequence govern action.

The applications are straightforward. A work team may need to turn its purpose into the one initiative that truly comes first on the strategy board. A student may need to convert broad academic direction into the course-load or study move that gets real first claim. In personal life, a deeper aim may have to decide what deserves the next hour before every worthy intention gets equal voice. Priority gives purpose operational force.

That is why Chapter 14 stands between purpose and productivity. Chapter 13 named the larger direction. Chapter 14 says that direction must now start ordering what comes first. It keeps the warning visible too: if everything still remains alive, then priority has not really happened. Once real first claim is clear, Chapter 15 can turn to productivity and ask how ordered focus should actually be carried out.
