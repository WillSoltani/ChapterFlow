Core book thesis:
The book argues that extraordinary results do not come from spreading attention widely. They come from identifying the most important priority at a given level of life or work, then organizing action so that this focal task makes other tasks easier or unnecessary. The book pushes back against common productivity myths and replaces them with a sequence of purpose, priority, and concentrated execution.

Chapter-level framing notes:
- The opening block establishes the core concept, the domino metaphor, and the claim that success leaves patterns that can be studied and repeated.
- Part 1 dismantles common myths about equal priorities, multitasking, perfect discipline, endless willpower, balance as a constant state, and fear of big ambition.
- Part 2 introduces the focusing question and shows how habit and better questions narrow attention toward leverage.
- Part 3 connects focus to purpose, priority, productivity, commitment, environmental and behavioral threats, and a final call to act without regret.
