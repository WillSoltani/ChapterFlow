Chapter Promise:
Show that success leaves patterns worth studying, so the reader learns to model what works without copying it blindly.

Opening Move:
Open by rejecting the idea that success is pure mystery, then frame strong outcomes as leaving clues that can be examined.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> success leaves clues
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 3-4 -> patterns can be studied and repeated in principle
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 5-6 -> adapt principles rather than copy surfaces

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: challenge the myth that success is too personal or mysterious to study
- P2: define clues as patterns left by effective action
- P3: explain why studying what works is practical rather than derivative
- P4: distinguish principle-learning from surface imitation
- P5: apply clue-reading to work, school, or personal settings
- P6: preserve the context limit and bridge into sharper ranking

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Learning from success means extracting transferable logic, not copying visible behavior without understanding why it worked.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Patterns can guide action, but they can also mislead when the surrounding conditions are ignored.

Threshold Question:
When does modeling success sharpen judgment, and when does it become imitation that ignores context?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: success as evidence
- Takeaway 2: clues and repeatable patterns
- Takeaway 3: adaptive modeling
- Takeaway 4: work / school / personal application
- Takeaway 5: context limit

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): studying what the strongest team process consistently did before copying tools
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): seeing that the best exam performance came from repeated method, not last-minute intensity
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): helping a friend learn from what repeatedly calms a conflict
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predicting what happens when a manager copies rituals without understanding the principle
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing between inventing a new system and adapting the study pattern that already proved effective
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing random self-improvement experiments with clue-based adjustment

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like a shared dashboard, marked-up flashcards, a notebook margin full of arrows, a kitchen calendar, or a Slack thread pinned for reference.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what happens when every task starts claiming equal importance, not just name Chapter 4.

Genericity Risks:
- sounding like generic “copy winners” advice
- treating a single example as sufficient proof
- losing the difference between principle and surface behavior

Forbidden Drift:
- no invented case studies
- no contamination language or source-splice phrasing
- no grand claims that context does not matter
- no brief-language leakage into reader-facing prose
