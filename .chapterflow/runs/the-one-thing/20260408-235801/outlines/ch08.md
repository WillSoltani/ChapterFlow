Chapter Promise:
Show that perfect balance is not a constant state, so the reader stops demanding static equilibrium and starts accepting purposeful imbalance when real priorities or seasons require it.

Opening Move:
Open on the comforting image of a perfectly balanced life, then expose how meaningful priorities create uneven seasons and tradeoffs.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> perfect balance as a false constant standard
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 3-4 -> meaningful priorities create seasons and tradeoffs
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 5-6 -> temporary imbalance can be responsible when tied to real consequence

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: surface the appeal of static balance
- P2: name constant balance as the chapter's lie
- P3: explain season logic and tradeoffs
- P4: show why important priorities naturally create temporary unevenness
- P5: apply purposeful imbalance to work, school, or personal settings
- P6: preserve the limit against chronic neglect and bridge to big ambition

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Purposeful imbalance is different from permanent disorder or self-serving neglect.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Temporary imbalance can be necessary, but it can also become a convenient story for ongoing avoidance of duty.

Threshold Question:
When does temporary imbalance serve a real season, and when does it become a comforting story for ongoing neglect?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: balance as a lie when treated as constant
- Takeaway 2: season logic and tradeoffs
- Takeaway 3: purposeful imbalance
- Takeaway 4: work / school / personal applications
- Takeaway 5: limit against chronic neglect

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): accepting that one deadline season requires more force for a time
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): realizing exam-week demands were uneven and pretending balance weakened the result
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): discussing a family season where one responsibility must temporarily dominate
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predicting what happens when a team demands perfect equilibrium during a high-stakes push
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing honest temporary imbalance during a key academic stretch
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing fake balanced scheduling with a clearer seasonal tradeoff

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like an exam-week wall calendar, a sprint board before launch, a family calendar with one dominating obligation, or a dinner table conversation about what has to give for now.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what happens when people get so afraid of uneven seasons that they start shrinking the size of their goals.

Genericity Risks:
- collapsing into vague work-life balance slogans
- sounding anti-care instead of anti-static-equilibrium
- using season language as a blanket excuse for neglect

Forbidden Drift:
- no invented statistics
- no contamination language or source-splice phrasing
- no fake burnout or wellness research
- no brief-language leakage into reader-facing prose
