Chapter Promise:
Show that big outcomes are built through a chain of right moves, so the practical question is not how to do everything now but what first move starts the sequence.

Opening Move:
Open on the temptation to imagine success as one dramatic leap, then break that illusion with the domino image of progress happening in sequence.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> domino metaphor
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 3-4 -> progress as sequential and cumulative
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 5-6 -> choosing the next move that starts the chain

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: expose the fantasy of giant-leap success
- P2: introduce the domino image as a better model
- P3: explain why sequence changes what later actions can do
- P4: distinguish useful small steps from random small steps
- P5: apply the idea to work, school, or personal decisions
- P6: keep the chapter's limit alive and bridge to learning from patterns

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Sequential progress is not the same as generic persistence. The right first move matters because it changes the leverage of later moves.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Domino thinking helps when steps are linked, but messy work does not always reveal its chain in advance.

Threshold Question:
When does sequential thinking clarify action, and when does it become a misleading oversimplification of messy work that does not unfold in a neat chain?

Takeaway Count Lock:

- Hard: 5

- Takeaway 1: domino effect as sequence

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choosing the first unblocker instead of fixing everything at once
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): realizing that broad review ignored the concept that later problems depended on
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): helping someone stop thinking only in giant outcomes
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predicting what happens when a team defines the first domino clearly
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing between many small tasks and the one step that creates later ease
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing scattered effort with a chain-starting first move

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like sticky notes across a Kanban board, color-coded study guides, a buzzing team chat, a half-built spreadsheet, or a gym bag left by the door.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what we can learn from patterns of successful sequence, not just name Chapter 3.

Genericity Risks:
- turning the chapter into bland "take one step at a time" advice
- treating any small task as a valid domino
- sounding motivational instead of structural

Forbidden Drift:
- no unsupported domino-physics claims
- no fake certainty that every chain is obvious in advance
- no contamination language or source-splice phrasing
- no brief-language leakage into reader-facing prose
