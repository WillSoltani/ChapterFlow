Chapter Promise:
Show that shrinking ambition can be a comfort move, so the reader stops treating scale as inherently suspect and starts distinguishing legitimate bigness from empty grandiosity.

Opening Move:
Open on the social comfort of small, plausible goals, then expose how quickly that comfort becomes a quiet argument against scale itself.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> big is bad as the lie title and chapter claim
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 3-4 -> people often treat size as arrogance, danger, or instability
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 5-6 -> shrinking the goal can be comfort rather than wisdom

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: surface why big feels suspect
- P2: name the lie directly
- P3: explain comfort-sized ambition
- P4: show how scale aversion protects identity and balance
- P5: apply legitimate scale in work, school, and personal settings
- P6: preserve the limit against fantasy and bridge to the Focusing Question

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Legitimate scale is different from reckless inflation or vague fantasy.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Reducing scale can be prudent in some cases, but it can also be a socially acceptable way of avoiding the demands of a bigger aim.

Threshold Question:
When is reducing scale a wise constraint, and when is it just a polished way of avoiding the demands of a larger goal?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: big is bad as a lie
- Takeaway 2: comfort-sized ambition
- Takeaway 3: social plausibility and scale aversion
- Takeaway 4: work / school / personal applications
- Takeaway 5: limit against fantasy or ego

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choosing whether to keep a meaningful target large instead of cutting it down for comfort
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): seeing how a smaller academic target protected identity but weakened growth
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): talking through a life goal that feels too big only because it disrupts comfort
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predicting what happens when a team always cuts ambitious scope to stay emotionally safe
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): deciding whether scale is unrealistic or simply demanding
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing a comfort-sized goal with a larger but still grounded one

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like a roadmap review, seminar-room whiteboard, dorm hallway conversation, or a planning page where the target keeps getting trimmed.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what question can keep a legitimately big aim from collapsing into vague ambition.
