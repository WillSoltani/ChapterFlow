Chapter Promise:
Show that success does not require a perfectly disciplined life, so the reader stops chasing universal self-control and starts building narrower, repeatable discipline around the One Thing.

Opening Move:
Open on the exhausting fantasy of being disciplined about everything, then expose it as overreach that confuses moral image with useful consistency.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> disciplined-life myth as overreach
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 3-4 -> selective discipline around one meaningful behavior
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 5-6 -> repeatable structure beats perfectionist identity-building

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: surface the appeal of a fully disciplined identity
- P2: name that ideal as the chapter's lie
- P3: explain why broad self-control is the wrong target
- P4: show how narrower consistency protects one leveraged behavior
- P5: apply selective discipline to work, school, or personal settings
- P6: preserve the limit against laziness and bridge to willpower

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter should separate universal discipline fantasy from the smaller amount of repeatable discipline needed to hold one important behavior in place.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Selective discipline is useful only when it creates real structure; otherwise it becomes another flattering slogan about habits.

Threshold Question:
When does selective discipline create a real support structure, and when does it shrink into another self-soothing slogan about habits?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: myth of a fully disciplined life
- Takeaway 2: selective discipline versus perfectionism
- Takeaway 3: repeated structure around one key behavior
- Takeaway 4: work / school / personal applications
- Takeaway 5: limit against self-excusing looseness

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choosing one protected start ritual for meaningful work instead of redesigning the whole day
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): realizing broad study vows failed while one repeated routine would have helped
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): explaining why one consistent family-planning reset beats constant guilt about total life order
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predicting what happens when a team member builds one stable routine instead of chasing complete self-overhaul
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing one non-negotiable review block around the hardest class
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing perfectionist daily rules with one repeatable anchor habit

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like a notebook opened to the same first page each morning, a timer placed beside a laptop, a backpack packed the night before, or a sticky note marking one recurring start ritual.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what happens when the right routine exists but the reader still assumes willpower should always be available on demand.

Genericity Risks:
- turning the chapter into generic habit advice
- making selective discipline sound like permission to coast
- importing later willpower concepts too explicitly

Forbidden Drift:
- no invented statistics
- no contamination language or source-splice phrasing
- no fake habit-timeline claims
- no brief-language leakage into reader-facing prose
