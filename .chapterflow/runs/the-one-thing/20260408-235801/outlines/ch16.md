Chapter Promise:
Show that productive focus needs commitments that protect it, so the reader stops treating follow-through as mood-dependent and starts seeing it as disciplined protection.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between caring about a path and being committed enough to keep that path intact under pressure.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> The Three Commitments as title and core claim
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 3-4 -> commitments as what protect sustained focus and productivity
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 5-6 -> commitment as disciplined protection rather than intensity alone

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: introduce commitment as the next issue after productivity
- P2: explain why productive execution still needs protection
- P3: define commitment as protective discipline
- P4: distinguish commitment from raw stubbornness or intensity theater
- P5: apply commitment logic in work, school, and personal settings
- P6: preserve the limit and bridge to the thieves

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Commitment protects the chosen path and productive focus, but only when it still serves the path rather than hardening into blind intensity.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Commitment can protect the one thing productively, but it can also harden into stubborn intensity that no longer serves the chosen path well.

Threshold Question:
When does commitment protect the one thing productively, and when does it harden into stubborn intensity that no longer serves the chosen path well?
