Chapter Promise:
Show that multitasking is flattering chaos, so the reader stops praising divided attention and starts protecting sequential force.

Opening Move:
Open on the modern pride of handling several things at once, then expose it as switching that weakens every serious task involved.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> multitasking as a false badge of productivity
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 3-4 -> split attention weakens quality and force
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 5-6 -> sequencing creates better output than simultaneous strain

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: surface the appeal and status of multitasking
- P2: name multitasking as a lie rather than a talent
- P3: explain switching and divided attention without fake science padding
- P4: show how errors, shallowness, or residue appear when attention keeps breaking
- P5: apply sequencing to work, school, or personal settings
- P6: preserve the limit about true coordination versus fake simultaneous cognition and bridge to discipline

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Monitoring several streams is different from trying to give full thinking force to multiple demanding tasks at once.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Some environments require interruption management, but that does not turn divided cognition into excellence.

Threshold Question:
When is coordinating multiple inputs part of the job, and when is multitasking just a flattering label for avoidable attention-switching?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: multitasking as status lie
- Takeaway 2: switching cost without unsupported science theater
- Takeaway 3: sequence over split force
- Takeaway 4: work / school / personal applications
- Takeaway 5: limit between real coordination and fake simultaneous thinking

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choosing between uninterrupted drafting and constant message replies
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): realizing fragmented study produced recall gaps because attention kept switching
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): confronting how half-present conversation and errand-handling weakens both
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predicting what happens when a manager keeps three high-load tabs active at once
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing one focused pass on the hardest section instead of juggling every assignment tab
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing split-attention planning with a protected sequence for one important household task

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like unread-count badges, stacked browser tabs, a vibrating phone on a desk, half-highlighted notes, or a grocery list folded beside a ringing device.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what people do after admitting multitasking fails but still assuming success requires a fully disciplined life.

Genericity Risks:
- turning the chapter into anti-phone scolding
- making multitasking sound like a universal moral failure instead of a specific cognitive mistake
- slipping into unsupported brain science language

Forbidden Drift:
- no invented statistics
- no contamination language or source-splice phrasing
- no flattening into generic focus advice
- no brief-language leakage into reader-facing prose
