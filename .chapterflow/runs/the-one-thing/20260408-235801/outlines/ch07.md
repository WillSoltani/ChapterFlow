Chapter Promise:
Show that willpower is not permanently on-call, so the reader stops treating mental force as endlessly available and starts placing important work where strength is most available.

Opening Move:
Open on the heroic fantasy that discipline can be summoned equally at any time, then expose how bad timing quietly weakens serious effort.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> willpower is not permanently on call
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 3-4 -> important work should respect timing and energy
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 5-6 -> discipline fails when it ignores availability of mental force

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: surface the appeal of heroic always-ready willpower
- P2: name endless willpower as the chapter's lie
- P3: explain why timing and freshness matter for hard work
- P4: show the bad-timing tax of placing meaningful work after strength is spent
- P5: apply wise placement to work, school, or personal settings
- P6: preserve the limit against excuse-making and bridge to balance

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Respecting willpower timing is different from waiting passively for ideal feelings.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Timing matters, but it cannot become a polished excuse for avoiding hard effort whenever the moment is imperfect.

Threshold Question:
When does respect for willpower timing become wise placement, and when does it turn into a polished excuse for avoidance?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: lie of endlessly available willpower
- Takeaway 2: timing and cognitive freshness
- Takeaway 3: bad timing tax
- Takeaway 4: work / school / personal applications
- Takeaway 5: limit against excuse-making

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): choosing to put the hardest task in the freshest block instead of after reactive work
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): realizing late-night rescue effort failed because the hard material was always pushed to a weak slot
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): discussing why an important decision should happen before fatigue and scattered obligations pile up
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predicting what happens when a team keeps scheduling hard judgment after a draining day of messages
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): choosing a fresh morning session over another tired late-night promise
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing key planning done while fresh versus after exhaustion sets in

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include details like an early calendar block, a drained evening desk, coffee gone cold beside notes, or a phone full of afternoon messages before the hard task even starts.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what happens when the reader tries to solve timing problems by chasing constant balance instead of accepting tradeoffs.
