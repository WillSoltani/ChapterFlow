Exponential growth can look harmless for longer than intuition expects. At first the increase seems manageable, almost calm, and then the curve starts rising faster than the mind had pictured.

That is the difficulty. People often imagine growth as if the same amount were being added each time, but exponential growth builds on what is already there. The structure is multiplicative, not linear.

This is easy to miss because the early stages do not always look dramatic. The numbers may move, but they still appear small enough to feel ordinary. That quiet beginning can trick judgment into projecting the future as a straight line.

The trouble starts when compounding keeps working while intuition stays linear. A debt balance grows faster than expected. An audience, backlog, or spreading process stays underestimated because the first steps looked modest. A repeated percentage change gets treated like simple addition, and the later acceleration arrives as a surprise. The problem is not that every increase is exponential. The problem is mistaking compounding for straight-line growth.

The practical correction is to test the structure directly. Is each increase building on the last one, or is the same amount just being added? That question forces judgment away from visual guesswork and toward the actual mechanics of change.

The chapter does not ask for fear of every curve. It asks for cleaner recognition. If growth is compounding, a slow-looking beginning may not stay slow for long.
