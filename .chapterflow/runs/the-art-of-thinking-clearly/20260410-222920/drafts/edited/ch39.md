Once one path is taken, the untaken paths start disappearing from view. The road you chose becomes vivid because you are living inside it, while the roads you rejected become abstract, thin, and easy to forget.

That is the distortion behind alternative paths. The chosen direction can start feeling natural, necessary, or obviously sensible not because it truly was the only strong option, but because the competing paths are no longer visible in the same way. Present reality has weight. Abandoned options lose it.

This happens in work, school, and personal life. A job choice feels clearly right because the other roles are no longer being lived. A major or study path feels obviously fitting because the road not taken no longer has daily texture. A purchase, move, or relationship decision starts looking self-evident because the alternatives faded into imagination while the chosen path became concrete.

The chapter does not say the chosen road was wrong. It says the chosen road can borrow false certainty from the disappearance of the others. A good current outcome does not prove the alternatives were poor, and an ordinary present does not prove the abandoned branches were fantasy.

The practical correction is to rebuild the live options that existed at the time. What realistic alternatives were actually on the table? What did those roads look like then, not only now? That reconstruction keeps the chosen path from feeling obviously best just because the others became invisible.

Better judgment remembers that the road you are on is not the only road that could have made a life, a project, or a result feel real.
