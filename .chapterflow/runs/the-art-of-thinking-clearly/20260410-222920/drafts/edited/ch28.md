One vivid case can wipe out a wider pattern fast. A specific person, story, or example arrives with detail and personality, and the mind starts trusting that case more than the general frequency information that should frame it.

That is base-rate neglect. Population-level reality fades because one case feels more concrete than the broader pattern it belongs to. The specific story starts outranking what usually happens.

This is easy to miss because case details feel alive. A broader rate can seem abstract, cold, or generic, while a specific example feels memorable and diagnostic. The story sticks harder than the background frequency.

The trouble starts when the case replaces the pattern. A team overreads one candidate and forgets the usual hiring outcomes. A student or teacher overreacts to one performance and loses sight of the broader distribution. A person hears one medical or social story and treats it as stronger than population-level reality. The problem is not that case details do not matter. The problem is letting them erase the base rate.

The practical correction is to bring the wider pattern back before concluding. What normally happens in the larger population? How representative is this case? Those questions restore context to the vivid example.

The chapter does not ask for ignoring individual details. It asks for cleaner judgment: one case is not the whole pattern. Better judgment makes the background frequency visible before it lets the story dominate the conclusion.
