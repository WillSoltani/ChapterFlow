# Quiz Blueprint: ch19

- chapterId: ch19
- title: Regression to Mean

## Concepts to test

1. regression to the mean means extreme results often soften naturally toward a more typical level
2. people misread praise, punishment, coaching, or policy changes as the cause of the rebound
3. the bias appears when ordinary variation is mistaken for intervention effect
4. correction requires asking what would likely happen after an extreme result even without the recent action
5. comparison against baseline variation helps stop false causal stories

## Question spread

- q01-q03: identify regression to mean in ordinary swings and rebounds
- q04-q06: named-character work or school cases where feedback is overcredited after an extreme result
- q07-q08: distinguish intervention effect from natural softening after unusual outcomes
- q09-q10: deeper transfer on baseline variation and causal restraint

## Distractor policy

- tempting wrong answers should claim the latest praise, punishment, or change fully caused the rebound
- another distractor should overcorrect by claiming interventions never matter
- one distractor can confuse one-off extremes with stable new levels
