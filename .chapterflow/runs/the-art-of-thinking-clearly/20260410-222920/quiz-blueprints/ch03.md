# Quiz Blueprint: ch03

- chapterId: ch03
- title: Clustering Illusion

## Concepts to test

1. random events can bunch without hidden design
2. people wrongly expect randomness to look smooth and evenly spaced
3. clusters become persuasive because the mind wants a cause or story
4. practical fix is to test whether the pattern is truly exceptional before explaining it
5. the illusion appears in work, school, and personal life

## Question spread

- q01-q03: identify clustering illusion in ordinary examples
- q04-q06: named-character work or school cases that overread bunching
- q07-q08: distinguish suspicious evidence from ordinary noisy clustering
- q09-q10: deeper transfer on when explanation is earned versus prematurely imposed

## Distractor policy

- tempting wrong answers should treat visible bunching as automatic proof of intent or cause
- another distractor should overcorrect by claiming no patterns are ever meaningful
- one distractor can confuse emotional salience with statistical significance
