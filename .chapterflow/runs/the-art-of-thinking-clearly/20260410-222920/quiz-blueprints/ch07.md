# Quiz Blueprint: ch07

- chapterId: ch07
- title: Confirmation Bias (Part 1)

## Concepts to test

1. prior conclusions can filter what evidence gets noticed first
2. confirming evidence often feels easier to trust or remember than conflicting evidence
3. the bias starts before full argumentation because attention and search are already tilted
4. the problem appears in work, school, and personal judgments
5. correction requires naming what contrary evidence would count before collecting more support

## Question spread

- q01-q03: identify selective noticing and evidence-filtering in ordinary examples
- q04-q06: named-character work or school cases where a prior view shapes what gets gathered
- q07-q08: distinguish healthy confidence from filtered evidence intake
- q09-q10: deeper transfer on disconfirming-evidence questions and self-correction

## Distractor policy

- tempting wrong answers should treat confirming evidence as sufficient simply because it feels easier to find
- another distractor should overcorrect by claiming people should distrust all prior beliefs
- one distractor can confuse confidence or familiarity with evidence quality
