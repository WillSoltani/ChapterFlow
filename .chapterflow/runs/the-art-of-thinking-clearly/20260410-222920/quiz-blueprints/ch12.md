# Quiz Blueprint: ch12

- chapterId: ch12
- title: The It'll-Get-Worse-Before-It-Gets-Better Fallacy

## Concepts to test

1. worsening conditions are not automatic evidence of future improvement
2. people often overcredit pain because struggle feels meaningful
3. some hard transitions are real, but the key question is whether a mechanism connects the pain to progress
4. the fallacy appears in work, school, and personal life when temporary deterioration gets defended without proof
5. correction requires asking what evidence shows that the present cost is buying a better later outcome

## Question spread

- q01-q03: identify scenes where pain is being mistaken for proof
- q04-q06: named-character work or school cases where worsening gets defended as progress too quickly
- q07-q08: distinguish meaningful short-term cost from empty friction
- q09-q10: deeper transfer on mechanism checks and false progress narratives

## Distractor policy

- tempting wrong answers should assume struggle is automatically productive
- another distractor should overcorrect by claiming hard transitions never matter
- one distractor can confuse emotional commitment with causal evidence
