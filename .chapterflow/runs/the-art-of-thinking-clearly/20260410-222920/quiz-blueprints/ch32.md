# Quiz Blueprint: ch32

- chapterId: ch32
- title: Loss Aversion

## Concepts to test

1. losses often feel heavier than equivalent gains
2. a choice framed as preventing a loss can feel stronger than the same choice framed as pursuing a gain
3. defensive reactions can be exaggerated by loss framing
4. fairer judgment checks whether the tradeoff would look different if stated in gain terms
5. correction requires comparing equivalent gains and losses more symmetrically

## Question spread

- q01-q03: identify loss aversion in ordinary decisions
- q04-q06: named-character work or school cases where loss framing drives the choice
- q07-q08: distinguish prudent caution from overweighting losses
- q09-q10: deeper transfer on reframing and equivalent-stake comparison

## Distractor policy

- tempting wrong answers should treat protecting against a loss as automatically wiser without checking equivalence
- another distractor should overcorrect by claiming losses and gains always feel identical
- one distractor can confuse loss aversion with rational caution based on evidence
