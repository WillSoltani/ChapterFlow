# Quiz Blueprint: ch47

- chapterId: ch47
- title: Self-Selection Bias

## Concepts to test

1. voluntary participation can distort what a sample appears to show
2. loud or visible respondents are often not representative of everyone else
3. strong incentives or strong feelings make some people much more likely to appear
4. silence does not automatically mean satisfaction or agreement
5. correction requires asking who is missing before trusting the sample

## Question spread

- q01-q03: identify self-selection bias in ordinary cases
- q04-q06: work or school feedback examples where volunteers distort the picture
- q07-q08: distinguish useful signal from representative sample
- q09-q10: deeper transfer on incentives, missing groups, and judgment repair

## Distractor policy

- tempting wrong answers should assume visible participants are automatically representative
- another distractor should treat silence as proof that the rest agree
- one distractor can overcorrect by claiming all reviews, surveys, or feedback are worthless
