# Quiz Blueprint: ch11

- chapterId: ch11
- title: Availability Bias

## Concepts to test

1. availability bias means memorable examples feel more common or important than they really are
2. vividness, recency, and emotion make some examples easier to recall
3. easy recall is not the same as representative evidence
4. the bias appears in work, school, and personal judgment when dramatic examples crowd out typical cases
5. correction requires asking what evidence is representative rather than what memory offers first

## Question spread

- q01-q03: identify recall-driven distortions in ordinary scenes
- q04-q06: named-character work or school cases where memorable examples overpower base-rate thinking
- q07-q08: distinguish vividness from actual frequency or importance
- q09-q10: deeper transfer on representative-evidence checks and memory-first judgment

## Distractor policy

- tempting wrong answers should treat easy recall as proof of commonness
- another distractor should overcorrect by claiming memorable examples never matter
- one distractor can confuse emotional intensity with statistical importance
