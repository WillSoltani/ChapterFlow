Chapter: The It'll-Get-Worse-Before-It-Gets-Better Fallacy

Frozen support for this chapter is narrow but sufficient:

- Google Books confirms the chapter heading "The It'll-Get-Worse-Before-It-Gets-Better Fallacy."
- The safest paraphrase from the heading and skeleton is that temporary pain or deterioration can be overread as proof of improvement even when no real causal link exists.
- The official Hachette source-notes PDF confirms the chapter heading and supports keeping the chapter in the family of common decision errors.

Safe paraphrase zone:

- worsening conditions are not automatic evidence of progress
- discomfort can feel meaningful and therefore get mistaken for proof
- a corrective move is to ask what mechanism links the present pain to a better later outcome

Unsupported zone:

- exact quotes
- medical or therapeutic claims about treatment trajectories
- claims that all painful transitions are false or useless
