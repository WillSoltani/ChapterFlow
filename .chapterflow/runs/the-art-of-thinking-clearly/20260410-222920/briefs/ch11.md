## Core idea

Vivid, recent, emotional, or easily recalled examples can crowd out representative evidence, making people judge likelihood and importance by what comes to mind fastest rather than by what is actually typical.

## Required anchors

1. availability bias means easy recall gets mistaken for real frequency or importance
2. vivid and recent examples feel more common or more predictive than they usually are
3. practical correction: ask what evidence is representative rather than only memorable
