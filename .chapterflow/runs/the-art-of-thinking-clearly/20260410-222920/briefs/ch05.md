## Core idea

People keep investing in a bad path because they confuse what has already been spent with what still deserves to be spent.

## Required anchors

1. past time, money, effort, or emotion gets misread as a reason to continue
2. quitting feels like admitting waste, so people protect the past by damaging the future
3. practical correction: decide from present and future value, not from unrecoverable cost

## Allowed support

- The frozen chapter title supports a narrow lost-investment reading across money, projects, relationships, and commitments.
- The official source-notes PDF confirms the chapter heading and supports keeping the chapter inside common decision errors rather than technical finance theory.

## Boundaries

- Stay narrow. Do not turn the chapter into a full accounting lecture.
- Do not imply that quitting is always wise or that persistence is always irrational.
- Do not drift into general regret management or shame psychology without tying it back to sunk-cost logic.

## Chapter target

- Canonical prose should land on one clean practical distinction: past cost is real, but it does not automatically justify future cost.
