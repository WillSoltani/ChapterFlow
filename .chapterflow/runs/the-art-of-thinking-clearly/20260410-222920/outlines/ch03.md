# Outline: ch03

## Governing principle

Randomness does not arrive evenly, so the mind keeps mistaking ordinary clustering for hidden design.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: a streak, cluster, or bunched event looks too neat to be accidental.
2. Mechanism: people expect randomness to look smoother than it really does.
3. Anchor: bunching and streaks trigger false story-making, causal leaps, and warning-sign fantasies.
4. Application: markets, school results, health scares, and social rumors all become more dramatic when coincidence gets overread.
5. Tension: some patterns are real, but pattern hunger makes weak signals look meaningful too early.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks whether the pattern is unusual or merely uncomfortable.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: random events can clump together without a secret cause.
- Medium: the mechanism is false expectation about how randomness should look.
- Hard: explanation should be earned, not triggered automatically by visual bunching.

## Threshold question for hard

What evidence tells you a pattern deserves explanation beyond the simple fact that it feels too neat?

## Example routing

- work: sales or product metrics that appear to reveal a trend too quickly
- school: clusters of grades, absences, or rumors that get overinterpreted
- personal: health scares, neighborhood events, or lucky streaks that invite story-making

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why randomness often looks less random than expected
- implementation plan should force one pause before telling a causal story about a cluster
