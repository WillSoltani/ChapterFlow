# Outline: ch14

## Governing principle

Once the ending is known, the road to it starts looking straighter than it really was.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: after events happen, people start saying they knew it all along.
2. Mechanism: outcome knowledge rewrites memory of how uncertain the situation felt beforehand.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal judgments all become vulnerable when known results start looking inevitable.
4. Application: people flatten live alternatives and inflate how obvious the final outcome was.
5. Tension: learning from outcomes matters, but known results can distort fair evaluation of earlier judgment.
6. Bridge: better judgment reconstructs what was actually known before the event and what other outcomes were possible.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: once you know what happened, it can feel like it should have been obvious.
- Medium: known outcomes distort memory of the uncertainty that existed beforehand.
- Hard: hindsight bias corrupts judgment when after-the-fact certainty rewrites what information and alternatives were live before the event.

## Threshold question for hard

What information and alternatives were genuinely live before the outcome arrived?

## Example routing

- work: postmortems, hiring calls, project forecasts, or strategy reviews where results now look inevitable
- school: exam reviews, historical events, or assignment outcomes that feel obvious only after the answer is known
- personal: relationship judgments, purchases, travel decisions, or family predictions that get rewritten after the result

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why knowing the answer now is not the same as having known it before
- implementation plan should force one before-the-outcome reconstruction whenever someone says it was obvious all along
