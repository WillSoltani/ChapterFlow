# Outline: ch13

## Governing principle

Coherent stories can feel true even when the underlying evidence is thin, partial, or unresolved.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: people relax when events fit a neat explanation, even if the explanation outruns the facts.
2. Mechanism: narrative coherence reduces uncertainty and therefore feels convincing.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal judgments all become vulnerable when a clean story beats a messy reality.
4. Application: a polished explanation can hide missing data, competing causes, or unresolved ambiguity.
5. Tension: stories are useful for understanding, but usefulness is not the same thing as proof.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what facts support the story and what uncertainty the story leaves out.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: a neat story can feel true just because it makes sense.
- Medium: coherent explanations often overpower weaker but more honest uncertainty.
- Hard: story bias corrupts judgment when narrative satisfaction outranks evidence quality.

## Threshold question for hard

What evidence supports this story, and what uncertainty has the story hidden?

## Example routing

- work: polished postmortems, founder myths, product narratives, or market explanations that sound better than the evidence supports
- school: historical explanations, essay theses, or study conclusions that are neat but selectively supported
- personal: relationship narratives, family stories, or self-explanations that flatten messy causes into one clean plot

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why coherence is not proof
- implementation plan should force one evidence-vs-story check after a neat explanation feels especially satisfying
