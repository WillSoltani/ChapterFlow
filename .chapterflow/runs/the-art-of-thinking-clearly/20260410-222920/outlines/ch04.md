# Outline: ch04

## Governing principle

Visible consensus can feel like proof even when the group is mostly copying itself under uncertainty.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: a crowded line, rising opinion, or copied choice starts to feel self-validating.
2. Mechanism: people treat group behavior as evidence because social agreement feels safer than independent judgment.
3. Anchor: imitation under uncertainty can create a feedback loop that looks like knowledge.
4. Application: markets, classrooms, workplaces, and everyday choices all become distorted when popularity gets mistaken for truth.
5. Tension: crowds sometimes do carry real information, but visible agreement alone does not settle the case.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks whether the group is contributing independent evidence or merely repeating a visible cue.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: people copy other people when they are unsure.
- Medium: the mechanism is social imitation plus mistaken evidentiary weight.
- Hard: consensus should be tested for independence before it is promoted into proof.

## Threshold question for hard

Does the crowd know something real, or is the crowd mostly seeing itself and calling that knowledge?

## Example routing

- work: team or market decisions shaped by visible momentum
- school: students copying group answers, opinions, or course choices
- personal: restaurants, trends, or social reactions that feel trustworthy because many people are doing them

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why visible agreement feels stronger than it is
- implementation plan should force one independence check before trusting crowd behavior as evidence
