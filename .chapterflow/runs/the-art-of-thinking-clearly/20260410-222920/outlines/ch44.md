# Outline: ch44

## Governing principle

People often judge harmful omissions more gently than comparable harmful actions because passivity feels less direct.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: doing nothing can feel cleaner than doing something harmful.
2. Mechanism: visible agency increases blame, while omission hides agency behind passivity.
3. Anchor: work choices, school decisions, health tradeoffs, and family situations all show softer judgment toward non-action.
4. Application: people can excuse omission because it feels less like intervention, even when the outcome remains serious.
5. Tension: action and inaction are not always identical, but passivity should not automatically receive moral discount.
6. Bridge: better judgment compares outcomes and responsibilities on the same scale.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: passive harm can feel less blameworthy than active harm.
- Medium: omission can receive a moral discount because agency feels less visible.
- Hard: judgment should test whether the softer reaction comes from outcome difference or from passivity alone.

## Threshold question for hard

Am I judging the omission more gently because it feels less active, or because its outcome is truly different?

## Example routing

- work: failing to warn, not intervening, ignoring a fix, avoiding a difficult call
- school: not helping, not reporting, letting a problem continue
- personal: family choices, health decisions, conflict avoidance, bystander situations

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why omission can feel morally lighter than commission
- implementation plan should force one comparison of action and inaction on the same outcome scale
