# Outline: ch27

## Governing principle

Limited access can inflate perceived value by turning availability itself into a signal of worth.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: the thing looks better the moment it starts feeling harder to get.
2. Mechanism: scarcity creates urgency and turns access pressure into value pressure.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal life all show rarity inflating desire.
4. Application: people chase items, opportunities, or choices because they are scarce rather than because they are good.
5. Tension: constraints can matter, but limited supply should not silently certify quality.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what remains attractive after scarcity pressure is removed.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: rarity can make something feel better than it is.
- Medium: limited access can create urgency that gets mistaken for value.
- Hard: fairer judgment asks whether the interest would survive if access were easy.

## Threshold question for hard

Would I still want this as much if it were abundant and easy to access?

## Example routing

- work: scarce roles, vendor slots, investment windows, or hiring candidates
- school: limited club spots, scarce materials, or exclusive opportunities
- personal: limited editions, flash sales, reservations, and hard-to-get experiences

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why rarity is not proof of quality
- implementation plan should force one step of removing scarcity pressure from the evaluation
