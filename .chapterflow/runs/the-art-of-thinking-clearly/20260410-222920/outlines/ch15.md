# Outline: ch15

## Governing principle

Confidence should answer to evidence, not to the force of the feeling itself.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: people often sound and feel more certain than the facts justify.
2. Mechanism: strong internal certainty gets mistaken for strong external support.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal judgments all suffer when confidence outruns evidence.
4. Application: forecasts, decisions, and explanations get pushed harder than the proof can carry.
5. Tension: confidence can help action, but unearned certainty distorts calibration.
6. Bridge: better judgment checks what evidence truly earns the current level of conviction.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: feeling sure can still mean being wrong.
- Medium: certainty can expand beyond the quality and amount of supporting evidence.
- Hard: overconfidence corrupts judgment when internal conviction outruns what the evidence can honestly justify.

## Threshold question for hard

What evidence actually earns this level of confidence?

## Example routing

- work: forecasts, hiring calls, strategy claims, or project estimates delivered with too much certainty
- school: exam answers, thesis claims, or problem solving where certainty outruns support
- personal: purchases, travel plans, relationships, or health guesses made with more conviction than evidence

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why certainty is not proof
- implementation plan should force one evidence-vs-confidence check whenever a judgment feels especially sure
