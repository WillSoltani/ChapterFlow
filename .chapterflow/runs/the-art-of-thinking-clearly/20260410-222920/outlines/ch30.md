# Outline: ch30

## Governing principle

An early reference point can distort later judgment by setting the range of what feels plausible.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: the first number lands and everything after it starts orbiting around it.
2. Mechanism: the mind adjusts from the starting point instead of building a fresh estimate.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal life all show first figures shaping later judgment.
4. Application: people accept ranges, prices, and estimates that feel reasonable only because the anchor moved the frame.
5. Tension: reference points can be useful, but arbitrary starting numbers should not control the conclusion.
6. Bridge: better judgment makes an independent estimate before reacting to the first figure.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: the first number can influence what later numbers feel like.
- Medium: anchors shift the range of what feels plausible even when they are arbitrary.
- Hard: fairer judgment asks what the estimate would be without the initial number.

## Threshold question for hard

What would I think this number should be if I had never seen the first figure?

## Example routing

- work: budgets, negotiations, targets, offers, and forecasts
- school: grade expectations, score guesses, and estimate ranges
- personal: prices, salaries, listings, discounts, and everyday estimates

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why first figures are not neutral
- implementation plan should force one step of making an estimate before exposure to the anchor
