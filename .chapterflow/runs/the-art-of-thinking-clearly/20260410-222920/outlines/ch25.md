# Outline: ch25

## Governing principle

Groups can become less accurate when the social cost of dissent rises above the perceived cost of a bad decision.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: the room gets quiet and everyone mistakes silence for certainty.
2. Mechanism: belonging pressure and fear of friction make dissent feel unsafe.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal groups all create false consensus when disagreement is costly.
4. Application: bad decisions mature because doubts stay private while the group moves forward.
5. Tension: cohesion matters, but unity without dissent can hide weakness.
6. Bridge: better judgment creates an active path for disagreement before closure hardens.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: silence in a group does not always mean agreement.
- Medium: belonging pressure can suppress honest objections and create false consensus.
- Hard: better group judgment asks what people would say if dissent were safe.

## Threshold question for hard

If disagreement were truly safe here, what would people say that they are not saying now?

## Example routing

- work: meetings, strategy calls, product reviews, or hiring panels with silent doubts
- school: group projects, clubs, or classroom discussions where disagreement gets swallowed
- personal: families, friend groups, or committees that value harmony over honest correction

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why silence is not consensus
- implementation plan should force one step of inviting or protecting dissent before commitment
