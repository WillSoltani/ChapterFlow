# Outline: ch39

## Governing principle

The visible chosen path can feel more natural or correct than it deserves because abandoned alternatives fade from view.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: once one road is taken, the untaken roads start disappearing from mental sight.
2. Mechanism: present reality becomes vivid while alternative branches become abstract.
3. Anchor: career, study, purchase, and relationship choices all invite overconfidence in the road that happened.
4. Application: people judge the present path without reconstructing what realistic alternatives looked like at the time.
5. Tension: one path may still be good, but its goodness is not proved by the invisibility of the others.
6. Bridge: better judgment deliberately rebuilds the lost alternatives before praising the chosen road.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: the chosen road can feel obvious because the other roads are no longer vivid.
- Medium: present reality crowds out realistic memory of the alternatives.
- Hard: evaluation should ask what options actually existed at the time and how plausible they were then.

## Threshold question for hard

What real alternatives existed at the time, and how would this outcome look if one of those roads had been taken instead?

## Example routing

- work: job choice, product direction, hiring decision, project route
- school: course choice, major, study plan, activity path
- personal: move, purchase, relationship choice, timing decision

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why chosen paths feel more natural after alternatives disappear
- implementation plan should force one step of naming at least two realistic paths that were available at decision time
