# Outline: ch35

## Governing principle

In competitive settings, winning can signal that your estimate or bid was too aggressive.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: the win feels like proof, but the win itself may contain the warning.
2. Mechanism: in competition, the highest estimate or most aggressive bid often wins.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal examples all show victory masking a bad deal.
4. Application: people celebrate the outcome before checking what the outcome reveals.
5. Tension: winning matters, but the price and informational meaning of winning matter too.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what the win implies about the underlying estimate.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: winning is not always evidence that the choice was smart.
- Medium: the fact that you were the most aggressive can itself be a clue.
- Hard: fairer judgment treats the win as data about your estimate, not only as a reward.

## Threshold question for hard

If I won, what does that suggest about how my estimate compared with everyone else's?

## Example routing

- work: bids, hiring offers, acquisitions, and negotiations
- school: competitive offers, project choices, and selection contests
- personal: auctions, purchases, and informal competitive decisions

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why winning can itself be a warning sign
- implementation plan should force one step of checking the informational meaning of the win before celebrating it
