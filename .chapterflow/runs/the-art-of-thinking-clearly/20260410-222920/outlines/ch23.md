# Outline: ch23

## Governing principle

Ownership can make an object seem more valuable than it would from a more neutral point of view.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: the moment something becomes mine, parting with it starts feeling unusually expensive.
2. Mechanism: possession changes perception and makes the owned object feel more valuable.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal life all show people overpricing or overprotecting what they already hold.
4. Application: sellers, negotiators, collectors, and ordinary decision-makers demand more because ownership itself is inflating the score.
5. Tension: familiarity and use can matter, but possession should not silently set the price.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what the item would be worth if it were not already owned.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: owning something can make it feel more valuable than it really is.
- Medium: possession changes pricing, reluctance to part, and judgment.
- Hard: fairer valuation asks what the object would be worth from outside ownership.

## Threshold question for hard

If this were not mine already, what would I honestly pay for it now?

## Example routing

- work: teams or founders overvaluing projects, features, or assets they already control
- school: students treating their own notes, ideas, or work as worth more because they made or own them
- personal: sellers, collectors, households, or hobbyists overpricing possessions because letting go feels too costly

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why ownership is not neutral evidence of value
- implementation plan should force one step of pricing or judging from the perspective of a non-owner
