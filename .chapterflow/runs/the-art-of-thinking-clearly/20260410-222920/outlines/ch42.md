# Outline: ch42

## Governing principle

Presentation can redirect judgment even when the underlying facts and options remain the same.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: the same choice can feel different when it is described differently.
2. Mechanism: gain framing, loss framing, labels, and emphasis guide emotion and attention.
3. Anchor: pricing, school feedback, health choices, and personal decisions all shift under wording changes.
4. Application: people react to the frame as if the substance changed when often only the packaging changed.
5. Tension: wording matters because attention and emotion matter, but the facts should still govern the choice.
6. Bridge: better judgment restates the option in another frame before deciding.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: wording can change how a choice feels.
- Medium: different frames can redirect attention without changing the underlying facts.
- Hard: judgment should test whether the preference survives a neutral reframing.

## Threshold question for hard

What remains if I restate the same decision in different wording while keeping the facts constant?

## Example routing

- work: pricing, project proposals, feedback, risk presentations
- school: grading language, assignment choices, club recruitment, teacher feedback
- personal: health choices, spending, relationship talks, household decisions

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why wording can move judgment without changing substance
- implementation plan should force one step of reframing the same choice before committing
