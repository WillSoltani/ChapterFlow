# Outline: ch32

## Governing principle

The pain of losing often exerts more force on judgment than the pleasure of gaining the same amount.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: the mind recoils faster from a possible loss than it leans toward an equal gain.
2. Mechanism: losses feel heavier, so defensive choices can seem automatically wiser.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal life all show people over-weighting what might be lost.
4. Application: equivalent stakes get judged differently depending on whether they are framed as keeping or losing something.
5. Tension: caution can help, but loss-weighting can also block fair tradeoffs.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks whether the loss is being given more psychological weight than an equivalent gain would receive.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: losses often feel bigger than equal gains.
- Medium: framing a choice as a loss can distort the tradeoff.
- Hard: fairer judgment tests whether the asymmetry is coming from evidence or from the heavier emotional weight of loss.

## Threshold question for hard

Would this choice still feel equally compelling if the same stake were framed as a comparable gain instead of a loss?

## Example routing

- work: abandoning weak projects, pricing, negotiation, and defensive planning
- school: grade decisions, course choices, and reluctance to risk a small setback for a larger upside
- personal: selling possessions, switching plans, and avoiding choices that feel like giving something up

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why equal gains and losses can feel unequal
- implementation plan should force one reframing step from loss language to balanced tradeoff language
