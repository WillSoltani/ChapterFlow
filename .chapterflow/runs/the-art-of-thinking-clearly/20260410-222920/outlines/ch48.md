# Chapter Outline: ch48

## Title

Association Bias

## Hook

A person, brand, or idea can feel better or worse simply because it stands next to something admired or despised, even when that borrowed feeling says little about the thing itself.

## Core mechanism

Emotion transfers through linkage. Respect, disgust, excitement, distrust, prestige, or embarrassment can spread from one symbol, person, outcome, or group to something nearby, causing judgment to overcredit or overblame.

## Chapter beats

1. nearby glow or stain changes evaluation before analysis becomes explicit
2. the thing being judged often borrows feeling from a celebrity, team, scandal, success, friend, or label
3. ordinary settings include brands, people, workplaces, social groups, and ideas linked to admired or disliked symbols
4. the chapter is not claiming associations never matter; it warns against treating transferred emotion as earned merit
5. correction: separate the object itself from the emotional residue carried by its associations

## Hard-depth threshold

- Keep this question explicit: what am I actually evaluating, and what nearby glow or stain am I letting do the work?
- Preserve the boundary between relevant context and lazy emotional transfer.
