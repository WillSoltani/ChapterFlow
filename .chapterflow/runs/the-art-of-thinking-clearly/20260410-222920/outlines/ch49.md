# Chapter Outline: ch49

## Title

Beginner's Luck

## Hook

One early win can feel like proof that you naturally understand the game, the market, the test, or the craft, even when the result may say more about luck than about durable skill.

## Core mechanism

An early favorable outcome gets promoted into a theory of ability before repetition has tested it. Randomness, easy initial conditions, weak competition, or good timing can masquerade as stable competence.

## Chapter beats

1. a first success arrives before enough evidence exists to trust a claim of durable skill
2. luck, timing, and low difficulty can create early wins that flatter identity
3. overconfidence grows when one result is treated like a long-term pattern
4. the chapter is not denying real talent; it warns against confusing one outcome with reliable repeatability
5. correction: ask what evidence shows the success can survive repetition, pressure, and less favorable conditions

## Hard-depth threshold

- Keep this question explicit: what evidence shows this success can survive repetition, pressure, and less favorable conditions?
- Preserve the boundary between encouraging evidence and inflated certainty.
