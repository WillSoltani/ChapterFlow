# Outline: ch16

## Governing principle

Fluent delivery can copy the surface of expertise without carrying the structure underneath it.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: people can sound informed long before they can think deeply with the material.
2. Mechanism: repeated performance can imitate expertise and fool both the audience and the performer.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal judgments all overtrust smooth explanation.
4. Application: pressure, follow-up, and transfer expose the gap between script and understanding.
5. Tension: communication matters, but fluency should not be treated as proof of mastery.
6. Bridge: better judgment tests depth instead of rewarding polish alone.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: sounding smart is not the same as understanding.
- Medium: smooth explanation can hide shallow grasp.
- Hard: chauffeur knowledge fails when the script has to survive nuance, pressure, and transfer.

## Threshold question for hard

What happens when the fluent performance has to answer real pressure or transfer?

## Example routing

- work: presentations, strategy talk, client meetings, or hiring interviews where polished fluency outruns understanding
- school: class discussion, oral exams, tutoring, or essay talk where recitation sounds deeper than it is
- personal: podcasts, advice, online debate, or conversation where memorized language gets mistaken for mastery

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why fluent explanation is not proof of understanding
- implementation plan should force one pressure-test question whenever someone sounds smoothly expert
