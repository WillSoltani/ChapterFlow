# Outline: ch29

## Governing principle

People often expect random sequences to balance themselves out even when each event is independent.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: after a streak, the opposite result starts feeling due.
2. Mechanism: the mind expects balance and projects correction onto random events.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal life all show people reading memory into independent outcomes.
4. Application: people change behavior because they think a streak has altered the odds.
5. Tension: patterns can matter in dependent systems, but independent random events do not self-correct on command.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks whether the next event is actually affected by the last ones.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: a random streak does not make the opposite outcome due.
- Medium: independence gets forgotten when the mind expects balance.
- Hard: fairer judgment asks whether the next event has any memory of the previous sequence.

## Threshold question for hard

Does the next event actually know what happened before, or am I imposing balance on randomness?

## Example routing

- work: forecasting from streaks, sales runs, or short random sequences
- school: test guesses, coin flips, classroom games, or grading streak assumptions
- personal: gambling, sports watching, luck beliefs, and ordinary chance events

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why a streak does not create a debt
- implementation plan should force one step of checking independence before inferring correction
