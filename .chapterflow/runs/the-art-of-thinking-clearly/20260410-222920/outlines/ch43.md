# Outline: ch43

## Governing principle

Pressure can make movement feel virtuous even when restraint would be more effective.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: when tension rises, doing something can feel safer than holding still.
2. Mechanism: visibility, anxiety, and responsibility make action feel like control.
3. Anchor: work decisions, school reactions, sports-like pressure, and personal conflicts reward visible movement.
4. Application: people often choose intervention because it relieves discomfort, not because it improves results.
5. Tension: action can be necessary, but motion is not automatically wisdom.
6. Bridge: better judgment tests whether the action changes the outcome or only changes the actor's emotional state.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: movement can feel better than waiting.
- Medium: pressure can make visible intervention feel more responsible than restraint.
- Hard: judgment should separate effective action from action that mainly soothes discomfort.

## Threshold question for hard

Does this action improve the outcome, or does it mainly relieve the discomfort of standing still?

## Example routing

- work: reacting to problems, needless meetings, rushed changes, intervention pressure
- school: overcorrecting study plans, jumping in too fast, reacting to setbacks
- personal: relationship conflicts, investing, parenting, health worries

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why action can feel wiser than it is under pressure
- implementation plan should force one pause to test whether the move changes substance or only reduces discomfort
