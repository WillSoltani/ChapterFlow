# Outline: ch21

## Governing principle

An expanding option set can increase effort, comparison, and regret faster than it increases real benefit.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: more options seem helpful until the choice starts feeling heavier instead of clearer.
2. Mechanism: comparison costs, opportunity cost awareness, and fear of missing a better option rise with the menu.
3. Anchor: shopping, careers, entertainment, tools, and everyday life all show choice sets becoming burdensome.
4. Application: too many options can weaken commitment and reduce satisfaction after the choice.
5. Tension: choice still matters, but more is not always better once overload begins.
6. Bridge: better judgment narrows the field and sets criteria before comparison expands.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: more options can make choosing harder.
- Medium: too many options can increase doubt and reduce satisfaction.
- Hard: extra options should earn their place by improving decisions rather than merely enlarging the menu.

## Threshold question for hard

Is this extra option improving the decision, or only increasing comparison and doubt?

## Example routing

- work: vendor selection, hiring pools, tools, strategies, or career choices where abundance creates friction
- school: course choices, extracurriculars, college options, or study resources that turn helpful menus into overload
- personal: streaming, shopping, dating, food, travel, or housing where option surplus reduces satisfaction

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why more options do not automatically improve the experience of choosing
- implementation plan should force one pre-commitment rule or selection criterion before entering a large menu
