# Outline: ch45

## Governing principle

Outcome stories often bend toward protecting ego rather than describing contribution evenly.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: a good result feels self-made, while a bad result often finds an external excuse.
2. Mechanism: ego protection makes success easier to internalize and failure easier to externalize.
3. Anchor: work performance, school results, relationships, and everyday conflicts all invite asymmetric storytelling.
4. Application: people rewrite outcomes to preserve competence, fairness, or identity.
5. Tension: self-respect matters, but distorted explanation blocks learning.
6. Bridge: better judgment uses the same evidentiary standard for wins and losses.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: people often take more credit for success than blame for failure.
- Medium: outcome interpretation shifts to protect identity.
- Hard: judgment should test whether the explanation stays stable when the result flips.

## Threshold question for hard

Would I use the same explanation if the result had gone the other way?

## Example routing

- work: promotions, missed goals, team success, project failure
- school: grades, preparation, teacher reactions, group work
- personal: arguments, dating, family conflict, money decisions

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why success and failure get explained asymmetrically
- implementation plan should force one comparison of how the same person explains a win versus a loss
