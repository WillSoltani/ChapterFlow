# Outline: ch22

## Governing principle

Affection and dislike can spill beyond feeling and distort evaluations that should be judged on their own merits.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: liking someone can make almost everything around them look better than it is.
2. Mechanism: positive feeling softens scrutiny and negative feeling sharpens it.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal life all show evaluations drifting because of affection or aversion.
4. Application: judgment becomes too generous toward liked targets and too harsh toward disliked ones.
5. Tension: relationships matter, but feeling should not write the whole verdict.
6. Bridge: better judgment separates the object being evaluated from the feeling attached to the source.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: liking can make judgment softer than it should be.
- Medium: affection and dislike spill into evaluations beyond the original feeling.
- Hard: a fair judgment should survive the removal of personal liking or dislike.

## Threshold question for hard

If I removed my feeling toward this person or source, would the judgment still look the same?

## Example routing

- work: colleagues, managers, brands, or clients getting too much trust or criticism because of liking
- school: teachers, classmates, subjects, or schools judged through affection or dislike
- personal: friends, celebrities, products, partners, or media sources getting soft or harsh treatment because of feeling

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why personal liking is not enough to justify a verdict
- implementation plan should force one step of separating source-feeling from object-evaluation
