# Outline: ch24

## Governing principle

Chance events can look rich with meaning because the mind dislikes leaving a striking overlap unexplained.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: two events line up and the mind rushes to make them significant.
2. Mechanism: pattern hunger and selective attention make chance feel too weak as an explanation.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal life all produce overlaps that seem more meaningful than they are.
4. Application: people turn coincidence into omen, signal, warning, proof, or destiny too quickly.
5. Tension: unusual overlap can deserve attention, but surprise alone is not proof of hidden meaning.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks how many opportunities there were for a striking overlap to appear.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: unusual overlap does not automatically mean something deeper is going on.
- Medium: coincidence gets overread because chance feels unsatisfying and the mind wants a pattern.
- Hard: fairer judgment checks surprise against the number of opportunities for overlap.

## Threshold question for hard

How surprising would this overlap be if I remembered how many chances there were for it to happen?

## Example routing

- work: timing overlap, repeated email language, or meeting events treated as hidden intent
- school: matching test items, repeated topics, or social overlaps treated as signs
- personal: birthdays, chance meetings, repeated numbers, or message timing treated as destiny or proof

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why surprise alone is not enough
- implementation plan should force one step of counting opportunities before assigning meaning
