# Outline: ch19

## Governing principle

Extreme outcomes often soften on their own, and the next less-extreme result invites false stories about whatever happened in between.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: after a standout high or low result, the next outcome often looks more ordinary.
2. Mechanism: natural variation makes extremes less likely to repeat immediately at the same intensity.
3. Anchor: praise, punishment, coaching, rituals, and policy changes all get overcredited after swings.
4. Application: people attach the rebound to the most recent intervention instead of to ordinary variation.
5. Tension: interventions can matter, but extreme starting points make false certainty especially easy.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what would likely have happened anyway before claiming success or blame.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: a very high or low result often moves closer to normal next time.
- Medium: natural variation can make feedback or interventions look more powerful than they are.
- Hard: causal claims after an extreme result need comparison against regression before they earn belief.

## Threshold question for hard

Would some of this change likely have happened anyway because the starting point was extreme?

## Example routing

- work: performance reviews, sales swings, bad months, excellent quarters, or turnaround claims after interventions
- school: unusually bad or strong scores followed by praise, punishment, or tutoring claims
- personal: dieting, workouts, illness, parenting, sports, or mood swings where rebound gets overcredited

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why the next ordinary result is not automatic proof that the last intervention worked
- implementation plan should force one comparison between extreme baseline and ordinary variation before judging the result
