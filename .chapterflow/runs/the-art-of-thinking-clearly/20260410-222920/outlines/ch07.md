# Outline: ch07

## Governing principle

Confirmation bias begins when an early conclusion starts filtering which information feels relevant, credible, or worth collecting.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: once a person has a view, matching signals stand out faster than conflicting ones.
2. Mechanism: the mind feels friction around disconfirming evidence and ease around confirming evidence.
3. Anchor: searches, conversations, memory checks, and examples all become selectively tilted toward the existing view.
4. Application: work decisions, school judgments, and personal impressions all become vulnerable when evidence intake is filtered.
5. Tension: confidence can feel like clarity even when the evidence stream has already been biased.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what contrary evidence would count before collecting more support.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: people notice what fits what they already think.
- Medium: prior beliefs influence search, attention, and trust before formal reasoning even starts.
- Hard: selective evidence intake can make a weak conclusion feel stronger because the input stream has already been screened.

## Threshold question for hard

What evidence would I need to take seriously if I wanted to prove myself wrong?

## Example routing

- work: hiring impressions, project beliefs, or strategy views that shape what data gets gathered
- school: study-group judgments, class interpretations, or teacher assumptions that filter examples and evidence
- personal: first impressions, health beliefs, purchases, or relationship narratives that make matching evidence pop out first

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why confirming evidence feels easier to notice
- implementation plan should force one deliberate search for disconfirming evidence before committing to a view
