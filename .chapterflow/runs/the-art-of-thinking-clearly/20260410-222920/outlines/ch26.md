# Outline: ch26

## Governing principle

A striking outcome can dominate judgment even when the underlying likelihood is low.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: the mind fixates on what could happen and forgets to ask how likely it is.
2. Mechanism: vivid fear or excitement makes the scenario feel bigger than its odds.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal life all show reactions driven by outcomes rather than likelihood.
4. Application: people overreact to rare dangers or opportunities because the story is more visible than the probability.
5. Tension: some outcomes matter greatly, but importance should not erase likelihood.
6. Bridge: better judgment puts the odds next to the story before deciding.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: a dramatic outcome is not automatically a likely one.
- Medium: vivid scenarios can crowd probability out of attention.
- Hard: fairer judgment asks what the decision looks like once the actual odds are restored.

## Threshold question for hard

What changes if I put the actual odds beside the story that is grabbing me?

## Example routing

- work: rare risks, unlikely wins, or low-probability threats driving team choices
- school: selective outcomes, unlikely failures, or exaggerated test fears overshadowing actual odds
- personal: travel fear, medical fear, lottery-like hope, or safety choices driven by vivid scenarios

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why vivid is not the same as likely
- implementation plan should force one step of naming probability before reacting
