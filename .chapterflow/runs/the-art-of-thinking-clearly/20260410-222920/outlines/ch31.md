# Outline: ch31

## Governing principle

A few observations can feel like enough evidence to support a much broader rule than they actually justify.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: a small pattern appears and the mind rushes to turn it into a general rule.
2. Mechanism: repeated observations, even a few, feel more stable than they really are.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal life all show people promoting thin samples into broad claims.
4. Application: judgments harden too quickly because a handful of cases start standing in for a full pattern.
5. Tension: patterns matter, but sample size and stability matter too.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks how much evidence the rule is really resting on.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: a few examples are not automatically a reliable rule.
- Medium: thin samples can feel stronger than they are.
- Hard: fairer judgment asks what evidence threshold would justify the broader claim.

## Threshold question for hard

How much evidence would I need before this pattern deserves to become a general claim?

## Example routing

- work: market trends, employee judgments, and team forecasts from thin evidence
- school: study outcomes, teacher impressions, and project conclusions from a few cases
- personal: social impressions, product beliefs, and everyday rules formed from limited experience

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why a pattern is not yet a law
- implementation plan should force one step of checking sample size before generalizing
