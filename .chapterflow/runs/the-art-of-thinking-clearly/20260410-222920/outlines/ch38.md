# Outline: ch38

## Governing principle

One visible strength or flaw can spread into unrelated judgments that it does not deserve to control.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: one impressive or irritating feature starts coloring everything else.
2. Mechanism: a salient cue becomes the lens for unrelated evaluation.
3. Anchor: charisma, polish, beauty, awkwardness, one mistake, or one virtue can distort total judgment.
4. Application: work, school, and personal choices all reward broad impressions built from thin cues.
5. Tension: the original trait may be real, but it does not prove every other positive or negative inference.
6. Bridge: better judgment separates the observed quality from the extra conclusions riding behind it.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: one good or bad trait can unfairly color the rest.
- Medium: salience creates spillover into areas the evidence never directly touched.
- Hard: judgment should ask which claims are truly supported and which are halo spillover.

## Threshold question for hard

What am I inferring here that the original visible trait does not actually prove?

## Example routing

- work: polished presenter, messy coworker, attractive resume, one mistake on a project
- school: popular student, awkward speaker, strong first answer, sloppy first impression
- personal: charming stranger, beautiful product, annoying habit, one visible flaw

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why one feature can distort unrelated judgments
- implementation plan should force one pause to list the specific evidence for each separate claim
