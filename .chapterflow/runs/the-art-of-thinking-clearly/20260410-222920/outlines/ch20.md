# Outline: ch20

## Governing principle

Visible results can overpower sober evaluation of how the decision was actually made.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: people often call a decision smart because it paid off and dumb because it failed.
2. Mechanism: outcomes are vivid, but decision quality is partly hidden inside process, uncertainty, and tradeoffs.
3. Anchor: investments, management calls, school choices, and personal risks all invite result-based overjudgment.
4. Application: luck can flatter weak reasoning and punish careful reasoning.
5. Tension: outcomes matter, but they do not fully settle whether the process was sound.
6. Bridge: better judgment inspects the reasoning, information, and tradeoffs before letting the result write the verdict.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: a good result does not automatically prove a good decision.
- Medium: sound process and visible outcome can come apart under uncertainty.
- Hard: decision quality should be tested against process, not only payoff.

## Threshold question for hard

Would I still judge this decision the same way if the process were identical but the outcome differed?

## Example routing

- work: hiring, strategy, investments, project bets, or management calls that get judged by result alone
- school: study choices, test tactics, admissions bets, or classroom judgments distorted by final score
- personal: health, travel, money, parenting, or relationship decisions that look wise or foolish mostly because of how they ended

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why result quality and decision quality can separate
- implementation plan should force one process-first review before praising or condemning a decision
