# Chapter Outline: ch47

## Title

Self-Selection Bias

## Hook

A page of furious reviews, a room full of enthusiasts, or a survey answered by volunteers can look like clear evidence until you notice that the people who stepped forward were not a random slice of everyone else.

## Core mechanism

Voluntary participation filters the sample before judgment begins. The people with stronger incentives, stronger feelings, more time, more dissatisfaction, or more enthusiasm are more likely to appear. Silence is not neutral evidence of agreement.

## Chapter beats

1. visible participants create a misleading picture when they are treated as representative
2. the distortion comes from who had reason to opt in, respond, complain, rate, or attend
3. ordinary settings include customer reviews, voluntary surveys, workshops, clubs, and internal feedback channels
4. the chapter is not anti-feedback; it warns against treating self-selected voices as the whole population
5. correction: ask who is absent and what incentives shaped participation

## Hard-depth threshold

- Keep this question explicit: what kind of person had a reason to show up here, and who had no reason to appear?
- Preserve the boundary between useful signal and distorted representativeness.
