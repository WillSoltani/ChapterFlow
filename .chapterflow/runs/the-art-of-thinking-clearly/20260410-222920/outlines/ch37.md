# Outline: ch37

## Governing principle

Sequence and correlation often get upgraded into causal certainty too quickly.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: something good or bad happens after an action and the mind declares a cause.
2. Mechanism: timing and repetition create a story even when proof is weak.
3. Anchor: rituals, habits, treatments, luck, and performance patterns all invite causal fantasy.
4. Application: work, school, and personal life reward confident explanations even when evidence is thin.
5. Tension: causes do exist, but pattern alone is not proof.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what evidence separates coincidence, correlation, and real cause.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: after does not automatically mean because of.
- Medium: repeated association can still be misleading.
- Hard: causal claims should survive alternative explanations and stronger evidence tests.

## Threshold question for hard

What evidence would show that this factor is actually causing the outcome rather than merely appearing alongside it?

## Example routing

- work: sales bump after a meeting, productivity after a tool change, hiring outcomes
- school: study habit linked to grade swings, classroom rituals, performance streaks
- personal: lucky rituals, supplements, weather, routines, and health claims

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why timing alone is weak proof of cause
- implementation plan should force one pause to name alternative explanations before accepting a causal story
