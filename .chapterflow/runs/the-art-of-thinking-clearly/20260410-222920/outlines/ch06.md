# Outline: ch06

## Governing principle

Reciprocity can distort judgment when a received favor gets mistaken for a binding reason to say yes.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: a free sample, small favor, useful introduction, or kind gesture creates pressure to give something back.
2. Mechanism: repayment instinct makes refusal feel rude even when the requested return is not wise.
3. Anchor: gifts, concessions, help, and attention can all create silent obligation.
4. Application: sales, work, school, and personal relationships all become vulnerable when gratitude slides into compliance.
5. Tension: reciprocity can support healthy social life, but that does not make every requested return legitimate.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks whether the return response still makes sense without the favor in the frame.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: people often say yes because receiving something first feels binding.
- Medium: the mechanism is social obligation plus discomfort with seeming ungrateful.
- Hard: a favor can create emotional debt without creating a sound reason for compliance.

## Threshold question for hard

If the favor had not happened, would I still agree to this response?

## Example routing

- work: favors, introductions, flexibility, or concessions that create leverage in meetings or negotiations
- school: help, notes, inclusion, or social gestures that pressure agreement
- personal: gifts, samples, invitations, or kindness that make boundaries feel rude

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why receiving first can distort later judgment
- implementation plan should force one no-favor counterfactual before agreeing to a requested return
