# Outline: ch08

## Governing principle

Confirmation bias deepens when an early social label starts deciding which traits, behaviors, and stories feel representative of a person or group.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: once a label lands, confirming behavior becomes easier to spot than conflicting behavior.
2. Mechanism: stereotypes feel durable because the mind keeps treating matching evidence as typical and mismatching evidence as exceptional.
3. Anchor: work judgments, school judgments, and personal impressions can all turn into selective interpretation systems.
4. Application: people get sorted too quickly into categories that then guide memory, trust, and prediction.
5. Tension: the label feels stable precisely because the mind keeps feeding it matching examples.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what individuating evidence would weaken the label before using it again.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: labels make people notice what fits the label.
- Medium: stereotypes survive because matching behavior feels typical while conflicting behavior gets downgraded.
- Hard: social confirmation bias is powerful because labels do not merely summarize people; they start organizing what future evidence seems to mean.

## Threshold question for hard

What would I have to see, remember, or count as meaningful before I would loosen this label?

## Example routing

- work: a manager sorting coworkers into dependable or difficult categories and then reading new behavior through the label
- school: a teacher or student deciding who is talented, lazy, disruptive, or serious and then filtering later signals
- personal: first impressions, neighborhood judgments, friend-group assumptions, or dating narratives that turn quick labels into sticky stories

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why contradictory behavior often gets treated as an exception
- implementation plan should force one individuating-evidence check before repeating a social label
