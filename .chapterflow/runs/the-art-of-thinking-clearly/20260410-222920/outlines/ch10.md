# Outline: ch10

## Governing principle

Contrast effect begins when the thing nearby becomes the measuring stick and distorts how the current target feels.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: the same thing can look different depending on what came right before it.
2. Mechanism: the mind judges relatively, not absolutely, when a comparison frame is fresh.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal decisions all become vulnerable to nearby contrasts.
4. Application: prices, performances, rooms, candidates, grades, and options can look better or worse because of sequence.
5. Tension: the target itself may not have changed at all.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what the item looks like without the contrast frame.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: what you saw first changes how the next thing feels.
- Medium: immediate comparisons can distort perceived value, quality, and difficulty.
- Hard: contrast effect corrupts judgment when sequence and framing change evaluation more than the target's actual properties do.

## Threshold question for hard

If I saw this on its own, away from the comparison frame, would I judge it the same way?

## Example routing

- work: candidate interviews, proposal reviews, pricing, performance ratings, or vendor comparisons
- school: grading, presentation order, examples in class, or assignment difficulty judgments
- personal: shopping, apartments, restaurants, dating profiles, rooms, or everyday choice architecture

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask what changed when the comparison frame changed
- implementation plan should force one isolated re-check after a strong contrast sequence
