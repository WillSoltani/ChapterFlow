# Outline: ch05

## Governing principle

Past investment can trap judgment when unrecoverable cost gets treated like a reason to keep paying.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: a bad movie, failing project, or expensive purchase keeps pulling more time because leaving now feels wasteful.
2. Mechanism: people protect past spending by continuing, even when continuation makes the total loss worse.
3. Anchor: time, money, effort, and pride can all be converted into fake reasons to stay committed.
4. Application: work, school, and personal life all suffer when previous investment outranks present evidence.
5. Tension: persistence can sometimes be wise, but not because the cost is already sunk.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what choice makes sense from here if the past cannot be recovered.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: people keep going because they hate wasting what they already spent.
- Medium: the mechanism is loss-avoidance plus identity-protection masquerading as rational continuation.
- Hard: the right decision must be made from current expected value, not from emotional attachment to prior investment.

## Threshold question for hard

If I were not already invested, would I still choose this path from here?

## Example routing

- work: teams or managers continuing weak projects because too much has already gone in
- school: students staying in courses or study paths for sunk effort alone
- personal: subscriptions, purchases, events, relationships, or hobbies kept alive by past investment

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why past cost feels like forward evidence
- implementation plan should force one restart-style question before adding more time, money, or effort
