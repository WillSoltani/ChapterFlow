# Outline: ch17

## Governing principle

More activity around an uncertain event can create a stronger feeling of control without changing the role of chance.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: people feel more in control when they are touching, choosing, or customizing an uncertain process.
2. Mechanism: action and ritual create agency-feeling even when the outcome remains mostly random.
3. Anchor: betting, investing, planning, games, and everyday choices all reward false ownership over chance.
4. Application: people overestimate what their effort, timing, or ritual can actually change.
5. Tension: some inputs are controllable, but the result is often less controllable than it feels.
6. Bridge: better judgment separates controllable actions from uncontrollable outcomes.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: being involved can feel like being in control.
- Medium: action and ritual can make chance outcomes feel owned.
- Hard: real control must be separated from emotional ownership and random variance.

## Threshold question for hard

What part of this outcome is actually under my control, and what part is still chance?

## Example routing

- work: forecasting, investing, sales pipelines, project timing, or dashboard monitoring that creates false control over uncertain outcomes
- school: test rituals, study superstitions, or performance routines that get overcredited for partly random results
- personal: gambling, sports viewing, parenting, health routines, travel timing, or shopping behavior where involvement feels causal

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why activity is not the same as control
- implementation plan should force one explicit split between controllable inputs and uncontrollable outcomes
