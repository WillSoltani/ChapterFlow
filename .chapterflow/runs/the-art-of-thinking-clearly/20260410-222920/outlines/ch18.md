# Outline: ch18

## Governing principle

Payoff structures can change judgment more deeply than people admit, because incentives reshape not only action but also attention and explanation.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: people often explain behavior by personality or values before checking what the system rewards.
2. Mechanism: incentives pull attention, soften resistance, and make certain choices feel normal or reasonable.
3. Anchor: work, school, and personal systems all create distorted responses when rewards are misaligned.
4. Application: people undercount how payoffs reshape what gets noticed, defended, or ignored.
5. Tension: character still matters, but reward structures can overpower stated intentions.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what conduct the system is paying for before trusting the surface explanation.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: rewards can pull behavior more than people notice.
- Medium: incentives reshape what people look at, believe, and justify.
- Hard: misread behavior becomes clearer when the reward structure is mapped before motives are moralized.

## Threshold question for hard

What is this system rewarding, and how might that reward be reshaping what people notice or defend?

## Example routing

- work: sales targets, metrics, bonus plans, promotion ladders, or reporting systems that distort behavior
- school: grades, participation points, rankings, or admissions incentives that bend learning and presentation
- personal: family approval, social status, online attention, discounts, or loyalty rewards that reshape choice

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why visible behavior can be misleading when incentives are hidden
- implementation plan should force one incentive-map question before judging conduct or policy
