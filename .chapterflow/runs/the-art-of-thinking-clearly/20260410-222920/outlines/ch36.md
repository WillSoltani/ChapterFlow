# Outline: ch36

## Governing principle

People overread character and underread context when explaining behavior.

## Canonical prose movement

1. Hook: a person behaves badly or strangely and judgment jumps straight to character.
2. Mechanism: the visible actor is salient while the situation is partly hidden.
3. Anchor: pressure, incentives, fatigue, stress, and setting can shape conduct more than observers admit.
4. Application: work, school, and personal life all reward fast moral stories about other people.
5. Tension: character matters sometimes, but context should be examined before traits are declared.
6. Bridge: better judgment asks what the situation is doing before naming the person.

## Depth intentions

- Easy: behavior is often judged too personally and not situationally enough.
- Medium: observers see the actor clearly and the pressure around the actor poorly.
- Hard: trait judgments should be delayed until context has been actively checked.

## Threshold question for hard

What in the situation could be shaping this behavior before I decide what kind of person this is?

## Example routing

- work: rude email, missed deadline, poor meeting behavior, harsh manager
- school: class participation, lateness, assignment quality, peer conflict
- personal: customer service, driving, family tension, stranger behavior

## Reinforcement surfaces

- recap retrieve should ask why people default to character explanations
- implementation plan should force one pause to inspect pressure, incentives, or context before judging the person
