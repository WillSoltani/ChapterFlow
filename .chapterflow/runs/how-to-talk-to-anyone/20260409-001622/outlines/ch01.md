Chapter Promise:
This chapter will make the reader see that first impressions are built from visible attention and composure before conversation content can rescue them.

Opening Move:
Open on the silent moment of entering a room and being read before a sentence is finished.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P5
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: make the reader feel the speed of the first read before speech
- P2: use `The Flooding Smile` to distinguish personal warmth from automatic smiling
- P3: use `Sticky Eyes` and `Epoxy Eyes` to show how attention makes people feel seen
- P4: use `The Big Baby Pivot`, `Hello Old Friend`, and `Limit the Fidget` to explain why full-body attention and stillness read as substance
- P5: use `Watch the Scene Before You Make the Scene` to show why social awareness matters as much as warmth
- P6: preserve the limit that performance without judgment feels fake, then bridge to the problem of what happens after hello

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is not confidence. It is person-directed attention plus composure. The social effect comes from making someone feel genuinely noticed while also looking settled enough not to demand the room's reassurance.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the line between intentional presence and visible performance. Hard depth should ask when deliberate first-impression control starts becoming too obvious.

Threshold Question:
How do you make a fast first impression feel personal instead of rehearsed?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: first impressions start before content
- Takeaway 2: warmth works only when it feels directed
- Takeaway 3: attention is visible through eyes and orientation
- Takeaway 4: stillness signals more authority than nervous motion
- Takeaway 5: room-reading prevents good tactics from landing badly

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): teaches choosing presence over rushed self-display when entering a meeting
- Scenario 2 (dialogue, school): teaches greeting a professor or student leader with warm steadiness instead of over-talking
- Scenario 3 (dilemma, personal): teaches how too much eagerness can flatten intrigue on a date or at a gathering
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): teaches why scattered attention weakens first impressions even when the words are correct
- Scenario 5 (before_after, school): teaches the difference between fidgety entrance energy and composed arrival
- Scenario 6 (postmortem, personal): teaches how failing to read the room can cancel otherwise good warmth

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, include one detail from the physical situation: paper coffee cup, lanyard at a campus mixer, conference-room screen glowing blue, coat half on at a doorway, music already loud in a crowded apartment.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what keeps initial interest alive once the first-contact moment is over. It must not simply summarize the next chapter.

Genericity Risks:
- drifting into generic body-language advice
- treating eye contact as the entire chapter
- making stillness sound like dominance theater

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: "be yourself and people will feel it"
- filler move 2: "confidence is silent" as a substitute for mechanism
- invented authority move 1: unsupported science or psychology claims
- brief-language leak 1: repeating "person-directed attention" until it sounds like notes instead of prose
