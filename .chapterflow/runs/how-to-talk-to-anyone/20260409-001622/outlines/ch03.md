This chapter will make the reader hear how status leaks through phrasing, restraint, and receiver-awareness rather than through bragging.

Opening Move:
Open on the strange moment when a conversation is already alive, yet a few clumsy words can still make someone sound smaller, hungrier, or less polished than they really are.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: name the real problem: people often lower their own status when they start describing themselves
- P2: use What Do You Do-Not! and The Nutshell Resume to show how compact framing sounds more substantial than verbal sprawl
- P3: use Personal Thesaurus, anti-cliche, Jawsmith's Jive, and Call a Spade a Spade to show that precise wording signals control
- P4: use Kill the Quick "Me, Too!", Comm-YOU-nication, and It's the Receiver's Ball to show that status is also heard in who gets the conversational center
- P5: use Big Shots Don't Slobber and Never the Naked Thank You to show how neediness leaks around prestige, praise, and gratitude
- P6: preserve the limit that polish can become performance, then bridge to the next chapter's question about entering unfamiliar groups

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is verbal status signaling. People sound more substantial when their phrasing is compressed, specific, receiver-aware, and free of visible approval-seeking.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve the line between composed phrasing and managed affectation. Hard depth should ask when polish stops sounding natural and starts sounding staged.

Threshold Question:
What makes polished speech feel grounded instead of thirsty for status points?
