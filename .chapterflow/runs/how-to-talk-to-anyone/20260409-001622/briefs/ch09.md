Core Claim:
This chapter argues that social intelligence matters most when people feel exposed, emotional, indebted, status-sensitive, or cornered. The named techniques in this part all point toward one larger mechanism: tact preserves connection by protecting face, reading motives carefully, pacing reciprocity, and giving people dignified ways to recover under pressure.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's high-stakes tact chapter. Its distinct job is to close the book by showing how social skill works when embarrassment, power, hidden motives, and emotional charge make ordinary friendliness insufficient.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter shows that tact often begins by covering embarrassment, lending verbal rescue, and refusing to spotlight another person's social wound.
Then it shows that buried motives, favors, reciprocity, and emotional unloading have to be read with patience instead of naivete or aggression.
Then it shows that escape hatches, boss-facing tact, listener leadership, and long-scorecard thinking turn short-term friction into safer long-term relationships.
