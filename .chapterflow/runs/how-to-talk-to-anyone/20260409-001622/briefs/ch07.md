Core Claim:
This chapter argues that phone communication succeeds when voice, timing, naming, and message craft replace the facial cues missing from the line. The named techniques in this part all point toward one larger mechanism: people feel more valued on the phone when they hear warm, specific attention rather than abrupt access or distracted efficiency.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's phone chapter. Its distinct job is to show how social intelligence survives when all you have left is voice, pacing, and a short verbal frame.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter shows that phone warmth depends on sounding physically engaged and personally oriented rather than flat or rushed.
Then it shows that line context matters: name use, the other person's time, competing interruptions, and household or work realities all change how a call feels.
Then it shows that voicemail, openings, and quick recaps can create or waste trust when the phone removes visual reassurance.
