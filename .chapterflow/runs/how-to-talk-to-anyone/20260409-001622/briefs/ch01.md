Core Claim:
The opening package chapter argues that first impressions begin before content does. People start reading attention, warmth, stillness, and social awareness before they have enough words to judge intelligence or character directly. The named techniques in this part point to one chapter-level mechanism: visible respect and composure make others feel recognized quickly, while scattered attention and anxious motion make you look less substantial before you have even spoken. The chapter's job is to show that intrigue is not loudness or performance. It is disciplined first-contact presence.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's nonverbal opening chapter. Its distinct job is to explain how smile timing, eye contact, full-body attention, fidget control, and room-reading create social position before conversation content has a chance to help you.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter frames first impression as a very fast judgment window.
Then it groups several named techniques around one idea: other people read whether your attention feels personal, calm, and deliberate.
Then it shows that room-reading and self-control matter because you are always entering an already active social scene, not a blank stage.

Required Anchors:
- `The Flooding Smile` as the contrast between instant-display smiling and slower, person-directed warmth
- `Sticky Eyes` and `Epoxy Eyes` as the claim that sustained gaze and attention make people feel seen
- `The Big Baby Pivot`, `Hello Old Friend`, and `Limit the Fidget` as the cluster showing full-body attention and composure
- `Watch the Scene Before You Make the Scene` as the boundary on rushing into a room without reading it
