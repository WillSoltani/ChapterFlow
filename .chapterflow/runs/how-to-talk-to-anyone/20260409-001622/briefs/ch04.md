Core Claim:
This chapter argues that outsiders stop feeling like outsiders when they learn enough of a group's language, hot-button topics, publications, and customs to ask intelligent questions and follow the live thread. The named techniques in this part all point toward one larger mechanism: belonging often starts with literacy, not charisma. People gain traction when they stop demanding that insiders translate everything from zero and start arriving with enough preparation to join the conversation honestly.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's insider-entry chapter. Its distinct job is to show how preparation and curiosity let someone enter an unfamiliar crowd without pretending to already be an expert.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter shows that feeling lost in a crowd is often a language problem before it is a confidence problem.
Then it shows that vocabulary, hot-button topics, and group reading habits reveal what insiders care about.
Then it shows that customs and limited bluffing only work when they help you ask better questions rather than fake mastery.

- `Scramble Therapy` as the rule that exposure and pattern collection reduce outsider panic

- `Bluffing for Bargains` as the narrow limit case where partial fluency helps you negotiate entry, but only if it does not become false expertise
