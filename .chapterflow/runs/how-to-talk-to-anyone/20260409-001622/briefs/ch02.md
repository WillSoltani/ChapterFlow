Core Claim:
This chapter argues that good conversationalists do not rely on constant originality. They keep talk alive by reading mood, noticing context, giving richer openings, and helping the other person expand what is already there. The named techniques in this part all point toward one larger mechanism: conversations stall when each person is forced to generate too much from nothing, and they flow when one person keeps providing usable social fuel. The chapter's job is to show that follow-up is built from attention and scaffolding, not from being naturally witty on command.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's conversation-continuation chapter. Its distinct job is to explain how to carry the interaction after the first-contact signals have already worked.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter shows that strong conversation starts with matching the room's mood instead of barging in at a fixed volume.
Then it shows that context objects, richer introductions, and listening for repeated words all create easy openings.
Then it shows that spotlighting, parroting, and encore-style follow-ups keep the other person supplied with reasons to continue.

Required Anchors:
- `The Mood Match` as the rule that the conversation has to start where the other person's energy already is
- `Always Wear a Whatzit`, `Whoozat?`, and the `Never the Naked...` cluster as the idea that context and richer setup give people something to respond to
- `Be a Word Detective`, `The Swivelling Spotlight`, `Parroting`, and `Encore!` as the listening-and-expansion engine that keeps talk moving
- `Ac-cen-tu-ate the Pos-i-tive` and `The Latest News... Don't Leave Home Without It` as reminders that conversational fuel can be prepared rather than improvised from panic
