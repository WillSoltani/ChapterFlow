Core Claim:
This chapter argues that status is often heard before it is proven. People sound more substantial not mainly by bragging, but by answering identity questions cleanly, choosing sharper words, resisting needy self-display, and keeping the conversation aimed at the receiver rather than at their own validation. The named techniques in this part all point toward one larger mechanism: verbal polish works when it reduces visible hunger and increases precision, restraint, and receiver-awareness.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's verbal-status chapter. Its distinct job is to show how phrasing and conversational discipline make someone sound more polished after the interaction is already moving.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter shows that weak self-description often sounds overeager or shapeless, and that concise framing raises perceived substance.
Then it shows that vocabulary choice, cliché avoidance, and concrete phrasing make speech sound more exact and less generic.
Then it shows that status is also revealed by social restraint: not lunging for false sameness, not slobbering over important people, and not making gratitude or teasing sound tacky.

- `What Do You Do-Not!` and `The Nutshell Resume` as the rule that self-description should be compact and shaped, not rambling or identity-starved
