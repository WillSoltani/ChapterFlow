Core Claim:
This chapter argues that praise becomes powerful when it sounds specific, believable, and identity-level, while flattery fails when it sounds like motive leaking through language. The named techniques in this part all point toward one larger mechanism: recognition changes people when it feels observed and socially credible, not when it feels oily or strategically obvious.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's praise chapter. Its distinct job is to separate genuine, elevating recognition from approval-seeking compliment behavior.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter shows that praise works best when it travels through people, context, and implication instead of blunt self-serving delivery.
Then it shows that killer compliments, little strokes, and knee-jerk reactions carry power when they feel specific and naturally timed.
Then it shows that boomeranging and the tombstone game reveal the deepest level of praise: naming lasting traits or legacies rather than cheap surface approval.
