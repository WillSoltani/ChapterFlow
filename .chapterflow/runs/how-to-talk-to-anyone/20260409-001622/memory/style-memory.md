# Style Memory — How to Talk to Anyone

Operating cheat sheet for writer, editor, and converter. Distilled from the v13 pack style files plus this run's frozen-source limits.

## Voice
Specific, socially observant, and practical. The prose should sound like a sharp human explainer translating tactical people-skills into concrete social reads, not like a generic confidence coach.

## Book-specific posture
- This book is built from many small social moves rather than one grand theory.
- Keep the focus on notice, timing, framing, and tact in real interactions.
- Use the named techniques as anchors, but synthesize them into a chapter-level argument instead of listing tips.
- The book is practical and performative, but it should not drift into manipulative coldness.

## Non-negotiables
- Use only claims supported by the brief and chapter source sidecar.
- No em dashes.
- No invented studies, page claims, celebrity anecdotes, or outcomes the frozen bundle does not support.
- No raw source splice into reader-facing prose.
- No contamination phrases, prompt leakage, or validator language.

## Banned drift
- Do not turn the book into generic networking advice.
- Do not make every chapter about "confidence" in the abstract.
- Do not flatten the techniques into shallow charisma clichés.
- Do not endorse manipulative behavior when strategic tact is the narrower claim.
- Do not write like a dating coach or sales script unless the chapter brief directly supports that narrow use.

## Depth contract
- Easy: one pictureable social frame, one core distinction, one practical implication.
- Medium: mechanism, social friction, and applied judgment.
- Hard: preserve a real limit, tradeoff, or boundary condition about when the move helps and when it backfires.

## Tone differentiation
- Gentle: lowers defensiveness and shows the human need underneath the tactic.
- Direct: names the social mechanism clearly and economically.
- Competitive: sharpens stakes, hierarchy, and missed-opportunity cost without sounding cruel.

## Book-specific craft checks
- Part 1 must stay nonverbal and impression-based rather than drifting into general confidence talk.
- Part 2 must stay on conversational recovery and small-talk movement, not broad relationship advice.
- Part 3 must preserve status signaling, verbal framing, and polish as distinct from basic friendliness.
- Part 4 and Part 5 must distinguish insider fluency from rapport mirroring.
- Parts 6 through 9 must keep praise, phone behavior, room-work, and difficult social situations distinct rather than blending into one etiquette bucket.
