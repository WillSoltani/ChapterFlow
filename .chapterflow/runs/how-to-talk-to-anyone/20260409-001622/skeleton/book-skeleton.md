## Chapter order and one-line intent
1. `How to Intrigue Everyone Without Saying a Word: You Only Have Ten Seconds to Show You're a Somebody`
   Establish that people start reading you before you speak, and show how visible attention, stillness, and first-contact warmth create instant social position.
2. `How to Know What to Say After You Say "Hi"`
   Show how good conversationalists move from greeting to momentum by reading mood, offering hooks, listening for openings, and keeping the other person supplied with response material.
3. `How to Talk Like a VIP`
   Explain how verbal framing, conversational restraint, and polished status signals make someone sound more substantial without resorting to bragging.
4. `How to Be an Insider in Any Crowd: What Are They All Talking About?`
   Show how outsiders gain traction by learning just enough language, topics, and norms to ask smart questions and enter unfamiliar groups without faking expertise.
5. `How to Sound Like You're Peas in a Pod: "Why, We're Just Alike!"`
   Show how rapport grows when people feel mirrored in rhythm, language, and outlook, while keeping the line between resonance and artificial imitation visible.
6. `How to Differentiate the Power of Praise from the Folly of Flattery`
   Show how praise becomes powerful only when it lands as specific, sincere, identity-level recognition rather than oily approval-seeking.
7. `How to Direct Dial Their Hearts`
   Translate the book's social intelligence into phone communication, where voice, timing, naming, and message craft have to replace missing facial cues.
8. `How to Work a Party Like a Politician Works a Room: The Politician's Six-Point Party Checklist`
   Show how strong room-workers manage entrances, approach choices, openness, recall, and follow-through so a crowded room becomes a field of intentional connections.
9. `How to Break the Most Treacherous Glass Ceiling of All: Sometimes People Are Tigers`
   Close on the book's highest-stakes tact: saving face, handling embarrassment, reading hidden motives, and surviving socially dangerous moments without making them worse.
