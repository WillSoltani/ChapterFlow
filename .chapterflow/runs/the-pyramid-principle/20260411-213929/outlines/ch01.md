Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that the pyramid is a way to spare readers from organizing raw detail for themselves and a way to force the writer to discover the real point before drafting.

Opening Move:
Open on the familiar pain of a long report or announcement that keeps pouring out information without revealing the answer, then pivot to Minto discovering that the real problem was the thinking behind the language.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2, the buried-point report and Minto editing reports at McKinsey
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4, pyramid hierarchy and the three logical rules
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5, grocery-list grouping as the concrete memory-and-clarity example

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: anchor, stage the reader burden of buried conclusions
- P2: mechanism, show Minto diagnosing thinking before language
- P3: mechanism, define the pyramid as a summary hierarchy with ordered groups
- P4: implication, explain why top-down delivery respects reader attention
- P5: anchor, use grouped-list example to make the value concrete
- P6: tension, reconcile bottom-up thinking with top-down communication
- P7: bridge, set up the need to understand the substructures inside the pyramid

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The non-generic distinction is between how writers often discover ideas and how readers need to receive them: bottom-up synthesis may produce the answer, but communication should move top-down once the answer is earned.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must show that answer-first communication is only as strong as the grouping beneath it. A vague top point or bad grouping turns clarity theater into confusion with a heading.

Threshold Question:
How do you know when you have earned the right to lead with the answer instead of merely front-loading an untested opinion?
