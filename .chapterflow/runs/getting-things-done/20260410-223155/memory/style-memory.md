# Style Memory

- Teach the idea directly. Reader-facing prose should almost never narrate "the chapter" or sound like notes to a writer.
- Prioritize specificity, concrete detail, distinct paragraph jobs, and one memorable line every few paragraphs.
- Open from pressure, conflict, event, or a live question, not a thesis announcement.
- Stay paraphrase-first and source-bounded. If the brief is thin, stay narrow instead of inventing.
- Avoid generic self-help tone, filler mechanism language, corporate polish, motivational-poster cadence, pseudo-science, and canned transitions.
- No em dashes. No made-up quotes. No unsupported studies or mechanisms.
- Easy must feel accessible, concrete, and clean. Medium must explain mechanism plus application. Hard must add real tension, limit, contradiction, cost, or synthesis.
- Tone variation must be functional, not adjective swaps. Competitive must feel credible, not theatrical.
- Reinforcement surfaces must all do different jobs. No recap replay, review-card echo, repeated suffixes, or repeated stems.
