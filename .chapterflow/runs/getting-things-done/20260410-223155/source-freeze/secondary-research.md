# Secondary Research Bundle

These sources are supporting interpretation only. They do not override the locked edition metadata or the frozen chapter map.

## Overview

Source: SuperSummary overview

- Frames the book as a stress-reduction and productivity system built around trusted external capture.
- Emphasizes the bottom-up approach, front-end thinking, and the psychological benefit of reducing open loops.
- Confirms the core GTD workflow vocabulary used throughout the book: capture, clarify, organize, reflect, engage.

## Part 1 support

Source: SuperSummary Part 1, Chapters 1-3

- Chapter 1 frames modern knowledge work as boundaryless overload and introduces open loops, next actions, and "mind like water."
- Chapter 2 presents the five stages of workflow mastery and the two-minute rule, delegation, deferment, and review logic.
- Chapter 3 develops the five phases of project planning: purpose, outcome visioning, brainstorming, organizing, and next actions.

## Part 2 support

Sources: SuperSummary Part 2, Chapters 4-6 and Part 2, Chapters 7-10

- Chapter 4 focuses on setting up space, tools, and a trusted operating environment.
- Chapter 5 details capture as a full sweep of physical and mental incompletes.
- Chapter 6 explains clarifying one item at a time and separating actionable from non-actionable inputs.
- Chapter 7 centers on discrete buckets and organizational categories.
- Chapter 8 makes the Weekly Review load-bearing.
- Chapter 9 explains choosing actions through context, time, energy, and priority, with the six horizons above the ground level.
- Chapter 10 returns to project control and creative planning once the ground game is under control.

## Part 3 support

Sources: SuperSummary Part 3, Chapters 11-12 and Part 3, Chapters 13-15

- Chapter 11 argues that the capture habit reduces psychic drag by clearing commitments out of the head.
- Chapter 12 argues that deciding the next action changes how people move through work and commitments.
- Chapter 13 emphasizes outcome focusing as a force multiplier when paired with practical workflow control.
- The final movement extends GTD from personal habit to organizational practice and front-end thinking.
