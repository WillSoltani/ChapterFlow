Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader understands the five-stage workflow and why work feels more controllable once each input is clarified before action choice.

Opening Move:
Open on the common mistake of trying to decide what to do while everything is still mixed together in one pile.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the chaos of mixed inputs and premature decision-making
- P2: introduce the five stages as a sequence
- P3: explain capture and clarify as separate jobs
- P4: explain actionable sorting: do, delegate, defer
- P5: explain non-actionable sorting: trash, incubate, reference
- P6: explain reflect and Weekly Review as system maintenance
- P7 if needed: bridge to engage and the difference between defined work and vague work

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The core distinction is between collecting inputs and deciding what they mean. Control comes from processing items one by one through a sequence instead of trying to choose actions from an unclarified pile.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The workflow creates control only if containers stay few, current, and regularly emptied. Hard depth must preserve the failure mode where too many buckets or skipped reviews recreate clutter under a more organized label.

Threshold Question:
What breaks first when the system captures more than it clarifies and reviews?
