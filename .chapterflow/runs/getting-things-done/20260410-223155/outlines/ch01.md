Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader understands why modern overload feels mentally sticky and why defining the next action inside a trusted system is the first real move toward control.

Opening Move:
Open on the familiar feeling of unfinished obligations pressing on attention during a moment when the reader cannot act on them.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for the overload setup and knowledge-work pressure
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for open loops, mental clogging, and the trusted-system contrast
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for the project exercise and next physical action shift

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: anchor the reader in modern overload and unfinished-pressure
- P2: explain why boundaryless knowledge work keeps producing new commitments
- P3: define open loops and the cost of carrying them mentally
- P4: introduce mind like water as the contrast state and clarify what changes it
- P5: show why high-level planning and standard lists are not enough without ground-level decisions
- P6: land on the next physical action exercise as the practical proof point
- P7 if needed: bridge into Chapter 2 by naming the need for a repeatable workflow

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between carrying a commitment mentally and translating it into an external next action inside a trusted system. That conversion is the mechanism that changes stress into control.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter promises relief through capture, but hard depth must preserve the limit that capture without clarification simply creates a different kind of pile.

Threshold Question:
What actually changes when a commitment leaves your head but still has not become a defined next action?
