## Chapter Order And Intent

1. Chapter 1, "A New Practice for a New Reality"  
   Intent: establish why modern open-loop overload breaks attention and why GTD starts by externalizing commitments into trusted next actions.
2. Chapter 2, "Getting Control of Your Life: The Five Stages of Mastering Workflow"  
   Intent: define the five-stage workflow as the operational core of GTD and show how incomplete decisions create stress.
3. Chapter 3, "Getting Projects Creatively Under Way: The Five Phases of Project Planning"  
   Intent: show how project progress improves when outcome visioning and next actions are paired with structured planning.
4. Chapter 4, "Getting Started: Setting Up the Time, Space, and Tools"  
   Intent: turn GTD from theory into a usable setup by defining the physical and environmental conditions for a trusted system.
5. Chapter 5, "Collection: Corralling Your \"Stuff\""  
   Intent: show why capture has to be total enough to close psychic loops instead of serving as a halfhearted inbox ritual.
6. Chapter 6, "Processing: Getting \"In\" to Empty"  
   Intent: teach the discipline of clarifying one item at a time so inputs become decisions instead of background pressure.
7. Chapter 7, "Organizing: Setting Up the Right Buckets"  
   Intent: explain how the right containers reduce friction by separating action lists, waiting-fors, calendars, reference, and someday items.
8. Chapter 8, "Reviewing: Keeping Your System Functional"  
   Intent: make the Weekly Review and maintenance rhythm the hinge between a trusted system and a stale one.
9. Chapter 9, "Doing: Making the Best Action Choices"  
   Intent: show how context, time, energy, and priority govern action choice once commitments are clarified.
10. Chapter 10, "Getting Projects Under Control"  
    Intent: reconnect daily execution to project-level control so multi-step work does not dissolve back into vague concern.
11. Chapter 11, "The Power of the Collection Habit"  
    Intent: explain why the capture habit changes psychological load, attention, and reliability beyond mere list-making.
12. Chapter 12, "The Power of the Next-Action Decision"  
    Intent: show why defining the very next visible action changes momentum, avoidance, and collaboration.
13. Chapter 13, "The Power of Outcome Focusing"  
    Intent: close on the leverage of clear outcomes and front-end thinking for both individuals and organizations.

## Rotation Plan For Examples

- Preserve a strict 2 work / 2 school / 2 personal mix per chapter.
- Rotate pressure types across chapters: inbox overload, ambiguous ownership, meeting drift, deadline fog, calendar misuse, handoff failure, review avoidance, and vague projects.
- Avoid repeating the same protagonist posture across adjacent chapters. If one chapter centers on overwhelm, the next should center on friction, ambiguity, or mis-sequencing.
- Keep examples concrete and modern, but narrow enough to stay supported by GTD logic rather than invented systems theory.

## School-Setting Plan

- Use a mix of high school, college, graduate, and school-club settings so school examples do not collapse into seminar-room repetition.
- Favor assignment backlog, research planning, group project ownership, and review preparation over generic study-tip scenes.
