Core Claim:
Projects feel more controllable when planning follows a natural sequence instead of jumping straight into scattered problem-solving. Allen's five phases, purpose and principles, outcome visioning, brainstorming, organizing, and next actions, turn project thinking into a creative but grounded process. The chapter argues that many people brainstorm too early, before they know what success is supposed to look like. Clear outcomes and next actions are what turn planning from abstract talk into relaxed control.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the project-planning chapter. It adds a second architecture on top of workflow control and distinguishes natural planning from the vague or backward planning patterns that leave projects feeling heavy.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author claims the mind already knows how to plan naturally.
Then the author shows the five planning phases and contrasts them with unnatural workplace planning.
Then the author concludes that clear outcomes, structured brainstorming, and next actions create relaxed project control.
