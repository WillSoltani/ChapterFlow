Core Claim:
Even a good workflow fails when the surrounding environment makes capture, review, and organization inconvenient. Chapter 4 argues that GTD becomes usable only when a person creates enough time, space, and tool readiness for the system to function in daily life. The chapter shifts from abstract control into practical operating conditions. A trusted system is supported by setup, not by intention alone.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the setup chapter. It translates the earlier workflow and planning logic into environmental requirements and practical readiness rather than new conceptual architecture.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from methodology into implementation conditions.
Then the author shows that time, space, and tools affect whether the system is easy enough to trust.
Then the author concludes that practical friction must be reduced or the system decays back into piles and avoidance.
