Core Claim:
Collection only works when it is comprehensive enough to gather loose incompletes instead of leaving them scattered across the mind and environment. Chapter 5 pushes capture from occasional note-taking into a full sweep of open loops and "stuff." The chapter argues that selective collection preserves distraction because the uncollected material keeps competing for attention. Full gathering is the first honest move toward an empty, trusted in-basket.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the full-capture chapter. It moves from setup readiness into actually gathering loose material and stresses comprehensiveness rather than clever filtering.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that incomplete, loose material must be gathered rather than mentally managed.
Then the author shows that collection has to be full enough to catch physical and mental incompletes.
Then the author concludes that partial capture preserves distraction while full gathering creates the conditions for later processing.
