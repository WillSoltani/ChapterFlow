Core Claim:
Processing turns a gathered pile into clarified decisions by forcing one item at a time through a simple question: is it actionable or not? Chapter 6 argues that collected material does not become trustworthy just because it has been gathered. The in-basket earns trust only when each item is clarified and routed until the basket returns to empty. Leaving items in vague limbo preserves drag even after collection.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the clarification chapter. It follows comprehensive collection by defining what to do with each input once it is in front of you and by insisting on one-item-at-a-time processing toward empty.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author moves from gathering to clarifying what each collected item means.
Then the author separates actionable from non-actionable inputs as the key decision split.
Then the author concludes that the in-basket becomes trustworthy only when items are processed down to empty instead of being reread and postponed ambiguously.

Specific Applications:
- taking one paper or note at a time from the in-basket instead of skimming the whole pile
- distinguishing trash, reference, and incubated items from true action items
- refusing to put half-decided items back into the basket

Common Misreadings:
