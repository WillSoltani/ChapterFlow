Core Claim:
The fourth night argues that healthier relationships require a shift away from self-centered ranking and vertical control toward community feeling, contribution, and horizontal relationship posture. The chapter must stay concrete enough to show that not being the center of the world is not humiliation, and that value can be felt without praise, rebuke, or domination.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the relational-reconstruction chapter. Chapter 3 separated tasks; Chapter 4 shows what kind of social ethic can replace approval dependence and control.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter names the goal of interpersonal relationships.
Then it rejects self-centrality without erasing dignity.
Then it critiques rebuke and praise as vertical postures.
Then it relocates value in contribution rather than domination.
Finally it insists that someone must start the move toward healthier relating.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter supports horizontal relationship posture, not the removal of all standards or structure. Its target is domination and dependency language. It should preserve dignity, agency, and contribution together.
