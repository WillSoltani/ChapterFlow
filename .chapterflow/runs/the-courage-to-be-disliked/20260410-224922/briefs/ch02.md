Core Claim:
The second night argues that much of human misery takes shape through comparison, ranking, inferiority, dominance, resentment, and the need to win against or above other people. Its job is to move the reader from a solitary explanation of suffering into a relational one. The chapter has to preserve one important boundary: "interpersonal relationship problems" should be read as a powerful organizing lens, not as a cartoon claim that biology, grief, or material hardship literally do not exist.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the rivalry chapter. It turns inward insecurity outward and shows how inferiority, braggadocio, competition, power struggle, and revenge belong to one family of relational misreading.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter reframes inferiority as relational rather than purely objective.
Then it shows that superiority performances often grow from the same insecurity they seem to deny.
Then it attacks the competition frame and traces how comparison escalates into power struggle and revenge.
Finally it opens toward life tasks and a less combative way of living among others.
