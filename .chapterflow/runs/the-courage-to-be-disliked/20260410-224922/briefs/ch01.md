Core Claim:
The opening night argues that the past should not be treated as a total explanation of who a person must become. Its job is to crack the fatalistic story that "I am this way because of what happened to me" and replace it with a more demanding claim: people organize their lives around purposes, habits, and self-protective choices that can be altered. The chapter must keep one careful limit alive the whole time: denying trauma as destiny is not the same as denying suffering as real.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the anti-fatalism opener. Its task is not to summarize Adlerian psychology in full. It has to unsettle cause-based identity stories, introduce purposive interpretation, and leave the reader with a sharper but riskier idea of freedom than they expected.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter stages the ordinary belief that people cannot really change because their past has already explained them.
Then it introduces a harder frame: behavior often serves a present purpose, even when the person experiences it as unavoidable.
Then it sharpens the point through anger and self-dislike, showing that even painful patterns can have a function.
Finally it lands on a new question: if the past is not the final ruler, what responsibility follows from that freedom?

Specific Applications:

- a graduate student who says public speaking panic proves a permanent wound
- a person who preserves distance in relationships by treating old pain as an identity constitution

Common Misreadings:
- reading the chapter as "pain is fake"
- treating the chapter as a moral accusation against people who suffer
- using "choice" to erase constraint, fear, or history
- flattening teleology into victim-blaming

Counterarguments or Limits:
The frozen bundle supports a challenge to past-cause fatalism, not a crude denial that painful events matter. The prose must keep the line precise: the chapter disputes the authority of the explanatory story, not the reality of injury. It also cannot pretend all change is easy. The pressure of the chapter comes from responsibility, not simplification.
