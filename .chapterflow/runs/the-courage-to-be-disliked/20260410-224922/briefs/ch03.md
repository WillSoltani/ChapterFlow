Core Claim:
The third night argues that much interpersonal suffering persists because people invade responsibilities that are not theirs. Freedom begins when you separate tasks by asking who is responsible for the choice, consequence, or evaluation at issue. The chapter has to sound firm without collapsing into selfish detachment. Task separation is not indifference. It is a boundary that makes non-dominating relationships possible.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the boundary chapter. It translates the anti-rivalry insight of Chapter 2 into a practical rule: stop living to satisfy others' expectations, stop controlling outcomes that belong to them, and stop handing your freedom to recognition hunger.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter rejects living in obedience to other people's expectations.
Then it defines task separation by locating responsibility.
Then it argues that much relational misery softens when people stop stealing or managing other people's tasks.
Then it shows how recognition hunger creates unfreedom.
Finally it insists that your stance remains yours even if another person dislikes it.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter supports clear boundaries, not contempt for obligation. It should say that contribution, care, and cooperation still matter. What gets rejected is control over another person's response or the surrender of your life to their approval.
