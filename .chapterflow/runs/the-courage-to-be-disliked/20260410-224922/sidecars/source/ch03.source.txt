Title: The Third Night: Discard Other People's Tasks

Chapter function in the book:
This chapter turns the anti-rivalry logic of Chapter 2 into a boundary argument. If life becomes distorted when other people are used as measuring devices, then freedom requires separating what belongs to your responsibility from what belongs to theirs. The chapter has to avoid sounding selfish, detached, or anti-contribution. It is a boundary chapter, not a coldness chapter.

Safe paraphrase zone:
- do not live in obedience to others' expectations
- separate tasks by asking who is responsible for the choice, outcome, or evaluation
- recognition hunger produces dependence and unfreedom
- relationship freedom begins when you stop trying to control another person's task
- the reader still controls their own stance even when another person dislikes it

Unsupported zones:
- no claim that people owe no care to anyone else
- no claim that all obligations are fake
- no family or workplace legal advice
- no reduction of task separation to apathy or emotional withdrawal

Preview bridge:
The chapter should end by showing that boundary-setting alone is not enough. Once tasks are separated, the next question becomes how to live with others without domination, praise addiction, or self-centeredness.
