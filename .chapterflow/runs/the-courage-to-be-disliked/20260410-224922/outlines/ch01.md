Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader sees the past as part of the story but not the final authority over who they must remain.

Opening Move:
Open in the few seconds after Nia snaps at her team and says it was automatic because that is what childhood taught her. Hold the room there before the chapter questions whether "automatic" is the whole truth.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3, P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5, P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: live work scene; anger feels automatic and inherited.
- P2: widen into the fatalistic story people tell about the past.
- P3: introduce "Why People Can Change" by shifting from cause to present purpose.
- P4: pressure-test that shift with "People Fabricate Anger" and clarify the narrow claim.
- P5: inward turn through self-dislike and chosen protective patterns.
- P6: compassion boundary; denying destiny is not denying pain.
- P7: bridge to the next night's focus on inferiority and interpersonal struggle.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's core distinction is pain versus explanatory tyranny. Pain can be real while still not having the right to dictate the whole shape of a life.

Hard-Depth Tension:
If people use old pain as a present-purpose shield, how do you expose that shield without becoming morally crude?

Threshold Question:
How do you free a person from the authority of the past without using freedom as an accusation?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- fatalism is emotionally protective because it reduces responsibility
- purpose-reading does not erase pain but reframes behavior
- anger can function as a tool for control or distance
- self-dislike can preserve a safe but smaller identity
- change becomes possible only when the explanatory story loosens
- compassion and accountability must stay linked

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): Nia decides whether to call her outburst destiny or a strategy
- Scenario 2 (dialogue, school): Elias argues with a seminar peer about whether fear explains everything
- Scenario 3 (dilemma, personal): Hana keeps a relationship distant by invoking an old wound
- Scenario 4 (postmortem, work): Owen reconstructs how his old story nearly closed the review before it began
- Scenario 5 (before_after, school): Tessa sees the difference between pain as context and pain as verdict
- Scenario 6 (predict_reveal, personal): Ravi notices self-dislike acting like protection against risk

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least 4 scenarios should include concrete room details such as a half-muted Zoom square, seminar chairs in a circle, a phone held face down during an argument, or a notebook margin filled before speaking.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what people start organizing their lives around if they stop making the past the sole authority.

Genericity Risks:
- motivational freedom talk with no cost or boundary
- cruelty disguised as agency
- therapy clichés about healing the inner child

Forbidden Drift:
- calling trauma unreal
- treating anger as always fake in a manipulative sense
- leaking Chapter 2's inferiority framework too fully
