## Governing Question
What happens when accountability stops being a mirror exercise and becomes a deadline-driven physical mission so extreme that failure looks sensible?

## Core claim
Chapter 3 turns self-honesty into radical commitment. Goggins, now obese and stuck in exterminator work, decides to lose massive weight fast enough to qualify for Navy SEAL training. The chapter's job is to show why impossible-looking missions can become tools for breaking a defeated identity, while also keeping the cost, absurdity, and risk visible.

## Required anchors
- Goggins is working as an exterminator, overweight, and disgusted by the gap between the life he wants and the life he is living.
- Seeing a program or documentary about Navy SEALs gives the goal a shape hard enough to command him.
- The time window and weight-loss requirement make the task extreme rather than aspirational.
- The chapter treats the mission as chosen suffering, not glamour.
- Progress comes through severe discipline, daily sacrifice, and refusal to negotiate with the old self.
- The chapter closes by turning honesty into action severe enough to reorder identity.

## Reader value
- understand why extreme goals can interrupt identity drift
- see the difference between fantasy goals and fully costed missions
- feel how Chapter 2's accountability expands into a near-absurd assignment

## Hard-depth boundary
The chapter must not romanticize recklessness or pretend that extremity is always wisdom. The boundary is that the mission works here because it is concrete, total, and identity-breaking, not because impossible tasks are automatically good.
