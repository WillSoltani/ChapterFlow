## Governing Question
What changes when a person with real reasons to feel defeated stops asking for relief first and starts asking for the truth about his own role?

## Core claim
Chapter 2 moves from surviving damage to confronting distortion. Goggins loses a stabilizing father figure, faces racist hostility and academic failure, and then introduces the Accountability Mirror as a tool for stripping away excuses without denying hardship.

## Required anchors
- Wilmoth Irving gives the family relief and then is murdered.
- Goggins absorbs more grief, alienation, racism, and academic collapse.
- The Air Force dream gives him a last meaningful target, but his poor test score exposes how far behind he is.
- The Accountability Mirror becomes the turning point from excuse-backed drift to chosen self-confrontation.
- The chapter closes with the idea that self-respect can be built through accountability rather than inherited confidence.

## Reader value
- understand why truth can feel harsher and more useful than comfort
- see accountability as confrontation with reality, not denial of pain
- feel how Chapter 1's history turns into Chapter 2's demand for action

## Hard-depth boundary
The chapter must not turn accountability into self-hatred or pretend that racism, grief, and trauma do not matter. The boundary is that honesty becomes useful only when it faces both the real obstacles and the reader's own evasions.
