Chapter Promise:
This chapter will make the reader see tunnel life not as a rumor or urban legend, but as a hidden social world produced by visible public failures.

Opening Move:
Open on the shock of learning that people are living beneath New York City, then immediately turn that shock away from spectacle and toward recognition.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: establish the hidden fact of people living under the city and the instinct to treat it like rumor
- P2: bring in Toth's reporting entry and the act of going below rather than talking from topside
- P3: show that the underground world contains fear, violence, and improvisation
- P4: show that it also contains community, leadership, and identity under pressure
- P5: argue that the tunnels are not separate from the city but a concentrated expression of visible neglect above ground
- P6: preserve the limit that looking can become voyeurism or myth-making, then bridge to a more concrete next chapter

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's core distinction is hidden suffering versus hidden fantasy. Its mechanism is that social invisibility allows readers to misread underground life as mystery when it is actually a concentrated form of public neglect.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve the ethical tension between revealing a hidden world and exploiting it as spectacle.

Threshold Question:
How do you look steadily at a hidden world like this without turning the people in it into symbols, legends, or scenery?
