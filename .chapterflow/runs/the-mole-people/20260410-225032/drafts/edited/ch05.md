After Mac's hard singularity, the next chapter has to widen without becoming foggy. The lawful summaries support that move, but only if the prose stays disciplined.

The first correction is basic. The underground population is not one type of person making one type of choice. The frozen bundle supports people living alone and people living in communities. That matters because hidden populations are easily flattened.

The chapter also has to keep motive plural. People move below ground for different reasons. Some are pushed by topside danger, humiliation, and instability. Some are pulled toward whatever order or belonging an underground community can offer. Some are reacting to institutions that failed them.

Once that variety is visible, invisibility looks different. A hidden population is easy to mythologize because observers usually see fragments. One resident becomes the type. One camp becomes the rule. One dramatic detail becomes the underground itself.

That is why scale matters. The frozen summaries support the claim that underground residents form part of the broader homeless population, but they do not authorize a census from this chapter alone. Restraint is method here, not weakness.

The chapter also must not romanticize community. Communities below ground may offer order or identity, but that does not make the setting simply safe. Likewise, loners are not automatically freer. The right scale is broader than a portrait and narrower than a myth.
