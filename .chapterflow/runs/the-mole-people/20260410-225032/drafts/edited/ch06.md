The underground cannot be understood as one generic dark place. That is the first correction this chapter has to make.

The lawful bundle is narrow, but it is enough to support that correction. The authorized summaries explicitly name subway, railroad, and sewage tunnels. Once those categories appear, the underground stops looking like one blurred hiding place and starts looking like different survival environments.

That is why tunnel type matters. A subway tunnel is not just a railroad tunnel with a different name, and neither is the same as a sewage tunnel. Even at the summary level, the categories imply different kinds of movement, access, visibility, smell, noise, danger, and habitability. The chapter gets stronger when it treats those differences as pressure differences. People do not merely occupy space. They are shaped by what the space allows and punishes.

This is the point where place stops being scenery. If a setting changes how exposed a person feels, how easily others can enter, how close transport and policing remain, and how sustainable daily routines become, then the setting is one of the forces organizing the life. The underground population from the previous chapter therefore cannot be understood apart from the underground spaces themselves.

That pressure runs both ways. Hidden space can offer concealment from topside humiliation or danger, but concealment is not the same as safety. The official summaries keep danger above and below ground in one frame, and the chapter should preserve that tension. Going underground may change the kind of risk a person faces without removing risk.

The chapter also has to resist the wrong kind of concreteness. Because place is vivid, there is a temptation to turn the underground into a guided tour. That would drift past the lawful bundle. The safe move is narrower: treat subway, railroad, and sewage tunnels as environment types that help explain why underground life cannot be summarized as one condition.

That narrower map is enough to deepen the book. Chapter 5 showed that the underground population is mixed. Chapter 6 shows that the underground world is mixed too. Different spaces shape different routines, dangers, and possibilities for staying hidden, moving, gathering, or enduring. Once that becomes visible, place is no longer background. It is part of the social reality the book is trying to describe.
