After Mac's hard singularity, the next chapter has to widen without becoming foggy. The lawful summaries support that move, but only if the prose stays disciplined.

The first correction is basic. The underground population is not one type of person making one type of choice. The frozen bundle supports people living alone and people living in communities. That matters because hidden populations are easily flattened. One vivid case gets mistaken for the whole group.

The chapter also has to keep motive plural. People move below ground for different reasons. Some are pushed by topside danger, humiliation, and instability. Some are pulled toward whatever order or belonging an underground community can offer. Some are reacting to institutions that failed them. The point is not to pick one master cause. The point is to keep multiple pressure routes visible.

Once that variety is visible, invisibility looks different too. A hidden population is easy to mythologize because observers usually see fragments. One resident becomes the type. One camp becomes the rule. One dramatic detail becomes the underground itself. The chapter earns trust by resisting that move.

That is why scale matters. The frozen summaries support the claim that underground residents form part of the broader homeless population, but they do not authorize a census from this chapter alone. Restraint is not weakness here. It is method. Honest description has to stop below the level of false precision.

The chapter also must not romanticize community. Communities below ground may offer order, identity, or company, but none of that makes the setting simply safe. Likewise, loners are not automatically freer. Isolation can reduce some conflict while increasing exposure.

So the chapter's best move is a narrower map. Many people live below in more than one social form for more than one reason, and hiddenness magnifies every simplification. Portraits like Seville and Mac still matter, but neither can become a false census. The right scale here is broader than a portrait and narrower than a myth.
