Mac's chapter refuses two bad readings at once. It does not ask the reader to gawk at him, and it does not ask the reader to admire him cheaply.

The lawful support gives a narrow but firm portrait. Mac is older, methodical, hungry, and practiced. He hunts rats, calls them track rabbits, cooks them, and keeps moving through a life where scarcity is not an accident but a climate. Those details matter because they show survival becoming organized. He is not improvising one desperate night. He has built a code for meeting pressure.

That code is partly verbal. Calling rats track rabbits does not clean up what he must eat. It gives him a usable name for it. The rename creates a little distance from disgust and humiliation. The chapter is stronger when it treats the phrase as survival language rather than cute eccentricity.

The same discipline shapes the rest of the portrait. Mac does not appear as a rugged legend. He appears as someone forced into competence by conditions that keep pressing. The competence carries dignity because it means he is not passive before deprivation. But the chapter weakens if competence is mistaken for freedom. A person can become highly capable inside a bad world without the world becoming less bad.

The Thoreau detail sharpens that point. Mac recites Thoreau, and the value of that fact is not uplift. It is contrast. Rough subsistence and reflective language can live in the same person. Mac is not only hunger. He is also memory, quotation, and self-conception. But Thoreau does not rescue the rats. It only complicates the man.

That is why the title works. Mac's war is not one documented battle. It is the siege logic of his life. He works against hunger, disgust, softness, and dependence. The routines protect dignity because they let him answer pressure with action. They also threaten to harden into identity armor. When survival becomes the code of self-respect, setting the code down can feel like setting the self down too.

So the portrait has to stay disciplined. Mac cannot become proof that deprivation makes people strong in any noble sense. He also cannot become a freak show built from food shock. The strongest reading keeps dignity, scarcity, language, and cost in one frame, then widens carefully toward the larger underground population without pretending Mac stands for everyone below ground.
