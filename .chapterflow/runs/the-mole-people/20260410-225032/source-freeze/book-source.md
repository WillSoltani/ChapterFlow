## Official work summary
The frozen official and authorized sources agree on the core frame: Toth reports on people living in New York City's subway, railroad, and sewage tunnels, including isolated individuals and organized underground communities, while tracing why they moved below ground and how they survive the dangers of both tunnel life and the world above.

## Narrow support extensions
- Chapter 4 `Mac's War`:
  - Publishers Weekly identifies Mac as a white man in his early 50s who hunts rats, called `track rabbits`, cooks them, and recites Thoreau.
  - This supports a restrained portrait centered on survival, improvisation, and damaged dignity, but not a full scene-by-scene biography.
- Chapter 5 `The Underground Population`:
  - Google Books and Syndetics support that tunnel dwellers live alone and in communities, form a large and growing part of the homeless population, and move underground for multiple reasons including shelter from street conditions, rejection of surface society, and identity within underground communities.
  - This supports a narrow descriptive systems chapter if it avoids settled counts and disputed lore.
- Chapter 6 `Underground Spaces`:
  - The authorized Google Books and official publisher summaries explicitly describe people living in New York City's subway, railroad, and sewage tunnels.
  - This supports a narrow environments chapter about different underground spaces as survival settings with distinct dangers, constraints, and social effects, but not a detailed geography or scene-specific map.
