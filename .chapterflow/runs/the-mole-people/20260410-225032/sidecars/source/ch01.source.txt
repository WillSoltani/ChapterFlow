Frozen support from the source bundle:
- the official and authorized summaries describe people living in New York City's subway, railroad, and sewage tunnels, sometimes alone and sometimes in communities
- the book presents the tunnels as hidden from most of the city above while still shaped by the same poverty, addiction, violence, fear, and need for belonging
- the Washington Post obituary says Toth first heard about tunnel families from a child she tutored, then spent about a year entering the tunnels and reporting there
- later reporting identifies Bernard Isaacs as a guide and protector during Toth's tunnel reporting
- professional reviews describe camps, mayors or spokesmen, graffiti artists, women, teenagers, and organized groups below ground
- later reporting and criticism note that some population claims and some wilder stories were disputed or hard to verify

Boundaries:
- keep the prose paraphrase-first
- do not state the underground population count as settled fact
- do not invent exact tunnel layouts, numbers of camps, or unseen introductory scenes
- do not romanticize the tunnels as a liberated hidden city
- keep the introduction focused on hiddenness, fear, neglect, and the social cost of looking away
