Core Claim:
This chapter widens from portrait to pattern. The lawful bundle supports a careful description of who lives below ground, how underground life includes both loners and organized communities, and why different people move there under different pressures. The point is not to produce a census or a legend. The point is to show that the underground population is socially mixed and structurally produced.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the first population-level chapter after several person-rooted openings. It changes scale from one life to a cautious systems view.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter rejects the idea that tunnel life is one type of person making one type of choice.
Then it shows the range between isolated dwellers and communities with order, leaders, and belonging.
Then it explains why the underground can persist as an answer to multiple kinds of pressure without claiming the answer is stable or safe.

Required Anchors:
- the underground population includes loners and communities rather than one social form
- people move below ground for multiple reasons, not one master cause
- invisibility and variety together help explain why outsiders misread the underground

Specific Applications:

Common Misreadings:
