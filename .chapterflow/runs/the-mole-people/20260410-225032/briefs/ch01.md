Core Claim:
The introduction establishes that there is a hidden underground social world beneath New York City that most people above ground do not see and do not want to see. Its purpose is not only to shock the reader with the fact of people living below the city, but to force a harder recognition: the tunnels are an extension of the same social failures already visible on the streets. The chapter frames tunnel life as dangerous, improvised, and often brutal, yet still structured by community, protection, and identity. It also makes the act of looking morally unstable. The reader is being asked to look directly at a hidden world without turning it into spectacle, fantasy, or urban legend.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's frame chapter. It introduces the hidden city below the city, the reporter's entry into that world, and the tension between genuine reporting and the culture's appetite for mythic underground stories.

Author Logic Chain:
First the introduction names the fact of people living beneath New York in spaces most of the city ignores.
Then it shows that this underground world includes both danger and community rather than fitting a simple horror story or rescue story.
Then it widens the lens: the tunnels are not another planet, but a compressed view of poverty, neglect, addiction, and social abandonment already present above ground.
