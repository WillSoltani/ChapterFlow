Core Claim:
This chapter turns the underground from a hidden population into a hidden physical world. The lawful bundle supports a narrow environments chapter because the authorized summaries explicitly name subway, railroad, and sewage tunnels. That is enough to argue that space itself shapes life below ground. Different underground settings change movement, danger, visibility, smell, noise, access, and the possibility of community. The point is not to produce a tour. The point is to show that underground life is structured by physical environments that are harsh in different ways.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the first chapter that treats place as an active force rather than as background for people. It follows the population map by asking what kinds of spaces hold that population.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter rejects the idea that all underground living happens in one generic dark place.
Then it uses the subway, railroad, and sewage tunnel categories to show that different spaces shape different kinds of risk and movement.
Then it argues that environment changes social life because the space itself sets limits on visibility, access, and habitability.
