# Style Memory — The 33 Strategies of War

Operating cheat sheet for writer/editor/converter. Distilled from `style/voice.md`, `constraints.md`, `grade-bands.md`, `bad-patterns.md`, `gold-patterns.md`, `gold-prose.md`, `gold-examples.md`, `gold-quiz.md`.

## Voice
Sharp human explainer with taste. Authored, not assembled. Specific over generic. Concrete over abstract. Insight over padding. Strong openings. Distinct paragraph jobs. Vivid examples. Honest limits. Memorable lines that still sound human. **Teach the idea, not the chapter.**

## Hard rules
- No facts, quotes, mechanisms, anecdotes outside the brief.
- No em dashes.
- No invented neuroscience (mirror neurons, oxytocin, vagal tone, cortisol, dopamine, evolutionary psych, millisecond claims) unless brief supports it.
- No raw source splices into breakdowns without quote-ledger approval.
- No leakage of brief / outline / seed / calibration / validator language into reader-facing content.
- Morally gray content → strategic awareness frame, never endorsement.

## Banned phrases (auto-fail)
delve, crucial, landscape, realm, "It's worth noting", "In today's world", Furthermore, Moreover, "In conclusion", "at its core", "the art of", navigating, harnessing, robust, synergy, "paradigm shift", "game-changer", facilitate, utilize, foster, "embark on", "a testament to", "shed light on", "This matters because", "This is significant because", "It is essential to".

## Banned contamination phrases
"keep the prose narrow and concrete", "the source is short and works by contrast", "used lazily, the point turns into", "keep this question alive", "one source pressure stays visible", "tied to the live constraint", "keep [concept] visible when you use it", "threshold question", "reading calibration", "unsupported zones", "motif watchlist", sourceAnchorPriority, "internal concept budget".

## Banned sentence skeletons
- "X is not Y. It is Z."
- "The real issue is..."
- "What matters is..."
- "This changes everything."
- "The difference is..."

## Grade bands
- **Easy** grade 8–9: pictureable opener in first 2 sentences, short/medium sentences, concrete nouns, one frame, one vivid example early. No stacked abstractions.
- **Medium** grade 10–11: mechanism + application; contrast & distinction; one or two live tensions.
- **Hard** grade 12: add genuine boundary condition / failure mode / threshold question / contradiction / synthesis. Hard ≠ medium with bigger words.

## Tone differentiation (functional, not adjectival)
- **Gentle:** lower resistance, warmer framing, softer entry, room to breathe.
- **Direct:** clearest mechanics, decision line, no decoration.
- **Competitive:** sharper stakes, leverage, consequence, who-eats-whom framing.
Tones must do **different jobs**, not say the same thing louder.

## Auto-fail moves
- Opening with thesis instead of live scene/tension.
- Hiding weak thinking behind smooth phrasing.
- Abstraction used to dodge a concrete claim.
- Producing canonical drafts from structured depth blocks.
- First sentence is thesis-first.
- Two paragraphs do the same job.
- Hard depth = longer medium.
- Examples polished but interchangeable.
- Implementation plan could apply to any chapter.
- Quiz distractors too weak to tempt.
- Could swap chapter title and keep 70% of prose.

## Read-aloud test
- Sentence sounds pre-manufactured → cut.
- Sentence sounds like a generic podcast summary → cut.
- Sentence sounds smarter than the brief supports → cut.
- Sentence sounds like a note to the writer → cut immediately.

## What gold prose does (gold-prose.md / gold-patterns.md)
1. Anchors chapter in named, specific stories that *carry* the argument (not decorative).
2. Easy/medium/hard do different jobs structurally.
3. Gentle/direct/competitive differ in *function*.
4. Examples are socially realistic — names, rooms, times, objects, stakes.
5. whatToDo is a precise reframing/conversational move the reader can picture.
6. whyItMatters explains downstream consequence, not a repeat of whatToDo.
7. Quiz tests judgment-in-situation; distractors are plausible; explanations teach.
8. Real analytical spine: distinctions, boundary conditions, advanced concepts.

## What this book specifically demands
- Strategic register, not therapeutic / self-help.
- Greene's amoral framing must be *reframed* as strategic awareness.
- No reuse of Greene's signature historical case studies (Napoleon, Hannibal, Sun Tzu, Mao, Patton, Rommel, Lawrence, Fouquet/Louis XIV, Galileo/Medici — those last two are *gold benchmark anchors* for the 48 Laws gold file, **not** for this book).
- All examples invented fresh for ChapterFlow.
- Modern professional / school / personal scenarios.
- Soften Greene's occasional admiration for ruthlessness; preserve the strategic provocation.
