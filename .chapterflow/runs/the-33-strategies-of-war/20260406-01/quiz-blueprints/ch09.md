## Q03 -- Remember/Understand | Easy | correctIndex: 2
**Concept:** Why fait accompli works
**Prompt:** Why does a sequence of small moves succeed where a single large move would provoke opposition?
**Choices:**
  0: Because the opponent is always too slow to notice anything.
  1: Because small moves are always ethically acceptable regardless of their cumulative effect.
  2: Because each individual move is below the threshold at which the opponent would mobilize, and by the time the cumulative effect is visible, reversal is impractical.
**Key:** 2 -- The strategy depends on each move being genuinely small enough to avoid triggering the response threshold.
**Bloom:** remember/understand
**Depth:** easy

## Q04 -- Apply/Analyze | Medium | correctIndex: 1
**Concept:** Applying framing in a competitive situation
**Prompt:** Margot competes for grant funding against national organizations with larger budgets. She presents real local outcome data that the national orgs cannot match. Is this legitimate framing or manipulation?
**Choices:**
  0: It is manipulation because she is deliberately choosing data that favors her organization.
  1: It is legitimate framing because the data is real and the selection highlights a genuine advantage the national orgs do not have.
  2: It cannot be judged without knowing whether the funders are aware she is competing.
**Key:** 1 -- Strategic framing uses real facts selectively. The data is genuine, the advantage is real, and the selection makes the case strongest.
**Bloom:** apply/analyze
**Depth:** medium

## Q06 -- Apply/Analyze | Medium | correctIndex: 2
**Concept:** Alliance reversal
**Prompt:** A professional aligns with a senior executive by presenting a project overlap that does not actually exist. The executive sponsors the professional's initiative based on this manufactured alignment. What risk does the chapter identify?
**Choices:**
  0: The executive will eventually lose interest regardless of whether the overlap is real.
  1: The professional's career will stall because alliances with executives are not effective.
  2: The alliance will collapse when the executive discovers the overlap was manufactured, and the professional's credibility for future alignments will be damaged.

## Q07 -- Apply/Analyze | Medium | correctIndex: 1
**Concept:** Fait accompli failure mode
**Prompt:** An operator has been accumulating small policy changes incrementally. After six months, a key stakeholder recognizes the pattern and mobilizes opposition before the cumulative position is irreversible. What went wrong?
**Choices:**
  0: The operator should have made one large move instead of many small ones.
  1: The individual moves were not small enough to stay below the response threshold, or the pattern became visible before the accumulation reached the tipping point.
  2: The fait accompli strategy never works in policy contexts because stakeholders are always vigilant.
**Key:** 1 -- The strategy depends on each move being genuinely below the threshold. If the pattern is recognized early, the opponent can mobilize.
**Bloom:** apply/analyze
**Depth:** medium

## Q08 -- Apply/Analyze | Medium | correctIndex: 0
**Concept:** Unpredictability reversal
**Prompt:** An operator deliberately varies every strategic move to avoid predictability. After a year, colleagues and competitors notice that the constant variation itself has become the pattern. What does this illustrate?
**Choices:**
  0: Unpredictability becomes its own pattern if used compulsively rather than selectively, and the opponent learns to expect the unexpected.
  1: The strategy was always flawed because consistency is more valuable than unpredictability.
  2: The operator should have varied their approach more extremely to avoid detection.
**Key:** 0 -- The reversal is that compulsive unpredictability becomes predictable. The discipline requires selective use, not constant variation.
**Bloom:** apply/analyze
**Depth:** medium

## Q09 -- Evaluate | Hard | correctIndex: 2
**Concept:** The structural argument for legitimate forms
**Prompt:** The chapter argues that the legitimate forms of all five strategies are not only ethically preferable but also structurally more durable. Why?
**Choices:**
  0: Because ethical behavior always produces better outcomes regardless of context.
  1: Because opponents are always more likely to cooperate with ethical operators.
  2: Because legitimate forms work with real facts, genuine overlap, and actual conditions, which are self-sustaining. Manipulative forms require ongoing maintenance, concealment, and repair, making them structurally fragile.
**Key:** 2 -- The structural argument matches Ch7 and Ch8: manufactured situations are fragile; real situations are durable. This is a strategic argument, not only an ethical one.
**Bloom:** evaluate
**Depth:** hard

## Q10 -- Evaluate | Hard | correctIndex: 1
**Concept:** When to put the unconventional toolkit down
**Prompt:** An operator has been successfully using framing, alliances, and incremental accumulation to build position. A new situation arises where the counterparty is not an opponent but a genuine collaborator. Should the operator continue using the unconventional toolkit?
**Choices:**
  0: Yes -- the strategies are always applicable regardless of the relationship.
  1: No -- when the counterparty is a genuine collaborator rather than an opponent, applying strategic positioning tools is likely to damage the collaboration because it treats a partner as a target.
  2: Only if the operator can use the tools without the collaborator noticing.
**Key:** 1 -- The chapter previews Ch10's meta-skill: knowing when strategic framing itself is the wrong frame. Not every relationship is a competitive engagement.
**Bloom:** evaluate
**Depth:** hard
