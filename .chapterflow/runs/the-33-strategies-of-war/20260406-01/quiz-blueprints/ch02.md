# Chapter Quiz Blueprint — Chapter 2

Chapter: Leading Through Other People

Core concepts to test:
- concept 1: Leadership through other people is a distinct discipline from self-directed work.
- concept 2: Mission-not-method requires that subordinates have enough context to operate on judgment; otherwise it is abdication.
- concept 3: Segmentation requires that each unit be genuinely capable; segmented incompetents are slower incompetents.
- concept 4: The crusade move requires the leader's sincerity; manufactured belief produces cynicism worse than none.
- concept 5: The earned-trust diagnostic is the prior skill underneath all three moves.
- concept 6: Cross-direction cost asymmetry — over-trust produces fragmentation/paralysis/cynicism; under-trust produces brittleness/attrition/micromanagement.

Bloom ceiling: evaluate (q09–q10).

Question plan:
- q01: recall — what is the chapter's organizing claim about leadership through other people?
- q02: anchor comprehension — what specific failure does Naya's experiment make visible?
- q03: term — mission-not-method is best characterized as which kind of trust?
- q04: scenario application — a manager whose team has gone silent in meetings should reach for which diagnostic first?
- q05: distinction — segmentation as a structural fix vs. segmentation as a competence test.
- q06: limit/misreading — when does mission-not-method become abdication?
- q07: transfer — a leader who recently took over an organization and started using cause-language is at the highest risk of which failure?
- q08: judgment — given a manager pushing initiative downward to a team that does not have the context, which intervention applies?
- q09: synthesis — why are mission-not-method, segmentation, and crusade not three independent moves but three shapes of the same underlying trust?
- q10: cross-chapter — connect to Ch1: how does an operator who skipped the four self-directed contests systematically misplace the trusts of Ch2?
