**Prompt:** What does the chapter mean by a "grand strategy"?

**Choices:**
A. A set of high-priority goals for the next quarter that helps the operator focus their energy on what matters most.

**Choices:**
A. The operator should revisit the plan frequently so it always reflects the most current priorities and opportunities.

**Key:** C. Revisable means distinguished from drift-rationalizing. A describes a quarterly review, not the revisability concept. B describes a plan with no real anchor, which the chapter explicitly warns against.

## Q07 — Bloom: analyze | Depth: medium | correctIndex target: 1

**Prompt:** A consultant has built a detailed model of her target client's business. She is about to enter a pricing negotiation. She assumes the client's primary concern is cost. Halfway through the negotiation she discovers the client's actual constraint is that they are under a board mandate to demonstrate a productivity improvement within six months — cost is secondary. She had not done intelligence work before the meeting. What does the chapter say this illustrates?

**Choices:**
A. Assumptions are inevitable in negotiation; the key is to update them quickly when contradicted, which the consultant did.
B. The cost of skipping intelligence work is attacking the constructed image of the opponent rather than the real one — the consultant entered a negotiation whose actual stakes she did not understand, and any agreement she reached before her discovery was built on a false model.
C. The consultant should have asked the client directly about their priorities before presenting pricing.

**Key:** B. The chapter's exact mechanism: attacking the constructed image. A reframes the failure as something she did well by updating quickly. C is a valid tactic but misses the structural point about investment-before-any-move.

## Q09 — Bloom: evaluate | Depth: hard | correctIndex target: 1

**Prompt:** Why does the chapter treat the distinction between "gathering that improves the move" and "gathering that delays it" as the central diagnostic for the intelligence strategy?

**Choices:**
A. Because gathering is expensive and operators should minimize it; the diagnostic is a cost-control measure that tells the operator when to stop.
B. Because intelligence-gathering produces diminishing returns beyond a threshold, and the operator who cannot recognize that threshold is using a discipline that was designed to sharpen action as a mechanism for avoiding it — the same practice, in different psychological conditions, produces opposite outcomes.
C. Because gathering takes time that could be used for action, and the diagnostic simply ensures the operator doesn't spend more time than the value of the intelligence justifies.

**Key:** B. The chapter's precise point: same activity, two different psychological functions. A is a cost-control framing (not wrong but not the chapter's point). C is a time-tradeoff framing that misses the psychological mechanism (avoidance masquerading as rigor).

## Q10 — Bloom: evaluate | Depth: hard | correctIndex target: 0

**Prompt:** An operator has a grand strategy and has done thorough intelligence work before a major move. The intelligence reveals that her long-horizon plan depends on a condition that no longer holds — the market structure she built the plan around has fundamentally changed. What does the chapter say about this situation?

**Choices:**
A. This is exactly the situation the revisability requirement is designed for: the intelligence has produced a genuine revision signal (changed facts, not comfort-drift), and the operator who cannot update the plan in this situation has turned grand strategy into rigidity — which the chapter identifies as the plan's primary failure mode.
B. The operator should hold the long-horizon plan and treat the market change as a temporary disturbance that the strategy should absorb rather than react to.
C. The intelligence finding may be premature; the operator should gather more information before deciding whether to revise the plan.

**Key:** A. The revisability diagnostic applies perfectly here: facts have changed, not comfort preferences, so the update is genuine revision not drift. B is exactly the rigidity the chapter warns against. C loops back into the gathering-as-delay failure mode and delays the required revision.
