## Q03 — Remember/Understand | Easy | correctIndex: 1
**Concept:** Ripening mechanism
**Prompt:** What does the ripening strategy accomplish that a direct attack does not?
**Choices:**
  0: It protects the operator's resources by avoiding any engagement with the opponent.
  1: It removes the conditions that sustain the opponent's position, so that the opponent's own assessment or actions lead to their decline rather than a direct confrontation.
  2: It signals strength to third parties by demonstrating patience and strategic restraint.
**Key:** 1 — Ripening is offensive, not passive. The point is that the opponent's position collapses from within rather than requiring the operator to force the collapse directly.
**Bloom:** remember/understand
**Depth:** easy

## Q04 — Apply/Analyze | Medium | correctIndex: 1
**Concept:** Finding the real seam (divide-and-conquer prerequisite)
**Prompt:** Fola is trying to fracture a competitor coalition. She believes the seam is a pricing disagreement between two large members. She acts on this belief without verifying it first and the attempt fails -- the coalition publicly reaffirms its unity. What most likely went wrong?
**Choices:**
  0: The timing was wrong; she should have waited for a public dispute to develop organically before acting.
  1: She targeted a seam she had not verified actually existed. A clumsy or wrongly targeted wedge attempt often produces solidarity rather than fracture.
  2: The coalition was too large for the wedge strategy to work; divide-and-conquer requires smaller coalitions.
**Key:** 1 — Unverified seam targeting is the classic divide-and-conquer failure. The strategy requires accurate intelligence about where the genuine divergence is.
**Bloom:** apply/analyze
**Depth:** medium

## Q05 — Apply/Analyze | Medium | correctIndex: 0
**Concept:** Flank identification vs. frontal attack
**Prompt:** Leif is competing against a dominant incumbent that is known for its superior customer support team and extensive documentation. The incumbent's product has a sophisticated pricing model that few customers fully understand but most accept. Leif is deciding where to compete. Which target is most likely the incumbent's flank?
**Choices:**
  0: The pricing model complexity, which the incumbent cannot simplify without restructuring their revenue architecture and which customers have quietly complained about.
  1: The customer support team, which is the incumbent's most-cited competitive advantage and the dimension customers value most.
  2: The documentation, because it is the most tangible deliverable customers interact with and could be directly compared.
**Key:** 0 — The pricing complexity is the flank: not the focus of the incumbent's competitive positioning, structurally hard to change, and a real customer pain point. Support and documentation are the front -- well-defended and the incumbent's prepared strength.
**Bloom:** apply/analyze
**Depth:** medium

## Q06 — Apply/Analyze | Medium | correctIndex: 2
**Concept:** Ripening vs. direct attack
**Prompt:** Theron is a regional nonprofit director whose territory is being entered by a much larger national organization. Rather than competing directly, he spends 18 months building the relationships the national org has not built, joining the standards committee, and providing honest input on funder metrics. The national org eventually de-prioritizes the territory. What did Theron use?
**Choices:**
  0: The flank attack -- he identified where the national org was weak and attacked that dimension directly.
  1: The wedge strategy -- he fractured the national org's internal coalition of supporters.
  2: The ripening strategy -- he removed the conditions that would have made the national org's entry easy, so that their own assessment led them away rather than any direct confrontation.
**Key:** 2 — Classic ripening: removing sustaining conditions, not attacking directly, so the opponent's collapse comes from their own decisions.
**Bloom:** apply/analyze
**Depth:** medium

## Q07 — Apply/Analyze | Medium | correctIndex: 1
**Concept:** Wedge ethical line
**Prompt:** Gus is trying to fracture an opposing coalition. He has found a genuine disagreement between two members about a specific provision. He is now considering amplifying the disagreement by sending accurate information about the provision to the concerned members, making the conflict clearer to them. Is this legitimate?
**Choices:**
  0: No -- any deliberate attempt to fracture a coalition, even using accurate information, is manipulation.
  1: Yes -- amplifying a genuine divergence using accurate information is legitimate strategic framing. The illegitimate form is manufacturing a false divergence through fabricated information.
  2: Only if both members have consented to the information being shared.
**Key:** 1 — The chapter's ethical line: strategic framing of real facts is legitimate; fabrication is not. Providing accurate information that makes a genuine conflict more visible is the legitimate form.
**Bloom:** apply/analyze
**Depth:** medium

## Q08 — Apply/Analyze | Medium | correctIndex: 0
**Concept:** Flank reversal -- when no flank exists
**Prompt:** Soren has been searching for a flank in a competitor's position for three months without finding one. The competitor appears to have strong positions on every dimension relevant to the market. Meanwhile, the competitor has been gaining market share. What does this situation suggest Soren should do?
**Choices:**
  0: Accept that no soft flank may exist and consider a direct attack or a different strategic approach -- prolonged search for a non-existent flank costs initiative and allows the opponent to continue advancing.
  1: Continue searching, because every position has a soft flank that is just not yet visible; more intelligence work will reveal it.
  2: Use the ripening strategy instead, which does not require finding a flank.
**Key:** 0 -- The reversal: some positions have no soft flank. The discipline includes recognizing when the direct attack is the only viable option. Indefinite searching costs the initiative advantage.
**Bloom:** apply/analyze
**Depth:** medium

## Q09 — Evaluate | Hard | correctIndex: 2
**Concept:** Legitimate vs. manipulative indirection
**Prompt:** An executive is trying to fracture a coalition blocking her project. She has no intelligence about genuine internal disagreements. She fabricates a rumor that one member of the coalition has been privately undermining another, sends it through intermediaries, and the coalition fractures in response. The project proceeds. Was this legitimate strategy?
**Choices:**
  0: Yes -- the goal (advancing the project) was legitimate, and the tactic was effective.
  1: Partially -- the fabrication was wrong, but the strategic goal of fracturing the coalition was valid, so the approach was an imperfect use of a legitimate tool.
  2: No -- the fabrication crosses the ethical line named in the chapter. Amplifying real divergences is legitimate; manufacturing false ones through fabricated information is manipulation, regardless of the goal's legitimacy.
**Key:** 2 -- The chapter is explicit: the legitimate/manipulative distinction is in the information, not the goal. A worthy goal does not make fabricated information legitimate.
**Bloom:** evaluate
**Depth:** hard

## Q10 — Evaluate | Hard | correctIndex: 1
**Concept:** Ripening failure modes and limits
**Prompt:** Kalani has been running a ripening strategy for ten months, quietly removing conditions from an opponent's position. In month eleven, the opponent unexpectedly discovers what has been happening. What is the strategic consequence of discovery, and what should Kalani expect?
**Choices:**
  0: Discovery is unlikely to change the situation; the conditions have already been removed and the opponent cannot reverse that.
  1: Discovery converts the ripening strategy into a direct confrontation Kalani was not prepared for. The strategy depends on the opponent not understanding what is being done to them; once they do, they can respond actively rather than declining passively.
  2: Discovery is only a problem if the ripening involved illegitimate actions; if Kalani used legitimate means, the opponent has nothing actionable to respond to.
**Key:** 1 -- The reversal: ripening requires secrecy. Discovery collapses the strategy into direct engagement the operator structured around avoiding. Choice 2 is wrong -- even legitimate ripening, when discovered, allows the opponent to respond actively and counter the condition-removal before it is complete.
**Bloom:** evaluate
**Depth:** hard
