# Chapter Quiz Blueprint — Chapter 1

Chapter: The First Enemy Is You

Core concepts to test:
- concept 1: Self-directed warfare is foundational; external strategies presuppose it.
- concept 2: Naming opposition is diagnostic, not hostile; failure mode is paranoia.
- concept 3: The operator most successful at the previous war is least able to see when it has ended.
- concept 4: Presence of mind is a personal practice; the discipline is having one and using it.
- concept 5: Death ground applies to the comfortable; applied to the overwhelmed it produces collapse.
- concept 6 (deeper, q09–q10): The pairing of presence of mind and death ground is a diagnostic problem before it is a strategic one — knowing which state you are in is the prior skill.

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate (q09–q10 should ask the reader to weigh which move applies and at what cost, not merely transfer).

Question plan:
- q01 (remember/understand, depth=easy): basic recall — what is the chapter's foundational claim about the first opponent in any conflict?
- q02 (understand, depth=easy): which named character in the chapter is fighting the last war, and what is the specific symptom?
- q03 (understand, depth=easy): the polarity move is best described as which kind of discipline?
- q04 (apply, depth=medium, named scenario): a junior PM notices a senior peer reframing her quarterly numbers in meetings; which move from the chapter applies first?
- q05 (analyze, depth=medium, distinction/trap): a manager who runs his startup at "wartime mode" through year five misreads which strategy?
- q06 (analyze, depth=medium, limit/misreading): under what condition does the death-ground move produce collapse instead of performance?
- q07 (apply, depth=medium, transfer): a graduate student preparing for a thesis defense in a field that has shifted should apply which strategy and why?
- q08 (analyze, depth=medium, scenario judgment): given a person who feels calm and unhurried about a stalling project, which of the four moves is most likely the right intervention?
- q09 (evaluate, depth=hard, deeper synthesis): the pairing of Strategy 3 and Strategy 4 looks like contradictory advice; what makes the pairing coherent rather than contradictory?
- q10 (evaluate/create, depth=hard, threshold connection): the chapter sets up a forward question — given that every later strategy presupposes the four self-directed moves, what is the cost of running a later strategy from an unearned position? (This is the only book-internal forward connection available; Ch1 is the opener.)

Distractor notes:
- Tempting wrong answer type 1 ("paranoia masquerading as polarity"): treat the polarity move as a license to assume every colleague is an opponent.
- Tempting wrong answer type 2 ("self-help register"): treat presence of mind as a mindfulness practice rather than a strategic discipline.
- Tempting wrong answer type 3 ("universal pressure rule"): treat death ground as universally good or universally bad rather than state-dependent.
- Tempting wrong answer type 4 ("collapse the tension"): resolve the Strategy 3 / Strategy 4 pairing into a single rule like "find the right level of stress".

Explanation diversity notes:
- Avoid repeated openers across the 10 direct explanations.
- No two direct explanations may share four or more opening words.
- Banned openers: "The strongest answer", "The best answer", "The correct response", "Correct.", "Right.", "Exactly."
- Each explanation must explain why the correct answer wins AND why the wrong answer tempts.

CorrectIndex plan:
- Target distribution roughly balanced: ~3 / ~3 / ~4 across 0 / 1 / 2 (or any balanced spread). No more than 2 consecutive identical correctIndex values.
- Final distribution to be confirmed by validator after generation.
