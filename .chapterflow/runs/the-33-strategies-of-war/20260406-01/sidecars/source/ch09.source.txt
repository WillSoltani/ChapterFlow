# Source Sidecar -- Ch09: The Line of Least Expectation

## Source strategies covered
Strategies 23 (Misperception), 24 (Ordinary-Extraordinary), 26 (Void), 27 (Alliance), 29 (Fait Accompli). Part V Unconventional Warfare, first teaching chapter.

## Strategy 23 -- Misperception: Shape the Opponent's Picture (paraphrased)

The skill of choosing which real facts to emphasize so the audience reaches a conclusion the operator needs them to reach. The distinction that matters: strategic framing uses true information selectively. Propaganda invents false information. Both reshape the opponent's picture, but the first is legitimate positioning and the second is fabrication.

Reversal: the framing collapses if the selected facts are later shown to be misleading or incomplete in a way that was clearly intended to deceive. The operator's reputation for credibility (Ch8's load-bearing infrastructure) takes the damage.

## Strategy 24 -- Ordinary-Extraordinary: Break the Pattern (paraphrased)

Opponents predict your next move based on your previous moves. The discipline is to vary the texture: sometimes the most ordinary move when the extraordinary is expected, sometimes the extraordinary when the ordinary is expected. The pattern is the absence of pattern. The opponent who cannot predict the operator's next move must defend against everything, which is expensive and exhausting.

Reversal: unpredictability for its own sake becomes a pattern itself. The opponent learns to expect the unexpected and adjusts. The discipline must be used selectively, not compulsively.

## Strategy 26 -- The Void: Deny Targets (paraphrased)

When weaker, the operator makes themselves diffuse, indirect, and hard to locate. No fixed position to attack, no central organization to disrupt, no public face to disgrace. The opponent's attacks dissipate into empty space. The guerrilla principle: the weaker party survives by refusing to present the concentrated target the stronger party needs.

Reversal: the void strategy requires giving up concentration and visible authority. The operator who becomes perfectly diffuse may also become unable to project power when the opportunity arises.

## Strategy 27 -- Alliance: Align with Stronger Parties (paraphrased)

The skill of finding arrangements where the operator's interests and the interests of a more powerful party genuinely overlap, so the more powerful party advances the operator while pursuing their own goals. The legitimate form finds real overlap. The manipulative form manufactures the appearance of overlap where none exists.

Reversal: the alliance collapses when the stronger party realizes the overlap is manufactured. The genuine-overlap version is more durable because both parties benefit when the facts are transparent.

## Strategy 29 -- Fait Accompli: Small Bites (paraphrased)

The opponent who would resist a single large move will not resist a sequence of small ones. Each individual move is beneath the threshold at which opposition mobilizes. The cumulative effect of many small moves reaches the same destination as the large move, and by the time the opponent realizes what has been built, the situation is past the point where reversal is practical.

Reversal: an opponent who recognizes the pattern early can mobilize before the accumulation reaches the tipping point. The strategy depends on each move being genuinely small enough to avoid triggering the response threshold.

## Connecting thread
All five strategies share the principle of asymmetry: the operator who cannot or chooses not to confront the opponent directly reshapes the field through selective framing, unpredictability, diffusion, alignment with stronger parties, and incremental accumulation. The underlying logic is that direct engagement is not always the most efficient path, especially when the operator is weaker.

## Moral flag
High. This is the first chapter where the ethical line between legitimate strategy and manipulation is most likely to blur. Strategic framing vs. propaganda. Genuine alliance vs. manufactured overlap. Each strategy has a legitimate form and a manipulative form, and the chapter must name both explicitly.
