# Chapter Brief — Ch06: Speed, Initiative, and the Decisive Point

## Core thesis
Three offensive disciplines that decide fast campaigns. Speed cycles the opponent's decision loop so that their responses are always targeting a position you have already left. Initiative forces the opponent to react to your terms rather than executing their own plan. The decisive point is the opponent's center of gravity — the one node whose loss collapses everything else — and going for it rather than the more visible but cheaper secondary targets is what makes offense efficient rather than merely aggressive.

## Anchor requirements

### Primary anchor 1: Phoebe
Context: Phoebe runs a small B2B software company competing against a slower, better-resourced incumbent. The incumbent's product is better and their sales team is larger. Phoebe's advantage is speed: she can decide in a week what takes the incumbent a quarter. The chapter's speed section is built around her decision tempo, not around any single heroic fast decision. Key detail: when the incumbent noticed her and moved to block, she had already secured two of their top accounts and a distribution channel the incumbent assumed was locked. The blocking move was targeting a position she had already vacated.

### Primary anchor 2: Declan
Context: Declan is a VP of product at a mid-size fintech challenging a dominant legacy provider. He does not attack the legacy system's interface, documentation, or pricing (the most visible targets and the ones the provider is prepared to defend). He attacks the assumption underneath the provider's whole model: that compliance reviews require human-in-the-loop sign-offs. Declan's platform automates the compliance layer. The provider's entire competitive advantage — depth of human expertise — is built on the assumption Declan targeted. Everything the provider does well depends on that assumption remaining true. Key detail: Declan identified this through the intelligence work described in Ch5 (he spent six weeks talking to the legacy provider's customers about their actual workflows).
