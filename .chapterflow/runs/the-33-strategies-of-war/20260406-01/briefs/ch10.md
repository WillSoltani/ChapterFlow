## Core teaching
The closing chapter handles the highest-cost and highest-ethical-risk strategies in Greene's system. The reader should leave with three things: a clear picture of what these edge-case strategies do in legitimate form, an equally clear picture of the ethical limits Greene underplays, and a final judgment about when to put the framework down entirely. The chapter's deeper claim is recursive: the first opponent was the self in Ch1, and the final test is whether the self can refuse a game it could win.

## Breakdown anchors (2)
1. **Leona** -- Chief operating officer at a regional food distributor. A supplier falsifies safety logs, threatens to blame Leona's company publicly, and leaves behind enough evidence for Leona to coordinate a full annihilation response: lawsuit, regulator packet, customer notices, lender calls, and board briefings that would leave the supplier no exit. She chooses a narrower move. She uses only what the record can support, keeps the moral claim proportional, and lets the supplier's own contradictions finish the case instead of feeding panic. He stops because the strategic frame is wrong. Carries inner-front, passive-aggression, one-upmanship, and the explicit no-framework zones: grief, intimacy, and family systems where "winning" would poison what remains.

## Ethical notes
- **Highest moral flag in the book.**
- Annihilation: legitimate when decisive advantage exists, repeated harm must be stopped, and leaving exits open means renewed damage. Illegitimate when used for humiliation, overkill, or convenience.
- Righteous posture: legitimate when the record is actually clean enough to bear scrutiny. Illegitimate when it is theater masking hypocrisy.
- One-upmanship: legitimate when the rival is already collapsing under their own contradictions and attacking would rescue them. Illegitimate when the operator baits or inflames the collapse.
- Communication: legitimate when persuasion respects agency and survives scrutiny. Illegitimate when the operator tries to bypass judgment through concealment, distortion, or emotional flooding.
- Inner-front: legitimate when exposing real contradictions in a dangerous or abusive structure. Illegitimate when normal disagreement is poisoned for private gain.
- Passive-aggression: legitimate as covert self-protection under domination or retaliation risk. Illegitimate when used to dodge honest conflict that should be addressed openly.
- Chain-reaction: legitimate as an ethical, dramatic move that forces a strategic reappraisal. Illegitimate as intimidation, panic-seeding, or repeated spectacle.
- The chapter must explicitly name the no-framework zones: intimacy, grief, genuine collaboration, and any situation where the counterparty is not actually an opponent.
