# Chapter Brief — Ch08: Negotiated Outcomes and Clean Exits

## Source strategies
Strategy 21 (Diplomatic-War: Negotiate While Advancing), Strategy 22 (Exit Strategy: Know How to End Things). Part IV Offensive Warfare, closing pair.

## Core teaching
Two underappreciated offensive skills. First: pair pressure with a sincere negotiating channel so the opponent always has a face-saving path to settlement. Second: know when and how to stop -- honor pre-committed exit conditions even when every psychological pressure says keep going. Both strategies depend on credibility. The exit must be real; the pre-committed condition must be honored. The operator's future capacity rests on whether stated positions can be trusted.

## Breakdown anchors (2)
1. **Hana** — A litigation director who escalates legal pressure on a competitor's patent claim while simultaneously keeping a genuine settlement channel open. The settlement offer is real: specific terms, real concessions, face-saving framing for the competitor. The pressure produces movement because the exit is visible and credible. Carries the negotiate-while-advancing discipline.
2. **Vaughn** — A program director at a tech company who sets explicit exit conditions for a major platform migration before the project begins. When the conditions are met (cost overrun exceeds 40%, user adoption below threshold at month 6), he honors them despite team investment and exec pressure to continue. Carries the pre-committed exit discipline.

## Ethical notes
- **Medium-high moral flag.** The ethical line is whether the exit is real or simulated.
- Sincere exit offer that gives the opponent a genuine path to settlement = legitimate paired pressure. Fake exit offer designed to waste time while advancing = manipulation.
- The fake exit, once discovered, poisons all future negotiations. This is both the moral argument and the strategic argument for sincerity.
- Pre-committed exit conditions that are publicly named and not honored = credibility damage. The operator's word becomes unreliable in all subsequent engagements.
- Medium breakdown: name the ethical line once (sincere vs. fake exit). Hard breakdown: full treatment -- why fake exits fail strategically (not just morally), why publicly broken commitments destroy future capacity, boundary conditions for when persistence is correct despite pre-committed exit conditions.
