# Chapter Brief -- Ch09: The Line of Least Expectation

## Source strategies
Strategies 23 (Misperception), 24 (Ordinary-Extraordinary), 26 (Void), 27 (Alliance), 29 (Fait Accompli). Part V Unconventional Warfare, first teaching chapter.

## Core teaching
Five unconventional strategies that share the principle of asymmetry. The operator who is weaker or who has better uses for their resources than direct confrontation reshapes the field through: selective framing of real facts (misperception), unpredictability in the texture of moves (ordinary-extraordinary), diffusion so the opponent has no concentrated target (void), alignment with stronger parties whose interests genuinely overlap (alliance), and incremental accumulation of small moves below the opposition threshold (fait accompli). The underlying logic: direct engagement is not always the most efficient path.

## Breakdown anchors (2)
1. **Margot** -- A communications director at a mid-size nonprofit competing for grant funding against much larger national organizations. She cannot match their scale, so she reshapes the field: frames her organization's local outcomes using real data the national orgs cannot match (misperception/framing), varies her approach to funder meetings so competitors cannot predict her pitch pattern (ordinary-extraordinary), and builds a genuine alliance with a university research center whose publication interests align with her program's outcomes (alliance). Carries the asymmetric-suite teaching.
2. **Ravi** -- A product manager at a startup entering a market dominated by two incumbents. He runs the fait accompli: a sequence of small product integrations with adjacent platforms, each individually too small for the incumbents to respond to, that cumulatively build an ecosystem position the incumbents cannot easily replicate. He also applies the void principle: the startup has no fixed position for the incumbents to target because it operates across integration seams rather than competing on any single dimension. Carries the accumulation and diffusion teaching.

## Ethical notes
- **High moral flag.** This chapter covers the strategies most likely to blur the line between legitimate positioning and manipulation.
- The ethical line for each strategy:
  - Misperception: strategic framing using real facts = legitimate. Fabricating facts or inventing false information = propaganda.
  - Alliance: finding genuine interest overlap = legitimate. Manufacturing the appearance of overlap where none exists = manipulation.
  - Fait accompli: accumulating small legitimate moves = positioning. Accumulating small deceptive moves = manipulation by increments.
  - Void: making yourself diffuse and hard to target = legitimate positioning. Using diffusion to evade accountability = evasion.
  - Ordinary-extraordinary: varying texture to avoid predictability = legitimate. Varying texture to create confusion for its own sake = waste.
- Medium breakdown: name the ethical line once per strategy (framing vs. propaganda, genuine vs. manufactured overlap).
- Hard breakdown: full treatment -- why the legitimate forms are more durable strategically (same structural argument as Ch7 and Ch8: manufactured situations are fragile, genuine situations are self-sustaining).
