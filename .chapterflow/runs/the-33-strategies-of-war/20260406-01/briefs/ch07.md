# Chapter Brief — Ch07: Indirection: Flank, Wedge, Long Maneuver

## Core thesis
Three offensive disciplines that require no direct force. The wedge move: find the genuine divergence of interest within an opposing coalition and amplify it until the alliance fractures, then address the pieces separately. The flank move: attack the soft, unprepared side rather than the strong defended front, which is cheaper, faster, and causes the opponent to degrade their frontal defense to respond. The long maneuver: remove the conditions the opponent depends on, one by one, until they have no good options left and collapse on their own. None of these moves require direct confrontation; all require patience and accurate intelligence.

The chapter's central ethical obligation: every indirect move has a legitimate form and a manipulative form. Strategic use of real information and real divergences is legitimate. Fabrication, manufactured conflict, and coercive interference are not. The hard breakdown must name the line explicitly.

## Anchor requirements

### Primary anchor 1: Maisie
Context: Maisie is a senior government affairs director working against a well-funded industry coalition trying to block a regulation her organization supports. The coalition has 12 members who publicly present a unified front. Maisie's research shows that three of the smaller members are uncomfortable with one specific provision in the coalition's public position that would harm their own regulatory standing. She does not invent this discomfort; she finds it through conversations and public filings. She selectively provides information to those three members that makes their specific conflict of interest more visible to them -- and to the coalition's larger members. The coalition fractures on the provision. The block fails. Key detail: Maisie never fabricated any information. She found a genuine seam and made it visible.

### Primary anchor 2: Theron
Context: Theron is the director of a small regional nonprofit that has been slowly losing ground to a much larger national organization that has been entering their service territory. Rather than competing directly for the same clients and funders, Theron spends 18 months quietly doing three things: building direct relationships with the community leaders who provide credibility to anyone operating in the territory (the national org has not built these), getting a seat on the committee that sets the service standards the funders require, and shifting the funder's preferred metrics slightly toward outcomes the regional org produces well and the national org does not. By month 18, the national org's entry into the territory looks more expensive and less credible than it did at month 1. They quietly de-prioritize the territory. The national org was not attacked; the conditions that would have made their entry easy were quietly removed. Key detail: everything Theron did was legitimate -- building real relationships, participating in standards-setting, providing honest input on metrics.
