# Chapter Outline — Ch08: Negotiated Outcomes and Clean Exits

## Opening question (P1)
Not stated directly. Opens on Hana's situation: a litigation director escalating legal pressure while keeping a genuine settlement channel open. The live tension: how does paired pressure and genuine exit produce movement that pressure alone cannot?

## Paragraph map (7 paragraphs)

### P1 -- Hook / The two disciplines
Opens on Hana's case: patent dispute, escalating legal pressure, and a settlement offer that is specific, real, and face-saving for the opponent. The chapter's two disciplines introduced through the concrete: negotiate while advancing (Hana's move), and the pre-committed exit (introduced, carried by Vaughn later). The underlying logic: both disciplines depend on credibility. Pressure without a genuine exit is coercion; exit conditions without the discipline to honor them are theater.

### P2 -- Negotiate while advancing: paired pressure and sincere exit
The first discipline explained through Hana. Talking and fighting are not opposites. The skilled operator does both simultaneously. The settlement channel works because the opponent faces two things at once: the escalating cost of resistance and the visible alternative of settlement that ends the cost. The face-saving exit is load-bearing infrastructure, not a courtesy. An opponent who sees no path to settlement that preserves some dignity will fight to the end regardless of cost. Named point: the sincerity of the exit is the mechanism. A sincere exit turns pressure into leverage. A denied exit turns pressure into desperation.

### P3 -- The fake exit reversal
The reversal of the negotiate-while-advancing discipline, stated explicitly. The strategy collapses if the negotiating channel is not sincere. An opponent who discovers the exit offer is fake will fight to the end -- and the operator loses the leverage the exit was supposed to provide. The ethical line named here: sincere exit offer = legitimate paired pressure. Fake exit = manipulation. The strategic argument for sincerity: the fake exit, once discovered, poisons all future negotiations. Observers learn that the operator's offers cannot be trusted.

### P4 -- The exit discipline: pre-committed conditions
The second discipline explained through Vaughn. Before the campaign begins, the operator defines what conditions would trigger exit. The difficulty: every psychological force pushes against exit when the moment arrives. Sunk-cost reasoning, public commitment, personal identification with the project, the narrative that one more push will produce the breakthrough. Vaughn's pre-committed conditions (cost overrun threshold, adoption threshold at month 6) and his honoring of them despite team investment and exec pressure. Named point: the pre-commitment matters because in the moment, the psychology of continued investment overwhelms rational assessment.

### P5 -- The public commitment trap and credibility
The harder reversal: what happens when exit conditions are named publicly and not honored. The operator who publicly states a limit and then violates it teaches every future opponent and observer that their stated limits are not real limits. Future negotiations begin from that lesson. The credibility cost is not a one-time penalty -- it is a structural reduction in the operator's strategic capacity. Brief callback to Ch5 (grand strategy): the exit serves the long view. Without the long view, exit looks like quitting. With it, exit becomes a move that preserves resources for a more important engagement.

### P6 -- Hard depth: the full ethical analysis of paired pressure and exit
The chapter's moral spine. Legitimate form of negotiate-while-advancing: real pressure paired with a real exit that serves both parties' interests in ending the engagement on tolerable terms. Illegitimate form: fake pressure designed to panic, paired with a fake exit designed to waste time. The distinction is not in the pressure itself but in the sincerity of the alternative. For the exit discipline: honoring the pre-committed exit is not only moral discipline -- it is load-bearing infrastructure for future negotiations. The operator who breaks their own exit conditions gains a short-term continuation and loses the structural capacity to make credible commitments in every subsequent engagement. Boundary condition: there are situations where persistence past the exit condition is correct because the conditions were poorly drawn or the situation has changed materially. The discipline is to distinguish between a genuinely changed situation and the sunk-cost psychology that makes every exit feel premature.

### P7 -- Bridge
Both disciplines share a common requirement: the operator's word has to mean something. The negotiating exit and the pre-committed stop are both credibility instruments. When they are honored, the operator builds the kind of reputation that makes future strategies possible. The prior chapter's indirection disciplines moved around. These move through -- pressure paired with settlement, commitment paired with the discipline to stop. The next chapter addresses what happens when the negotiating channel fails and escalation becomes live: counter-force, escalation management, and operating inside chaos.
