Chapter Promise:
By the end of the chapter the reader understands that leadership through other people is three different disciplines (give mission instead of method, segment monoliths into autonomous units, bind the work to a cause the leader actually believes), and that all three depend on a prior diagnostic — whether the people the leader is about to trust are actually ready to receive that trust.

She has the cursor in the middle of a sentence she has rewritten four times. The room is the moment of decision, not the result.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Hook. Naya at her desk on a Friday evening, the memo, the rewrites. The room is small and the stakes are visible.
- P2: Frame. The chapter's claim. Leadership through other people is three disciplines, all of which trust subordinates with work the leader cannot do, and the diagnostic of whether the trust is earned is the work that comes before any of them.
- P3: Strategy 5 (mission-not-method). Naya's experiment, the two senior managers asking for the old instructions back, the line she holds. The silence-wins mechanism. The reversal: novices and crisis terrain still need command-and-control, and giving them mission is abdication.
- P4: Strategy 6 (segmentation). Pivot to Tomas. The 90-person climate-software company, the three pods of fifteen, the VP who quits in protest. The principle that small autonomous units can fail without bringing the whole down.
- P5: The seven shipped features by month nine. The reversal: segmented incompetents are still incompetents.
- P6: Strategy 7 (crusade). Camila at the all-staff meeting. The specific words she does not use. The two senior staff who leave in six weeks, the eight who stay, the most effective campaign in a decade. The reversal as the chapter's most important sentence: this works because she believed it; manufactured belief is detected and produces cynicism worse than none.
- P7: The unifying tension. All three moves are different shapes of trust the leader extends to people the leader cannot replace. The diagnostic that comes first is whether the trust is earned. Bridge to Ch3: the next chapter turns from how to lead a team toward which fights the team will fight at all.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The earned-trust diagnostic. The chapter's analytical spine is that mission-not-method, segmentation, and crusade are not neutral tools. Each places a specific trust in subordinates (judgment, autonomy, belief), and each fails in a specific way when the trust is misplaced (paralysis, fragmentation, cynicism). The diagnostic step that tells the leader whether her people can carry the trust she is about to extend is the move underneath all three moves.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The leader's instinct to push initiative downward can be either leadership or abdication, and from inside, the two feel the same. The same is true of segmentation (devolution vs. fragmentation) and crusade (sincerity vs. performance). Hard depth must hold the question: how does the leader run the diagnostic on herself before extending trust she may not have earned the right to extend?

Threshold Question:
"When you are about to push initiative, autonomy, or belief downward to your team, how do you tell whether you are leading or abdicating, and what does the cost of guessing wrong look like in each direction?"

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: Leadership through other people is a different discipline from self-directed work, and the three moves of this chapter are not interchangeable.
- Takeaway 2: Mission-not-method works only when the people receiving the mission have enough context to act on judgment; otherwise it is abdication.
- Takeaway 3: Segmentation works only when each unit is genuinely capable; segmenting incompetents produces a slower version of the same problem.
- Takeaway 4: A team bound to a cause it actually shares with the leader will outwork a team paid for the same hours, and the leader's sincerity is the gating variable.
- Takeaway 5: The single most expensive failure in this chapter is the manufactured crusade — it produces cynicism worse than no crusade at all and cannot be repaired without replacing the leader.
- Takeaway 6: The earned-trust diagnostic is the prior skill underneath all three moves; without it, the moves still fire, but they fire at the wrong people and the leader does not see the misalignment until the costs are sunk.
