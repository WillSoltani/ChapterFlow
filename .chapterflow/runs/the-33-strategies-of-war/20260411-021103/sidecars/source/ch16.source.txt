Greene's center-of-gravity chapter says the decisive target is rarely the most visible one. Every opponent has a load-bearing point: the fact, alliance, assumption, supply line, confidence source, or political support that keeps the rest of the structure standing. The operator wins by attacking that point instead of wasting force on impressive but secondary targets.

The practical skill is diagnosis. You ask what, if weakened, would make everything else harder for the opponent to sustain. The modern version is attacking the assumption beneath a rival's whole strategy rather than its loudest surface strength.

The reversal is severe. If you misidentify the center of gravity, the attack is wasted and can leave you exposed. Greene ties this chapter back to intelligence for that reason. Better targeting depends on a truer map first.
