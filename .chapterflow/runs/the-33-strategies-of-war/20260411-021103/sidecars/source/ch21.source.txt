Strategy 21 turns diplomacy into a form of pressure rather than a pause from pressure. The operator keeps the channel open while advancing materially, so the opponent experiences both the cost of continued conflict and the visible off-ramp that would end that cost. Settlement becomes attractive because it feels like relief rather than humiliation.

The modern versions are straightforward. A company opens the legal or public-pressure track while privately offering terms the counterparty can actually accept. A negotiator escalates visible pressure but pairs it with a face-saving path that ends the pain. The key is simultaneity. Talking and pressing are not opposites here. They are coordinated.

The reversal is sincerity. If the exit is fake, insulting, or impossible to take, the whole strategy breaks. The opponent realizes the channel is decorative, concludes the operator wants surrender rather than settlement, and chooses to fight harder instead of yielding. The teaching emphasis should stay on real off-ramps, not theatrical diplomacy.
