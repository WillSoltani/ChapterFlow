Supported source notes:

1. People work harder for a cause than for a job.
   - The source supports the claim that morale rises when work carries a meaning larger than the task list.
   - The team identity has to attach to that meaning, not merely to compensation or routine.

2. Morale has mechanics.
   - The source supports a named opposing side, which may be a problem, idea, or competitor instead of a literal enemy.
   - The source also supports a named cause and leader behavior that visibly embodies the cause.

3. Long campaigns reveal the difference.
   - The source supports morale as a decisive factor in work that demands sustained effort, patience, and pain tolerance.
   - Mission language changes how teams describe the same labor.

4. The reversal is sincerity.
   - A manufactured crusade fails when the leader does not truly believe the cause.
   - The source treats cynical mission language as worse than no crusade because it breeds distrust.
