Strategy 20 is about creating weakness indirectly. Instead of striking immediately, the operator makes a sequence of smaller moves that leaves the opponent with worse and worse options until the final bad choice is made by the opponent itself. The decisive move is often delayed. The setup does the work.

The modern examples make the mechanism concrete. A negotiator structures the early phases of a deal so that the counterparty's only live option later is to accept terms that would have been rejected at the start. A competitor shapes conditions over years so the rival's eventual decline looks self-caused. The key is not one dramatic hit but careful positioning that narrows the opponent's future choices.

The reversal is patience. Greene warns that the strategy requires a longer time horizon than most operators can hold. If the operator becomes impatient and strikes too early, the slow shaping work is wasted. If the operator waits without actually narrowing the opponent's options, the strategy turns into passive fantasy. The teaching emphasis should therefore stay on deliberate option-shaping, not passive delay.
