Greene's counterattack strategy says the strongest return often comes just after an opponent has attacked. In that moment they have already committed forces, exposed intent, and tilted themselves off balance through the energy of their own move. The operator's job is not instant retaliation. It is controlled absorption. Stay calm long enough to see the shape of the attack, notice what the attacker has revealed, and then move through the opening the attack created.

The timing rule is strict. Counter too early and the opponent has not fully committed, so they can adjust without paying much. Counter too late and they have regained balance, so the opening closes. The chapter therefore treats patience as tactical discipline rather than softness.

The modern applications are concrete: let an accusation overextend before answering with evidence, or let an extreme first demand become the basis for a stronger counteroffer. The limit matters too. Counterattack depends on being attacked first. If the opponent is content to grind slowly without overcommitting, waiting forever turns into attrition loss instead of strategic patience.
