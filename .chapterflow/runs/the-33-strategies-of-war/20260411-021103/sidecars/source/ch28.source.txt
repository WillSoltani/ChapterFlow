Strategy 28 shifts from alliance to disciplined noninterference. Some rivals are already on a path to self-destruction, and the operator gains more by giving them room than by attacking. The key move is recognizing when pressure from outside would rescue the rival by unifying factions or clarifying purpose.

The source makes the chapter sharper than simple patience. The operator does not passively wait in every contest. The operator identifies rivals whose ego, internal conflict, or overreach is already driving collapse, then avoids the attack that would interrupt that collapse. Instead, the operator lets the rival take what it wants, watches the burden accumulate, and steps into the opening left behind.

The fair modern reading is strategic restraint around self-defeating opponents. The package should separate smart patience from cowardly avoidance. The point is not to do nothing. The point is to avoid the move that would save the rival from its own momentum.
