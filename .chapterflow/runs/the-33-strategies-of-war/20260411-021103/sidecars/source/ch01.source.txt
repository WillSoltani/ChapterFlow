Supported source notes:

1. Part I sets the frame.
   - The book opens by treating the self as the first opponent.
   - Strategy 1 sits inside that larger claim: clarity about opposition is part of self-mastery.

2. Strategy 1 is about naming real opposition.
   - The supported logic is that people often refuse to admit who is actually working against their interests because the admission feels uncivil.
   - The cost of that refusal is late, weak, or confused action.
   - The skill is to identify the specific people whose interests are actively opposed to yours and stop relabeling the conflict as a misunderstanding.

3. The modern translation is structural, not theatrical.
   - The frozen source supports a narrow modern reading: in an organization, a small number of people may advance only if you stall.
   - The move is not to attack them for emotional satisfaction.
   - The move is to know who they are so you stop exposing yourself to avoidable harm.

4. The reversal is mandatory.
   - The strategy fails when applied indiscriminately.
   - Treating everyone as an enemy turns the move into paranoia.
   - The supported limit is explicit: the point is to identify real opposition, not manufacture it.
