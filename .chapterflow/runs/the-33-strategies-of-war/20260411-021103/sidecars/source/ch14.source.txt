Greene's blitzkrieg chapter treats speed as a weapon in its own right. When one side can decide and execute faster than the other side can process what is happening, it cycles through several moves before resistance fully forms. The slower side ends up answering positions that no longer exist.

The practical skill is operational tempo: fast observation, orientation, decision, and action linked tightly enough that the opponent keeps lagging behind the current reality. Greene's modern version is the startup or competitor whose speed is structural rather than theatrical.

The reversal is necessary. Speed by itself is not intelligence. Fast movement in the wrong direction only produces wrong outcomes sooner. The chapter assumes that tempo is paired with judgment, not substituted for it.
