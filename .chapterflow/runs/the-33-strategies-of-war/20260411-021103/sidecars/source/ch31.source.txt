Strategy 31 shifts from persuasion to inner fracture. Some opponents cannot be beaten cleanly from the outside because external force only strengthens their cohesion. They break only when their own contradictions, factions, and mistrust become unmanageable. The strategic move is to identify those internal tensions and apply pressure that amplifies them. The source itself puts this close to subversion, and the teaching prose has to keep that moral proximity visible. The legitimate modern reading is narrow: exposing real contradictions, surfacing information, and refusing to artificially unify a system that is already divided against itself. The illegitimate reading is corrosive manipulation for its own sake: manufacturing mistrust, planting falsehoods, or treating every group as something to fracture from within. The chapter should teach diagnosis, limits, and the difference between revealing internal instability and creating rot where it was not already present.
