Strategy 18 says every defense has a strong front and a weak flank. Attacking the front is expensive and slow, while locating the flank is cheaper and faster. The hard part is discipline: the front is obvious, but the flank often requires reconnaissance and patience. The modern versions are competitive and negotiated. One operator refuses to challenge the incumbent on its strongest dimension and instead attacks the dimension where the incumbent cannot adapt. Another ignores the counterparty's rehearsed issue and presses the one they did not prepare for. The reversal matters. Some defenses really do have no soft flank. When every approach is covered, the search for a weak side becomes a delay that costs initiative.
