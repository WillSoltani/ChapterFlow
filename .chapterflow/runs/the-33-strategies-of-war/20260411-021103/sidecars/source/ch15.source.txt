Greene's forcing chapter shifts from raw speed to initiative. The operator does not simply react well. She sets the time, the place, the order of discussion, and the pace so the opponent is constantly answering a frame she chose first.

The practical skill is control of the dynamic. Agenda, sequence, meeting design, deadlines, and framing all become tools for making the other side play on your field instead of theirs. Greene's modern example is the negotiator or leader who structures the engagement before the engagement appears to start.

The reversal matters. Initiative without purpose becomes random aggression. Taking the lead only helps if the terms you impose actually serve the larger goal rather than satisfying ego or restlessness.
