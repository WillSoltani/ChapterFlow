Strategy 17 says a united opponent is stronger than the sum of its parts, while a divided opponent is weaker than the sum of its parts. The operator therefore avoids smashing the whole coalition head on. She studies where members already differ, drives a wedge into that seam, and then deals with each fragment after the alliance loosens. The modern versions are political, commercial, and organizational: peeling one member away over an issue that strains the coalition, or amplifying a contract term that makes a partnership uncomfortable. The reversal is sharp. A clumsy wedge can do the opposite and create solidarity among groups that were only loosely aligned. The skill lies in the delicacy of the split, not the noise of the attack.
