Strategy 19 presents envelopment, here rendered as annihilation in the native chapter list. The move is to deny the opponent viable directions of retreat, support, or recomposition by surrounding the structure they depend on instead of merely striking one exposed edge. The source examples point toward total political or operational isolation: cut off constituencies, routes, or dependencies until the rival has nowhere useful to turn.

The logic is severe. A partial attack leaves fallback paths. Envelopment removes fallback by coordinating pressure across multiple sides at once. The strategy therefore belongs to moments when the operator already has decisive advantage, enough resources to sustain the full move, and a reason strong enough to justify closing every escape route.

The reversal is the chapter's real guardrail. Envelopment is high-cost and high-risk. It commits the attacker's resources fully and leaves almost no reserve if the encirclement fails. Operators who attempt it without the material advantage to complete it expose themselves catastrophically. In teaching terms, the strategy should be framed less as a glamorous offensive and more as a final, expensive closing move against an entrenched opponent who will keep regenerating if left any path out.
