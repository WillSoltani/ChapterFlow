Strategy 22 is about ending a campaign before sunk cost and identity trap the operator inside it. The difficult part is not understanding that some campaigns should end. It is acting on that understanding when prior investment, public commitment, and ego all argue for staying in too long. The skill is to exit on the best terms still available while those terms still exist.

The modern examples make the problem concrete. A founder shuts down the failing product line at the right moment instead of carrying it until it damages the whole company. A professional leaves the role at the right time instead of staying until the relationship rots. The chapter treats exit as a discipline that has to be decided partly in advance, before the campaign begins, because later psychology will resist it.

The reversal matters. Sometimes persistence is correct and the apparent exit signals are misleading. The judgment is whether continuing truly costs more than stopping, or whether fear and fatigue only make it feel that way. The teaching emphasis should stay on precommitted exit conditions, sunk-cost resistance, and the need to distinguish real deterioration from temporary discomfort.
