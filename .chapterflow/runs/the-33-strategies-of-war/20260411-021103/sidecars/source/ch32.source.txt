Strategy 32 is built for asymmetric weakness. A weaker operator who openly resists a stronger adversary loses too early. The strategic alternative is outward compliance combined with hidden construction: parallel relationships, quiet capacity, and leverage that grows without looking like a challenge. The source modernizes this through junior employees, insurgent competitors, and blind-spot disruption. The legitimate reading is careful and narrow. Sometimes a weaker actor really does need concealment because direct confrontation would be crushed before any viable alternative exists. The illegitimate reading is simply bad faith: perform submission while quietly undermining legitimate authority or trust. The teaching prose has to hold both truths at once. Concealed capacity can be prudent under domination or asymmetry. It can also become corrosive duplicity when used where direct good-faith disagreement was available.
