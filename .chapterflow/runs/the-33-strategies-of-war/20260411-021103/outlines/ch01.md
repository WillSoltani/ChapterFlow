# Chapter 1 Outline

Chapter ID: ch01-the-polarity-strategy-declare-war-on-your-enemies
Chapter Title: The Polarity Strategy: Declare War on Your Enemies

Paragraph 1:
Open inside Talia's reorganization meeting. She sees a slide that "consolidates duplication" and realizes the other division head's calm language only works if her team disappears. Concrete pressure first.

Paragraph 2:
Define the chapter's move. Enemy means structural opposition, not personal dislike. Politeness often hides the fact that someone's gain depends on your loss.

Paragraph 3:
Explain how readers misread opposition. They relabel repeated moves as misunderstanding, style difference, or temporary friction because naming conflict feels ugly.

Paragraph 4:
Show the practical test. Look at incentives, repeated patterns, what information keeps leaking, and who benefits when your position weakens. Add the Talia consequence.

Paragraph 5:
Use Soraya as the contrast case. She is tempted to call a classmate an enemy, but the incentives are actually shared. This paragraph teaches the reversal and prevents paranoia.

Paragraph 6:
Name legitimate applications and limits. Promotions, negotiations, litigation, elections, and zero-sum competitions reward clarity. Intimacy, genuine collaboration, and shared-build settings do not.

Paragraph 7:
Close on the threshold question: what does it cost to name the conflict too late, and what does it cost to name it where it is not real? Bridge to Chapter 2, where the reader has to stop fighting the last war as well as stop misnaming the current one.
