# The Divide-and-Conquer Strategy: Defeat Them in Detail

1. Open on Aveline refusing to argue with a vendor coalition as if it were one mind. She notices two members disagree over liability exposure.
2. Define Greene's claim: a united opponent is stronger than the sum of its parts, so the fight is against the alignment, not only the members.
3. Explain wedge mechanics. The operator studies seams, incentives, and discomforts, then widens an existing split instead of inventing a random provocation.
4. Return to Aveline and show how peeling one member away changes the bargaining field from one block into separate, weaker conversations.
5. Shift to Maceo in a graduate coalition governance seminar. He sees a reform bloc lose unity once the enforcement disagreement is forced into the open.
6. Deliver the reversal: clumsy wedge work can harden solidarity. Delicacy matters because a loud attempt can turn a loose coalition into a committed one.
7. Bridge forward. Chapter 16 found the hinge inside one system. Chapter 17 breaks a coalition into pieces. Chapter 18 will attack the soft flank of what remains.
