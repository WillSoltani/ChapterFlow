1. Open on Amina launching the formal compliance step while still keeping a private repair offer alive.
2. Define diplomatic war as pressure-plus-exit: advance materially while keeping a live settlement lane open.
3. Explain why the exit must feel usable and face-saving or the whole strategy turns fake.
4. Return to Amina and show why the vendor accepts once continued pressure and real relief are visible at the same time.
5. Shift to Davin in a graduate infrastructure finance seminar, where bargaining starts only after every escalation is paired with a settlement path the other side can actually take.
6. Deliver the reversal: decorative negotiation hardens the opponent because a fake exit feels like disguised surrender.
7. Bridge forward. Chapter 20 narrowed future options quietly. Chapter 21 negotiates under pressure. Chapter 22 will decide when the campaign should end.
