Core Claim:
The chapter argues that the decisive target is usually not the most visible one. The operator wins by identifying the load-bearing point whose loss weakens the rest of the opponent's structure.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 15 controlled the dynamic of the engagement. Chapter 16 narrows the lens to target selection: where to apply force so the whole system starts failing.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter defines center of gravity as the decisive support point. Then it modernizes the move through assumptions, credibility, and strategic dependencies. Then it shows how operators waste energy when they attack visible strength instead of load-bearing structure. Finally it gives the reversal: misidentifying the center of gravity is fatal because the strike leaves you exposed and out of position.

Required Anchors:
- Inara, a market strategist, stops attacking a rival's flagship product and instead attacks the assumption that the flagship depends on: guaranteed premium retention. The friction is that the obvious target is more tempting and easier to explain. The consequence is that the rival's whole product stack becomes less stable once the assumption breaks.
- Lucan, in a graduate-health-policy-workshop, realizes that the opposing proposal stands or falls on one disputed data premise rather than on its polished moral language. The friction is that everyone wants to answer the loud surface claims. The consequence is that once the premise is undermined, the rest of the case weakens quickly.

- resisting the lure of obvious but nondecisive targets

Common Misreadings:
- confusing visibility with decisiveness
- assuming the strongest-looking target is the decisive one
- treating every weakness as equally important
- attacking a symbolic point that does not actually move the system

Counterarguments or Limits:
- diagnosis can be wrong and the cost of error is high
- the center of gravity may shift over time
- some systems have multiple supports and require sequence rather than one strike
- the strategy depends on accurate intelligence from Chapter 13
