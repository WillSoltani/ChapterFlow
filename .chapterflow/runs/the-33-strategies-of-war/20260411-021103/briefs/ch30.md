Core Claim:
Communication becomes strategic when it is shaped for how the audience thinks and decides rather than for how the speaker wants to sound.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 29 worked through quiet accumulation below reaction threshold. Chapter 30 works inside the receiver's mind, where language and framing can change later choices without changing the external structure first.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter defines strategic communication as speech, writing, or signaling that gets inside another person's decision frame. Then it makes the fair-versus-unfair split explicit: respectful persuasion versus manipulation. Then it modernizes the move through leadership, teaching, and negotiation settings. Finally it gives the reversal: if the message only works by bypassing informed judgment, the operator has crossed from persuasion into control.

Required Anchors:
- Nerina has to win support for a difficult internal shift, but her first memo fails because it is written from her perspective, not from the audience's fears, language, or incentives. She rewrites it around what the readers are actually deciding and their resistance drops without any need for trickery. The consequence is that the chapter teaches adaptation rather than performance.
- Sabir, in a graduate-rhetoric-colloquium, hears classmates celebrate messaging that "gets around" the audience's guard. He draws the line: persuasive communication can respect the audience while still moving them, but once the message depends on concealment, distortion, or emotional override that would fail under inspection, the strategy has turned manipulative.

Common Misreadings:
- assuming persuasion is always manipulation
- assuming any emotional appeal is illegitimate
- focusing on speaker self-expression instead of audience reception
- praising message effectiveness without checking how it achieved compliance

Counterarguments or Limits:
- some audiences resist for reasons no message can ethically solve
- persuasive adaptation can slide into manipulation when truth is bent
- message skill cannot substitute for bad incentives or bad policy
- communication that bypasses judgment may work short-term and poison trust later
