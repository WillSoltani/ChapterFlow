Core Claim:
The operator can gain leverage by shaping what the other side thinks is happening, but the move is legitimate only when it stays on the side of strategic framing rather than fabrication or false belief.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 22 ended on restraint and disciplined exit. Chapter 23 opens the dirty-war section by moving into perception management, where the same mechanics can serve either fair framing or outright propaganda depending on how truth is handled.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter defines misperception as control over the opponent's picture of reality. Then it separates fair framing from unfair fabrication. Then it modernizes the move through messaging, negotiation, and public-facing examples. Finally it names the ethical line: once core reality is hidden or false belief is induced, the strategy has crossed into manipulation.

Required Anchors:
- Salome, a communications lead, sequences the release of accurate information so a hostile counterparty keeps reacting to the wrong implication without ever being outright lied to. The friction is that her team wants a more aggressive deception campaign. The consequence is that the counterparty misallocates attention while Salome stays on the defensible side of the line.
- Torin, in a graduate-media-ethics-seminar, watches classmates blur framing and propaganda into the same thing. He rebuilds the distinction by asking whether the audience is being guided through true emphasis or pushed into false belief. The friction is that the room prefers simple moral slogans. The consequence is that the class can finally judge the move by what it does to truth and consent, not by whether it sounds clever.

Specific Applications:
- sequencing truthful information to guide interpretation without fabricating facts
- shaping public or negotiation attention toward selected true signals
- stress-testing whether a messaging plan relies on omission or on false belief
- deciding not to use the move where trust and informed consent matter more than positional gain

Common Misreadings:
- treating all framing as propaganda
- treating any clever deception as strategically admirable
- ignoring the difference between emphasis and fabrication
- assuming the move is harmless because it uses some true facts

Counterarguments or Limits:
- even truthful framing can erode trust if it is too manipulative in intent
- some contexts require full clarity rather than strategic sequencing
- the opponent may detect the framing move and punish trust aggressively
- high-stakes or consent-sensitive settings narrow the legitimate space sharply
