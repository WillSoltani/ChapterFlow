Core Claim:
The chapter argues that the operator wins initiative by controlling the time, terms, sequence, and pace of the engagement so the other side is reacting inside a frame that was chosen in advance.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 14 weaponized tempo. Chapter 15 broadens the idea: instead of merely moving fast, the operator structures the whole interaction so the opponent is following.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter defines initiative as control of the dynamic. Then it modernizes the move through negotiation and meeting design. Then it shows how framing and sequencing create pressure before the obvious conflict begins. Finally it gives the reversal: initiative without purpose becomes random aggression.

Required Anchors:
- Selene, a negotiator, circulates the agenda, timing, and decision rules before the meeting starts so the counterparty is forced to answer within her sequence. The friction is that this can look controlling if the frame is too obvious. The consequence is that the other side stops dictating the conversation and starts responding on her terrain.
- Keaton, in an undergraduate-urban-planning-studio, realizes his team can regain control of a critique by setting the order of evidence, the design question, and the timeline for responses before a rival group takes over the room. The friction is that initiative without clarity looks like posturing. The consequence is that the discussion finally moves toward his team's real objective.

Common Misreadings:
- confusing initiative with loudness
- equating control with domination for its own sake
- setting terms that do not serve the real objective
- assuming the first speaker automatically controls the dynamic

Counterarguments or Limits:
- forcing too visibly can trigger resistance
- a bad frame imposed early is still a bad frame
- initiative without purpose burns trust and time
- control of the dynamic fails if the operator cannot sustain the terms she sets
