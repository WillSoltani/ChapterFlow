Chapter Title: The Fait Accompli Strategy: Take Small Bites
Position In Book: Twenty-ninth chapter of the native 33-strategy structure. It follows one-upmanship by moving from inherited openings to cumulative position built below reaction threshold.
Source Sidecar Path: sidecars/source/ch29.source.txt

Core Claim:
Large opposition can sometimes be avoided by sequencing many small moves that each look too minor to resist, even though their aggregate effect becomes major and hard to reverse.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 28 gained by refusing to rescue a self-destructive rival. Chapter 29 gains by changing the field gradually enough that each step stays under the threshold that would trigger strong resistance.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter defines fait accompli as cumulative sequencing rather than one big push. Then it explains why opponents often evaluate moves individually instead of aggregating them. Then it modernizes the move through policy drift, influence building, and negotiation examples. Finally it gives the reversal: incremental accumulation becomes ethically unstable when it hides the true cumulative aim from people who would object to the whole.

Required Anchors:
- Oriana stops pitching one large process change and instead breaks the shift into several smaller commitments that each clear review because none looks decisive on its own. The friction is that her colleagues want the clean drama of one big proposal. The consequence is that the full operating change is in place before opponents recognize how much the aggregate has moved.
- Dashiell, in a graduate-public-administration-seminar, hears classmates describe incrementalism as neutral. He corrects the frame by asking what the small steps add up to and whether the aggregate aim is being hidden from the people absorbing it. The friction is that the room wants to treat smallness as innocence. The consequence is that the class can separate disciplined sequencing from concealed cumulative coercion.
