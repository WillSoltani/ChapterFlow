Core Claim:
The operator protects long-term strength by defining exit conditions early and leaving while workable terms still exist, instead of staying until sunk cost, pride, and identity destroy the remaining choices.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 21 kept pressure and settlement active at once. Chapter 22 asks when the campaign itself should stop. The central skill is not pressing better. It is ending before the remaining exits disappear.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter frames exit as a discipline operators resist psychologically. Then it explains why exit conditions should be set partly in advance, before commitment and identity blur judgment. Then it modernizes the strategy through product, career, and institutional examples. Finally it gives the reversal: not every hard stretch is a genuine stop signal.

Required Anchors:
- Mireya, a product lead, shuts down a weakening initiative after the agreed benchmarks fail for the third review cycle, even though her team wants one more quarter. The friction is that everyone has already tied status to the project. The consequence is that the wider portfolio survives because she exits while the losses are still containable.
- Stellan, in a graduate-labor-economics-seminar, watches a negotiation team keep pushing a dead proposal because they cannot bear to admit how much time they have already spent on it. He reframes the discussion around predeclared exit conditions and current marginal cost. The friction is that the room confuses endurance with seriousness. The consequence is that the team stops before the remaining terms collapse entirely.

Specific Applications:
- shutting down a failing product or initiative before it harms the broader system
- leaving a role or project before the relationship fully rots
- setting explicit stop rules before a negotiation or campaign begins
- distinguishing real decline from a temporary hard phase

Common Misreadings:
- treating exit as weakness by definition
- waiting until the last possible moment to preserve pride
- confusing sunk cost with evidence of future value
- mistaking temporary strain for proof that a campaign should end immediately

Counterarguments or Limits:
- some hard periods really are temporary and do not justify exit
- precommitted stop rules can be too rigid if context changes materially
- teams can weaponize exit language to avoid necessary persistence
- the operator still has to judge marginal cost, not obey a slogan
