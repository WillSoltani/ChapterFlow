Core Claim:
The chapter argues that disciplined operators preserve time, energy, money, attention, and political capital by refusing most fights. The skill is evaluative: ask what winning gets you and what losing costs you, then walk away when both answers are small.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 7 elevated morale and cause. Chapter 8 imposes restraint on that momentum. The chapter has to make selectivity feel strong rather than timid.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter says resources are finite. Then it argues that most available fights have low return. Then it gives the screening questions that separate valuable conflict from draining conflict. Then it marks the reversal: some visible fights matter because declining them sends a signal to an audience whose judgment counts.

Required Anchors:
- Tessa, an operations manager, notices that she keeps spending political capital on minor internal disputes and has nothing left when a staffing decision with real consequences arrives. The friction is that small wins are satisfying and reputation is always in the room. The consequence is that she begins filtering fights by payoff, cost, and audience.
- Karim, an undergraduate urban-planning-clinic lead, sees his team waste time arguing over small presentation details while ignoring a zoning objection that could sink the whole proposal. The friction is that some classmates think every concession looks weak. The consequence is that the chapter can show the difference between harmless pride fights and signal fights that the right audience is actually watching.

Common Misreadings:

- assuming every slight deserves correction
- ignoring the audience question in reputational conflict
- spending capital to feel strong instead of to gain something meaningful

Counterarguments or Limits:
- some visible fights do matter because refusal sends a signal
- repeated small concessions can accumulate if the wrong audience is watching
- selectivity requires clarity about what you are saving resources for
- not every winnable fight is worth the expenditure
