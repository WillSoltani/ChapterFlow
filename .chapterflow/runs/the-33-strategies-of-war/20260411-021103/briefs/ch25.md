Core Claim:
In conflicts watched by third parties, the side read as just gains structural advantage, but that advantage holds only when the operator's conduct can survive scrutiny and the framing matches the conduct.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 24 worked on the opponent's forecast. Chapter 25 works on audience judgment. The operator is no longer only maneuvering against the immediate rival but also against the public cost structure that surrounds the conflict.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter defines moral high ground as a structural advantage under observation. Then it explains the two parts of the move: substantive consistency and recognizable framing. Then it modernizes the strategy through coalition, classroom, and public-facing examples. Finally it delivers the reversal: one exposed hypocrisy can collapse the posture instantly.

Required Anchors:
- Leonie, a coalition spokesperson, narrows her side's demands and keeps its conduct visibly disciplined before a labor dispute turns public. The friction is that some allies want retaliatory rhetoric and symbolic overkill. The consequence is that when the other side lashes out, observers read aggression and cost it accordingly.
- Phelan, in a graduate-constitutional-law-seminar, watches classmates treat moral high ground as branding. He reframes it as a credibility structure that requires real consistency before any message can carry it. The friction is that the room prefers clever slogans to costly discipline. The consequence is that the class can see why a single hypocrisy can collapse an apparently strong righteous posture.

Specific Applications:
- narrowing demands or conduct so third parties can read your side as measured and legitimate
- framing a dispute in terms observers already recognize as just
- avoiding retaliatory moves that would erase sympathy capital
- stress-testing whether your own side can sustain the posture it wants to claim

Common Misreadings:
- treating the strategy as branding detached from conduct
- assuming moral high ground means passivity
- confusing public sympathy with private innocence
- believing one speech can repair repeated hypocrisy

Counterarguments or Limits:
- some audiences are polarized and will not judge fairly
- substantive consistency can impose real tactical restraint on your own side
- the posture can become brittle if it depends on purity performance instead of stable principle
- public moral language without disciplined conduct creates faster collapse, not protection
