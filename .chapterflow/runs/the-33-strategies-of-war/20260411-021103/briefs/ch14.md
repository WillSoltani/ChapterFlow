Core Claim:
The chapter argues that speed and suddenness can function as force multipliers. When the operator moves faster than the other side can process and respond, resistance forms too late.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 13 aimed the move accurately. Chapter 14 asks what happens once the picture is good enough: tempo itself becomes a weapon.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter defines speed as a weapon. Then it frames operational tempo as the linked speed of decision and execution. Then it modernizes the move through startups and fast-cycle competitors. Finally it gives the reversal: fast movement in the wrong direction only multiplies error.

Required Anchors:
- Rhea, a product lead, sequences three rapid launches before a larger rival can finish evaluating the first one. The friction is that moving this fast stresses the team and looks reckless to slower observers. The consequence is that the rival keeps reacting to a position that no longer exists.
- Petra, in a graduate-operations-strategy-lab, learns that her team's advantage is not raw brilliance but faster decision loops than a better resourced team. The friction is that speed can feel sloppy if the team cannot explain its judgment. The consequence is that tempo becomes structural rather than frantic.

Specific Applications:
- using quick release cycles to stay ahead of a slower incumbent
- chaining fast decisions so the opponent responds to stale positions
- designing a team process around reduced decision lag
- distinguishing useful speed from panicked motion

Common Misreadings:
- equating speed with chaos
- treating tempo as urgency theater
- assuming faster is always better regardless of accuracy
- confusing constant motion with operational advantage

Counterarguments or Limits:
- speed without judgment amplifies mistakes
- some domains punish premature movement heavily
- tempo has to be systemic, not just personal hustle
- fast cycles break down if the team cannot absorb feedback

Medium. Speed can become coercive or careless if it is used to outrun scrutiny rather than resistance.
