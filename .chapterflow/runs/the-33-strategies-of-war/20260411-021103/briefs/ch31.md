Chapter Title: The Inner-Front Strategy: Destroy from Within
Position In Book: Thirty-first chapter of the native 33-strategy structure. It follows communication pressure by moving from shaping perception into amplifying contradictions already inside the target.
Source Sidecar Path: sidecars/source/ch31.source.txt

Core Claim:
Some opponents can only be weakened when their own factions, incentives, and contradictions are allowed to pull against each other instead of being unified by outside attack.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 30 worked by entering the audience's mind from the outside. Chapter 31 works by diagnosing fault lines already inside the target and deciding whether external pressure should expose rather than repair them.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter defines the inner front as the zone of contradiction inside a group or opponent. Then it explains why external attack can accidentally unify what internal pressure would divide. Then it modernizes the move through organizational factions and coalition instability. Finally it gives the reversal: this is morally close to subversion, so the chapter must separate exposing real fault lines from manufacturing rot.

Required Anchors:
- Sorin sees a rival leadership bloc that looks powerful from the outside but is already split by incentives, timelines, and distrust. He stops pushing frontal attacks that would unify them and instead makes those contradictions harder to ignore. The consequence is that the chapter teaches diagnosis over aggression.
- Anwen, in a graduate-organizational-psychology-seminar, hears classmates praise internal destabilization as if any fracture were fair game. She draws the line: exposing genuine contradictions in a true opponent is one thing, but inventing suspicions or seeding falsehoods to break people apart is corrosive and cannot be treated as neutral strategy.

Common Misreadings:
- treating every disagreement as an exploitable fracture
- assuming destabilization is clever by default
- ignoring the difference between revealing and inventing contradictions
- carrying this framework into ordinary collaboration

Counterarguments or Limits:
- some divided groups still rally under outside pressure
- exposed contradictions can spill collateral damage beyond the target
- false internal-fracture tactics can permanently corrode trust and legitimacy
- many contexts are not true opponents and should not be treated this way
