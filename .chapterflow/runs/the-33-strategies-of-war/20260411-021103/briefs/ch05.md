Core Claim:
The chapter argues that capable groups still make bad decisions when hierarchy punishes dissent and rewards silence. Greene's answer is not flatter rhetoric or fake collaboration. It is command designed around mission, context, and initiative: leaders set purpose and constraints, while people closer to the ground adapt the method. The reversal is essential: new teams, real emergencies, and unfamiliar terrain sometimes require more explicit direction.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The book turns from self-directed warfare to team warfare here. The central problem is no longer personal drift or self-control. It is how competence gets trapped inside status, fear, and overcontrol. The chapter has to preserve both halves of the idea: dissent upward and initiative downward.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter identifies groupthink as a structural failure rather than an intelligence failure. Then it argues that command should transmit mission more than procedure. Then it shows how adaptive teams outperform micromanaged teams once conditions change. Then it draws the reversal line: when people lack context or experience, tighter instruction can be the safer choice.

Required Anchors:
- Raina, an operations lead, sees a launch plan moving forward because nobody wants to challenge the vice president who proposed it. The friction is that the room is full of competent people who all see a weak assumption. The consequence is that once Raina asks each lead for the risk they would raise if rank were invisible and shifts the team to mission-based ownership, the plan gets smarter fast.
- Malcolm, a student editor in an undergraduate public-health lab publication, notices that his adviser gets stronger reporting from students when she assigns the story goal and standards but not the exact reporting script. The friction is that some newer writers still need more structure. The consequence is that the chapter can separate adaptive autonomy from negligent vagueness.

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as anti-leadership rather than anti-rigid hierarchy
- confusing mission-based command with absence of standards
- assuming all dissent is useful without requiring accountability
- applying autonomy to people who do not yet have enough context

Counterarguments or Limits:
- emergencies can require centralized instruction
- novice teams may need more explicit procedure at first
- mission-based command fails if the objective is vague
- dissent without shared responsibility can become noise
