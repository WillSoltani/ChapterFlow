Core Claim:
The chapter argues that comfort can be its own trap. When people have too many exits, too much time, and too little consequence, they often underperform because nothing forces concentration. Greene's answer is to create chosen pressure by removing fallback options, making commitments public, or constraining resources until seriousness becomes unavoidable. The strategy is not thrill-seeking. It is deliberate compression for people who are coasting. The reversal is non-negotiable: this move breaks people who are already overwhelmed.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 3 taught the reader not to obey panic. Chapter 4 explains when pressure should be manufactured on purpose. Its central tension is that the same force that sharpens the comfortable can crush the overextended. The chapter has to hold both truths at once or it becomes burnout propaganda.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter claims that comfort dulls effort by leaving too many soft exits. Then it shows that chosen desperation can force focus by making retreat more expensive than completion. Then it translates that logic into modern scenes like public deadlines, quitting the side route, or entering the race that makes half-effort impossible. Then it draws the reversal line hard: adding pressure to a person already near collapse is not strategy, it is damage.

Required Anchors:
- Marisol, a documentary producer, keeps talking about finishing a limited series while protecting every freelance escape hatch that lets her postpone the real cut. The friction is that her flexibility looks responsible from the outside. The consequence is that once she commits publicly to a release screening and shuts down other side work for six weeks, the project finally becomes concrete enough to finish.
- Theo, a graduate journalism student, notices that his thesis reporting stays vague until he puts himself on a real deadline by booking interviews, reserving a public presentation slot, and telling his advisor the full draft date. The friction is that he cannot tell whether the pressure is productive or self-punishing. The consequence is that the chapter gets to mark the difference between chosen seriousness and overload.

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as blanket praise for stress
- confusing already-overwhelmed with merely undercommitted
- creating theatrical pressure that changes optics but not effort
- using the strategy to justify harmful expectations for other people

Counterarguments or Limits:
- some comfort is recovery, not weakness
- chosen pressure works only when the person still has reserve capacity
- public commitments can backfire if they are disconnected from actual execution
- the strategy is strongest when self-imposed, weakest when imposed on the depleted
