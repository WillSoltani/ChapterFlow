Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that Rogers describes the good life as an open, flexible process of fuller functioning rather than a finished state of comfort or control.

Opening Move:
Open on the temptation to define the good life as stable happiness or permanent composure, then turn toward Rogers's process account.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for the chapter title and move from goals to the good life
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for fuller functioning as openness and process
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for the limit against comfort-ideal and chaos-ideal

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: ask what kind of life congruent goals serve
- P2: reject static happiness as too shallow a standard
- P3: define the good life as process and fuller functioning
- P4: explain openness to experience and flexible living
- P5: mark the limit against indulgence or chaos
- P6: show why fuller functioning can feel less defended rather than more protected
- P7 if needed: bridge to persons and science in Chapter 10

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes fuller functioning from both static comfort and undisciplined expressiveness.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The good life can be better because it is more open, not because it is more protected. Hard depth must preserve the paradox that fuller functioning may involve more exposure to reality rather than less.

Threshold Question:
How can a life become better by becoming less defended and more open to risk, feeling, and reality?
