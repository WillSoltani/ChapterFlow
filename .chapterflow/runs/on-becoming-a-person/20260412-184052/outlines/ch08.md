Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that Rogers frames personal goals in terms of becoming more real and congruent rather than more compliant or image-managed.

Opening Move:
Open on the temptation to define personal goals by external ideals, then turn toward Rogers's stricter account of more truthful selfhood.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for the chapter title and move into Part IV
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for personal goals as congruence rather than compliance
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for the acceptance-change tension and its limits

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: ask what goals psychotherapy should serve
- P2: reject external-compliance framing as too shallow
- P3: define goals in terms of more real and congruent selfhood
- P4: explain how acceptance and truthful awareness support change
- P5: mark the limit against passivity or self-indulgence
- P6: show why borrowed ideals can deepen falseness
- P7 if needed: bridge to the good life / fully functioning person

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes congruent selfhood from both compliant adaptation and impulsive self-expression.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Acceptance supports change, but it can sound like passivity if handled loosely. Hard depth must preserve why acceptance is an enabling condition rather than a surrender of growth.

Threshold Question:
How can accepting what is true about oneself become the condition for less false change rather than an excuse to stop changing?
