Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that personal growth is helped by a relationship built from genuineness, warm acceptance, and empathic understanding rather than from pressure or superior control.

Opening Move:
Open on the practical question hidden in Part II: how can I actually be of help without taking over another person's process?

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for the move from Chapter 1 into the concrete helping question
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for genuineness, acceptance, and empathic seeing as a demanding trio
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for what those conditions make possible in the other person and why that differs from permissiveness

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: open on the danger of trying to help by pressure or superiority
- P2: introduce the shift from expert control to facilitating relationship
- P3: explain genuineness as non-fake presence
- P4: explain warm acceptance and empathic seeing without sentimentality
- P5: show what these conditions do to defensiveness and self-contact
- P6: mark the limit that warmth alone is not enough
- P7 if needed: bridge to the characteristics of a helping relationship

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes facilitating relationship conditions from help that manages, judges, or quietly colonizes the other person.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The same language can become soft or manipulative if it loses discipline. Hard depth must ask what separates real facilitation from permissiveness, role-played empathy, or helper self-soothing.

Threshold Question:
What makes a relationship genuinely growth-facilitating instead of merely warm, compliant, or emotionally reassuring?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- help versus control
- genuineness without performance
- acceptance without indulgence
- empathy as disciplined perception
- growth conditions versus sentimental warmth

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): whether a manager should push advice or create safer contact first
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): a teacher reviews how judgment shut a student down
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): naming the difference between agreeing and understanding
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): a team member opens up after genuine non-defensive contact
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): whether to correct fast or stay with the student's frame longer
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): from fixing someone's feelings to making room for them

Scenario Vividness Targets:
Use details like a half-erased whiteboard, a chair pulled closer in an office, a red-marked paper, a kitchen table, or a paused voice memo.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what a helping relationship actually looks like when these conditions are present, not restate the whole next chapter.

Genericity Risks:

- therapeutic language that could fit any chapter

Forbidden Drift:
- exact quote imitation
- empathy as agreement
- permissiveness framed as wisdom
