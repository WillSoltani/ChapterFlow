Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand what makes a relationship genuinely helpful rather than merely warm, polished, or controlling.

Opening Move:
Open on the practical test: what lets a person tell more truth inside one relationship than inside another?

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for the move from conditions to real helping relationship
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for trust, reduced defensiveness, and non-dominating contact
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for the limit between supportive feel and actual growth-facilitation

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: open on the difference between being around help and feeling helped
- P2: connect Chapter 2 conditions to a lived relationship shape
- P3: explain what trust-building contact changes
- P4: show why judgment or quiet control distorts help
- P5: clarify the signs of a genuinely helping relationship
- P6: mark the limit that pleasantness alone is not proof
- P7 if needed: bridge to psychotherapy knowledge in Chapter 4

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes a relationship that lowers defensiveness and increases truthful self-contact from one that merely manages emotions or behavior.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Supportive feeling and actual facilitation are not the same. Hard depth must ask what evidence shows the relationship is helping growth instead of flattering the helper.

Threshold Question:
What proves a relationship is helping another person grow rather than simply making the interaction feel nicer?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- what makes a relationship helpful
- trust versus managed comfort
- reduced defensiveness as evidence
- subtle control as corruption of help
- actual growth signs versus helper self-satisfaction

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): whether to coach through pressure or create safer candor first
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): why a student stayed polite but hid the real issue
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): naming the difference between comfort and actual help
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): honest feedback lands after trust is built
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): whether to keep the room easy or ask a truer question
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): from feeling supported to actually becoming clearer

Scenario Vividness Targets:
Use details like a half-open office door, a marked rubric, a coffee mug gone cold, a pause on a video call, or a folded note.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what we can really know about psychotherapy once a helping relationship is in view, not summarize the next chapter.

Genericity Risks:
- generic trust language
- vague psychological safety slogans
- support that sounds kind but analytically empty

Forbidden Drift:
- management buzzwords
- exact quote imitation
- sentimentality as evidence
- brief-language leak
