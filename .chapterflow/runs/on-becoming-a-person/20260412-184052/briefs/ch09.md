Core Claim:
This chapter should describe the good life not as static happiness, moral tidiness, or completed self-mastery, but as a living process of fuller functioning marked by openness to experience, flexibility, and more direct contact with reality. It should preserve the sense that the good life is dynamic and risky rather than settled and finished.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the good-life chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 8 because the focus shifts from what personal goals should be to what sort of life those goals open into.

Author Logic Chain:
First Rogers asks what kind of life emerges when growth is aimed at congruence.
Then he frames the good life as process, openness, and fuller functioning rather than fixed happiness.
Then he clarifies that fuller functioning is not comfort, perfection, or impulse-worship.

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not promise that the good life removes conflict, pain, or uncertainty. Its point is narrower: fuller functioning names a more open, flexible, reality-contacting way of living. Keep visible that this life can be less defended and more alive without becoming chaotic or sentimental.
