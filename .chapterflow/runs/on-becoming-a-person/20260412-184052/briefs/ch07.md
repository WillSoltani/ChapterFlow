Core Claim:
Once Rogers has described therapeutic direction and the meaning of becoming, he turns to psychotherapy itself. The chapter should frame psychotherapy not as a fixed technique imposed from outside, but as a living process in which the person's experiencing, awareness, and relation to self can unfold with less distortion. The emphasis should stay on process conception, not on branded method.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter asks what psychotherapy is if the person is process. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 6 because the focus shifts from personhood as becoming to therapy as a process-form that meets becoming without freezing it.

Author Logic Chain:
First Rogers asks what sort of thing psychotherapy must be if becoming is process.
Then he presents therapy less as a technique and more as an unfolding relational process.
Then he clarifies that process language matters because fixed formulas can distort the living movement they claim to help.

Common Misreadings:

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not claim that technique is irrelevant or that all structure is harmful. Its narrower point is that psychotherapy cannot be understood truthfully as a fixed external method if the person in therapy is a living process. Keep visible that process still involves discipline, attention, and relational conditions.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 6 defined becoming a person as process; this chapter asks what psychotherapy must look like if that is true.
Next Chapter Bridge: once psychotherapy is conceived as process, Rogers can turn toward the personal goals that such a process serves.

Moral Complexity:
Medium. The chapter values process, but it should not romanticize looseness or imply that any unstructured encounter counts as psychotherapy.
