Core Claim:
After asking what psychotherapy can be known objectively and subjectively, Rogers now asks what movement becomes visible when therapy is genuinely helping. The core move is directional rather than procedural: therapy does not advance as a neat checklist, but there are recurring shifts Rogers sees in clients as they become less defensive, more open to feeling, and more able to live from their own experience. The chapter should feel like movement coming into view, not like a technique manual. It should end with the sense that therapy can be recognized by its direction of travel even when each person's path remains individual.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the visible-movement chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 4 because it moves from how psychotherapy is known to what directions of change become evident within it.

Author Logic Chain:
First Rogers asks what shifts can actually be seen in therapy over time.
Then he traces recurring movement from defensiveness toward more direct contact with feeling and experience.
Then he clarifies that the value lies in directionality, not in forcing every person through the same staged script.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that everyone changes through identical steps or at equal speed. Its point is narrower: there are recognizable tendencies in therapeutic movement, but they are tendencies, not a mechanical ladder. Keep visible that movement can be partial, uneven, and reversible.

Previous Chapter Bridge: Chapter 4 asked how psychotherapy is known; this chapter asks what actual movement becomes visible once that process is watched and lived honestly.
Next Chapter Bridge: once these directions are visible, the next step is to ask what it really means to become a person through that movement.
