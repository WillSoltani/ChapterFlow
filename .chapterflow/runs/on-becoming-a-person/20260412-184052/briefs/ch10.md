Core Claim:
This chapter should examine the philosophical tension between scientific inquiry and the irreducible reality of persons. It should not become anti-science. The point is narrower and harder: science offers real power and knowledge, yet persons cannot be exhausted by the categories and controls that science prefers. The chapter should preserve that tension without caricature.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the persons-versus-science chapter. It should feel impossible to confuse with Chapter 9 because the focus shifts from flourishing to the philosophical stakes of studying persons scientifically.

Author Logic Chain:
First Rogers asks what happens when the reality of persons is placed beside scientific method.
Then he clarifies the power and legitimacy of science while resisting reduction of persons to scientific objects alone.
Then he frames the problem as philosophical rather than as a simple rejection of empirical inquiry.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not deny the value of research or objective inquiry. Its narrower point is that persons cannot be fully contained by the perspective that treats them only as objects of study. Keep the philosophical question visible and do not overclaim beyond the support envelope.
