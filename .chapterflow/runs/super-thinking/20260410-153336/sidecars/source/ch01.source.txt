Chapter 1 title: Being Wrong Less

Frozen authorized support:

1. Inverse Thinking
- Frames the chapter around making good decisions by trying to be wrong less instead of simply right more.
- Positions mental models as a tool set for navigating a complex world more effectively.
- Section tag: Introduction.

2. Unforced Error
- Uses tennis as the anchor: an unforced error is a mistake caused by poor judgment or execution rather than an unstoppable outside force.
- Chapter implication: stronger judgment comes from reducing your own avoidable mistakes consistently.
- Section tag: Introduction.

3. Antifragile
- Distinguishes systems that merely resist shocks from systems that improve under bounded volatility and stress.
- Useful as a chapter limit condition: not every stressor is bad if the system can learn from exposure.
- Section tag: Introduction.

4. Arguing From First Principles
- Described as the practical starting point to being wrong less.
- Means thinking from the bottom up using the basic building blocks of what you think is true.
- Frames first principles as the foundation beneath conclusions.
- Section tag: Keep It Simple Stupid.

5. De-risking
- Supports the move from cleverness to downside control.
- Implies a chapter-level strategy of lowering the cost of error before scaling confidence.
- Source quote remains thinner, so prose should paraphrase narrowly.

6. Minimum Viable Product (MVP)
- Supports the idea of testing cheaply before overcommitting.
- Helps connect wrong-less thinking to low-cost experiments and limited downside.
- Source quote remains thinner, so prose should paraphrase narrowly.

Authorized factual boundaries for Chapter 1:
- The chapter is about reducing avoidable error, not eliminating uncertainty.
- It leans on sports error reduction, first-principles reasoning, antifragility, and low-cost testing.
- It should preserve a tension between caution and learning: some volatility teaches, but unforced mistakes still compound.
- Avoid importing unsupported neuroscience, forecasting jargon, or later statistical concepts from later chapters.
