## Core Claim
Time decisions are powerful not because every moment is huge, but because repeated small choices compound, every commitment crowds out an alternative, and present bias keeps stealing from the future unless you design against it.

## Source Truth
- Compound Interest gives the chapter its opening growth logic.
- Opportunity Cost supplies the scarcity rule: every choice excludes another.
- Present Bias explains why people keep favoring immediate relief over long-term progress.

## Required Anchors
1. Compounding as repeated gains building on previous gains.
2. Opportunity cost as the chapter's anti-overcommitment rule.
3. Present bias as the internal obstacle that keeps readers from acting on the first two ideas.

## Live Tension / Boundary
- If you treat every small choice as trivial, compounding never gets a chance to help you.
- If you try to do everything, opportunity cost defeats you anyway.
- If you know the right move but keep choosing relief now, present bias drains the plan.

## What Must Stay Out
- No generic hustle rhetoric.
- No unsupported productivity systems presented as book fact.
- No imported financial advice beyond the narrow compound-interest illustration.

## Reader Payoff
The reader should leave with one usable rule: invest attention where gains compound, admit the cost of every yes, and make the future easier to choose before present bias gets its turn.
