## Core Claim
Some systems get stronger not through forceful control but through fit, momentum, thresholds, and reinforcing participation. Better thinking here means working with those forces instead of pretending every outcome is linear and equally controllable.

## Source Truth
- Natural Selection frames adaptation as fit to environment over time.
- Flywheel explains why beginnings are heavy and momentum later changes the effort curve.
- Tipping Point gives the threshold-change logic.
- Network Effects shows how value can rise as the network grows.

## Required Anchors
1. Natural selection as the chapter's adaptation lens.
2. Flywheel as the effort-to-momentum transition.
3. Tipping point and network effects as the chain-reaction pair.

## Live Tension / Boundary
- If you fight the system's natural dynamics, you waste effort.
- If you worship momentum blindly, you miss the conditions that made it possible.
- The chapter should resolve this by teaching readers to read fit, thresholds, and reinforcing loops.

## What Must Stay Out
- No unsupported biological determinism.
- No startup mythology presented as universal law.
- No imported systems jargon that outruns the frozen support.

## Reader Payoff
The reader should leave with a usable rule: look for the environment, the threshold, and the reinforcing loop before deciding how much force to apply.
