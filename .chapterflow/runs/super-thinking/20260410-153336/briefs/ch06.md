## Core Claim
Better decisions come from turning a messy choice into visible options, branches, and consequences so uncertainty can be examined instead of hand-waved.

## Source Truth
- Decision Tree gives the chapter its main structure for handling complex choices.
- The chapter should frame decisions as branchable paths with consequences that can be compared more clearly once they are laid out.

## Required Anchors
1. Decision tree as the chapter's complexity-taming structure.
2. Branches and outcomes as the core way to inspect uncertainty.
3. Better judgment through decomposition rather than vague intuition alone.

## Live Tension / Boundary
- If you keep a complex choice as one emotional blur, you miss important branches.
- If you try to formalize everything beyond the frozen support, the chapter outruns its source.
- The chapter should resolve this by teaching visible decomposition and consequence comparison without drifting into unsupported math.

## What Must Stay Out
- No unsupported expected-value formulas or finance talk.
- No invented probability tables.
- No imported optimization doctrine treated as if it came from the frozen support.

## Reader Payoff
The reader should leave with one usable rule: split a hard choice into branches and likely consequences before trusting the first overall feeling.
