## Core Claim
Conflict improves when the reader stops treating it as pure argument and starts seeing interaction, incentives, authority, and strategic refusal as part of the situation.

## Source Truth
- Game Theory supports seeing conflict as a responsive interaction where the other side changes the shape of the choice.
- Authority supports the idea that credibility and legitimacy affect how persuasion and pressure land.
- Loss Leader Strategy supports the idea that not every conflict should be fought head-on or won at the smallest visible level.

## Required Anchors
1. Conflict as an interaction with responses and counter-moves.
2. Authority as part of why some moves land and others fail.
3. Strategic refusal or redirection when a direct fight is the wrong contest.

## Live Tension / Boundary
- If you treat conflict as one isolated statement, you ignore incentives and response loops.
- If you overformalize the chapter, it outruns the thin source base and turns into imported game-theory doctrine.
- The chapter should resolve this by teaching visible strategic awareness without pretending to offer a complete conflict science.

## What Must Stay Out
- No unsupported payoff matrices or formal equilibrium talk.
- No military, diplomatic, or political doctrine treated as source-backed.
- No broad persuasion systems imported from elsewhere.

## Reader Payoff
The reader should leave with one usable rule: before pushing harder in conflict, ask what game is being played, how authority is shaping the exchange, and whether the best move is to redirect or refuse the wrong fight.
