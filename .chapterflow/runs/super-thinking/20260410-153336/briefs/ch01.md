## Core Claim
The opening move in Super Thinking is not to become omniscient. It is to make fewer self-inflicted mistakes by using mental models to stress-test assumptions, simplify to fundamentals, and lower the cost of being wrong.

## Source Truth
- Inverse Thinking frames the chapter around being wrong less rather than right more.
- Unforced Error provides the central concrete anchor: mistakes created by your own poor judgment or execution.
- Arguing From First Principles gives the chapter its clean reasoning move: work from foundational assumptions upward.
- Antifragile prevents the chapter from collapsing into timid safety; some systems improve under bounded stress.
- De-risking and MVP support a practical tactic: cheap tests and limited downside before scaling confidence.

## Required Anchors
1. Tennis unforced-error frame as the cleanest picture of avoidable self-created failure.
2. First-principles reasoning as the chapter's practical foundation.
3. Antifragile as the limit condition that distinguishes useful stress from destructive fragility.

## Live Tension / Boundary
- If you focus only on avoiding mistakes, you can become timid.
- If you seek growth without reducing unforced errors, you compound preventable damage.
- The chapter should resolve this by favoring bounded experimentation over reckless certainty or passive caution.

## What Must Stay Out
- No later-chapter statistical or game-theory material.
- No unsupported claims about forecasting science or brain chemistry.
- No generic productivity or mindset slogans.
- No claims that all volatility is good; only some systems are antifragile.

## Reader Payoff
The reader should finish with a usable operating rule: before chasing brilliance, cut avoidable mistakes, inspect the assumptions underneath your conclusion, and make learning cheaper than failure.
