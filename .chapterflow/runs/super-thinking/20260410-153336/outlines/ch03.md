# Chapter Outline

## Hook
Open on a tiny daily choice that seems harmless until months later reveal that it either built momentum or quietly drained it.

## Build
1. Introduce compounding as the chapter's basic force.
2. Shift from growth to scarcity through opportunity cost.
3. Bring in present bias as the reason readers fail to protect compounding and tradeoff discipline.

## Deliver
1. Show why repeated gains matter more than dramatic isolated effort.
2. Explain that every commitment blocks an alternative.
3. Use present bias to explain procrastination and preference for immediate relief.
4. Land on design logic:
   - make good repetition easier
   - make tradeoffs visible
   - reduce the power of the near-term temptation

## Bridge
Close by moving from personal time allocation toward wider natural and systemic forces that create momentum, tipping, and selection effects.

## Threshold Question For Hard
How do you honor long-term compounding without turning life into a joyless spreadsheet that ignores real human limits?
