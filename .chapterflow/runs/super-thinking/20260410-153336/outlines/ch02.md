# Chapter Outline

## Hook
Open on a well-meant rule or target that produces exactly the wrong behavior. The point is immediate: good intentions do not protect a system from bad outcomes.

## Build
1. Reframe the problem from isolated mistakes to unintended consequences.
2. Use Murphy's Law as a preparedness lens, not a slogan of doom.
3. Introduce perverse incentives and cobra effect as the chapter's cleanest mechanisms for goals gone crooked.

## Deliver
1. Show how people respond to incentives instead of the abstract mission behind them.
2. Use the Atlanta testing case as the concrete anchor for metric distortion.
3. Explain cobra effect as the narrower case where the fix worsens the original problem.
4. Extend the logic with short-termism:
   - immediate wins can conceal future weakness
   - short horizons can look disciplined while actually creating fragility
5. Broaden to market failure:
   - systems can produce bad outcomes without anyone intending them
   - correction itself requires caution and preparation

## Bridge
Close by tightening the rule: once you accept that systems misbehave, you need tools for deciding under uncertainty rather than trusting intention alone.

## Threshold Question For Hard
How do you prepare for unintended consequences without becoming so cynical or defensive that you stop making useful moves at all?
