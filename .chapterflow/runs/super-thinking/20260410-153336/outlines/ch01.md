# Chapter Outline

## Hook
Open on the tennis image of an unforced error: nothing spectacular beat you; you handed the point away. Translate that into thinking errors people create for themselves.

## Build
1. Reframe success from being right more to being wrong less.
2. Explain why mental models matter: they are tools for navigating complexity, not trophies.
3. Introduce first-principles reasoning as the chapter's main discipline.

## Deliver
1. Show how unforced errors happen when people reason from surface stories instead of foundations.
2. Use first principles to strip a decision down to what is actually known.
3. Introduce antifragility as the crucial distinction:
   - some exposure teaches and strengthens
   - some exposure only creates preventable damage
4. Land on de-risking and MVP logic:
   - shrink downside
   - test cheaply
   - learn before escalating commitment

## Bridge
Close by opening the next question: once you start cutting your own avoidable errors, you still have to contend with systems and side effects that can go wrong even when you act carefully.

## Threshold Question For Hard
How do you avoid unforced errors without turning yourself into a person who avoids every useful stressor?
