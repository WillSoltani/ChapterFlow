# Chapter Outline

## Hook
Open on a conflict that gets worse because each side treats it like isolated expression instead of interactive strategy.

## Build
1. Introduce conflict as a game with responses, incentives, and counter-moves.
2. Show why authority changes what lands and what gets ignored.
3. Show that some conflicts reward refusal, reframing, or indirect play more than direct escalation.

## Deliver
1. Teach the reader to ask what game the other side thinks they are playing.
2. Show how authority, status, or legitimacy changes the same message.
3. Land on the practical move:
   - identify the interaction
   - inspect the incentive structure
   - decide whether to engage, redirect, or not play

## Bridge
Close by framing the chapter as a social-strategy capstone rather than a license for manipulation.

## Threshold Question For Hard
How do you stay strategically aware in conflict without pretending every human interaction can be reduced to a clever tactical game?
