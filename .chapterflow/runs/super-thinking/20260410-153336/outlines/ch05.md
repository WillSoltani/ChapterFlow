# Chapter Outline

## Hook
Open on a dramatic statistic or story that feels decisive until the reader learns the background rate changes what it means.

## Build
1. Introduce base rate fallacy as the chapter's opening correction.
2. Show how vivid evidence and isolated numbers distort judgment when context is stripped away.
3. Keep the chapter narrow: better reading of numbers, not math-class performance.

## Deliver
1. Teach the reader to ask what usually happens before trusting the memorable case.
2. Show why context and comparison matter when numbers look persuasive.
3. Keep skepticism practical:
   - ask for the broader rate
   - ask what frame is missing
   - avoid swinging into total distrust

## Bridge
Close by setting up the next chapter as a move from reading uncertain evidence to making structured choices under uncertainty.

## Threshold Question For Hard
How do you stay statistically skeptical without becoming the kind of person who rejects every number just to feel safe?
