The group gathers to tell the story of what was done to them. The injuries are vivid. The humiliations are easy to recite. The losses feel morally clarifying. By the end of the telling, one more thing has happened almost without notice: the next hard response is already beginning to feel necessary. That is the opening pressure of this chapter. Self-justification has moved from the household into the group and turned pain into identity.

That is why the chapter's scale matters so much. In personal conflict, self-justification protects one person's innocence. In group conflict, it protects a collective story about who we are, what we have suffered, and why our aggression is different from yours. The larger the group story becomes, the more individual complexity gets pressed flat beneath it.

This begins with selective group memory. Communities do not only remember events. They remember them through the question of what those events prove. What was done to us becomes central, concrete, and morally instructive. What we did in return becomes blurred by context, necessity, or silence. Injury is retrieved as identity. Retaliation is retrieved as response.

That is why the chapter insists on the difference between remembering harm and building a grievance identity. Harm can be real, devastating, and morally serious. Grievance identity begins when the group's pain becomes the main explanation for who it is and what it is therefore allowed to do. Once that happens, history stops being only memory. It becomes permission.

Retaliation then acquires a special moral force. Our violence is framed as reluctant, forced, or overdue. Your violence is framed as proof of what you are. The same act gets processed through two different stories. Ours is response. Yours is revelation. Self-justification no longer has to argue very hard once that asymmetry takes hold. It begins operating as common sense.

That is what keeps conflict renewing itself. Each side experiences its own hardening as defensive necessity and the other side's hardening as evidence of menace. Every counterstrike becomes another exhibit. Every retaliation arrives already morally explained. The loop deepens not because people have no reasons, but because the reasons are being sorted through innocence stories that keep selecting what counts.

This is why the chapter cannot be reduced to empty both-sides language. Real harms are not erased here. The point is harder than equivalence. Real injury can become part of a self-protective collective story that makes further injury easier to justify. Pain remains true and still gets recruited into a machine that keeps producing more pain.

That machine becomes especially powerful when loyalty attaches to the grievance story itself. To question the group's innocence can start feeling like betrayal of the wounded. To remember the group's aggression can start feeling like treasonous softness. Self-examination now has a social cost. It threatens belonging as well as belief.

That is why repair becomes so difficult at scale. It is not only that facts are disputed. It is that innocence has become collective property. If the group has built its dignity around what was done to it, then loosening that story can feel like surrendering the dead, the injured, or the humiliated. Self-justification presents itself as fidelity.

The chapter's hard question therefore is not whether wounds are real. It is what would have to change before a group could remember its pain without using that pain as a standing license. What evidence, what moral shift, or what form of courage would allow people to see their own side's aggression without feeling disloyal to those who truly suffered?

Seen that way, this chapter extends the marriage chapter cleanly. There too, grievance became an archive that kept rewriting the present. Here the archive is collective. The indictment is shared. The innocence claim becomes cultural or political instead of only personal. The machinery remains recognizable.

That is why the next and final move toward letting go and owning up matters so much. Once self-justification has become part of memory, identity, duty, and loyalty, repair will require more than better facts. It will require surrendering the story that has made innocence feel like the only honorable position left.
