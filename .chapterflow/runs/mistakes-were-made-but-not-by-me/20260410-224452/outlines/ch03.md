Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader understands that blind spots and prejudice persist because people experience their own perspective as reality, not as a defended interpretation.

Opening Move:
Open with an ordinary disagreement where each side feels calm, fair, and factual while already discounting the other's motives.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 naive realism and bias blind spot
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 prejudice as protected moral innocence
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 conflict escalation when both sides claim neutrality

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: anchor, disagreement that feels one-sidedly obvious
- P2: mechanism, naive realism and the blind spot
- P3: anchor, prejudice surviving a fair-minded self-image
- P4: implication, how selective interpretation preserves innocence
- P5: anchor, two-sided certainty escalating conflict
- P6: bridge, why defended perception points toward defended memory
- P7 if needed: limit, naming bias in others rarely fixes the problem in oneself

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's distinct mechanism is that people defend identity not only by rationalizing choices but by treating their own interpretation as neutral reality.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must ask why accusations of bias so often rebound into stronger self-certainty and sharper suspicion of the other side.

Threshold Question:
If both sides feel they are simply seeing the facts clearly, what would count as evidence to either side that their own vision is distorted?
