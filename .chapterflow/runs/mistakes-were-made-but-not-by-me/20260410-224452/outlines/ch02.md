Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader understands cognitive dissonance as the tension that pushes people to defend choices, beliefs, and identities after contradiction appears.

Opening Move:

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's distinct mechanism is that people reduce contradiction by changing interpretation faster than they change identity-threatening behavior.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must ask why a committed person can experience stronger certainty at the very moment the evidence against them has gotten worse.

Threshold Question:
When contradiction becomes painful, what gets edited first: the facts, the memory of the facts, or the cost of admitting what they mean?
