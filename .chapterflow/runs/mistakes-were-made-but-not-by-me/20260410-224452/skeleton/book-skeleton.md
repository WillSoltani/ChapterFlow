## Chapter Order
1. `Knaves, Fools, Villains, and Hypocrites: How Do They Live with Themselves?`
Intent: establish the book's core puzzle, show why obvious wrongdoing does not feel obvious from the inside, and set up self-justification as the governing mechanism.
Source richness: medium
Concept density estimate: medium
Moral complexity flags: high
Thin-chapter risk: moderate because the frozen bundle supports framing better than scene-level detail.

2. `Cognitive Dissonance: The Engine of Self-justification`
Intent: name the mechanism directly and explain why people change beliefs, memory, and interpretation to protect a stable self-image.
Source richness: high
Concept density estimate: high
Moral complexity flags: medium
Thin-chapter risk: low

3. `Pride and Prejudice . . . and Other Blind Spots`
Intent: show how self-justification fuses with naive realism and prejudice, making people treat their own view as neutral and other views as distorted.
Source richness: medium_high
Concept density estimate: high
Moral complexity flags: high
Thin-chapter risk: low

4. `Memory, the Self-justifying Historian`
Intent: explain how memory edits the past to protect the present self and why certainty about memory is a weak guide to accuracy.
Source richness: medium_high
Concept density estimate: high
Moral complexity flags: medium
Thin-chapter risk: low

5. `Good Intentions, Bad Science: The Closed Loop of Clinical Judgment`
Intent: show how professional certainty plus confirmation bias can turn helping systems into harm systems.
Source richness: medium_high
Concept density estimate: high
Moral complexity flags: high
Thin-chapter risk: low

6. `Law and Disorder`
Intent: trace how self-justification can turn police, prosecutors, and witnesses into defenders of bad judgments long after facts shift.
Source richness: high

7. `Love's Assassin: Self-justification in Marriage`

Thin-chapter risk: low

8. `Wounds, Rifts, and Wars`

9. `Letting Go and Owning Up`

## Rotation Plan For Examples
- Ch1: apology, blame, and first-step rationalization
- Ch2: decision entrapment and post-choice belief repair
- Ch3: prejudice, status, and blind-spot recognition
- Ch4: recollection conflict, family memory, and witness certainty
- Ch5: helping professions, diagnosis, and self-sealing expertise
- Ch6: interrogation, evidence filtering, and institutional face-saving
- Ch7: recurring domestic conflict and moral scorekeeping
- Ch8: factional conflict, grievance loops, and retaliation narratives
- Ch9: apology, repair, and conditions for real ownership
