Core Claim:
This chapter argues that self-justification scales from personal injury into group conflict by turning wounds into identity and retaliation into moral duty. The practical danger is that each side remembers injury, minimizes its own aggression, and treats counterviolence as proof of the other's nature. The chapter matters because grievance can become self-renewing once a group uses pain to explain itself and accuse the other side. At that scale, self-justification no longer protects only one ego. It protects a collective story about innocence, victimhood, and righteous force.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the escalation chapter. It moves from intimate blame into faction, us-versus-them identity, and reciprocal injury that keeps justifying itself.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show how group conflict turns selective memory and self-justification into collective grievance.
Then they show how each side treats its own violence as necessary response and the other side's violence as revelation.
Then they show that once injury becomes identity, repair is blocked by the moral cost of loosening the group's innocence story.

Required Anchors:
- Us-versus-them grievance loop: friction between remembered injury and the need to preserve group innocence; consequence is reciprocal accusation.
- Retaliation as moral response: friction between one side's violence and the story that it was forced, reluctant, or defensive; consequence is self-renewing escalation.
- Collective memory and identity: friction between complex history and simplified victim narrative; consequence is hardened rift and long-lived conflict.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- grievance identity: group belonging organized around remembered injury

Specific Applications:
- noticing how groups remember what was done to them more vividly than what they did
- recognizing that retaliation narratives can make every counterstrike feel morally required
- asking what complexity gets lost when injury becomes the group's central self-story

Common Misreadings:
- group conflict is mainly driven by irrational hatred with no self-protective logic
- both sides being self-justifying means all harms are equivalent

Counterarguments or Limits:
