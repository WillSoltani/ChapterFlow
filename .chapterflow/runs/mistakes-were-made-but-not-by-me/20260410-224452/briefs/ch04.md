Core Claim:
This chapter argues that memory is not a neutral archive. It is an active reconstruction that often edits the past to make the current self easier to live with. The practical effect is that certainty about a memory can coexist with serious distortion, especially when the remembered event touches blame, conflict, or identity. The chapter matters because self-justification does not only protect what people are doing now. It also repairs what they believe they did, meant, or suffered before.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the past-tense chapter. It takes the mechanism from present judgment into recollection and shows how memory becomes a historian that serves the ego before it serves accuracy.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors shift from defended perception in the present to reconstructed memory of the past.
Then they show that memory is selective and revisable, especially where blame, conflict, and self-image are involved.
Then they show that confidence in recall is a poor guarantee of accuracy because the remembered story often tracks present needs more than past facts.

Required Anchors:
- Self-justifying historian frame: friction between the wish for faithful recall and the mind's pressure to preserve a workable self-story; consequence is a polished memory that feels true.
- Relationship or family recollection conflict: friction between two people remembering the same event differently; consequence is that each memory supports the rememberer's current innocence.
- Totalitarian ego metaphor: friction between contradictory evidence and a stable self-image; consequence is that inconvenient facts are edited, minimized, or erased in recollection.

- self-justifying historian: memory selecting and arranging the past to protect identity

Specific Applications:
- noticing why two sincere people can remember the same conflict incompatibly
- seeing how apology and blame disputes are shaped by revised recollection
- recognizing that confidence in recall is not the same thing as accuracy

Common Misreadings:
- memory distortion means people are always lying
- if a memory feels vivid, it is probably accurate

Counterarguments or Limits:
The frozen bundle supports memory as reconstruction, family or spouse recollection conflict, and the totalitarian-ego framing. It does not support a technical survey of memory science, trauma debates, or courtroom eyewitness doctrine beyond narrow bridge language.
