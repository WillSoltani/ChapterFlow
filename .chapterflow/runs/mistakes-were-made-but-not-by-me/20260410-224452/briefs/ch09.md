Core Claim:
This chapter argues that self-justification does not relax on its own. People and groups rarely own up until something becomes more valuable than defended innocence. The practical question is what makes that shift possible. The chapter matters because apology, repair, and learning require surrendering a flattering story about the self without pretending the original conflict did not matter. Letting go is not simple forgetting. Owning up is not decorative remorse. Both demand a moral change in what counts as dignity.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the release chapter. It does not add another arena of self-justification. It asks what interrupts the machinery and what conditions make honest admission survivable.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors close the loop by showing that defended innocence is costly but hard to surrender.
Then they shift from diagnosis to the rare conditions under which people can admit fault, stop protecting the ego story, and face what they did.
Then they show that repair depends on ownership with consequence, not on cosmetic apology or strategic image management.

Required Anchors:
- defended innocence versus learning: friction between protecting the self-story and seeing the damage clearly; consequence is stalled growth until dignity gets redefined.
- apology versus ownership: friction between image-saving regret language and real admission of agency; consequence is that repair fails unless responsibility becomes explicit.
- letting go without erasure: friction between preserving pain and releasing grievance as identity; consequence is that repair requires memory without permanent moral prosecution.
