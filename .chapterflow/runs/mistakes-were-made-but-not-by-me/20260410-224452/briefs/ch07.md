Core Claim:
This chapter argues that self-justification becomes especially corrosive in marriage and close relationships because each partner can explain their own hurts situationally while reading the other's hurts as character. The practical danger is that small conflicts harden into recurring moral scorekeeping, revised memory, and escalating blame. The chapter matters because intimacy gives self-justification a daily stage where love, resentment, and righteousness keep feeding each other. What could have remained a repairable mistake turns into a story about who each partner really is.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the intimate-life chapter. It brings the mechanism out of institutions and into recurring private conflict where both people have memory, motive, and grievance on their side.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show how partners excuse their own bad moments through circumstance while explaining the partner's bad moments through character.
Then they show how repeated self-justification reshapes memory, blame, and the purpose of arguments themselves.
Then they show that repair becomes possible only when one or both sides drop the defended story long enough to see pain rather than only accusation.

Required Anchors:
- Attribution split in marriage: friction between how I explain my own hurtful behavior and how I explain yours; consequence is asymmetric blame.
- Escalating marital conflict: friction between small repeated injuries and the need to stay morally innocent; consequence is cumulative grievance and harsher interpretations.
- Conditions for repair: friction between anger, apology, and letting go of accusation; consequence is that mutual or unilateral shifts can reopen empathy.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- self-serving attribution in relationships: using circumstances to excuse oneself while using character to indict the partner
- grievance accumulation: repeated conflict becoming a larger story of victim and perpetrator
- repair through dropping self-justification: loosening accusation long enough for apology, pain, and joint change to matter

Specific Applications:
- noticing when an argument is serving blame more than understanding
- separating situational explanations from character verdicts in intimate conflict
- recognizing that apology fails when each partner's memory still protects innocence first

Common Misreadings:
- unhappy couples mainly suffer from large dramatic betrayals rather than repeated justified misreadings
- empathy alone fixes conflict without either side changing their blame story

Counterarguments or Limits:
