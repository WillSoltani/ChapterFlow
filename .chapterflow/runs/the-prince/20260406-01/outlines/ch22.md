Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why Chapter 22 treats the prince's secretaries as the first visible proof of his judgment and how choosing, testing, rewarding, and binding them belong to the same logic.

Opening Move:
Open on the fact that a prince can reveal himself before he speaks, simply by the caliber and alignment of the people nearest him.

- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3 for second-rank judgment, praise, correction, and the servant who cannot deceive
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P4 for the never-failing self-interest test
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P5 for honouring, enriching, kindnesses, and shared honours and cares

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define appointments as a diagnostic of princely understanding
- P2: explain the Venafro and Pandolfo example and the three ranks of intellect
- P3: show why second-rank intelligence is still real wisdom when it can judge, praise, and correct
- P4: give the never-failing test of bad service through self-interest
- P5: explain the prince's reciprocal duty to keep a good servant honest through honour, riches, kindnesses, and shared cares
- P6: preserve the dependence clause, mutual trust, and bridge to flatterers

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between brilliance and alignment. A minister may be capable, but if he serves himself first he fails. A prince may not be the first class of intellect, but if he can recognize, reward, and correct well, he still rules wisely.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve that Machiavelli is not merely praising obedience. He is defining a structure in which the prince's judgment, the servant's incentives, and the servant's inability to stand alone all work together.

Threshold Question:
If the prince is not the originator of every good idea, what keeps a brilliant secretary from becoming the real ruler?
