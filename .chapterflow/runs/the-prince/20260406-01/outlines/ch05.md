By the end, the reader should understand why free cities are harder to retain than places used to a prince, why nominal autonomy is unstable when liberty remains remembered, and why Machiavelli's three options narrow toward destruction or residence.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is not mere disobedience. It is remembered liberty. A city used to its own laws retains a political identity that time, benefits, and partial accommodation do not dissolve.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Keep the reader inside the chapter's hardest tension: the apparently milder option of leaving laws in place may actually be less safe than the harsher options, because it leaves the memory of freedom alive and organized.

Threshold Question:
When a free city appears calm under tribute and a friendly oligarchy, are you looking at stability, or at a pause before liberty finds its next rallying moment?
