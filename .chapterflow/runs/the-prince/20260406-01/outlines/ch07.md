Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's load-bearing distinction is not simply luck versus skill. It is rise before roots versus roots before rise. The ruler lifted by others receives office, territory, and visibility before he has faithful forces, ordered subjects, and insulation against succession. The whole case study tests whether those missing foundations can be built afterward quickly enough.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter praises Borgia's sequencing and still ends in failure. Hard depth must preserve that tension. If the reader leaves thinking either that fortune makes planning pointless or that skill fully masters contingency, the chapter has been flattened.

Threshold Question:
When borrowed power has already placed the ruler at the summit, which missing root must be built first so the whole structure does not fall at the patron's first decline: friends, forces, public order, or succession insulation?
