By the end, the reader should understand why Chapter 24 treats the loss of states as a diagnostic of neglected preparation, not as a clean excuse for blaming fortune.

Opening Move:
Open on the reversal that matters most: Machiavelli says a new prince can be more secure than an old one if present ability is visible and disciplined.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: explain why present performance can outbind ancient blood
- P2: show the double standard of glory for new rulers and disgrace for hereditary failures
- P3: name the concrete defects Machiavelli finds in Italian losses
- P4: use Philip to prove that rulers can survive pressure without vast territory if they keep the right supports
- P5: accuse fortune-blaming as a cover for sloth and unpreparedness
- P6: close on the hard distinction between desperate recall and self-secured durability

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key mechanism is not fortune versus skill in the abstract. It is whether the ruler used calm periods to build dependable supports in arms, people, and nobles before the storm arrived.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve that Machiavelli is making two arguments at once: present competence can create stronger legitimacy than inheritance, and later collapse exposes earlier neglect more than sudden bad luck.

Threshold Question:
What combination of supports and preparations must a ruler build in quiet times so that later pressure does not strip away the state?
