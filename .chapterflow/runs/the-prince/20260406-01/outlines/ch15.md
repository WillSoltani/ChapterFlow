Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why Machiavelli turns from imagined virtue to real political conditions, why a prince must know how to do wrong according to necessity, and why seeming virtue can still ruin a ruler.

Opening Move:
Open on the methodological break. Machiavelli announces that he is leaving the safe language of ideal conduct and moving toward what is actually useful for holding a state.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1 for real truth versus imagination
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2 for how one lives versus how one ought to live
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3 for knowing how to do wrong according to necessity
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P4 for the praise-and-blame list and the impossibility of possessing all good qualities
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P5 for avoiding state-losing vices, tolerating reproach, and the ruin-versus-security reversal

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: state Machiavelli's break with ideal political writing and his demand for usefulness
- P2: explain the gap between lived reality and moral ideal, and why that gap ruins naive rulers
- P3: explain what it means to know how to do wrong according to necessity
- P4: present the field of praised and blamed qualities and the limit imposed by human conditions
- P5: state the prudential rule about state-losing vices and the harder reversal about seeming virtue and seeming vice
- P6 if needed: not needed; the close should point directly into Chapter 16

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is between what sounds morally admirable in the abstract and what preserves rule under actual conditions. The chapter judges conduct through necessity and consequence rather than through ideal appearance alone.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve both halves of the chapter's severity: reality is harsher than moral fantasy, and prudence is narrower than simple vice because it is still governed by necessity and preservation.

Threshold Question:
If praised qualities are good, why is it not prudent for a prince to try to display all of them?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 7

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: usefulness requires real truth rather than imagination
- Takeaway 2: how people live differs too much from how they ought to live
- Takeaway 3: the prince must know how to do wrong according to necessity
- Takeaway 4: praised and blamed qualities cannot all be possessed together
- Takeaway 5: prudence means avoiding the faults that actually lose the state
- Takeaway 6: reproach may have to be accepted for necessary faults
- Takeaway 7: seeming virtue may ruin while seeming vice may secure

- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): teaches that a household governed by image can become less stable than one willing to bear blame
