Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why civil principality depends on the source of civic favour, why popular support is safer than noble support in adversity, and why durable rule requires citizens to keep needing the state and the prince.

Opening Move:
Open on the surprise that Machiavelli treats ordinary civic conflict as a source of principality. The reader should feel that the real question is not mere popularity but which side's desire built the ruler's position.

Paragraph Job Map:

- P4: sharpen the security difference between hostile people and hostile nobles

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The load-bearing distinction is not nobles bad and people good in a moral vacuum. It is that the people's demand is limited while the nobles' demand is expansive, and that difference changes what kind of security a prince can build.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve both claims at once: popular favour is safer, yet it only truly holds for a prince who can command, encourage, and build durable dependence in adversity. If the chapter becomes mere popularity advice, it has failed.

Threshold Question:
When a prince rises by noble favour, what must he secure first for adversity: the affection of the nobles who think themselves his equals, or the friendliness of the people who want protection?
