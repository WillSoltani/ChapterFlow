Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why liberality can politically ruin an established ruler, why meanness can be safer, and why spending one's own goods differs from spending others' goods.

Opening Move:
Open on the uncomfortable reversal: liberality sounds praiseworthy, but once an established ruler tries to keep that reputation alive, he may have to consume himself and burden his people.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1 for the reputational problem and visible liberality
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2 for spending property, taxation, odium, poverty, and the reproach of meanness
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3 for why a wise prince should accept mean reputation and how this avoids burdening people
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P4 for Julius II and the King of Spain
- anchor 5 used in paragraph(s): P5 for prince in fact versus prince on the way to becoming one
- anchor 6 used in paragraph(s): P6 for one's own versus others' goods, Cyrus/Caesar/Alexander, and the hatred close

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: define the liberal reputation problem
- P2: show the burden cascade from generosity to taxation, odium, and poverty
- P3: explain why meanness can be safer and how it is liberal toward those from whom the ruler does not take
- P4: use Julius II and the King of Spain as confirmation
- P5: explain the aspirant exception and Caesar's case
- P6: distinguish one's own from others' goods and close on hatred versus reproach

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key distinction is not generosity versus selfishness in the abstract. It is whether the ruler is spending his own resources or forcing those under him to finance his reputation.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve both major distinctions: prince in fact versus prince becoming one, and spending one's own versus spending others' goods.

Threshold Question:
Why can a reputation for liberality make an established prince more hated and less secure than a reputation for meanness?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 7

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: liberal reputation becomes costly when it must be sustained visibly
- Takeaway 2: spending property leads to taxation, odium, poverty, and contempt
- Takeaway 3: mean reputation can preserve revenues, defence, and freedom from rapacity
- Takeaway 4: the ruler becomes liberal toward those from whom he does not take
- Takeaway 5: recent successes by rulers considered mean
- Takeaway 6: aspirant liberality versus established-prince thrift
- Takeaway 7: one's own goods versus others' goods, and hatred versus reproach

Genericity Risks:
- reducing the chapter to ordinary budget prudence
- missing the reputational mechanism that turns liberality into burden
- flattening the aspirant and others-money exceptions
- making meanness sound admirable in itself
