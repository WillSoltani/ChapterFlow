Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The load-bearing distinction is not simply old versus new. It is mixed acquisition under two conditions: territories that resemble the original state and can be absorbed with minimal change, versus territories whose laws, customs, and language make distance, resentment, and outside intervention much more dangerous.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter keeps insisting that force matters, yet it also says force cannot solve everything because the ruler still needs goodwill, local presence, and power balance. Hard depth must preserve that tension instead of turning the chapter into either pure coercion or pure administration.

Threshold Question:
If a new ruler in a mixed state begins with disappointed supporters, injured subjects, and nearby powers watching for advantage, which failure starts the collapse first, and how does the answer change when the territory is similar versus unlike his own?
