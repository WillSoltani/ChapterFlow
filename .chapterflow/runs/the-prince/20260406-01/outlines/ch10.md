By the end, the reader should understand how Machiavelli measures strength, why a wall-backed principality needs more than walls, and how a prince can keep citizens steadfast even after visible loss.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The load-bearing distinction is not strong versus weak in a vague sense. It is whether the prince can support himself openly in the field or must survive by defending a fortified core.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep both material and civic conditions together. Fortifications, stores, labour, artillery, and ordinances matter because they help make the city difficult to attack and the people easier to hold steady.

Threshold Question:
If a prince cannot meet the enemy in the field, what should he secure first: the whole countryside, outside assistance, or a fortified city whose people do not hate him?

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: strength is measured by self-support versus dependency
- Takeaway 2: field-capable rule differs from rule that survives behind walls
- Takeaway 3: fortify and provision the town rather than dissipating force across the whole country
- Takeaway 4: a strong city must also avoid odium among the people
- Takeaway 5: the German cities show visible difficulty plus civic order, stores, work, and exercises
- Takeaway 6: outside losses test morale, but hope, fear, and support can keep citizens steadfast
- Takeaway 7: people may bind themselves more strongly through losses incurred in common defence
