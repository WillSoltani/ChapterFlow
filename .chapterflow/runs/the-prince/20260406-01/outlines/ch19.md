Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why hatred and contempt are the politically fatal reputational states, why public goodwill blocks conspiracy better than private fear alone, and why the Roman emperors force selective rather than total imitation.

Opening Move:
Open on the counterintuitive compression: a prince can survive many complaints, but once he is hated or despised the rest of his defenses become much less reliable.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: rank the real dangers and explain why hatred and contempt outrank ordinary reproach
- P2: distinguish the concrete sources of hatred from the concrete sources of contempt
- P3: show how esteem and popular goodwill make conspiracy hard to recruit and harder to survive
- P4: use France to show how rulers manage blame and favor institutionally, not only emotionally
- P5: introduce the Roman emperors as a harder test case because soldiers form a third political pressure
- P6: show why Marcus succeeds, why Pertinax and Alexander fail, and why good works can still destroy a ruler
- P7: contrast Severus with Caracalla, Commodus, and Maximinus, then close on selective imitation and bridge to Chapter 20

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The essential mechanism is not "be admired." It is that hatred and contempt produce different but converging vulnerabilities. Hatred grows from felt injury and dispossession. Contempt grows from visible instability and loss of dignity. Both make conspiracy thinkable, but they arise from different princely failures and therefore require different discipline.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must keep the chapter's rule narrow while letting the Roman complication stay real. A prince cannot always avoid being hated by someone, and a corrupt body can punish good works. Yet that does not dissolve the rule. It sharpens the need to identify which body can ruin the prince and how hatred or contempt hardens there.

Threshold Question:
If some rulers must accept reproach somewhere, why does Machiavelli still insist that hatred and contempt come first?
