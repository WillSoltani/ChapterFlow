# Style Memory

- Write like a sharp human explainer with taste: specific, concrete, readable, and authored.
- Open with curiosity or tension, not a thesis statement.
- Give each paragraph one clear job. If two paragraphs do the same work, cut or merge.
- Teach the idea directly. Avoid narrating the artifact with lines like "the chapter says."
- Use the brief and source sidecar as the factual ceiling. If the source is thin, stay narrow instead of inflating.
- Easy should feel like an accessible doorway; medium should explain mechanism and application; hard should preserve a live limit, contradiction, or threshold question.
- Tone differences must be functional. Do not simulate tone with adjective swaps.
- Examples should feel vivid and chapter-specific, not like generic workplace or relationship filler.
- Avoid textbook tone, fake profundity, corporate language, and planning-note phrasing.
- No em dashes. No contamination phrases. No banned filler phrases like "crucial," "moreover," or "this matters because."
- In apex_flagship mode, stop early on tone collapse, meta-distance, source-splice leakage, or generic framing.
