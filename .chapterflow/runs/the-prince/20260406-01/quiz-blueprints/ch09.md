# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch09 - Concerning a Civil Principality

Core concepts to test:
- civil principality by favour of fellow citizens
- the two civic humors of people and nobles
- why rule by popular favour is safer than rule by noble favour
- why the people's object is more righteous and easier to satisfy
- the danger of hostile nobles in adversity
- the classification of nobles into bound, weak-useful, and ambitious-dangerous
- the need to win the people if raised by nobles
- Nabis and the rebuttal to the proverb about building on the people
- the danger of magistrate-dependent rule
- the final principle that citizens must always need the state and the prince

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate what a prince raised by nobles must secure first for real safety in adversity, especially when magistrates and insider allies are unreliable

Question plan:
- q01: recall of the two opposing desires in every city
- q02: recall of why rule by nobles is harder to maintain than rule by the people
- q03: comprehension of why the people are easier to satisfy
- q04: work decision-point scenario about elite-backed promotion and the need to protect the broader body
- q05: school postmortem scenario about broad student support versus a small officer bloc
- q06: personal dialogue scenario about ambitious insiders versus the larger household that wants peace
- q07: analysis question on how to treat nobles who are not bound to the prince's fortune
- q08: analysis question on why a prince raised by nobles must win the people over
- q09: analysis question on Nabis and why building on the people is not simply building on mud
- q10: hard-threshold question on what citizens must continue to need if civil rule is to hold in troubled times

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: confusing popular security with mere popularity
- tempting wrong answer type 2: assuming noble backing is safer because elites are fewer
- tempting wrong answer type 3: treating magistrates as a substitute for direct authority in crisis

Explanation diversity notes:
- keep explanations tied to protection, oppression, obedience, and adversity rather than generic trust language
- vary the treatment of nobles so not every wrong answer fails for the same reason
- let hard-question distractors fail because they misread the source of security, not because they sound absurd

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution roughly balanced across 0 / 1 / 2
