# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch23 - How Flatterers Should Be Avoided

Core concepts to test:
- flatterers as a persistent danger
- why truth from everyone makes respect abate
- the third course between openness and secrecy
- selected wise men speaking truth only on asked matters
- questioning on everything and then forming one's own conclusions
- rewarding freer speech inside the gate
- listening to no one outside the gate and remaining steadfast
- Maximilian consulting no one yet never getting his own way
- counsel only when the prince wishes, plus constant inquiry and patient listening
- the final axiom that good counsels are born of the wisdom of the prince

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate how a leader can build a truth channel strong enough to resist flattery without dissolving authority into unmanaged opinion

Question plan:
- q01: recall of the main danger named in the opening
- q02: comprehension of why truth from everyone weakens respect
- q03: recall/comprehension of the third course
- q04: work decision-point scenario on setting a bounded truth channel
- q05: school postmortem scenario on changing too often under hallway opinion
- q06: comprehension of Maximilian's opposite failure
- q07: comprehension of the counsel-only-when-he-wishes rule
- q08: analysis of why a prince who is not wise himself cannot take good advice well
- q09: analysis of why one prudent governor or many competing advisers both create danger for an unwise prince
- q10: threshold question on how truth can be invited without letting respect and resolutions collapse

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: praising total openness as the solution
- tempting wrong answer type 2: praising total secrecy as the solution
- tempting wrong answer type 3: assuming good advisers can manufacture wisdom in any ruler
- tempting wrong answer type 4: confusing patience in listening with surrender in judgment

Explanation diversity notes:
- vary language around flattery, truth, inquiry, restraint, steadiness, counsel, secrecy, and respect
- keep explanations tied to Chapter 23's speech-and-power logic rather than generic feedback advice
- let hard distractors fail because they erase the gate, the prince's own judgment, or the danger of instability

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution at 4 answers for `0`, 3 answers for `1`, and 3 answers for `2`
