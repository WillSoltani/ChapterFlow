# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch03 - Concerning Mixed Principalities

Core concepts to test:
- why mixed principalities are unstable at entry
- injured enemies, disappointed supporters, and goodwill of natives
- same-country annexation versus unlike-country annexation
- residence and colonies versus garrisons
- weaker powers, stronger powers, and foreign footholds
- Roman foresight, deferred war, and Louis XII as anti-model
- the closing rule about making another power powerful

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate how different maintenance rules fit different acquisition conditions and explain why Louis XII's mistakes compound

Question plan:
- q01: recall of why people first change rulers willingly in mixed acquisition
- q02: recall of what the new prince still needs despite armed force
- q03: comprehension of the same-country rule about former ruling family, laws, and taxes
- q04: application in a school decision-point scenario about absorbing a committee whose members expected improvement
- q05: postmortem scenario about a blended household that injured old stakeholders and disappointed the allies who invited change
- q06: work dialogue scenario about why the incoming lead must spend time onsite
- q07: analysis question on colonies versus garrisons and lighter versus heavier injury
- q08: analysis question on Romans, future troubles, and deferred war
- q09: evaluation question on Louis XII's Italian error chain
- q10: hard-threshold question asking which danger in a mixed state deserves attention first when goodwill, similarity, and outside powers all pull in different directions

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: generic confidence, charisma, or communication language with no injury, goodwill, or power-balance logic
- tempting wrong answer type 2: treating all acquisitions as if they can be handled the same way regardless of laws, customs, or language
- tempting wrong answer type 3: assuming force or remote oversight can replace local presence and political memory

Explanation diversity notes:
- avoid repeated explanation openers across direct mode
- keep scenario explanations specific to the pressure of mixed acquisition
- in hard questions, make the wrong answers fail for structural reasons, not because they sound obviously silly

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution roughly balanced across 0 / 1 / 2
