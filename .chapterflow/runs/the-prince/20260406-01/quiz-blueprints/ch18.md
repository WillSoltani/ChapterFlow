# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch18 - Concerning the Way in Which Princes Should Keep Faith

Core concepts to test:
- why keeping faith is praised but insufficient
- the two ways of contesting
- beast and man
- fox and lion
- when a prince may not keep faith
- why men being bad matters to the argument
- disguise, pretending, and dissembling
- Alexander VI as example
- appearing virtuous versus being bound to every virtue
- judging by the eye and by result

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate why Machiavelli treats fidelity as conditional while still making appearance of virtue politically necessary

Question plan:
- q01: recall of what is praiseworthy
- q02: recall/comprehension of the two ways of contesting
- q03: comprehension of fox and lion
- q04: work decision-point scenario on disclosure versus maneuvering room
- q05: school postmortem scenario on overpromised openness losing leverage
- q06: personal dialogue scenario on showing every plan to a bad-faith actor
- q07: analysis question on conditional fidelity
- q08: analysis question on appearance of virtue
- q09: analysis question on eye versus hand and judging by result
- q10: threshold connection to Chapter 15 on appearance and necessity

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: treating the chapter as blanket permission to lie
- tempting wrong answer type 2: keeping the fox and forgetting the lion
- tempting wrong answer type 3: assuming inner virtue alone controls public judgment

Explanation diversity notes:
- vary language around faith, craft, appearance, necessity, leverage, and result
- keep explanations tied to Chapter 18's political logic, not generic workplace spin
- let hard distractors fail because they ignore conditions, force, or public judgment

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution at 4 answers for `0`, 3 answers for `1`, and 3 answers for `2`
