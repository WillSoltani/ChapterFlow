# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch07 - Concerning New Principalities Which Are Acquired Either by the Arms of Others or by Good Fortune

Core concepts to test:
- easy ascent and hard keeping under fortune-built elevation
- roots and foundations laid after accession
- Sforza versus Borgia
- dependence on borrowed arms and factions
- Sinigalia and the cutting down of dangerous dependence
- the Romagna sequence with Ramiro d'Orco
- the four precautions before Alexander's death
- the role of sickness, succession, and malign fortune
- the final rule about new benefits not erasing old injuries

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate which missing root must be built first when a ruler has been lifted by borrowed power but has not yet secured loyal force, local order, or succession insulation

Question plan:
- q01: recall of why fortune-built rulers rise easily and keep with difficulty
- q02: recall of what must be laid afterward if it was not laid before accession
- q03: comprehension of the Sforza-Borgia contrast
- q04: work decision-point scenario about sponsor-backed promotion and missing roots
- q05: school postmortem scenario about favored appointment collapsing when backing recedes
- q06: personal dialogue scenario about inherited position versus faithful support
- q07: analysis question on why Borgia stops depending on the arms and luck of others
- q08: analysis question on the Romagna sequence and why Ramiro d'Orco matters
- q09: analysis question on the four precautions and the role of sickness and Alexander's death
- q10: hard-threshold question on whether fresh favors can erase old injuries when a decisive succession choice arrives

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: treating title or patronage as if it were already a secure foundation
- tempting wrong answer type 2: flattening Borgia into generic ruthlessness with no roots, faction, or succession logic
- tempting wrong answer type 3: assuming new benefits erase old fear or hatred

Explanation diversity notes:
- keep scenario explanations tied to foundations, factions, order, or injuries rather than generic politics
- avoid repeating the same sentence frame for every Borgia-related explanation
- in hard questions, make wrong answers fail because they misread sequence or memory, not because they sound silly

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution roughly balanced across 0 / 1 / 2
