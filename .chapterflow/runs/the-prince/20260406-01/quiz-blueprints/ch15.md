# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch15 - Concerning Things for Which Men, and Especially Princes, Are Praised or Blamed

Core concepts to test:
- real truth versus imagination
- why ideal republics are politically misleading
- how people live versus how they ought to live
- why neglecting the real for the ideal brings ruin
- knowing how to do wrong according to necessity
- the field of praised and blamed qualities
- human limits on possessing all good qualities
- avoiding the vices that lose the state
- tolerating reproach for necessary faults
- seeming virtue versus security, seeming vice versus prosperity

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate why perfect virtue is not Machiavelli's governing standard for a prince who wants to hold his state

Question plan:
- q01: recall of the real-truth claim
- q02: recall of how one lives versus how one ought to live
- q03: comprehension of why ideal conduct can ruin a ruler
- q04: work decision-point scenario about blameless appearance versus necessary preservation
- q05: school postmortem scenario about losing control while trying to look entirely fair
- q06: personal dialogue scenario about looking good versus keeping the house sound
- q07: analysis question on knowing how to do wrong according to necessity
- q08: analysis question on why princes cannot possess all praised qualities
- q09: analysis question on avoiding the vices that lose the state
- q10: hard-threshold question on why seeming vice may sometimes be safer than seeming virtue

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: reading the chapter as permission for unlimited wrongdoing
- tempting wrong answer type 2: assuming admired appearances are automatically politically safe
- tempting wrong answer type 3: importing later chapter conclusions before they are argued

Explanation diversity notes:
- vary language around necessity, reproach, preservation, appearance, and ruin
- keep explanations tied to this chapter's method claim, not later doctrine
- let hard distractors fail because they confuse praise with prudence

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution roughly balanced across 0 / 1 / 2
