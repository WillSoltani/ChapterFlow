# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch17 - Concerning Cruelty and Clemency, and Whether It Is Better to Be Loved Than Feared

Core concepts to test:
- why clemency can be misused
- Borgia and the Florentines
- why a few severe examples can spare the whole people
- why a new prince cannot avoid some imputation of cruelty
- why fear is safer than love
- love as obligation versus fear as punishment
- fear without hatred
- manifest cause and proper justification
- property boundary
- Hannibal and Scipio

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate why controlled severity can be more merciful than indulgence, and why fear must still avoid hatred

Question plan:
- q01: recall of what every prince should desire to be considered
- q02: comprehension of why a few harsh acts can be more merciful
- q03: recall/comprehension of why new princes face a cruelty reputation
- q04: work decision-point scenario on firm corrective action versus ongoing disorder
- q05: school postmortem scenario on avoiding harshness and spreading wider harm
- q06: personal or work scenario on fear without hatred and the property boundary
- q07: analysis question on why fear is safer than love
- q08: analysis question on hatred, manifest cause, and property
- q09: analysis question on Hannibal and Scipio
- q10: hard-threshold question connecting Chapter 17 to Chapter 15's realism about seeming virtue

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: treating mercy as automatically kind no matter what disorder it permits
- tempting wrong answer type 2: treating fear as permission for arbitrary abuse
- tempting wrong answer type 3: ignoring the difference between being feared and being hated

Explanation diversity notes:
- vary language around disorder, punishment, trust, obligation, hatred, and discipline
- keep explanations anchored to Chapter 17 rather than generic management lessons
- let hard distractors fail because they miss public injury, manifest cause, or control

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution at 4 answers for `0`, 3 answers for `1`, and 3 answers for `2`
