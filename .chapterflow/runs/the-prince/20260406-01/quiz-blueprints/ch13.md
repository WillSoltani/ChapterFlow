# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch13 - Concerning Auxiliaries, Mixed Soldiery, and One's Own

Core concepts to test:
- the definition of auxiliaries
- why auxiliaries are more dangerous than mercenaries
- the captive-or-undone paradox
- the Florentines, the Greeks, and Pope Julius as warning examples
- the rule about preferring loss with one's own arms to victory with others
- Cesare Borgia's sequence from auxiliaries to mercenaries to his own men
- the borrowed-arms image from David and Saul
- mixed forces as an intermediate but inferior case
- France and the Swiss as the mixed-arms warning
- the final principle that no principality is secure without its own forces

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate why apparent victory with foreign force still counts as insecurity

Question plan:
- q01: recall of what auxiliaries are
- q02: recall of the line about losing and winning under auxiliaries
- q03: comprehension of why auxiliaries are more hazardous than mercenaries
- q04: work decision-point scenario about inviting another institution's full intervention team
- q05: school postmortem scenario about borrowed disciplined force transferring control
- q06: personal dialogue scenario about borrowed family strength versus one's own
- q07: analysis question on Cesare Borgia's rising reputation
- q08: analysis question on David, Saul, and borrowed arms
- q09: analysis question on mixed forces and the French example
- q10: hard-threshold question on why foreign victory can still be near-defeat

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: treating auxiliaries as safe because they are brave
- tempting wrong answer type 2: assuming mixed forces solve the dependence problem entirely
- tempting wrong answer type 3: reducing the chapter to prideful self-reliance instead of command and security

Explanation diversity notes:
- keep explanations tied to obedience, command, victory, reputation, and one's own strength
- vary the language around captivity and dependence so the explanations do not all sound identical
- let hard distractors fail because they mistake tactical success for political security

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution roughly balanced across 0 / 1 / 2
