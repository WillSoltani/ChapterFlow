# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch22 - Concerning the Secretaries of Princes

Core concepts to test:
- the first opinion of the prince comes from the men around him
- bad servants expose the prince's prime error in choosing
- Antonio da Venafro and Pandolfo Petrucci
- the three classes of intellect
- second-rank intelligence as the ability to recognize good and bad in another
- praise and correction as part of keeping a servant honest
- the never-failing self-interest test
- the servant who thinks first of the prince rather than himself
- honouring, enriching, kindnesses, and shared cares
- making the servant see that he cannot stand alone

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate how a prince can still rule wisely through selection, judgment, and structured loyalty even if he is not the first class of intellect

Question plan:
- q01: recall of what the first opinion of a prince comes from
- q02: recall/comprehension of the three classes of intellect
- q03: comprehension of what Pandolfo and Venafro prove
- q04: work decision-point scenario on choosing a deputy whose interests align with the office
- q05: school postmortem scenario on empowering an aide who used the role for personal leverage
- q06: direct comprehension of the never-failing self-interest test
- q07: comprehension of why praise and correction matter
- q08: analysis of why honour, riches, kindnesses, and cares keep service honest
- q09: analysis of why letting the servant see he cannot stand alone is part of trust
- q10: threshold question on what a non-first-rank prince must still do so that a brilliant secretary proves his wisdom rather than replaces it

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: treating brilliance alone as enough
- tempting wrong answer type 2: treating loyalty as blind obedience detached from judgment
- tempting wrong answer type 3: imagining that rewards alone, without dependence, solve the problem
- tempting wrong answer type 4: forgetting that the minister also reveals the prince

Explanation diversity notes:
- vary language around judgment, selection, service, self-interest, correction, rewards, dependence, and trust
- keep explanations tied to Chapter 22's political logic rather than generic leadership talk
- let hard distractors fail because they erase discrimination, alignment, or the mutual structure of trust

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution at 4 answers for `0`, 3 answers for `1`, and 3 answers for `2`
