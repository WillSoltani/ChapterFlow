# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch20 - Are Fortresses, and Many Other Things to Which Princes Often Resort, Advantageous or Hurtful?

Core concepts to test:
- why the chapter refuses one final abstract judgment
- new princes arming subjects
- why disarming breeds hatred
- annexed provinces versus new principalities
- why factions fail under external pressure
- the possibility of using former distrust profitably
- the warning about supporters who helped only from discontent
- fortresses as conditional rather than universal
- fearing the people versus fearing foreigners
- why not being hated by the people is the best fortress

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate why visible devices of security can fail when the prince has neglected the deeper political condition of popular goodwill

Question plan:
- q01: recall of what new princes usually do with subjects' arms
- q02: recall/comprehension of why disarming breeds hatred
- q03: comprehension of why factions fail when war comes
- q04: work decision-point scenario on stripping authority from a newly absorbed team
- q05: school postmortem scenario on managing through factions
- q06: personal dialogue scenario on former skeptics versus easy flatterers
- q07: analysis question on opportunistic supporters and reasons for favour
- q08: analysis question on fortresses and people versus foreigners
- q09: analysis question on the Countess of Forli case
- q10: threshold question on why the best fortress is still not being hated by the people

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: universalizing one device across all conditions
- tempting wrong answer type 2: treating factions as clever balance
- tempting wrong answer type 3: assuming visible defenses outrank public feeling
- tempting wrong answer type 4: assuming first supporters are automatically the safest allies

Explanation diversity notes:
- vary language around particulars, distrust, dependence, factions, defenses, and hatred
- keep explanations tied to Chapter 20's device-versus-condition logic
- let hard distractors fail because they ignore the state's condition or the people-versus-fortress limit

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution at 4 answers for `0`, 3 answers for `1`, and 3 answers for `2`
