# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch06 - Concerning New Principalities Which Are Acquired by One's Own Arms and Ability

Core concepts to test:
- the archer image and imitation as a high standard
- the difference between fortune as opportunity and ability as form-giving power
- why founders acquire with difficulty but keep with greater ease
- the old-beneficiary versus lukewarm-beneficiary problem
- prayers versus force and the instability of persuasion alone
- Savonarola as the negative example
- Hiero as the practical example of replacing old soldiers and alliances

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate which foundation a founder must secure first when opportunity exists but the new order is still facing organized resistance and weak support

Question plan:
- q01: recall of the archer image and why Machiavelli uses great examples
- q02: recall of what Machiavelli says about relying least on fortune
- q03: comprehension of opportunity plus ability through the great founders
- q04: work decision-point scenario about copying admired operators without changing the structure underneath
- q05: school postmortem scenario about a lab team where old beneficiaries resist and new beneficiaries defend weakly
- q06: personal dialogue scenario on opportunity as material rather than finished success
- q07: analysis question on why introducing a new order is so difficult
- q08: analysis question on prayers versus force, persuasion, and armed versus unarmed founders
- q09: analysis question on Hiero replacing old soldiery and alliances
- q10: hard-threshold question asking which foundation must be secured first once the opening is visible but the old order is still organized

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: generic confidence or vision language with no force, resistance, or alliance logic
- tempting wrong answer type 2: treating opportunity as if it were already durable success
- tempting wrong answer type 3: softening force into persuasion alone even after the chapter explicitly rejects that move

Explanation diversity notes:
- avoid repeating "new order" as the first phrase in every explanation
- keep scenario explanations tied to the chapter's real mechanisms rather than to generic leadership advice
- let wrong answers fail because they mistake applause, opportunity, or imitation for actual founding power

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution roughly balanced across 0 / 1 / 2
