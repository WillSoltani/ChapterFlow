# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch05 - Concerning the Way to Govern Cities or Principalities Which Lived Under Their Own Laws Before They Were Annexed

Core concepts to test:
- the three courses for holding a free city
- the appeal and weakness of the oligarchy-and-tribute option
- Sparta versus Rome as a test of indirect rule
- liberty and ancient privileges as a rallying cry
- the failure of time and benefits to erase remembered freedom
- Pisa as evidence that long subjection does not settle the problem
- the contrast between free cities and places used to a prince
- the closing safest-way rule: destroy them or reside there

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate why milder control can become more dangerous when liberty memory survives beneath outward compliance

Question plan:
- q01: recall of the chapter's three courses for holding a free city
- q02: comprehension of why the oligarchy option can look attractive at first
- q03: comprehension of what Sparta and Rome show when compared
- q04: work decision-point scenario on inheriting a self-governing team and mistaking compliance for settled loyalty
- q05: school postmortem scenario on a dorm council left under its old charter and friendly insiders
- q06: personal dialogue scenario on why half-control over a self-governing family committee stays unstable
- q07: analysis question on liberty and ancient privileges as a rallying cry
- q08: analysis question on Pisa and why time or benefits do not solve the problem
- q09: synthesis question on the contrast between republics and places accustomed to a prince
- q10: evaluate whether calm under tribute and local laws shows that liberty has been forgotten or only delayed

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: assuming benefits eventually erase political memory
- tempting wrong answer type 2: treating the oligarchy option as automatically safe because it is milder
- tempting wrong answer type 3: confusing a city used to a prince with a city used to freedom

Explanation diversity notes:
- avoid repeating liberty-memory phrasing in every explanation
- let wrong answers fail because they underestimate how durable self-rule memory is
- keep the chapter's harsh asymmetry visible rather than smoothing all three courses into equal options

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution roughly balanced across 0 / 1 / 2
