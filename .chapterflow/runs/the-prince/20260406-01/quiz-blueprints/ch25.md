# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch25 - What Fortune Can Effect in Human Affairs and How to Withstand Her

Core concepts to test:
- the common fatalist opinion
- fortune governing about one-half of actions
- the raging river metaphor
- defenses and barriers raised in fair weather
- fortune striking where valour has not prepared resistance
- the spirit-of-the-times rule
- different methods succeeding or failing depending on fit
- fixed nature and past success making adaptation rare
- Julius II as a time-fit example
- the closing preference for adventurousness and its limits

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate whether a failure came from contingency alone, from lack of preparation, or from a method that no longer fit the times

Question plan:
- q01: recall of the partial-free-will claim
- q02: comprehension of the river metaphor
- q03: recall/comprehension of why barriers matter
- q04: work decision-point scenario on building buffers during a calm cycle
- q05: school postmortem scenario on one style failing after the times changed
- q06: comprehension of the spirit-of-the-times rule
- q07: comprehension of why fixed nature makes adaptation rare
- q08: analysis of Julius II as a case of fit rather than universal boldness
- q09: analysis of why a prince can be ruined without changing character
- q10: threshold question on whether fortune is better resisted by prior barriers, altered method, or both

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: turning the chapter into pure fatalism
- tempting wrong answer type 2: turning it into pure self-mastery
- tempting wrong answer type 3: assuming one method is always best
- tempting wrong answer type 4: reading the final preference for adventurousness as a timeless rule detached from the argument about changing times

Explanation diversity notes:
- vary language around fortune, barriers, preparation, method, fit, change, character, and boldness
- keep explanations tied to Chapter 25's paired logic of preparation and adaptation
- let hard distractors fail because they erase either the flood metaphor, the spirit-of-the-times rule, or the problem of fixed nature

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution at 4 answers for `0`, 3 answers for `1`, and 3 answers for `2`
