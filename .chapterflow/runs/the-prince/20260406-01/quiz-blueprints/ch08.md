# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch08 - Concerning Those Who Have Obtained a Principality by Wickedness

Core concepts to test:
- the distinction between wicked acquisition and glory
- Agathocles as the first example
- Oliverotto as the second example
- why some cruel rulers remain secure while others fail
- severities properly used versus badly used
- injuries done all at once and benefits given little by little
- the warning about keeping the knife in hand
- living among the people so late reactive measures do not fail

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate which sequence, stopped harm with later stabilization or recurring harm with belated kindness, actually gives a wickedly acquired regime more security without confusing that with glory

Question plan:
- q01: recall of the chapter's opening distinction between wicked acquisition and civil favour
- q02: recall of what Machiavelli says wicked methods may gain and may not gain
- q03: comprehension of why Agathocles is not credited to fortune yet still denied glory
- q04: work decision-point scenario about a dirty internal seizure followed by the choice between one closed punitive phase and recurring punishment
- q05: school postmortem scenario about a shocking procedural ambush that secured office but not admiration
- q06: personal dialogue scenario on why repeated household punishment keeps attachment from reforming
- q07: analysis question on properly used severities versus badly used severities
- q08: analysis question on injuries all at once and benefits little by little
- q09: analysis question on why the ruler who repeats wrongs must keep the knife in his hand
- q10: hard-threshold question on what presence among the people prevents when crisis arrives

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: treating political durability as moral praise
- tempting wrong answer type 2: softening the chapter into generic firmness with no sequence of harms and benefits
- tempting wrong answer type 3: assuming late kindness can erase the effects of repeated injury

Explanation diversity notes:
- keep the chapter's moral refusal explicit in at least some explanations
- vary the explanation openings so not every answer starts with Agathocles or cruelty
- let wrong answers fail because they confuse glory with security or because they ignore sequence

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution roughly balanced across 0 / 1 / 2
