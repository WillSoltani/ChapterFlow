# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch19 - That One Should Avoid Being Despised and Hated

Core concepts to test:
- why hatred and contempt outrank other reproaches
- hatred from rapacity and violation of property and women
- contempt from fickleness, frivolity, weakness, and irresolution
- esteem and irrevocable judgment
- two fears, from within and without
- why popular goodwill blocks conspiracy
- the Bentivogli example
- France, parliament, and leaving reproach to others while keeping grace
- the Roman emperors and the third pressure of soldiers
- why Marcus and Severus are selective, not total, models

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate why hatred and contempt remain the first faults to avoid even when a ruler cannot satisfy every political body at once

Question plan:
- q01: recall of what makes a prince hated above all
- q02: recall/comprehension of what makes a prince contemptible
- q03: comprehension of why conspiracy is weak when the people are not hostile
- q04: work decision-point scenario on clawing back something the team already counts as theirs
- q05: school postmortem scenario on a student leader becoming contemptible through reversals and unserious conduct
- q06: school predict-reveal scenario on reproach, procedure, and preserving goodwill
- q07: analysis question on the Roman emperors' third difficulty
- q08: analysis question on Marcus versus Pertinax and Alexander
- q09: analysis question on Severus and Marcus as selective extracts
- q10: threshold question on why hatred and contempt come first if some reproach is unavoidable

Distractor notes:

- tempting wrong answer type 2: confusing hatred with contempt
- tempting wrong answer type 3: assuming conspiracies succeed mainly because of clever plotting rather than public hostility
- tempting wrong answer type 4: treating Marcus and Severus as universal copies rather than situational lessons

Explanation diversity notes:
- vary language around injury, dignity, goodwill, conspiracy, blame, and regime fit
- keep explanations tied to Chapter 19's ranked political logic, not generic leadership tone
- let hard distractors fail because they ignore which body matters, how esteem works, or why hatred and contempt trigger collapse
