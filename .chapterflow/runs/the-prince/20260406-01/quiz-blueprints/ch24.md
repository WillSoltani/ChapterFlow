# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter:
- ch24 - Why the Princes of Italy Have Lost Their States

Core concepts to test:
- why a new prince may become more secure than a hereditary one
- why present ability binds more tightly than ancient blood
- the double glory versus double disgrace contrast
- the shared defects: weak arms, hostile people, or unsecured nobles
- why states able to keep an army in the field are not easily lost in the absence of those defects
- Philip of Macedon as a counterexample to excuse-making
- why princes should not accuse fortune first
- the accusation of sloth in calm times
- flight versus defense
- why hoped-for recall is not a reliable substitute for self-secured defense

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate whether a collapse should be read as sudden bad luck or as evidence that visible preparation and political supports were neglected long before the crisis

Question plan:
- q01: recall of why a new prince may be more secure than an old one
- q02: comprehension of why men are attracted more by the present than by the past
- q03: recall of the double glory and double disgrace contrast
- q04: work decision-point scenario on whether calm conditions justify delaying preparation
- q05: school postmortem scenario on losing volunteers or insiders before a contested event
- q06: comprehension of the defects Machiavelli finds in the lost Italian states
- q07: comprehension of what Philip of Macedon proves
- q08: analysis of why blaming fortune hides earlier sloth
- q09: analysis of why flight plus hoped-for restoration is a weak plan
- q10: threshold question on how to tell whether loss came from neglected preparation rather than fortune alone

Distractor notes:

- tempting wrong answer type 2: isolating arms and ignoring people or nobles
- tempting wrong answer type 3: treating fortune as a full excuse that erases neglected preparation
- tempting wrong answer type 4: praising later restoration as if it were equal to self-secured defense

Explanation diversity notes:

- let hard distractors fail because they erase the calm-against-tempest rule, the political supports, or the final self-reliance test
