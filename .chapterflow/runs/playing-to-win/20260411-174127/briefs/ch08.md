Core Claim:
Strategy should shorten the odds under uncertainty rather than pretend certainty can be achieved first. The chapter argues for reverse engineering from a desired strategic outcome toward the conditions that would have to be true, then testing barriers and uncertainties directly. Its pressure point is that teams often seek confidence instead of improving the odds.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is not a generic risk chapter. Its distinct mechanism is reverse engineering through what-would-have-to-be-true logic. The chapter is specifically about turning uncertainty into testable conditions and strategic discipline rather than into paralysis or theater.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show that uncertainty cannot be removed before strategy.
Then they explain why the real task is to improve the odds rather than chase certainty.
Then they frame reverse engineering and what-would-have-to-be-true logic as the operating mechanism.
Then they push the reader to surface barriers and assumptions directly instead of hiding them.
