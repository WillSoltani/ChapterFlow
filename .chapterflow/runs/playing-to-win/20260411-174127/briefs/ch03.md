Core Claim:
Strategy gets real when the company chooses the field on which it intends to win. Where to play is not one decision but a set of field decisions about customers, segments, channels, geographies, categories, and sometimes vertical scope. The chapter uses Bounty to show that focus can strengthen a business more than broad presence can. Its pressure point is that leaders often call breadth ambition when it is actually refusal to choose.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is not a generic focus chapter. Its distinct mechanism is that field choice determines where the company will spend attention and what kind of advantage can even make sense later. The chapter is specifically about the dimensions of field choice and the traps that make teams avoid them.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show that where to play is the next real narrowing move after winning aspiration.
Then they use Bounty to show how segmentation and geography choices change the business.
Then they explain the main dimensions of where-to-play choice.
Then they warn against trying to serve everyone, trying to buy your way out, or accepting the current field as fixed.

Required Anchors:
- Bounty as the chapter's main proof case for choosing a stronger playing field.
- The dimensions of where to play: customers, channels, categories, geography, and related field boundaries.
- The traps of serving everyone, buying your way out, and treating the current field as immutable.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter can be misread as if narrower is always better. Hard depth must preserve that the right field has to be chosen strategically, not just made smaller for its own sake. The limit is that focus without a usable route to win becomes disciplined irrelevance.
