Core Claim:
Capabilities are the distinct things the organization must be able to do in order to deliver its chosen how-to-win choice. The chapter argues that strengths only matter strategically when they fit the chosen field and edge and when they reinforce one another as an activity system. Gillette is the chapter's visible anchor for capability fit.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is not a generic strengths chapter. Its distinct mechanism is that capabilities are selected by strategy rather than admired in the abstract. The chapter is specifically about capability fit, reinforcement, and the difference between a loose list of good traits and a system that sustains an edge.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show that a chosen edge is empty unless the organization can actually deliver it.
Then they use Gillette to show capability fit as a strategic issue rather than a talent slogan.
Then they explain that capabilities work as a reinforcing system, not a collection of isolated strengths.
Then they warn against admiring strengths that do not match the field or edge.

Required Anchors:
- Gillette as the chapter's main proof case for capability fit.
- core capabilities as the chapter's main mechanism.
- activity system as the reinforcing structure that turns strengths into a real advantage.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- capabilities: the distinct things the organization must do well to make the chosen edge real.
- capability fit: alignment between the chosen field, the chosen edge, and what the organization can actually do.
- activity system: the reinforcing set of activities that make the capability set coherent.

Specific Applications:
- checking whether a strategy depends on capabilities the organization does not really have
- spotting when one admired strength is being mistaken for a full system
- testing whether capabilities reinforce the chosen edge or only look impressive on their own

Common Misreadings:
- capabilities are just a list of internal strengths
- any strength is useful if the team is proud of it
- capability building can be postponed until after the strategy is already working

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter can be misread as if existing strengths should always dictate strategy. Hard depth must preserve that capabilities matter because they support the chosen edge, not because the company happened to build them first. The limit is that a capability set without strategic fit becomes expensive vanity.
