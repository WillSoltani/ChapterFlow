Core Claim:
Management systems keep strategy alive by reinforcing the earlier choices through reviews, measures, routines, and accountability. The chapter argues that even a good field, edge, and capability set will decay if the systems around them still reward the old game. Its pressure point is that review processes can either create real strategic dialogue or collapse into corporate theater.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is not a generic execution chapter. Its distinct mechanism is that systems determine whether the strategy stays reinforced or gets hollowed out by habit. The chapter is specifically about measures, review process, and the difference between real dialogue and performance theater.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show that strategy fails when the surrounding systems still reward the old behavior.
Then they explain why management systems are part of strategy rather than a neutral implementation layer.
Then they focus on review processes and measures as reinforcement tools.
Then they warn against corporate theater that looks like discipline but avoids real strategic dialogue.
