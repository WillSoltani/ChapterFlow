# Book Skeleton

## Metadata
- Book ID: `playing-to-win`
- Title: `Playing to Win: How Strategy Really Works`
- Authors: `A.G. Lafley, Roger L. Martin`
- Locked edition: `Harvard Business Review Press, 2013 English trade family`
- Variant family: `EMH`
- Wave size: `2`

## Chapter Order
1. `Strategy Is Choice`
Intent: establish that strategy is an integrated set of choices rather than a vision statement, budget, or optimization exercise.
Source richness: strong
Concept density estimate: medium
Moral complexity flags: low

2. `What Is Winning`
Intent: define winning aspiration and show why playing to win differs from merely participating.
Source richness: strong
Concept density estimate: medium
Moral complexity flags: low

3. `Where to Play`
Intent: show that strategic focus starts by choosing the field, segments, channels, and geographies where the firm will compete.
Source richness: medium-thin
Concept density estimate: medium
Moral complexity flags: low

4. `How to Win`
Intent: explain that winning requires a defensible value equation rather than a generic plan to compete harder.
Source richness: strong
Concept density estimate: medium
Moral complexity flags: medium because partnerships and alternate routes can look like retreat if misread.

5. `Play to Your Strengths`
Intent: connect where-to-play and how-to-win choices to the capability system required to make them real.
Source richness: medium-thin
Concept density estimate: medium-high
Moral complexity flags: low

6. `Manage What Matters`
Intent: show that management systems, reviews, and measures either support the strategy or hollow it out.
Source richness: strong
Concept density estimate: medium-high
Moral complexity flags: medium because the chapter pressures leaders to redesign meetings, metrics, and accountability.

7. `Think Through Strategy`
Intent: give the reader a disciplined way to analyze the landscape and generate strategic possibilities.
Source richness: strong
Concept density estimate: high
Moral complexity flags: low

8. `Shorten Your Odds`
Intent: close by replacing certainty-seeking with reverse engineering, barrier testing, and practical decision discipline under uncertainty.
Source richness: strong
Concept density estimate: high
Moral complexity flags: low
