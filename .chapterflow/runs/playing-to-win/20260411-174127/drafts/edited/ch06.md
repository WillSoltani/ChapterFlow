Many teams say they have a strategy review process when they really have a reporting ritual. The dashboard is full, the meetings recur on schedule, and everyone can explain what happened last week. Yet the strategy still drifts because the system is rewarding old behaviors, old measures, and old comfort. The chapter's first correction is that management systems are not neutral administration. They are part of strategy because they decide whether the chosen field, edge, and capabilities stay reinforced or quietly decay.

Management systems come last in the cascade and still matter deeply. Once the company has chosen where to play, how to win, and what capabilities matter, it has to build routines, reviews, and measures that keep those choices alive. If the system still rewards the old game, then the strategy will slide backward no matter how sharp the language sounds. Measures matter here, but only when they point attention at the right consequences instead of creating noise.

The strategy review process gives the chapter its operating center. A real review is not a ceremony for status updates. It is a place where the strategy gets pressure-tested, contradictions get named, and choices get refreshed when the evidence says they should. This is why the chapter contrasts corporate theater with dialogue. Theater looks disciplined because the calendar is full and the slides are polished. Dialogue is harder because it forces the room to confront whether the system is really reinforcing the chosen game.

That difference matters because a company can drown strategy in process while believing it is strengthening execution. More metrics do not automatically help. More meetings do not automatically help. More reporting can even become a way to protect appearances while the actual edge weakens. The chapter keeps returning to one standard: does the system reinforce what the strategy says matters, or does it reward something older, safer, or easier to count?

This is where accountability becomes strategic rather than procedural. If the review process, measures, and routines point at the wrong behavior, then the organization learns the wrong lesson about what winning requires. A field choice can blur. An edge can soften. A capability system can decay. The chapter insists that management systems are what keep those choices from sliding back into slogans.

The limit matters because process can become theater very easily. A company can build regular meetings, clean dashboards, and disciplined reporting habits that all work smoothly together, yet still avoid the real strategic issues. The chapter's correction is narrower than generic management advice: systems only matter when they keep the chosen game alive. Once that is clear, the next question follows naturally. How should a team generate and test strategic possibilities instead of only reinforcing the answers it already has?
