Core Claim:
The chapter uses non-human animal behavior to establish the book's central move before turning to humans. Social grooming among primates looks like hygiene on the surface, but the deeper function is political alliance management. Competitive altruism among Arabian babblers looks group-serving, but the behavior makes more sense as prestige competition. These cases train the reader to separate public-looking function from social payoff.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the chapter that earns the book's method before the human cases begin. Its distinctness comes from the two animal anchors and the way they lower the reader's defenses: hidden motives look less like a moral accusation when they first appear in primate grooming and babbler prestige contests.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- hidden motives: outwardly acceptable explanations that can differ from the social payoff actually driving behavior
- political behavior: behavior that manages alliances, standing, or leverage inside a group
- competitive altruism: generous-looking action used to compete for prestige

Specific Applications:
- ask what social payoff a generous-looking act might buy
- separate surface function from alliance function in group behavior
- read prestige contests that are disguised as service

Common Misreadings:
- the chapter says all helping is fake
- animal analogies are decorative rather than methodological

Counterarguments or Limits:
Animal analogies are suggestive, not final proof of every later human claim. The chapter's value is diagnostic training, not a total explanation of all cooperation.
