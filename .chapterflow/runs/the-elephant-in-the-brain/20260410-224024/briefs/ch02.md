Core Claim:
The chapter argues that human physiology and behavior were shaped not only by nature in the broad sense, but by competition within groups. It identifies three major competitive games: sex, social status, and politics. In each game, people send and read signals about what kind of mate, friend, ally, or coalition partner they would be. This chapter scales the mixed-motive logic from animal analogies into human social life.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is where the book names the recurring human games instead of only hinting at them through animal cases. The distinct move is the three-game frame and the claim that signaling is central across sex, status, and politics.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors argue that intra-group competition shaped major parts of human behavior.
Then they separate that competition into the games of sex, social status, and politics.
Then they show that each game depends on sending and receiving signals of partner quality.
