Core Claim:
The chapter argues that conspicuous consumption is only one part of a wider consumer-signaling system. Purchases can signal wealth, conscientiousness, adventurousness, and identity, not just raw spending power.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the chapter where private buying becomes public signaling. Its distinctness comes from expanding beyond obvious luxury display into subtler forms of consumer identity and trait signaling.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors place conspicuous consumption inside a broader signaling framework.
Then they show that purchases can signal wealth but also traits like conscientiousness and adventurousness.
Then they connect consumer choices to identity display rather than to utility alone.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- consumption: purchasing and visible use of goods as social communication
- conspicuous consumption: publicly legible spending that signals wealth
- trait signaling: purchases signaling conscientiousness or adventurousness
- identity display: using goods to communicate who one is or wants to be seen as

Specific Applications:
- notice what trait a purchase signals beyond simple usefulness
- ask whether a buying choice is about wealth, identity, or conscientiousness
- distinguish utility from public meaning in consumer choices
