Core Claim:
The chapter explains that humans are unusual for their ability to make and enforce norms within groups. Norms suppress selfishness and support collective welfare, helping groups partly escape the competitive games from the prior chapter. The chapter roots this in hunter-gatherer life, where egalitarian groups relied on weapons, coalition enforcement, gossip, and reputation. It also highlights subtle norms against bragging, currying favor, subgroup politics, and visible selfish motives.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the chapter where the book explains what keeps competition from exploding into open fracture. Its distinctness comes from norm enforcement, egalitarian coalition control, gossip, reputation, and anti-selfishness rules.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors introduce norms as a uniquely important human capacity.
Then they show how egalitarian groups enforced norms through coalitions, weapons, gossip, and reputation.
Then they argue that many subtle norms target selfish display and political maneuvering directly.
