Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why purchases can signal wealth, conscientiousness, adventurousness, and identity rather than mere usefulness.

Opening Move:
Open from the mismatch between what a product does and what owning it publicly says about the person who chose it.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 conspicuous consumption as one pattern among several
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 purchases signaling wealth and conscientiousness
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 purchases signaling adventurousness and identity

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: narrow luxury-display model is too small
- P2: broader consumer-signaling frame
- P3: wealth signaling through purchases
- P4: conscientiousness signaling through purchases
- P5: adventurousness and identity signaling
- P6: utility remains but public meaning rides along
- P7 if needed: bridge into art

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The mechanism is not that utility disappears. It is that visible purchases can carry additional public meaning about status, character, and identity.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How can consumption remain genuinely useful while also serving as a layered signaling system about wealth, discipline, taste, and group membership?

Threshold Question:
When is a purchase mainly solving a practical problem, and when is it also communicating something about who the buyer is or wants to appear to be?
