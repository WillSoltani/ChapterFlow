Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that much human social behavior is shaped by competition for mates, status, and coalition power, and that signaling runs through all three.

Opening Move:
Open from the pressure of everyday human competition rather than abstract theory, then name the three games.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 the sexual game
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 the status game
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 the political game and coalition signaling

- P1: open with pressure that humans are not just surviving nature but reading each other
- P2: sexual game and partner signaling

- P4: politics as coalition competition

- P6: tension between cooperation and competition
- P7 if needed: bridge into norms

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The key mechanism is that people compete inside groups by sending readable signals of their value as mates, friends, allies, or leaders.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How can humans depend so heavily on cooperation if core behavior is still shaped by these competitive games?

Threshold Question:
When does a cooperative act mainly strengthen the group, and when is it mostly a bid inside one of the three games?
