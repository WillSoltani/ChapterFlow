Words often get treated as the serious part of communication, but people do not always trust them most. Chapter 7 starts there. After spoken reasons lost authority, the book turns to body language and asks why nonverbal behavior often seems more credible than the story people tell.

The chapter's answer is that much body language works as honest signaling. These cues are not just decoration. Posture, gaze, timing, distance, and tone help communicate readiness, confidence, discomfort, interest, and social intent.

The chapter is not saying body language always tells the whole truth. It says many nonverbal cues are harder to fake cleanly than spoken reasons. That makes them useful as more honest signals when the stakes are high and direct speech would be costly or strategically polished.

This matters because body language helps coordinate socially sensitive behavior in sex, politics, and status. People can read interest, hesitation, dominance, alignment, and threat without forcing everything into explicit speech.

That does not make body language pure leakage. Nonverbal behavior is still part of strategic social life. The chapter's sharper claim is that some body signals remain more constrained than words, so they can reveal motive or state with less verbal management. The next chapter turns to laughter, where coordination is mixed with softened danger and deniability.
