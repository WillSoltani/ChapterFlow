Religion is often described as private belief, but the chapter argues that this is too narrow. Religious life also helps groups bond, display loyalty, and enforce norms.

Its main idea is that visible practice matters. Sacrifice, ritual synchrony, badges, sermons, and supernatural enforcement can all help make commitment public. Religion can therefore show who belongs, who aligns, and what the group expects.

This does not erase sincere belief. The stronger point is that belief and social function can coexist. Religious acts can deepen solidarity while also making loyalty and norm enforcement more visible.

The hard lesson is overlap. A ritual can bond people, display allegiance, and reinforce expected behavior at the same time. That bridge leads into politics, where group loyalty, strong emotions, low information, and sacrifice become central again.
