School is usually described as a place for learning, but the chapter argues that this story is too small. School often looks like it also rewards endurance, compliance, and credential completion in ways that go beyond direct skill transfer.

Its first major explanation is signaling. School may signal intelligence, conscientiousness, and conformity. A record of showing up, finishing work, and moving through a demanding institution can communicate ability, discipline, and fit.

The chapter then adds other functions. School can babysit by structuring time, network by connecting people, and shape norms through indoctrination or domestication. These functions widen the picture beyond pure instruction.

This does not mean school never teaches. Real learning can still happen. The stronger point is that education can teach while also serving as a credential and a social-management system.

The hard lesson is coexistence. School can produce knowledge while also signaling intelligence, conscientiousness, and conformity. That bridge leads into medicine, where visible caring and sacrifice matter alongside health claims.
