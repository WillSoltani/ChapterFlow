People often keep talking after the useful facts are already on the table. That is the opening clue. The chapter argues that conversation is not only for exchanging information.

Talk also lets people show what they can know and learn. In conversation, a person can display awareness, memory, interpretation, curiosity, and range. Bringing in the right example or asking the sharp next question can function as public evidence of intellectual value.

This does not erase the practical side of speech. Conversation still helps people share updates and solve problems. The chapter's stronger claim is that these practical uses do not explain all the energy people pour into talk.

News matters here because being informed gives people material for socially valuable conversation. Academic research matters for a similar reason. Discussing studies or ideas can signal seriousness, discipline, and the ability to handle complex knowledge.

The hard point is mixed motive. Conversation can genuinely share ideas while also serving as a stage for intellectual display. That bridge leads naturally into consumption, where display shifts from speech to visible purchases.
