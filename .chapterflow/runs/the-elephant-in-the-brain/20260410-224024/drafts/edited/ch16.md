Politics is often described as policy optimization, but the chapter argues that this is too narrow. Democratic participation may express group loyalty as much as careful policy reasoning.

Its main idea is visible side-taking. Strong emotions, low information, and sacrifice all fit a loyalty account better than a purely rational model. Political behavior can therefore show which coalition someone belongs to, not only what policies they endorse.

This does not erase real policy goals. The stronger point is that civic action can aim at outcomes while also functioning as a signal of allegiance. People can care about results and still display loyalty through emotionally charged, costly participation.

The hard lesson is coexistence. Politics can pursue real collective aims while also operating as public group display. That sets up the conclusion's push toward humility, self-awareness, and better institutional design.
