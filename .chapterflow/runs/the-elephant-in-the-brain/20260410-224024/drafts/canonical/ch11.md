Some artistic objects seem to gain value when they look difficult, wasteful, or impractical. A hand-worked piece can feel more important than an easier equivalent. A performance can seem more impressive because of the burden it visibly carries. The chapter begins from that odd pattern and argues that direct utility cannot explain all of what people value in art.

The proposed explanation is costly signaling. Art can function as a signal because visible cost, effort, and waste are hard to fake cleanly. This is where the handicap principle enters. A burden that would be pointless from a narrow functional perspective can still help make the signal credible. The very impracticality of the work can become part of why it is socially legible as valuable.

That does not mean art loses all intrinsic appeal. The chapter does not need people to be pretending when they enjoy or admire a work. Its stronger claim is that visible burdens and difficulties can help support the social value attached to the work. Costliness and appreciation can coexist.

Impracticality matters because it separates the work from ordinary function. The less a piece looks like a straightforward tool, the easier it can become to read it as carrying value beyond direct use. Extrinsic features matter for a similar reason. The conditions around the work, not just the work's immediate function, can shape what it signals about status, seriousness, or worth.

This makes art a sharper version of the signaling logic from the previous chapter. Consumer goods can carry meaning while still being practical. Art often pushes further by leaning into difficulty, visible expense, or apparent waste. Those features help explain why artistic value can feel partly social without becoming wholly fake.

The chapter's tension is therefore mixed motive again. Art can be genuinely moving while also drawing part of its value from costly signaling and handicap logic. Once that is visible, the move into charity becomes easier to see: if art can signal through visible burden, giving can signal through visible sacrifice and moral display.
