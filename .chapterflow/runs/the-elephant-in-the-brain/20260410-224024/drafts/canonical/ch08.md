A laugh can make a risky moment feel safer without making it meaningless. That is the strange social fact Chapter 8 opens with. After body language showed how signals coordinate sensitive interaction, the book turns to laughter and asks why this sound so often changes the stakes of a moment.

The chapter's answer is that laughter is a play signal. It does not merely express joy. It marks an interaction as less dangerous than it might otherwise seem. The laugh says, in effect, that the move should be read with some softness, some room, some reduction in threat.

This matters first for physical danger. Play signals help creatures interact in ways that look rough, testing, or unstable without escalating into full harm. But the chapter pushes beyond physical play into social life, where danger is often reputational, relational, or political rather than bodily.

That is where laughter becomes especially revealing. A joke, tease, provocation, flirtation, or challenge may carry real social content. Laughter does not erase that content. Instead it softens the edge, lowers the immediate temperature, and helps people stay in the interaction without having to commit to full offense or full retreat.

This is why laughter is tied to deniability. A person can say something risky and then laugh. If the move lands well, the signal can carry forward. If it lands badly, the laugh creates space to pull back: it was only a joke, only play, only not quite serious. The content is still there, but the social commitment to it is softened.

That makes laughter a flexible tool in ambiguous interactions. It can reduce danger, preserve connection, test boundaries, signal playfulness, and keep multiple interpretations alive at once. The chapter does not treat laughter as pure innocence. A laugh can carry aggression, attraction, alliance, mockery, or nervous release while still doing the work of softening risk.

The hard part is that laughter therefore mixes safety with real meaning. It helps people approach danger without fully stepping into it. The signal says the moment is not all the way serious, yet everyone in the room may still understand that something important has been floated, tested, or implied.

That is why the next chapter turns to conversation. If laughter helps people manage risk and deniability in brief signals, conversation expands the field into a longer social performance where people display what they know and how quickly they can learn.
