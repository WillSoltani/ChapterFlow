It is easy to notice a flashy purchase and call it status display. A luxury watch or expensive car seems to make the point obvious. The chapter starts by arguing that this familiar picture is too small. Conspicuous consumption matters, but it is only one pattern inside a wider system of consumer signaling.

The broader claim is that purchases can signal more than wealth. Buying choices can also communicate conscientiousness, adventurousness, and identity. A person may choose something that looks careful and disciplined, something that looks bold and exploratory, or something that marks belonging to a certain kind of group. The item can still be useful, but usefulness does not exhaust what it says in public.

This is why the chapter does not reduce consumption to vanity alone. People often buy things for real practical reasons. The stronger point is that visible purchases can carry layered meaning at the same time. A product can solve a problem while also sending information about character, taste, or social fit.

Wealth signaling remains important because some purchases plainly indicate resources. But the outline insists that this is not the whole picture. Consumption also helps people show that they are organized, careful, daring, or aligned with a certain identity. Those signals do not have to involve the loudest or most expensive goods.

Identity matters especially because consumer choices often become a visible language for who someone is or wants to be seen as. What a person carries, wears, uses, or is seen choosing can function as a public cue about values and group membership. That does not make the utility false. It means utility rides alongside expression and signaling.

The chapter's tension is therefore similar to earlier chapters. Consumption can be practical and expressive at once. Once that mixed motive is visible, the next move into art becomes easier to understand. If ordinary purchases can signal through visible choice, art may show an even stronger version of signaling through cost, impracticality, and display value.
