Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader can name the specific intimacy-avoidance function that each marital game is performing, and can see how each antithesis requires the stopping player to risk the real thing.

Opening Move:
Open inside Parveen and Dev's Sunday planning conversation. The trap is set and sprung before the reader has a name for what just happened.

Paragraph Job Map:
P1: scene — Parveen/Dev, the Corner trap. Both moves available; both lose.
P2: name the category. Marital games are intimacy-avoidance mechanisms in partnership form. Introduce the central claim: the game is the substitute; genuine vulnerability is what would replace it.
P3: Parveen/Corner anatomy. Thesis, antithesis (name the trap), dynamics (her need to confirm Dev is hopeless so she doesn't have to name what she actually wants), payoff (avoidance of real request).
P4: Luca/IWFY anatomy. Berne's structural insight: Luca chose Nadia partly BECAUSE she provided the excuse. The restriction is not imposed; it was invited in. This is the chapter's hardest analytical move and it must not be softened.
P5: Vera/Sweetheart anatomy. The story at the dinner party. The affectionate framing. The veiled insult at the end. Bastian cannot object. The payoff is aggression with cover and stored grievance released cleanly.
P6: the limit. The antithesis for all three requires the stopping player to say something real and vulnerable. That is the thing the game was built to avoid. The person who names the trap in a Corner game has to say what they actually want. The person who gives up IWFY has to try the thing they've been afraid of failing at. The person who stops Sweetheart has to say they are hurt. Each antithesis is the exact move the game was protecting against.
P7: bridge. These games live longest inside the closest relationships because the stakes of real vulnerability are highest there. The party games in the next chapter run the same avoidance strategy in shorter, cheaper form.
