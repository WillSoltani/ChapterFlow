Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader can recognize the shape of a long-running game — not a bad afternoon but a bad decade — and can apply both the anatomy from Ch3 and the energetics from Ch4 to a real pattern in their life or someone they know.

Opening Move:
Open with the time dimension: some transactions last an afternoon; some last thirty years. The opening scene shows Greta signing a new business contract with a partner who seems very promising and who will, six months from now, do exactly what the last three partners did.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Greta, the fourth contract. The reader recognizes the shape before Greta does.
- P2: name the category. Life games are the ones that run at biographical scale. The thesis repeats. The switch arrives. The payoff is collected. And then the game resets and runs again. Introduce four patterns and signal that each will be walked through the anatomy.
- P3: Greta's pattern (exploitability game). Thesis, antithesis, dynamics, payoff. The payoff is the confirmation: "the world takes what it can from me." What the game is protecting is the position's evidence base.
- P4: Felix's pattern (righteous-outrage game). Thesis: collect offenses, spring the trap. Antithesis: impeccable procedure that gives no opening. Dynamics: Felix needs the stored anger to feel just. Payoff: guilt-free moral superiority. The game is confirming "I'm OK / you're not OK" and has been for twenty years.
- P5: Ivar's pattern (displaced-blame game). Thesis: make a mistake, blame the nearest witness. Antithesis: refuse the blame. Dynamics: Ivar's avoidance of a position he cannot hold (I caused this). Payoff: the mistake is erased, the witness is the problem. Briefly: Mona's cycle (over-commit, fail visibly, collect sympathy), named as a variant of the same displaced-responsibility structure.
- P6: the limit. For games running at this scale, the antithesis is not a sentence; it is a project. Breaking Greta's pattern requires Greta to have a different relationship with her own exploitability, which requires a different stroke supply and a different life position, both of which have been the same for thirty years. Seeing the pattern is always worth it. The seeing does not make the exit cheap.
- P7: bridge to Ch6. Life games often run most visibly inside the closest relationships. The next chapter looks at the specific versions that organize a marriage or a long-term partnership.

Takeaway Count Lock: Easy 3, Medium 5, Hard 6.

Hard Takeaway Topics:
1. Life games run at biographical scale — the thesis repeats yearly, not per conversation.
2. The exploitability game: sets up conditions of exploitation and collects the proof.
3. The righteous-outrage game: stores offenses until a morally justified release point.
4. The displaced-blame game: offloads errors onto the nearest available witness.
5. All three share a common structure: the antithesis requires refusing the role the game assigned, which costs something specific.
6. At biographical scale, the antithesis is not a conversation — it is a multi-year project requiring a new stroke supply and a position update.
