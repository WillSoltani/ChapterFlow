Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader can draw the two arrows of any single conversation in their head, see whether the arrows are parallel or crossed, and use that picture to decide what to do next.

Opening Move:
Open inside the Tomas/Aiya stand-up. The crossed transaction happens in the second exchange. The reader feels the break before they have a name for it.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 (Tomas/Aiya cross): P1, P2, returned in P5.
- anchor 2 (Reyes/Diego complementary-and-empty): P3.
- anchor 3 (Olivia/Kenji): P4, returned in P6.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Tomas opens a stand-up by asking Aiya a clean Adult-to-Adult question about whether the API change is going to land Friday. Aiya answers from Parent. Show, in two lines, the moment the meeting changes shape.
- P2: name what just happened. Introduce the idea of a transaction as a unit, and the idea that any transaction has a sender state and a receiver state and a return vector. The new reader still does not have the word "complementary" or "crossed." That is fine. The picture comes first.
- P3: cut to Coach Reyes and Diego. Show a clean complementary transaction: Nurturing Parent to Adapted Child, returned in kind. Show that the conversation could run for years on this vector and never break. Then say the harder thing: "stable" is not "good." This is the paragraph that prevents the reader from collapsing the chapter into "complementary good, crossed bad."
- P4: introduce the names. Complementary: response from the addressed state, returned to the sender state. Crossed: response from a different state, or aimed somewhere else. Use Olivia and Kenji as the worked example for crossed. Olivia asks Child-to-Parent for comfort; Kenji answers from Adult. Both are technically right, and the call ends with a small wound nobody will mention next week.
- P5: return to Tomas and Aiya. Now that the reader has the language, draw the cross. Tomas's Adult to Aiya's Adult; Aiya's Parent back to Tomas's Child. Show what the second move did to the rest of the meeting.
- P6: the limit. Drawing the cross does not tell you what to do about it. Sometimes the move is to recross deliberately, send Adult back into Aiya's Parent and refuse the Child role. Sometimes the move is to accept the cross, name it, and ask which conversation she wants to have. Sometimes there is nothing to be done in the moment and the recognition is for later. The chapter must say all three.
- P7: bridge to Ch3. Two arrows describe what is visible in the room. The next chapter introduces what is happening underneath, and what changes when the underneath is the message that actually counts.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish "stable" (the technical fact about complementary transactions) from "good" (a value judgment that does not belong in the diagram). The same distinction prevents the reader from treating "crossed" as "wrong."

Hard-Depth Tension:
Drawing the diagram tells you where the bones are misaligned. It does not tell you which of three responses (recross, accept-and-name, wait) is the right one. That decision belongs to the person, not the diagram.

Threshold Question:
When you see a cross, how do you know whether to cross back, accept it, or step out?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: a transaction is a vector — sender state, receiver state, return vector — and the smallest unit the model operates on.
- Takeaway 2: complementary means the response comes from the state that was addressed and is aimed back at the sender state. The conversation is stable on that vector.
- Takeaway 3: stable is not good. A long-running complementary loop on the wrong vector can run for years and serve no one.
- Takeaway 4: crossed means the return vector lands somewhere unexpected. The conversation breaks in the second exchange, almost always before either party can name what happened.
- Takeaway 5: drawing the cross gives you a diagnosis, not a prescription.
- Takeaway 6: the three available moves on a cross are recross deliberately, accept and name it, or wait and recognize later. Each is right in a different setting.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must hold a question, not summarize. The question is: if every transaction has two visible arrows, what happens when there is also a hidden third one?

Genericity Risks:
- risk 1: turning the chapter into a "good communication" lecture. The chapter is not about communication. It is about a notation.
- risk 2: implying that crossed transactions are caused by people being thoughtless or selfish.
- risk 3: any drift into "active listening" vocabulary — that is a different framework.

Forbidden Drift:
- introducing ulterior transactions in any depth (Ch3 territory).
- introducing games, payoffs, or strokes (Ch3 / Ch4).
- using the names from Ch1 (continuity state has them locked).
- any implication that the diagram fixes the conversation.
