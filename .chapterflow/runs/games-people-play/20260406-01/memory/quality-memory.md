# Quality memory — condensed

## Auto-fail conditions (any one = reject)
- Invented facts, quotes, studies, mechanisms.
- Generic enough to fit another chapter.
- Paragraph-job repetition.
- Hard depth = medium lengthened.
- Pseudo-science / filler neuroscience / fake precision.
- Moral complexity framed as endorsement.
- First sentence is thesis-first instead of curiosity-first.
- Contamination leakage from brief/outline/seed.
- Tone collapse in required learning surfaces (identical variants).

## Required craft checks before scoring
- First sentence creates curiosity.
- ≥2 anchors from brief used load-bearingly.
- Real tension / limit / failure mode present.
- Readable, concrete, not inflated.
- Ending opens a next question (not flat summary).
- ≥1 memorable line every 200–300 words.

## 12-point rubric (6 categories × 0/1/2)
1. Chapter Specificity — unmistakably this chapter.
2. Anchor Use — anchors carry thinking, not decorative.
3. Analytical Value — real mechanism / distinction / tension.
4. Paragraph Motion — every paragraph earns place.
5. Prose Quality — sharp, vivid, authored.
6. Hook and Bridge — opening pulls, ending opens next question.

Pass threshold: ≥10/12 AND no auto-fail.

## Chapter structure rules (converter contract)
- Easy: chapterBreakdown tone object, 140–175 words/tone, exactly 3 takeaways (point only, no moreDetails), no prompts except flat oneMinuteRecap.
- Medium: chapterBreakdown tone object, 330–420 words/tone, 5–6 takeaways (point + moreDetails), activationPrompt, singular selfCheckPrompt (tone object), oneMinuteRecap {retrieve, connect, preview}.
- Hard: chapterBreakdown tone object, 490–600 words/tone, 5–7 takeaways (point + moreDetails), activationPrompt, exactly 2 selfCheckPrompts (tone objects), predictionPrompt, oneMinuteRecap {retrieve, connect, preview}.
- 6 examples — all 6 canonical formats exactly once, 2 work / 2 school / 2 personal, 6 ending types each used once.
- 5 review cards, 2 easy / 2 medium / 1 hard.
- Scenario, whatToDo, whyItMatters = tone objects always. Tone collapse = hard fail.
- moreDetails extend not restate; no fictional names inside moreDetails; no overlap with examples.

## Scenario tone policy
- gentle = lower resistance, warmer framing, softer entry
- direct = clean mechanics and decision line
- competitive = sharper stakes and consequence
Hard fail: plain string, identical variants, adjective-swap collapse.

## Quiz rules
- Exactly 10 Qs, exactly 3 choices, correctIndex ∈ {0,1,2}, explanation = tone object.
- q01–q03 simple (remember/understand); q04–q08 standard (apply/analyze); q09–q10 deeper (evaluate/create).
- q04–q06 include named-character scenarios.
- q09/q10 connect across chapters where supported.
- Explanations: direct explanations must each begin differently; no "The strongest answer...", "The best answer...", "The correct response..."; no two direct explanations share 4+ opening words.
- Explain why correct wins AND why wrong tempts.
- No invented facts; no chapter titles in quotes; avoid canned phrasings ("best applies", "best reflects").
- All 10 prompts vary opening shape; no two Qs test same principle.
- Roughly balanced correctIndex distribution.

## Contamination & source discipline
- Source-of-truth order: brief → outline → edited draft → rules → blueprint → mode rules.
- Evidence anchors must tie to brief's required anchors, not reader invention.
- No bulk generators. No "synthesize the rest". No one-pass synthesis.
