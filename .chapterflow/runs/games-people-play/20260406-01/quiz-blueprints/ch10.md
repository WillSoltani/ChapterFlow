## Question plans

**q01** (remember, correctIndex 2): What does Berne mean by "autonomy" in the closing section of his book?
- A: The ability to make independent financial decisions
- B: The absence of any emotional response to other people
- C: The capacity to live without games, constituted by awareness, spontaneity, and intimacy [CORRECT]

**q02** (understand, correctIndex 0): In Berne's use, awareness is best understood as...
- A: Perceiving what is actually present rather than through Parent programming or Child reaction filters [CORRECT]
- B: Mindful meditation practiced regularly each morning
- C: An intellectual understanding of how games work

**q03** (understand, correctIndex 1): What distinguishes Berne's "spontaneity" from common uses of the word?
- A: Spontaneity means acting before thinking in all situations
- B: Spontaneity means responding from whichever ego state is actually appropriate to the situation, rather than the one your history keeps reaching for [CORRECT]
- C: Spontaneity means avoiding all forms of planning or routine

**q04** (apply, named: Solenne, correctIndex 2): In the chapter, Solenne notices the pre-formed defensive explanation in her throat before she decides to speak. What quality is this a small instance of?
- A: Spontaneity, because she is choosing not to speak
- B: Intimacy, because she is being honest with herself in a group setting
- C: Awareness, because she is perceiving the sentence as something assembled rather than something she is simply saying [CORRECT]

**q05** (analyze, named: Casimir, correctIndex 0): When Casimir asks Valeria "tell me what starting looks like when it goes wrong" instead of explaining the concept again, what makes this an instance of spontaneity rather than just good teaching?
- A: The question comes from a state the situation called for rather than from his default reach, surprising him as much as her [CORRECT]
- B: He is deliberately practicing spontaneity as a teaching technique
- C: He has learned from Berne that explanations do not work with frustrated students

**q06** (apply, named: Takeshi + Fenna, correctIndex 1): The chapter describes a Saturday conversation between Takeshi and Fenna as an example of intimacy in Berne's sense. What specifically makes it intimate rather than just pleasant?
- A: They discuss difficult personal topics they have never talked about before
- B: Neither is running a pattern on the other; both are present with what the other is actually saying [CORRECT]
- C: They have been friends for eleven years, long enough for complete trust

**q07** (analyze, no named char, correctIndex 0): The chapter says awareness can collapse back into observation-as-practice. Why is this a failure mode rather than an improvement?
- A: Because observation-as-practice becomes its own script, and the person is performing directness rather than perceiving it [CORRECT]
- B: Because stopping to observe interrupts the flow of genuine experience
- C: Because observation-as-practice leads to over-analyzing other people's behavior

**q08** (analyze, no named char, correctIndex 2): The chapter calls intimacy Berne's rarest quality. What makes it rarer than awareness or spontaneity?
- A: It requires formal training in communication and conflict resolution
- B: It requires both people to have read about Berne's model first
- C: It requires two people to be simultaneously free of their patterns, rather than one person making a solo move [CORRECT]

**q09** (evaluate, no named char, correctIndex 1): A reader argues that naming the autonomy triad is pointless because reaching it is so difficult that most people never will. How would the chapter respond?
- A: By encouraging the reader to try harder and commit to the practices
- B: By pointing out that naming a direction is not the same as promising arrival; the catalog chapters have been useful to people who will never fully leave the games in them, and the triad is useful in the same way [CORRECT]
- C: By admitting the reader has a point and that the triad is aspirational rather than achievable

**q10** (create, no named char, correctIndex 2): If you wanted to design a single weekly practice that built toward awareness rather than observation-as-practice, which approach would Berne's framing most support?
- A: Journaling every evening about the patterns you noticed during the day
- B: Reading the previous chapters repeatedly until the pattern vocabulary is automatic
- C: Noticing, in one specific recurring moment, whether your response arrived before you chose it, and doing nothing else with that noticing [CORRECT]
