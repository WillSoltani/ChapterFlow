## Core Claim

Berne named three qualities that together constitute autonomy — the capacity to live without games. They are awareness (perceiving directly in the present rather than through Parent programming or Child reaction), spontaneity (choosing and expressing from the full range of ego states rather than from pre-programmed response), and intimacy (game-free directness in relation to another person). None of these is a technique. They are what remains when the games slowly fall away. The chapter is honest that this is rare and that it requires giving up the strokes that games provide, which is much harder than it sounds.

---

## Author Logic Chain

1. Ch4 left open a debt: leaving games requires replacement strokes and life position updates. Ch10 pays that debt by naming what the replacement actually is.
2. Ch9 established the see-and-leave-alone discipline for good games. Ch10 extends that into positive territory: what are you building toward when the destructive games are slowly left?
3. The answer is the autonomy triad. But the chapter refuses the self-help read. Each quality has a failure mode. The chapter names them.
4. The triad is the book's final argument and the chapter's ending closes the ten-chapter arc rather than opening a new question.

## Required Anchors (3 total, one per quality)

### Anchor 1 — Awareness — Solenne
Solenne is in a meeting where something has gone wrong that is partly her fault. The defensive explanation is already assembled in her throat before she has decided to speak: the same words, the same framing she has used perhaps forty times over the last decade. This time she notices, for three seconds, that the words are already there before she has chosen them. She watches the sentence. That pause, that noticing from a small distance, is awareness. It is not a practice. She has not been taught to do this. It simply happens, and the fact that she can see the sentence as something she assembled rather than something she is simply saying is what Berne means.

### Anchor 2 — Spontaneity — Casimir
Casimir runs the tutoring lab at a community college. When a student comes in frustrated, he explains the concept more carefully. It is his automatic reach. One afternoon a student named Valeria says she has read the explanation four times and understands it fine; what she cannot do is make herself sit down and start. Casimir, instead of explaining again, says: "Tell me what starting looks like when it goes wrong." The question surprises him as much as Valeria. It did not come from his usual reach; it came from somewhere that the situation called for rather than his history. That is spontaneity.

### Anchor 3 — Intimacy — Takeshi and Fenna
Takeshi and Fenna have been friends for eleven years. They meet for coffee the first Saturday of every month. Most of those Saturdays follow a familiar shape: work, what each is carrying, where things are hard. One Saturday in March the conversation is different and both of them can feel the difference before they can name it. Neither has a crisis. Neither is performing understanding. Both are present with what the other is actually saying rather than with what they have already decided to say back. It is short. It is also the clearest example of what Berne meant by intimacy: game-free contact between two people who are, in that hour, running nothing on each other.

## Moral Complexity

Low flags for this chapter. The main risk is drifting into self-help optimism. The chapter must hold genuine tension: the triad is available but rare, and naming it is not a promise it will be frequent. Each failure mode must be named with the same structural honesty the catalog chapters used for destructive games.

## Concept Budget
3 vocabulary items (awareness, spontaneity, intimacy) plus one structural claim (together they constitute autonomy) plus one honest limit (the triad is available, not installable). No new game catalog entries. No new structural concepts beyond the triad.

## Hard-Depth Minimum
Hard depth must name:
- The failure mode of each quality (awareness → observation-as-practice; spontaneity → compulsive reaction; intimacy → structural imitation of presence)
- Why the triad is the answer to Ch4's debt specifically (replacement strokes vs. replacement states vs. game-free contact)
- The threshold question: if the triad is rare even for people who have done the work, is naming it still worth doing? The chapter answers yes and explains why.
