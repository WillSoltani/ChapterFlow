Chapter Title: Professional and Therapy-Room Games
Position: Fourth catalog chapter. Games that run inside professional helping relationships, where the helper's role itself provides the cover.
Source Sidecar: .chapterflow/runs/games-people-play/20260406-01/sidecars/source/ch08.source.txt

Core Claim:
The most durable games in the catalog are the ones that hide inside the structure of a professional helping relationship. Berne observed these in psychiatric consultation rooms, but the structures show up anywhere one person's job is to help another. Three patterns covered here. I'm Only Trying To Help You: the helper offers help that the recipient predictably refuses or fails to use, and when the help fails, the helper has proof that the recipient is hopeless and the helper is blameless. Wooden Leg: the player cites a real or claimed limitation as a permanent exemption from normal accountability, and the helper accepts the exemption because the limitation is real. Greenhouse: the patient (or client or student) becomes an enthusiastic amateur of the helping vocabulary and uses theory talk to substitute for actual change. All three run because the helper's professional role is the cover that makes the game invisible.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the only chapter where the helper is structurally part of the game by profession. The helper is not a naive bystander who accidentally got caught in someone else's pattern; the helper has a role that requires them to offer help, and the game uses that role. The chapter must say this honestly: helping professionals are not innocent of these games, and the games cannot be diagnosed by looking only at the person being helped.

Required Anchors:
- Ife, a clinical supervisor at a community mental health center, who has a supervisee named Rémy who has been in weekly supervision for three years. Rémy presents the same cluster of difficult cases every week. Ife offers perspective, theoretical framing, suggestions. Rémy thanks her and returns the next week with a new case that is structurally identical. Ife has started to notice that she has been 'helping' for three years and nothing has changed, and that Rémy is, by most external measures, an effective clinician. The help is not the point. The weekly hour is.
- Bo, a student success coach at a community college, and a student named Caleb who has been in his office every Monday for two years citing a real and documented learning disability as the reason he has not made progress on his degree. The disability is real. Caleb is also not doing the things that would work around it, and has not tried the accommodations available. Bo has been accepting the explanation because the disability is real, which is exactly what the game requires.
- Tamsin, a therapist working with a client named Zara who has read extensively about attachment theory and can describe her own patterns in sophisticated vocabulary. Every session, Zara articulates a new self-observation with considerable insight. Nothing in Zara's life has changed in the two years they have been working together. Zara loves the sessions. Tamsin is starting to notice that Zara's insights function as a substitute for experience, not as a route toward it.

Moral complexity: HIGH. These are games played against backdrops of real needs, real disabilities, real emotional difficulty. The chapter must NOT moralize the clients or suggest they are being manipulative. And it must NOT absolve the professionals; the games run because both parties are getting something. Berne's original Indigence game (welfare caseworker + client) is NOT used — the 1964 framing is too dated. Use contemporary helping-profession contexts instead.

Concept budget: 3 (I'm Only Trying To Help You, Wooden Leg, Greenhouse). Berne's Psychiatry and Stupid games are mentioned briefly in hard depth as related structures.

Hard-depth minimum: The helper's role in each game is the hardest thing the chapter has to hold. ITHY requires a helper who is invested in the help being needed. Wooden Leg requires a helper who does not challenge the limitation. Greenhouse requires a helper who reinforces the theory talk. In each case, the helper is not accidental; the game cannot run without them. The threshold: what does the helper have to give up to break the game, and is that loss acceptable to them?

Forbidden: Ch10 vocabulary; catalog games from Ch5-7; Ch1-7 character names; neurochemistry; DSM language for anything except accurate mention of real conditions in the anchors.
