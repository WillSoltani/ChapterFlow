Core Claim:
Marital games are not caused by bad people or bad partnerships. They are intimacy-avoidance strategies running inside the frame of closeness. Corner, Courtroom, If It Weren't For You, and Sweetheart each produce reliable strokes without requiring the players to be genuinely vulnerable with each other. The chapter's central distinction: genuine intimacy is game-free. Every marital game is, at its psychological level, a structured way to have the form of closeness without the risk of the real thing.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the only chapter that holds a specific structural claim about what the games are avoiding (intimacy). No other catalog chapter makes that claim. Every example here has to carry that distinction: the game is the substitute, and the intimacy is what would replace it.

Required Anchors:
- Corner: Parveen and her partner Dev. Parveen engineers situations where any choice Dev makes proves the accusation against him. He agrees to something she suggested; she says he only did it to humor her. He declines; she says he never tries. There is no winning move. Payoff: confirmation that Dev is hopeless, avoidance of the conversation about what Parveen actually wants.
- If It Weren't For You (IWFY): Luca, who has spent seventeen years explaining that he would have been an architect if his wife Nadia had not needed him to take the practical job. The game's structural insight (Berne's most important in the chapter): Luca chose Nadia partly BECAUSE she provided a reason not to try. The "restriction" is not imposed from outside; it was invited in. Payoff: protection from the fear of trying and failing.
- Sweetheart: Vera, who tells warm stories about her partner Bastian at dinner parties in which the punchline is always a veiled insult that Bastian cannot object to without looking humorless. Payoff: aggression with plausible deniability, plus stored grievance released in a socially acceptable form.

Moral Complexity: HIGH. Berne's 1964 originals are gendered in ways that cannot be ported directly. ALL anchors use neutral, non-gendered framings. The structural patterns are real; the 1964 case studies are not imported.

Concept Budget: 4 (marital games as intimacy-avoidance, the three pattern structures, the intimacy-as-replacement claim).

Hard-Depth Minimum:
The hard-depth question: if both players are avoiding intimacy through the game, what would it take for one of them to stop? The antithesis for Corner, IWFY, and Sweetheart each require the stopping player to expose something real — which is exactly what the game was protecting against. The threshold question: is the game's protection worth what it costs, and who decides?
