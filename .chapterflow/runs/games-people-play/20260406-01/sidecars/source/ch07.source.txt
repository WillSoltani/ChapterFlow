--- Slice A: Part II, Party Games (source lines 314-345) ---
Ain't It Awful (AIA): participants gather to deplore the state of the world. Content is real complaints; function is bonding through shared grievance. Payoff: social strokes via agreement; avoidance of intimacy or action. Antithesis: reframe toward curiosity or action ('what would you actually do about it?').

Blemish: player scans for a flaw, derives satisfaction from finding it first. Payoff: superiority; protection from feeling inadequate by finding others inadequate first. Antithesis: acknowledge flaw calmly and move on.

Why Don't You Yes But (WDYYB): player presents a problem, invites suggestions, rejects each with 'yes but'. Goal: not a solution but to demonstrate that no one can solve the problem. Payoff: confirmation of insolubility; strokes from others trying. Antithesis: refuse to offer solutions; ask what the player has already tried.

--- Slice B: scope ---
Ch7 covers short-duration social group patterns. 'Short-duration' distinguishes them from life and marital games. The chapter's honest claim: not all complaining is AIA and not all critique is Blemish. The dynamics (moving toward something vs circling) distinguish the game from the legitimate activity.
