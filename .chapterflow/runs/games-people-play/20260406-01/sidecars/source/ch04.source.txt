--- Slice A: source Part I, Chapter 3 (lines 102-124), strokes and structuring ---
Berne distinguishes ways people structure time with others. The relevant fact for this chapter is the concept of stroking: people need a certain amount of acknowledgment from other people to stay psychologically healthy. When they cannot get it through intimacy, they get it through pastimes and games. This is why games are so persistent — they are reliable stroke-delivery systems, even when the strokes are negative.

Negative strokes (criticism, fights, angry messages) are not equivalent to positive strokes, but they are metabolically better than no strokes. Stroke deprivation degrades human functioning. The persistence of games at the population level is largely a fact about stroke supply, not character.

--- Slice B: source Part I, Chapter 5 (lines 146-186), payoffs and life positions ---
Berne lists six payoff types in the original (internal psychological, external psychological, internal social, external social, biological, existential). Reader-facing usage compresses these to functional categories: strokes, position confirmation, structure, avoidance.

Life positions: the four fundamental stances a person takes toward themselves and others.
  - I'm OK, you're OK: healthy, intimate, game-free.
  - I'm OK, you're not OK: projective, blaming, often persecutory.
  - I'm not OK, you're OK: depressive, self-critical, often martyr-like.
  - I'm not OK, you're not OK: futile, often the position behind serious self-destruction.

Games are played to confirm the player's existing life position, over and over. The reason therapy that focuses only on game-stopping fails is that the position remains, and the player will find a new game to confirm it.

--- Slice E: Hard-depth tension ---
Order of operations matters. A reader who tries to leave a painful game without first finding a different stroke supply will not become free; they will become deprived in a different shape and will return to the game within weeks. The chapter must hold this honestly. The replacement plan is the actual work, not the leaving.
