--- Slice A: Part I, Chapter 2 of source document (lines 74–100) ---
A "transaction" is a single unit of social exchange: one person says or does something, and the other person responds. Berne's contribution was to classify transactions by which ego state sent the message and which ego state it was aimed at.

Three transaction types:
  A. COMPLEMENTARY — The response comes from the ego state that was addressed, and is aimed back at the ego state that sent the original message. These transactions are stable: the conversation can continue indefinitely because expectations are met.
     Examples given: Adult-to-Adult request for information, Adult response. Parent-to-Child instruction, Child compliance. Child-to-Parent appeal for comfort, Parent soothes.
  B. CROSSED — The response comes from an unexpected ego state, or is aimed at a different ego state than the sender. These transactions break the conversation. One person expects an Adult answer and gets a Parent scolding; or one person addresses the other's Adult and the other responds from Child. Crossed transactions are the moment conversations "go sideways."
  C. ULTERIOR — The transaction has two levels: a social level (what is visibly said) and a psychological level (what is really going on). The words are one thing, but the actual message and response happen underneath. Berne argues that games are always built on ulterior transactions.

Teaching implication: Most communication failures are crossed transactions, and most manipulation is ulterior. Learning to spot both in real time is a practical skill.
