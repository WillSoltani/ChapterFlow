--- Slice A: Part I, Chapter 1 of source document (lines 39–72) ---
Berne's core claim: at any moment, a person operates from one of three distinct ego states. These are observable — they are ways of talking, thinking, and behaving, not abstractions about the psyche.

The three states:
  1. Parent. Patterns learned from authority figures in childhood. Speaks in rules, judgments, shoulds, and shouldn'ts. Carries the voice of what was modeled by caregivers. Has two faces: Critical (restrictive, fault-finding) and Nurturing (supportive, caretaking). A line like "that's just how it's done" is usually the Parent speaking.
  2. Adult. The reality-processing state. Observes, gathers data, evaluates probabilities, makes decisions based on current information. Neutral in tone. Present-focused. The only state capable of updating beliefs on new evidence.
  3. Child. Patterns from one's own childhood, still live inside the adult person. Contains original emotions, creativity, curiosity, and old adaptations to authority. Variants: Natural / Free Child (spontaneous, expressive), Adapted Child (compliant or rebellious in response to Parent figures), Little Professor (intuitive, clever, manipulative in a childlike way).

Key insight: everyone has all three states, and healthy functioning means being able to move between them appropriately. Problems arise when one state contaminates another (Parent prejudices presenting as Adult "facts") or when one is excluded (someone who cannot access their Child for play or emotion).

Teaching implication: the model gives the reader a vocabulary to notice which part of themselves is speaking, and to consciously shift — though "consciously shift" should be softened in our reader-facing prose because the real-world experience is mostly unchosen observation. It is less a diagnostic system and more a live observational tool.

--- Slice B: criticism-and-limits.md (global guidance) ---
The 1964 framings in the original book are gendered and dated. Examples for ChapterFlow must be drawn from neutral modern settings. Do not reproduce Berne's specific case studies. The observational content of the model — three states, their markers, their shifts — is not what needs updating. The examples are.

Chapter 1: The Three Voices Inside You (Ego States). Teach Parent / Adult / Child as three live behavioral states an adult moves between. Focus on the observational skill — recognizing a state shift in real time. Derive modern examples, not gendered 1964 ones. Set up the vocabulary the rest of the book will use; do not introduce transactions, games, strokes, or payoffs here (those belong to Ch2, Ch3, Ch4).

--- Slice D: key insight on "unchosen shifts" (structural, from source Part I chapter 1) ---
The states are something you move through, not something you are. A person slipping from Adult into Parent is not making a decision; the shift happens without their permission. The point of the model is to give the person a way to notice, after the fact, where they were standing.
