--- Slice A: Part II, Life Games (source lines 200-244) ---
Berne's life games category: Alcoholic, Debtor, Kick Me, NIGYSOB, SWYMD. 

Kick Me: player behaves in ways that invite rejection/punishment/criticism, then reacts with hurt astonishment when the predictable response arrives. Payoff: confirmation of I'm not OK; strokes from pity. Antithesis: do not kick; respond with neutral Adult courtesy.

NIGYSOB (Now I've Got You SOB): player looks for justified reasons to be outraged, uses the moment of catching someone to release stored anger. Payoff: guilt-free rage; moral superiority. Antithesis: impeccable procedural correctness that gives no opening.

SWYMD (See What You Made Me Do): player's mistake is blamed on the person who interrupted/distracted. Real mistake could have happened alone; the presence of a witness lets the player offload responsibility. Payoff: avoidance of responsibility; others learn to keep distance. Antithesis: refuse to accept the blame.

Debtor: player takes on obligations they cannot realistically meet, structures life around the drama of near-miss survival. Payoff: sense of drama; proof world is against them. Antithesis: refuse to lend; clean limits that remove the drama.

Note: the original "Alcoholic" game is explicitly NOT in this chapter's scope. It requires a more careful modern framing that would distort this catalog chapter. Handle it only in the Appendix treatment if needed.

--- Slice B: moral complexity note ---
From criticism-and-limits.md: some of Berne's life game examples (especially Kick Me and Alcoholic) carry risk of pathologizing people who may be experiencing genuine harm, structural victimization, or trauma. The chapter must distinguish between the self-perpetuating pattern (the game layer) and structural causes of repeated harm (not the game layer). The model is for reading patterns, not for blaming players.
