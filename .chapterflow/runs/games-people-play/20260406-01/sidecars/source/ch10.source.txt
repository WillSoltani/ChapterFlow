SLICE 1 — The Autonomy Triad (lines 559–572)
Source section: PART III — BEYOND GAMES > Autonomy

Berne's closing section names three qualities that together constitute autonomy:
1. AWARENESS — the capacity to perceive the world directly, in the present, rather than through the filters of Parent programming or Child reaction.
2. SPONTANEITY — the freedom to choose and express feelings from the full range of one's ego states, rather than being locked into pre-programmed responses.
3. INTIMACY — game-free directness in relation to another person.

These three qualities are not a technique. They are what remains when the games fall away. Berne's closing position is that autonomy is available, but it requires giving up the strokes that games provide, and this is much harder than it sounds.

SLICE 2 — The Context (lines 535–558)
Source section: PART III — BEYOND GAMES > The Significance of Games / The Players / A Good Life

Games are how most people spend most of their social time. They are learned early, handed down across generations, and deeply sticky because they deliver strokes on a reliable schedule.

Berne is not contemptuous of people who play games. He notes that games are a creative adaptation to the problem of needing strokes in a world that does not always offer them freely. The problem is not that people play games; the problem is that they do not have access to anything better.

Berne proposes that the goal is not to win games but to become able to stop playing them — not because games are morally wrong, but because there is something better available to a person who does not need them. That something is intimacy: direct, ulterior-free contact between people.

SLICE 3 — Hard-Depth Tension
Source derived from Berne's framing (teaching notes, chapter-map.md):

Autonomy is easier to describe than to reach. The chapter has to be honest about the failure modes of each quality:
- Awareness can collapse back into observation-as-practice, which is itself a kind of pattern.
- Spontaneity can collapse into compulsive reaction, which is also a pattern.
- Intimacy is Berne's rarest quality; it requires two people to be simultaneously free of their patterns, which is why most conversations that feel intimate are structurally more organized than they appear.

The threshold question: if the triad is rare even for people who have done the work, is naming it still worth doing? Answer: naming a direction is not the same as promising arrival. The catalog chapters have been useful to readers who will never fully leave the games in them. The triad is useful in the same way.
