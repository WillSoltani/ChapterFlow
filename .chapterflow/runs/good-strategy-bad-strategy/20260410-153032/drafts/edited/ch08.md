Once force has been concentrated, the next problem is direction. Chapter 8 asks how a strategist should keep action moving when the whole path ahead is still uncertain. Its answer is proximate objectives: objectives that are near enough and concrete enough to guide action without pretending the future has become fully visible.

The chapter matters because leverage by itself does not tell a team what to do next. A leader can identify the pivot point and still drift if the next objective is vague, remote, or inflated beyond what the situation can justify. Proximate objectives turn concentrated force into guided movement by naming the next credible target.

This is why the chapter rejects two opposite errors. Vague aspiration does not guide action because it gives force no usable destination. False precision is also weak because it claims a full map that the situation has not earned. Proximate objectives sit between those mistakes. They are specific enough to move on and modest enough to survive uncertainty.

The logic of the chapter makes uncertainty manageable rather than paralyzing. A strategist does not need total foresight before acting. The strategist needs enough clarity about the next stretch to move intelligently. A proximate objective gives action a direction that is concrete without pretending to settle every later move in advance.

The chapter also distinguishes a proximate objective from a mere task list. It still serves strategy. Its job is to keep force aimed at the obstacle by defining the next objective that actually changes the contest. When that objective is well chosen, action stays coherent even though the longer route is still unfolding.

This makes Chapter 8 the natural partner to Chapter 7. Leverage explained that force must be concentrated where it matters most. Proximate objectives explain what near-term target should now receive that concentrated force so the strategy can advance without dissolving into wishful language or frozen overplanning.

The practical lesson is to choose the next objective that is close enough to be credible and strong enough to focus action. Once that discipline is visible, later chapters can ask how design and coordinated moves carry strategy beyond the next step without losing coherence.
