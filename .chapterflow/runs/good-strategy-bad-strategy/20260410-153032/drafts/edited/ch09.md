Once a strategy has a credible next objective, another question takes over. Will the system actually move if improvement advances broadly, or is one weak necessary link still capping the whole result? Chapter 9 answers by turning to chain-link systems, where performance is limited by the weakest indispensable link.

The chapter matters because some systems do not reward average strength. A team can improve many parts, praise its strongest areas, and still fail if one necessary step keeps breaking. In that kind of system, performance is not set by the average quality of the links. It is set by the weakest one the whole chain depends on.

This is why the chapter pushes against compensation thinking. Strong links elsewhere do not cancel failure at an indispensable weak link. A system can have excellence in many places and still underperform because one required connection keeps capping what the whole can do.

The chapter also distinguishes chain-link systems from systems where strengths can add up more loosely. In a chain-link system, one broken required part can dominate the result. The strategist therefore has to ask whether broad improvement is missing the real limit instead of solving it.

The practical implication is that repair has to focus on the limiting link. Effort spread across the whole system may feel balanced, but it can leave the true bottleneck untouched. If one indispensable link is capping performance, the right move is often to strengthen that link first rather than polish strengths that are already sufficient.

The chapter also warns against the comfort of local success. Teams often prefer visible wins in stronger areas because those wins are easier to claim and more pleasant to report. But if the weak indispensable link remains weak, the whole system can continue to fail despite those local gains.

The practical lesson is to identify whether the system behaves like a chain and, if it does, find the limiting link that the whole result depends on. Once that logic is clear, later chapters can move deeper into design, constraints, and the structures strategy has to respect.
