Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader will understand that good strategy has a kernel made of diagnosis, guiding policy, and coherent action.

Opening Move:
Open by contrasting a full-looking plan with a compact kernel that actually explains how strategy works.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 diagnosis
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 guiding policy
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 coherent action and the full kernel

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: introduce the kernel as the minimum structure of real strategy
- P2: explain diagnosis as naming the governing obstacle
- P3: explain guiding policy as the directional response to that obstacle
- P4: distinguish guiding policy from slogan or aspiration
- P5: explain coherent action as coordinated reinforcing moves
- P6: show why missing any one part collapses the kernel
- P7: bridge to the next chapter on sources of power

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The kernel works only when diagnosis, guiding policy, and coherent action reinforce one another.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard must show that the parts are interdependent and that partial kernels degrade back into bad strategy.

Threshold Question:
When does a plan actually contain a kernel rather than only a target, a slogan, or an activity list?

Takeaway Count Lock:

- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

- Takeaway 1: the kernel is the compact core of strategy
- Takeaway 2: diagnosis determines what the policy must face
- Takeaway 3: guiding policy is directional, not decorative
- Takeaway 4: coherent action turns policy into reinforcing moves
- Takeaway 5: missing any one element collapses the whole structure
