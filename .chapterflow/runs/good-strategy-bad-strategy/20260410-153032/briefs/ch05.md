Core Claim:
Good strategy has a kernel: diagnosis, guiding policy, and coherent action. On the support available in this run, the chapter should treat that kernel as the positive answer to the earlier failures of decorative ambition, ambiguity, and initiative piles. The chapter's practical lesson is that strategy becomes real when a leader names the governing obstacle, chooses a guiding approach, and aligns actions that reinforce that approach.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is distinct because it supplies the book's central positive structure. Earlier chapters explained what strategy is not, where power can sit, and why bad strategy persists. Chapter 5 defines the compact architecture that makes strategy real.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author presents the kernel as the minimum structure of real strategy.
Then he defines diagnosis as the act of naming the key challenge or obstacle.
Then he defines guiding policy as the overall approach for dealing with that challenge.
Then he defines coherent action as the coordinated moves that carry the policy into effect.
