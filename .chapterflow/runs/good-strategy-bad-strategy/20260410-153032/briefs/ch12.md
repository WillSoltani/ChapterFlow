Core Claim:
After strategy has concentrated force, the next task is to judge growth more carefully. On the support available in this run, Chapter 12 should present growth as a strategic outcome with costs, constraints, and selective logic rather than as an automatic virtue or default goal.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is distinct because it shifts from the discipline of concentration to the discipline of judging expansion. Chapter 11 argued that strategy gains force when effort is concentrated. Chapter 12 asks when growth preserves that force, when it dilutes it, and why expansion should be chosen selectively rather than worshiped automatically.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from concentrated force to the question of whether expansion supports or weakens that force.
Then he frames growth as a strategic outcome rather than an automatic virtue.
Then he highlights costs, constraints, and tradeoffs inside growth.
Then he contrasts selective growth logic with automatic expansion logic.
Then he prepares later dynamics and inertia chapters by showing that movement and scale can create both strength and burden.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Growth: strategic expansion judged by whether it strengthens the whole rather than by size alone.
Expansion cost: the tradeoff or burden growth introduces into a strategy.
Selective growth logic: the discipline of choosing growth only where it fits the strategy's force and constraints.

Specific Applications:
- testing whether a growth move preserves concentrated force or diffuses it
- judging expansion by fit, cost, and strategic consequence rather than prestige alone
- deciding what kinds of growth should be refused even if they look attractive

Common Misreadings:
- treating growth as automatically virtuous regardless of strategic fit
- assuming larger scale always means stronger strategy
- confusing motion or expansion with strategic success
