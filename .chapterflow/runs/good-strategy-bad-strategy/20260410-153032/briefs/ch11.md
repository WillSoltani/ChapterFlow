Core Claim:
After a strategist has designed a coherent whole, the next task is deciding where effort should be concentrated. On the support available in this run, Chapter 11 should present focus as disciplined concentration of effort that beats diffusion when constraints and competition make spread too weak to matter.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is distinct because it shifts from coherence to concentration. Chapter 10 argued that strategy gains power from fit and mutual reinforcement. Chapter 11 asks where that designed whole will place emphasis so it can matter under real limits and competitive pressure.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from designing a coherent whole to concentrating force within that whole.
Then he frames focus as selective intensity rather than universal coverage.
Then he contrasts concentration with diffusion across too many priorities.
Then he shows that constraints and competition make spread costly.
Then he prepares later growth logic by showing that strength comes from selective commitment, not equal attention everywhere.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Focus: concentration of effort where it can create the strongest strategic effect.
Diffusion: spreading effort across too many priorities so none gains enough force.
Selective intensity: choosing the few places where additional effort matters most.

Specific Applications:
- deciding where a designed strategy should put its heaviest emphasis
- cutting or downweighting priorities that dilute force
- testing whether effort is concentrated enough to matter under competition

Common Misreadings:

- assuming every worthy priority deserves equal strategic weight
- confusing broad coverage with strength
