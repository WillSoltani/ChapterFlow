Core Claim:
After a strategist recognizes that some systems are constrained by indispensable weak links, the next task is not isolated repair alone but design. On the support available in this run, Chapter 10 should present design as the deliberate arrangement of choices so that parts reinforce one another instead of operating as disconnected moves.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is distinct because it shifts from identifying the limiting link to composing a whole. Chapter 9 argued that some systems are ruled by weakest-link logic. Chapter 10 asks how strategy should be built so its parts fit together and reinforce one another.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shifts from structural diagnosis to structural construction.
Then he frames design as arranging parts into a coherent whole.
Then he contrasts mutual reinforcement with isolated strong moves.
Then he shows that strategy design requires fit, exclusion, and disciplined choice.
Then he prepares Chapter 11 by making clear that a designed whole cannot support unlimited priorities at once.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
Design: the deliberate arrangement of choices so they reinforce one another as a whole.
Mutual reinforcement: the condition in which one choice strengthens the effect of another.
Structural fit: alignment among choices, actions, and constraints so the whole works together.

Specific Applications:
- testing whether choices support one another or merely coexist
- building strategy as a coherent whole instead of a list of attractive moves
- deciding what to exclude so the whole can reinforce itself

Common Misreadings:
- treating design as style or presentation rather than strategic structure
- assuming a pile of individually good moves automatically forms a strong whole
- confusing broad activity with reinforcing fit
