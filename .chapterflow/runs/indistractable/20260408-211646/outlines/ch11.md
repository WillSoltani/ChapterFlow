# Ch11 Outline — Ask the Critical Question

1. Open on the fact that external triggers often feel like commands because they arrive faster than reflection
2. Insert one explicit question between trigger and response
3. Define the test in traction terms: does this serve prior intent now or pull me away from it
4. Keep the filter quick and practical across work, school, and personal settings
5. Preserve the limit that real obligations still matter and the question is not an excuse for denial
6. Bridge to Chapter 12 by showing that work interruptions are easier to hack back once they are questioned instead of obeyed automatically

## Hard-depth threshold
- the question must be behaviorally sharp, not inspirational wallpaper
- the chapter must avoid turning trigger evaluation into overthinking
