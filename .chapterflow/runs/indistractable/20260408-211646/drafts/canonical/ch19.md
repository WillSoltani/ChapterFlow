# Canonical Draft — Ch19 "Hack Back Feeds"

Feeds become distracting because they are designed as sequences without a strong natural finish. One item slides into the next, refresh offers another batch, and novelty keeps making the next post feel easier to sample than the original purpose feels to remember. The person may think they are checking quickly, but the feed is built to keep checking from ending on its own.

That is why Eyal's correction has to stay specific to feeds. The issue is not just that the content is digital or social. The issue is that a feed is a moving chain of prompts with weak stopping cues. Hacking back feeds means refusing to treat that chain as a neutral place to linger. Enter with a reason, keep a boundary, and leave before novelty gets to redefine the session.

The chapter matters because feeds make drift feel harmless in tiny pieces. Each post is small. Each refresh feels optional. Each detour can be excused as only a minute. But the sequence does the real work. It keeps offering another small permission until prior intent dissolves.

The stronger move is to turn feeds into retrieval instead of roaming. Check for something specific. Reduce the number of places where feeds greet you by default. Avoid opening a feed just to see what is there. Give the session a stopping point before the feed has a chance to negotiate one for you.

The chapter also keeps an important limit. Useful ideas, updates, or people can appear in feeds. The point is not to deny that. The point is to stop letting the sequence architecture stay in charge of attention just because something worthwhile occasionally appears inside it.

That leads naturally into precommitments. Once a person sees how often endless sequence beats present intention, the next question is what to set up in advance so the future moment becomes harder to betray. Chapter 20 answers that question.
