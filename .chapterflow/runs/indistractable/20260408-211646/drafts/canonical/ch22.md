# Canonical Draft — Ch22 "Prevent Distraction with Identity Pacts"

Identity pacts matter because people act more steadily when follow-through feels tied to who they are trying to be. Eyal's point is not that a label magically changes behavior. It is that a chosen identity can make consistency feel less like a temporary stunt and more like coherence.

That is why the chapter has to stay concrete. An identity pact is not empty affirmation. It links self-concept to repeated action. The person does not merely say, "I am focused." They behave in ways that make that claim increasingly true. Identity gains credibility through proof.

The chapter matters because distraction often wins when the plan feels optional or episodic. But when the person understands a boundary, a routine, or a follow-through behavior as part of who they are trying to become, the choice can carry different weight. The plan is no longer only a task. It is also a piece of integrity.

The stronger claim is still modest. Identity does not replace the earlier work of triggers, traction, and other pacts. It supports that work. It gives repeated action a narrative spine. Without action, the identity talk becomes sentimental and weak.

That is why behavior-level anchors are essential. The reader should know what someone with this chosen identity does, refuses, schedules, or protects. Otherwise the chapter turns into slogans.

That leads naturally into workplace culture. Once identity is understood as a force that shapes behavior, the next question is what happens when a whole environment teaches people what kind of worker they are expected to be. Chapter 23 widens the lens from individual pacts to cultural norms.
