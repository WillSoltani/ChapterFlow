# Canonical Draft — Ch30 "Be an Indistractable Lover"

The book began by asking what pulls people away from what they said they wanted to do. The final chapter asks who bears that cost when the pattern keeps repeating at home. The answer is not only your calendar or your output. It is often the person closest to you.

That is why the chapter closes with love. A partner can be physically near and still keep receiving only fragments. The eyes drift. The phone lights up. Work spills across the evening. A hard feeling arrives, and attention quietly escapes instead of staying in the room.

The chapter matters because it brings the whole framework into intimacy. Internal triggers still matter because emotional discomfort can make distraction feel attractive. External triggers still matter because devices and interruptions keep inserting themselves into shared space. Time for traction still matters because closeness needs defended room. Pacts still matter because some boundaries hold better when they are spoken clearly in advance.

The chapter also keeps an important limit. A scheduled dinner, a date night, or a no-phone rule cannot create tenderness by itself. Structure can protect the conditions for intimacy, but presence, warmth, honesty, and repair still have to be brought into that protected space.

That is what makes this the real ending rather than a bonus essay. Attention is revealed as relational. To become indistractable is not only to manage yourself better. It is also to decide who will receive your defended presence while you still have it to give.
