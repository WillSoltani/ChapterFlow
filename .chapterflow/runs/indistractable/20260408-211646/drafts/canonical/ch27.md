# Canonical Draft — Ch27 "Help Them with External Triggers"

If Chapter 26 gives the family clearer traction to protect, Chapter 27 asks what in the environment is helping or hurting that traction. Eyal's external-trigger framework says cues in the environment and from other people shape behavior. That applies to children too. But the stronger move is not just adult control. It is helping children notice and shape those cues with support.

That is why the chapter has to stay developmental. Adults still scaffold. They still set limits. But children should gradually learn to recognize the cues that pull them away from what matters and to participate in changing those cues. The room, the device setup, the study space, the social prompt, and the family routine are all part of the environment.

The chapter matters because bans alone teach less than participation. A child who only experiences rules from above may obey temporarily without understanding how cues work. A child who helps shape the environment begins to learn what supports traction and what quietly undermines it.

The stronger claim is not that adults disappear. It is that the goal is movement from imposed control toward shared shaping. The external trigger is not only something the parent fights for the child. It becomes something the child can increasingly see and manage with guidance.

That keeps the chapter distinct from the adult trigger chapters. The developmental angle matters. The child is learning an environment skill, not just living inside a cleaned-up room someone else designed in secret.

That leads directly into the next chapter. Once children can see and shape cues around them, they are better prepared to make their own pacts instead of relying only on parental control.
