# Edited Draft — Ch18 "Hack Back Online Articles"

Online articles become distracting when reading stops being one bounded act and turns into a browser pathway. One article opens, then another tab looks promising, then a sidebar suggests something related, and before long the person is no longer reading with a purpose. They are wandering through adjacent prompts.

That is why Eyal's answer is not to stop reading. The stronger move is to contain the reading session. Choose the article on purpose. Read it inside a block. Save later items instead of opening them immediately. Keep one reading path from multiplying into many at the speed of curiosity.

The chapter matters because online reading often feels productive even while it drifts. A link looks relevant. A recommendation looks useful. Another tab looks like smart preparation. But the browser quietly turns one chosen act into an expanding field of alternatives.

Hacking back online articles therefore means treating reading as something that deserves boundaries. Let the article be the task. Keep the next tempting route from automatically becoming the current one. Preserve the value of reading without letting the browser turn reading into roam.

The chapter also keeps an important limit. Articles can be genuinely valuable for study, work, and curiosity. The point is not suspicion of reading. The point is to stop mistaking browser sprawl for sustained learning.

That leads naturally into feeds. If online articles expand through links and recommendations, feeds remove even more friction and make endless next-item drift the default. Chapter 19 follows by confronting that stronger loop directly.
