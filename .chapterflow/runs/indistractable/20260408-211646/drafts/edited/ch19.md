# Edited Draft — Ch19 "Hack Back Feeds"

Feeds distract not because every individual post is powerful but because the sequence keeps replacing endings with next items. One scroll reveals another option. A refresh supplies another batch. Novelty keeps whispering that the next thing might matter more than the reason you opened the feed in the first place.

That is why the chapter treats a feed as a next-item engine rather than a neutral information list. Hacking back feeds means changing the relationship. Enter with a purpose. Give the session a border. Reduce how often feeds appear by default. Stop treating endless refresh as harmless background behavior.

The sequence design matters because it hides drift inside tiny permissions. Each post is short. Each refresh feels reversible. Each extra minute looks trivial. But those small permissions add up into a session that no longer belongs to prior intent.

The stronger correction is to turn feeds into bounded retrieval. Check for something specific. Leave when that purpose is met. Avoid opening the feed merely to see what might be there. A stopping rule chosen in advance is stronger than trying to improvise one after novelty is already in charge.

The chapter also avoids a lazy conclusion. Feeds can contain useful updates, people, and ideas. The problem is not that useful material never appears. The problem is that the sequence itself is built to keep going. If the reader forgets that, usefulness becomes an excuse for lawless wandering.

That is why Chapter 20 follows. When feed loops keep defeating present intention, future-facing constraints become the next serious tool.
