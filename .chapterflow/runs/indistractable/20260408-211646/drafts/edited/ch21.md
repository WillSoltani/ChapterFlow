# Edited Draft — Ch21 "Prevent Distraction with Price Pacts"

Price pacts work because distraction often lies about its price. In the moment, skipping the block, checking the feed, or abandoning the plan can feel almost free. Eyal's move is to make that lie harder to believe. A price pact adds an explicit consequence chosen in advance so the distracting path no longer gets to masquerade as cheap.

That sequencing matters. A price pact is not supposed to replace internal-trigger work, timeboxing, or external-trigger redesign. It is a late-stage support for a plan that already matters. If the reader forgets that, the chapter turns into gimmicks and dares.

The value of the pact is clarity. Once a cost is attached ahead of time, the weak moment changes shape. The person is no longer choosing only between effort and relief. They are also choosing whether to pay a consequence they deliberately accepted when they were thinking clearly.

The chapter also keeps a moral boundary. This is not punishment theater. The cost should protect a genuine value rather than becoming a dramatic stunt. A price pact that is random, theatrical, or cruel is usually covering for the absence of earlier groundwork.

That is why the chapter treats price as one pact type, not the whole answer. Cost can be powerful when distraction keeps feeling falsely cheap, but it is not the only way to make follow-through sturdier.

That prepares Chapter 22. After explicit cost, the next form of pact ties consistency to identity so traction stops feeling like a temporary act and starts feeling like the kind of person you are trying to become.
