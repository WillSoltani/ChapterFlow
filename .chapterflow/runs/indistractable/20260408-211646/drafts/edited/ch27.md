# Edited Draft — Ch27 "Help Them with External Triggers"

Chapter 27 says children should be helped with external triggers, not just ruled by bans. The same book logic still applies: cues in the environment and from other people shape behavior. But in a family context, the goal is not only to remove temptations from above. It is to help children notice those cues and increasingly participate in shaping them.

That is why the chapter stays developmental. Adults still scaffold. They still decide some boundaries. But the stronger move is to teach children how rooms, routines, devices, people, and visible cues can either support or sabotage the traction the family is trying to protect.

The chapter matters because participation teaches more than pure obedience. A child who helps shape the study space, bedtime setup, or device placement learns something about how the environment works. A child who only receives bans may comply for a while without gaining the skill of recognizing triggers.

The chapter also keeps an important limit. This is not an argument for turning the child loose without structure. Adult scaffolding still matters. The point is that the environment should increasingly become something the child can understand and influence, not just something controlled for them in secret.

That is what keeps the developmental angle central. The chapter is not a copy of the adult trigger-cleanup chapters with smaller furniture. It is about growing skill, participation, and environmental awareness in children.

That prepares Chapter 28. Once children are better able to recognize and shape cues around them, they are closer to making pacts they can actually own.
