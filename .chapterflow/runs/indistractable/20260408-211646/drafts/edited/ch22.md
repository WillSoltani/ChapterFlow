# Edited Draft — Ch22 "Prevent Distraction with Identity Pacts"

Identity pacts work because behavior becomes steadier when it feels connected to who you are trying to be. Eyal is not claiming that a flattering label will do the work for you. The stronger point is that a chosen identity can make follow-through feel less like an isolated performance and more like coherence between values and action.

That is why the chapter must stay anchored to behavior. An identity pact is not vague self-talk. It connects a self-concept to repeated proof. The person does not just call themselves indistractable, reliable, or deliberate. They protect time, keep a boundary, return to the planned task, or refuse the familiar detour in ways that make the identity credible.

The chapter matters because distraction often thrives when the plan feels temporary. If focus is treated like a short-term stunt, it is easy to abandon. But when consistency is tied to a chosen identity, the same action can feel like evidence for the kind of person the reader means to become.

The chapter also keeps a limit. Identity does not replace the earlier work of understanding triggers, timeboxing traction, or using other pacts. It supports those efforts. Without behavior-level anchors, identity language collapses into slogans and nice feelings.

That is why the chapter keeps asking what the identity actually does. What boundaries does it keep? What routines does it protect? What proof does it produce? A believable identity pact lives there, not in affirmations floating above practice.

That prepares Chapter 23. Once the book has shown how identity shapes individual behavior, it can widen to the social environment and ask what workplace culture teaches people to normalize, protect, or betray.
