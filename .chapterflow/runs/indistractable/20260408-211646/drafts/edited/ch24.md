# Edited Draft — Ch24 "The Indistractable Workplace"

Chapter 24 turns the culture diagnosis into design. An indistractable workplace is not a slogan and it is not one heroic person with perfect habits. It is a shared environment where norms, routines, and permissions make focused work easier to protect.

That means expectations are clearer. Meetings have better purpose and boundaries. Interruptions are not treated as harmless by default. Response norms are less frantic. People can defend focus without sounding antisocial. The workplace becomes something built rather than something merely complained about.

The chapter matters because culture only becomes real when it becomes operational. A company cannot claim to value attention while leaving every routine unchanged. The real question is what the team actually does: how it coordinates, how it distinguishes urgency from noise, how it handles interruptions, and how it protects planned traction.

The chapter also keeps the unit of change shared. One employee can improve personal habits, but an indistractable workplace appears when the group treats those habits as normal. That applies in offices, remote teams, and hybrid settings alike because the channel changes less than the norm behind it.

The chapter avoids utopian fog. It does not promise a flawless workplace. It describes a healthier one: clearer expectations, better defaults, and more legitimate focus protection. That is enough to make attention less disposable in everyday work.

That creates a light bridge into the next section. Once the book has shown how workplaces shape adult behavior through norms, it can move to how children face their own inner discomfort and distraction.
