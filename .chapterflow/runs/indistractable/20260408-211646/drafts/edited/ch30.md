# Edited Draft — Ch30 "Be an Indistractable Lover"

Chapter 30 closes the book by refusing to leave attention inside the category of private productivity. Distraction does not only waste time or damage output. It also teaches the people closest to you what they can expect to receive from your presence. A partner may not describe the problem in framework language, but they can still feel the pattern of fragments.

That is why the final chapter lands in intimacy. A close relationship is one of the clearest places where the whole framework becomes impossible to fake. Internal triggers show up as the urge to escape discomfort through scrolling, work, or small evasions. External triggers show up as buzzing devices, spillover obligations, and ambient interruption. Time for traction matters because closeness needs protected room. Pacts matter because some relational boundaries hold better once they are spoken clearly rather than hoped for.

The chapter matters because repeated partial attention can thin a bond quietly over time. The damage is often cumulative, not dramatic. Another person keeps meeting only pieces of you, and eventually the relationship starts adapting to your absence even while you are still physically there.

The chapter also keeps its balance. It does not confuse logistics with love. A protected walk, a device boundary, or a scheduled evening does not produce tenderness, curiosity, repair, or honesty on its own. Structure protects the room. People still have to bring real presence into it.

That is what gives the ending its weight. To choose your life is also to choose who receives your full self instead of your leftovers. The book closes by making attention a relational act, not just a personal competency.
