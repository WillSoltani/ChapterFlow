# Edited Draft — Ch26 "Make Time for Traction Together"

Chapter 26 moves from inner understanding to shared planning. If a family can see the discomfort that pulls a child toward distraction, the next step is not endless supervision. It is to make time for traction together. In the book's language, traction means actions aligned with values and prior intent, and that idea can guide family routines too.

That is why the chapter stays collaborative. This is not generic family calendar advice and it is not a command post for parents. The stronger move is shared routines, visible plans, and agreements that children can understand, help shape, or at least meaningfully anticipate.

The chapter matters because shared traction reduces conflict later. Homework, play, rest, chores, and family connection become less likely to be negotiated only at the moment of temptation. A clearer plan does not remove friction entirely, but it gives the family prior intent to return to when distraction shows up.

The chapter also keeps values in view. The goal is not maximum efficiency or perfect household control. It is to make time for what the family says matters. Without that link, the chapter degrades into command-and-control scheduling.

That is why the chapter keeps the shared-contract logic load-bearing. A child does not need equal power over every plan, but traction together works better when routines feel legible and connected to real priorities instead of to arbitrary enforcement.

That prepares Chapter 27. Once a family has clearer shared traction, it becomes easier to shape the external triggers around those commitments instead of fighting every distraction one at a time.
