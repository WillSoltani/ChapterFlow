# Edited Draft — Ch16 "Hack Back Your Smartphone"

The smartphone is one of the strongest trigger sources in modern life because it travels everywhere, stays within reach, and keeps presenting cues that ask for immediate response. The device is not merely available. It is arranged to stay available.

That is why Eyal does not make the answer total abstinence. Most people are not going to stop carrying a phone, and many should not. The stronger move is reconfiguration. Change how the phone presents itself. Reduce unnecessary notifications. Rearrange the home screen. Add friction around tempting apps. Make the easiest path less distracting by design.

The chapter matters because repeated resistance is weak when the trigger source remains portable, visible, and frictionless. A phone with every alert turned on and every tempting app placed in direct sight keeps asking the person to win the same battle over and over. Better design asks fewer battles of willpower.

Hacking back the smartphone therefore means treating the device like environment, not destiny. Keep what is useful. Remove what is gratuitously noisy. Change defaults so the phone helps intentional action more than automatic checking. The goal is not punishment. The goal is governance.

The chapter also keeps an important limit. The phone still matters for communication, logistics, navigation, safety, and work. The point is not to pretend it can disappear. The point is to stop letting it function like a portable trigger machine.

That sets up the next chapter well. Once the person sees how a phone can be redesigned, it becomes easier to extend the same logic to the computer environment that shapes longer blocks of work. Chapter 17 moves from the pocket trigger source to the desktop one.
