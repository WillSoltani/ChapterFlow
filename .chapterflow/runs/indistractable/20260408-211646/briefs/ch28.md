## Governing Question
How can children move from parental control toward self-authored commitments they can realistically own?

## Core claim
The chapter should apply the pact framework to children in a developmental way. The point is not to make children miniature self-optimization adults or to pretend autonomy appears instantly. The stronger move is teaching children to participate in forming commitments they can understand and increasingly own, with adult scaffolding that respects autonomy limits.

## Required anchors
- pacts are precommitments that shape future behavior
- children can increasingly learn to make their own pacts rather than living only under imposed rules
- adult scaffolding and autonomy limits must stay visible
- self-authored commitments should feel understandable and ownable, not performative
- this chapter should bridge naturally into peer norms and social antibodies

## Reader value
- see how child-owned commitments can differ from externally imposed rules
- identify one kind of pact a child could realistically begin to own with support
- understand why developmental limits matter for self-authorship

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not make the chapter sound overly neat, as if children can instantly manage themselves through perfect pacts. Preserve scaffolding, gradual ownership, and the difference between guidance and abandonment.
