## Governing Question
Once values have claimed time, what part of the work inside that time can be owned with integrity even when the final result stays uncertain?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that people often attach too much emotional weight to outcomes they cannot fully control. A stronger approach is to control inputs: the time reserved, the effort applied, the repetitions completed, and the process kept. This makes traction steadier and less dependent on immediate results.

## Required anchors
- outcomes are partly outside the person's control
- inputs are where prior intent becomes actionable
- a good calendar block protects controllable effort, not guaranteed success
- process focus reduces emotional whiplash and false self-judgment
- this chapter should bridge toward scheduling relationships in Chapter 9

## Reader value
- stop measuring integrity only by visible result
- make calendar commitments around controllable behaviors
- understand why process control is stronger than result obsession

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not imply that outcomes do not matter. Preserve the limit that outcomes still matter for feedback and direction, but they are not the cleanest object of daily control.

## Source limits
- paraphrase-first only
- no invented studies
- no extreme anti-goal rhetoric
- keep examples tied to concrete inputs, effort, and process signals
