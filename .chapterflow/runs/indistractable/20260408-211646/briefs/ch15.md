## Governing Question
How can meetings stop functioning like default calendar seizures and start earning their claim on time?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that meetings become distracting when they are treated as the default way to resolve ambiguity, prove activity, or gather people without clear need. Hacking back meetings means making them earn time through purpose, agenda, right attendance, and stronger asynchronous alternatives.

## Required anchors
- meetings are preventable attention sinks when they lack clear purpose
- calendar time should be claimed only when a meeting materially helps the work
- agenda, scope, attendance discipline, and async alternatives are stronger than automatic invites
- this chapter should treat meetings as design choices, not office weather
- this chapter should bridge naturally into smartphone triggers

## Reader value
- stop treating every uncertainty as a reason to reserve a room or call
- identify one meeting norm that could be tightened or replaced
- understand meetings as a scarce shared resource, not a default reflex

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not turn the chapter into a generic anti-meeting rant or pretend all synchronous coordination is waste. Preserve that some meetings are useful when they clearly earn time.
