## Governing Question
What if much of distraction begins not with the device but with the discomfort that makes the device attractive?

## Core claim
The chapter should reframe motivation around relief from discomfort as much as pursuit of reward. The visible distraction is often downstream of an internal trigger such as boredom, uncertainty, stress, or emotional friction.

## Required anchors
- internal triggers come before many visible distractions
- technology is often the channel rather than the deepest cause
- relief-seeking explains why shallow fixes reroute instead of resolving the problem
- this chapter establishes the logic that later chapters will carry into time management, traction, and pacts

## Reader value
- stop blaming only tools
- locate the feeling that made escape profitable
- understand why substitution happens after shallow cleanups

## Hard-depth boundary
Diagnosis is not yet mastery. The chapter should preserve the limit that naming the trigger helps without pretending that awareness instantly dissolves it.
