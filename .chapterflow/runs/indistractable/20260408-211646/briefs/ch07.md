## Governing Question
If values matter, where do they appear in time instead of only in intention or aspiration?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that values become real only when they claim time on a calendar. A person cannot say something is important while consistently refusing to reserve time for it. Turning values into time makes intention visible, testable, and harder to flatter.

## Required anchors
- traction means acting according to values and prior intent
- values without scheduled time stay sentimental and easy to misreport
- calendar commitments reveal what matters more honestly than to-do lists alone
- scheduling is not about control theater but about making priorities concrete
- this chapter should bridge into controlling inputs rather than outcomes

## Reader value
- stop confusing declared priorities with time-backed priorities
- use a calendar as a values contract rather than a guilt surface
- see how timeboxing supports traction

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not imply that every unscheduled value is fake or that life can be perfectly controlled. Preserve the limit that the calendar is a test and a design tool, not a total guarantee.

## Source limits
- paraphrase-first only
- no invented studies
- no unsupported claims about productivity systems
- keep examples concrete and calendar-specific rather than generic time-management slogans
