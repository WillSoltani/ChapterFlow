## Governing Question
Why do workplace interruptions multiply when expectations stay vague, and how does proactive syncing protect traction without becoming workplace theater?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that many work distractions come from ambiguity among managers, peers, clients, and collaborators. When nobody knows response windows, urgency rules, or focus-time boundaries, they interrupt by default. Syncing with stakeholders means aligning expectations before those interruptions multiply. This is not etiquette theater or selfish hiding. It is coordination that protects both traction and collaboration.

## Required anchors
- workplace distraction often grows from unclear expectations, not only from devices
- syncing means making response norms, escalation paths, and availability visible
- remote work and videoconferencing intensify the cost of ambiguity
- protected focus at work should be aligned with stakeholders, not defended secretly
- this chapter should bridge toward asking the critical question when triggers arrive
