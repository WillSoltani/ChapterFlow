## Governing Question
How can group chat stop functioning like a permanent demand for visible responsiveness and become a bounded coordination tool?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that group chat creates distraction not only through message volume but through norms of ambient presence and rapid response. The answer is intentional constraint: channel purpose, muted defaults, clearer urgency lanes, and norms that stop every message from demanding immediate acknowledgment.

## Required anchors
- group chat is a stronger real-time trigger loop than email because presence and response feel socially visible
- chat norms, not just chat technology, drive distraction
- channel purpose, urgency separation, muting, and asynchronous expectations reduce pressure
- not every message deserves immediate acknowledgment
- this chapter should bridge naturally into meetings

## Reader value

- identify one chat norm that could be constrained or redesigned
- understand why chat needs different rules from email
