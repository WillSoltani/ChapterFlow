## Governing Question
How can a person protect prior intent by deciding in advance what the future moment will and will not allow?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that precommitments, or pacts, are future-facing constraints that shape behavior before temptation arrives. They matter because a person often knows in advance where later negotiation will fail. The answer is to make that future negotiation harder by adding structure, friction, or cost on purpose. This chapter should introduce the pact category without exhausting the specific price and identity forms that follow.

## Required anchors
- pacts are precommitments that shape future behavior
- precommitments belong after internal triggers, traction, and external-trigger work, not before
- the goal is to protect prior intent by changing the future choice environment in advance
- the chapter may preview effort, price, and identity flavors without consuming the dedicated chapters that follow
- this chapter should bridge into price pacts next
