## Governing Question
How can explicit cost make distraction less appealing when the moment keeps lying about how cheap it is?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that a price pact adds a real cost to breaking prior intent so the distracting path no longer feels falsely cheap. The cost can be social, financial, or otherwise explicit, but the key is that the plan carries a consequence chosen in advance. The order-of-operations rule must stay load-bearing: price pacts come after earlier work on internal triggers, traction, and external triggers, not instead of that work.
