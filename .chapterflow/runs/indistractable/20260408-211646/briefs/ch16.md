## Governing Question
How can the device that travels everywhere with you stop functioning like a portable trigger machine and start behaving like a tool under clearer control?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that the smartphone is powerful as a distraction source because it is portable, personal, and engineered to stay close at hand. The answer is not unrealistic abstinence. It is reconfiguration: change notifications, defaults, home-screen visibility, and friction so the phone stops presenting temptation as the easiest path.

## Required anchors
- the smartphone is a concentrated portable source of external triggers
- reconfiguration beats relying on constant willpower or total abstinence
- notifications, home-screen layout, app placement, and friction design are core levers
- the chapter should make environment design more important than heroic self-control
- this chapter should bridge naturally into the desktop chapter

## Reader value
- stop treating the phone as something that must always remain maximally available and stimulating
- identify one concrete smartphone reconfiguration to reduce triggers
- understand why portable environment design matters more than repeated resistance

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not turn the chapter into generic anti-phone shame or fantasy detox language. Preserve that the phone is useful; the task is to redesign the relationship, not to pretend the device can disappear.

## Source limits
- paraphrase-first only
- no invented app statistics or behavioral claims
- keep the guidance concrete and environment-centered
- avoid spilling into desktop or feed-specific advice that belongs in later chapters
