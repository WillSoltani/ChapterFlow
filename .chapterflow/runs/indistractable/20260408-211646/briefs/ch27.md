## Governing Question
How can children learn to shape the external triggers around them instead of only being managed through bans imposed from above?

## Core claim
The chapter should apply the external-trigger framework to children in a developmental way. The point is not simply that adults remove temptations. The stronger move is helping children see cues in their environment and participate in shaping those cues so the environment supports the shared traction from Chapter 26. The developmental angle must stay central.

## Required anchors
- external triggers come from cues in the environment or from other people
- children should learn to shape their environment rather than only obey bans
- adults still have a scaffolding role, but the chapter should move toward child participation and recognition
- the chapter should stay tied to the family routines and priorities already made visible
- this chapter should bridge naturally into self-authored pacts
