## Governing Question
If discomfort is partly attached to how a task is experienced, how can the task itself be changed so starting and staying with it produces less avoidable resistance?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that some distraction pressure can be reduced by changing the way a task is framed, started, chunked, or made engaging. The point is not to fake pleasure. It is to reduce unnecessary resistance that makes escape feel smarter than traction.

## Required anchors
- task design can raise or lower friction
- smaller starts and clearer next actions reduce avoidable resistance
- novelty, challenge, and visible progress can make attention easier to sustain
- the goal is not childish gimmicks or denial of real difficulty
- this chapter should bridge toward temperament and identity rather than staying at the task-design layer forever

## Reader value
- see that some avoidance comes from bad task design, not only weak discipline
- learn to reshape entry into a task before escape begins
- distinguish useful redesign from cosmetic gamification

## Hard-depth boundary
Not every hard task should feel easy. Preserve the limit that the chapter reduces avoidable friction, not the real cost of meaningful work.
