## Governing Question
How can email stop functioning like an always-open interruption stream and return to being a bounded communication tool?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that email becomes distracting when it is treated like a live feed instead of like a queue that can be processed with clearer rules. The answer is not inbox-zero theater or total neglect. It is bounded handling: defined check windows, clearer expectations, better subject discipline, and a refusal to let the inbox impersonate constant urgency.

## Required anchors
- email is an external-trigger system that rewards reactive checking when left unbounded
- the inbox should be handled in scheduled windows rather than as a permanent open loop
- routine email rarely deserves immediate response
- better rules and expectations beat heroic willpower
- this chapter should bridge naturally into group-chat norms and default responsiveness

## Reader value
- stop checking the inbox like a live scoreboard
- identify one rule that would make email calmer and more bounded
- understand email as a queue to process rather than a stream to obey

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not turn the chapter into inbox-zero ideology or contempt for correspondence. Preserve that some email matters, but most of it does not merit instant attention.
