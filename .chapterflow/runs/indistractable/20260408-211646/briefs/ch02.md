## Governing Question
What if many calendar problems are really discomfort-avoidance problems wearing a more respectable name?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that much procrastination and drift are not best described as neutral planning failure. They are attempts to move away from tasks that feel boring, uncertain, vulnerable, tedious, or emotionally costly.

## Required anchors
- scheduling problems often hide avoidance problems
- respectable substitute tasks can camouflage pain avoidance
- a full day can still be an avoidant day
- planning remains useful but must respect emotional reality
- this chapter should bridge toward dealing with distraction from within

## Reader value
- distinguish logistical delay from discomfort-driven delay
- notice when busywork acts like camouflage
- see why prettier systems often fail when the painful task stays unchanged

## Hard-depth boundary
Not every delay is emotional avoidance. Preserve the limit that some delays are structural, vague, or genuinely overloaded.
