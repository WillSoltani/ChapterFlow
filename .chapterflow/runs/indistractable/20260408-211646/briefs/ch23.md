## Governing Question
Why is chronic distraction at work often a cultural and coordination problem rather than only a personal weakness?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that persistent workplace distraction reflects company culture: what people are allowed to say, what interruptions are normalized, what managers reward, and how coordination gets handled. The chapter should resist blaming individuals for patterns that the environment keeps producing. Remote work and videoconferencing may appear as updated contexts, but the center of gravity is cultural norms, not technology panic.

## Required anchors
- distraction at work is framed as a company-culture problem, not only an individual willpower problem
- norms around interruption, responsiveness, meeting behavior, and silence shape focus
- workers need psychological permission and coordination safety to protect traction
- the chapter should resist simplistic personal blame
- this chapter should bridge naturally into the operational habits of an indistractable workplace

## Reader value
- see why distraction can be structural even when individuals are trying hard
- identify one workplace norm that quietly rewards distraction
- understand why culture is tested by whether people can surface problems without punishment
