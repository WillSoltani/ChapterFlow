## Governing Question
If relationships are genuinely important, why are they so often left to leftover time instead of protected time?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that important relationships deserve the same traction logic as other values. Love, care, and friendship should not depend only on spare attention after everything else is done. Scheduling relationships is not mechanizing affection. It is protecting what already matters from being crowded out.

## Required anchors
- relationships are values that should claim real time
- unscheduled care is often displaced by reactive demands
- scheduling relationships is a protection move, not an attempt to quantify love
- repeated protected presence matters more than dramatic intention
- this chapter should bridge toward syncing with stakeholders at work

## Reader value
- stop treating close relationships as whatever survives the week
- understand why protected time can support warmth instead of replacing it
- see how traction applies to people, not only tasks

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not imply that affection can be reduced to a calendar or that spontaneity has no value. Preserve the limit that scheduling protects connection; it does not replace emotional quality.
