## Governing Question
If the task is no longer the whole problem, how much distraction is still being protected by the story a person tells about what they are "just like"?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that identity claims such as "I am distractible," "I have no discipline," or "I am just not built for focus" can become permissions that stabilize future drift. Reimagining temperament means treating tendencies as patterns to work with, not destinies that excuse repetition.

## Required anchors
- temperament stories can quietly authorize distraction
- a tendency is not a verdict
- identity language shapes what effort feels possible
- the chapter should not deny real differences or overpromise total self-reinvention
- this chapter should bridge from internal-trigger work into turning values into time

## Reader value
- notice when self-description is functioning like permission
- replace fixed identity talk with more workable language
- see that focus becomes easier when the self-story stops helping distraction

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not imply that all differences are imaginary or that everyone can simply rename themselves into perfect focus. Preserve the limit that temperament exists, but fatalistic identity stories are not the only way to interpret it.

- paraphrase-first only
- no invented psychology studies
- no medical or diagnostic claims
- keep examples concrete and behavior-level rather than abstract personality discourse
