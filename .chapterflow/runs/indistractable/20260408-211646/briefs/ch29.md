## Governing Question
How can children keep what matters visible when peer norms and friendships start pushing in the other direction?

## Core claim
The chapter should show that friends and group norms can either magnify distraction or reduce it. The stronger move is not a cliche anti-peer-pressure speech. It is helping children build social antibodies: practical phrases, agreements, ally patterns, and peer norms that make focused behavior socially possible instead of socially costly.

## Required anchors
- external triggers can come from other people as well as devices and rooms
- peer norms can spread distraction or protection
- social antibodies should be concrete, social, and usable in live interactions
- the chapter should test whether earlier child-owned pacts can survive among friends
- the goal is healthier peer culture, not isolation from friendship
- this chapter should bridge naturally into the final relationship chapter

## Reader value
- see why distraction can become socially contagious among children
- identify one social antibody a child could realistically use with friends
- understand why peer culture matters for durable focus

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not flatten the chapter into a generic bad-friends sermon. Preserve the social-learning angle, the need for usable peer tools, and the difference between shaping culture and withdrawing from all peers.
