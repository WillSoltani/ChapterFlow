## Governing Question
If distraction pressure starts inside the person, what does it mean to deal with it there instead of only fighting tools after the urge is already running?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that many distraction battles are lost before the phone, inbox, or interruption becomes visible. The decisive moment is often the internal urge to escape discomfort. Dealing with distraction from within means noticing that urge as an event instead of obeying it as an order.

## Required anchors
- internal triggers are active before many external distractions appear
- an urge is not yet an action
- dealing from within means creating space between discomfort and response
- external controls still matter, but inner awareness is the first leverage point here
- this chapter should bridge toward reimagining the internal trigger rather than treating it like an enemy command

## Reader value
- notice the urge sooner
- separate feeling pressure from actual choice
- understand why tool-only defenses stay late and fragile

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not pretend that noticing an urge makes it disappear. The chapter should preserve the limit that discomfort remains real even when it no longer gets automatic obedience.
