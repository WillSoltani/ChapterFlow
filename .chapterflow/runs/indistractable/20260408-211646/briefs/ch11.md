## Governing Question
What question can interrupt automatic obedience to external triggers before a person clicks, answers, opens, or checks?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that external triggers gain power when people answer them reflexively. The way to weaken that reflex is to insert one clear question between the trigger and the action: does responding now serve prior intent, or is the trigger simply commandeering attention? The question must be concrete enough to change behavior in the moment.

## Required anchors
- external triggers come from cues, alerts, requests, and other people
- the critical question should filter whether the trigger serves traction or distraction
- the chapter should make trigger evaluation fast and practical, not mystical
- not every trigger should be obeyed immediately, even if it feels urgent
- this chapter should bridge toward making work interruptions legible and negotiable

## Reader value
- stop treating every trigger like a command
- gain a fast mental filter for pings, prompts, and requests
- prepare for the more concrete external-trigger chapters that follow

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not make the question vague, therapeutic, or abstract. Do not imply that the reader should ignore genuine obligations. Preserve that the question is a brief judgment tool that tests alignment with prior intent.

## Source limits
- paraphrase-first only
- no invented neuroscience or habit claims
- keep trigger examples concrete and ordinary
- stay practical and brief in the decision logic
