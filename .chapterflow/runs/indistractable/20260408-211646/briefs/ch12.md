## Governing Question
How can work interruptions stop being treated as unavoidable background noise and start becoming visible, negotiable costs?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that many work interruptions persist because they are treated as ambient reality instead of as design choices with real costs. Once interruptions are surfaced, counted, and discussed, teams can create better norms around batching, escalation, office hours, and response timing. Hacking back interruptions means making them legible enough to negotiate.

## Required anchors
- work interruptions carry real switching and recovery costs
- interruptions should be surfaced instead of normalized
- batching, office hours, update rhythms, and escalation lanes are stronger than constant availability
- this chapter should treat interruption patterns as designable, not inevitable
- the chapter should bridge naturally into hacking back email

## Reader value
- see interruptions as a system problem rather than as personal failure
- identify one work-interruption pattern that can be negotiated
- understand how clearer norms protect both focus and collaboration

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not turn the chapter into contempt for coworkers or fantasies of zero availability. Preserve that some interruptions are legitimate and that the goal is better interruption design, not total isolation.
