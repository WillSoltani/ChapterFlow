## Governing Question
Once an internal trigger becomes visible, how should the reader start understanding it differently so discomfort stops sounding like an unquestionable order?

## Core claim
The chapter should argue that internal triggers should not be treated as proof that something is wrong or as commands to escape immediately. They can be reimagined as temporary signals of discomfort that can be noticed, named, and handled without automatic obedience.

## Required anchors
- discomfort is not a reliable command signal
- reimagining the trigger changes the relationship to the feeling, not the fact that the feeling exists
- curiosity and naming reduce false urgency
- the reader should move from panic or resentment toward observation and choice
- this chapter should bridge toward reimagining the task in Chapter 5

## Reader value
- stop treating discomfort like a verdict
- weaken the feeling that every trigger must be answered with escape
- see how interpretation changes response quality

## Hard-depth boundary
Do not romanticize pain or imply that every trigger is useful. The chapter should preserve the limit that discomfort can stay unpleasant even when it is no longer granted automatic authority.

- paraphrase-first only
- no invented studies
- no therapeutic overclaims
- examples should stay concrete, practical, and clearly instructional
