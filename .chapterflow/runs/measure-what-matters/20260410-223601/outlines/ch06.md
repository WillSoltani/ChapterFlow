Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand how the Nuna case turns commitment from a stated value into a company discipline of carrying chosen priorities through real behavior.

Opening Move:
Open on the weakness of public agreement without carried burden, then show how commitment binds a company to what it chose.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for Nuna as the commitment case
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for commitment as carried burden and behavioral buy-in
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for hedging, decorative OKRs, and the bridge into alignment

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: establish the company case as the practical version of commitment
- P2: show why stated priorities stay weak without carried burden
- P3: define commitment as behavior change rather than awareness
- P4: show why buy-in matters for execution force
- P5: preserve the threat of hedging and decorative agreement
- P6: bridge into multi-team alignment

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes a company that agrees with priorities in language from a company that lets those priorities govern calendars, tradeoffs, and follow-through.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The reader must feel that commitment is not forced compliance yet still has to be costly enough to displace old behavior.

Threshold Question:
How do you tell whether a team is truly committed to chosen priorities or only performing agreement while protecting the old stack underneath?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- commitment as carried burden
- behavioral buy-in
- decorative agreement
- hedging against chosen priorities
- force without coercion

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): leader tests whether a team truly reorganized around a chosen priority
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): lab team fails because everyone agreed but nobody carried the hard work
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): family project exposes the difference between support and ownership
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): team says it is committed while old obligations still control calendars
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): nonprofit board must choose between symbolic unity and real carried commitments
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): side project becomes real once commitments displace old habits

Preview Constraint:
