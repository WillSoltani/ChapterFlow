Core Claim:
This chapter argues that OKRs create accountability when progress stays visible enough to be tracked, adjusted, and owned rather than left as a static statement of intent. Tracking should feel like live operational feedback, not punitive surveillance.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the conceptual bridge from connected execution into measurable accountability and should explain why priorities weaken when nobody can see movement, slippage, or course correction in time.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author moves from connection across teams into the need to see whether work is actually moving.
Then the author shows that tracking makes priorities actionable by keeping progress visible and discussable.
Then the author concludes that accountability grows from live visibility, not from fear alone.
