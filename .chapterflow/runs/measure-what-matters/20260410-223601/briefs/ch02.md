Core Claim:
The second chapter argues that OKRs are not just a useful framework. They reflect Andy Grove's deeper management belief that expertise without disciplined execution produces mediocre results. Grove built OKRs to force clarity, ownership, and measurable progress inside Intel, and Doerr treats that origin as proof that the method is a demanding operating discipline rather than a motivational accessory.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the only chapter centered on Andy Grove as the builder of the OKR discipline and on the managerial logic that made the method necessary before Google ever adopted it.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author presents Andy Grove as the source of the method.
Then the author shows that Grove cared about execution, clarity, and accountability more than decorative goal language.
Then the author concludes that OKRs work because they carry that managerial severity into the organizations that adopt them.

Required Anchors:
