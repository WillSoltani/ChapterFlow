Core Claim:
The opening chapter argues that fast-growing organizations do not fail only because they lack ambition. They also fail when ambition outruns visible priorities and shared measures. Doerr uses Google's early growth pressure to show why a team with great technology and huge aspirations still needed a system that forced choices, made progress measurable, and kept goals visible across the organization. The chapter's job is to make OKRs feel like an operating discipline born from execution pressure, not a management slogan.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the only chapter that introduces OKRs by placing them directly into Google's early scaling problem and by showing Doerr as the bridge between Intel's discipline and Google's ambition.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows Google at a moment of huge ambition and weak operating clarity.
Then the author introduces Doerr's Intel-inherited OKR logic as the missing execution discipline.
Then the author concludes that visible objectives plus measurable key results give ambitious teams a way to focus, coordinate, and track progress without shrinking the ambition itself.

Specific Applications:
- turn a vague ambition into a small set of visible priorities
- separate an inspiring objective from the evidence that shows progress
- use shared goals to reduce drift when a company is scaling fast

Common Misreadings:
- treating OKRs as a fancy name for any goal list
- assuming measurement kills ambition instead of sharpening it
- treating transparency as surveillance instead of coordination

Counterarguments or Limits:
This chapter is strong on the need for visible priorities but thinner on the interpersonal management layer that later CFR chapters handle. It also risks sounding like a growth-company story only, so the prose has to keep the underlying operating logic visible.
