Core Claim:
This chapter argues that commitment becomes real inside a company when teams stay bound to the priorities they chose and do not let old obligations, hedged effort, or symbolic alignment drain force from those priorities. The Nuna story should make commitment feel like carried burden, not passive agreement.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the first company story dedicated to commitment after the focus case and should translate Chapter 4's buy-in logic into a concrete company frame without inventing unsupported Nuna specifics.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author moves from narrowed priorities into the problem of staying bound to them.
Then the author shows that commitment worked because the company treated chosen priorities as obligations that governed behavior.
Then the author concludes that OKRs fail when teams agree in language but hedge in execution.
