Core Claim:
This chapter argues that OKRs prove their worth under pressure because they help a company realign around one decisive fight instead of letting urgency scatter into noise. The Intel Operation Crush story shows how Grove's execution discipline worked when time was short, the stakes were real, and feedback had to move quickly across the organization. The chapter's job is to make OKRs feel like a crisis-grade alignment tool, not just a planning system.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the only chapter centered on Intel's Operation Crush case and on company-wide realignment under competitive and time pressure.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author presents Intel under an existential competitive threat.
Then the author shows how Grove and the company used OKR discipline to realign around one urgent priority.
Then the author concludes that clarity, feedback, and coordinated execution matter most when the organization has the least room for drift.

Required Anchors:
- Intel faced an existential threat in the microprocessor battle
- Andy Grove and Intel realigned the company quickly around Operation Crush
- feedback from the field and company-wide focus were necessary to make the plan work

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

Specific Applications:
- use one urgent objective to unify a company under pressure
- let field feedback change priorities quickly instead of protecting hierarchy
- distinguish frantic activity from coordinated execution in a crisis

Common Misreadings:
- treating Operation Crush as only a heroic war story
- assuming crisis alignment means top-down command without feedback
- turning the chapter into generic urgency worship

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should preserve the pressure and speed without implying every organization should operate in crisis mode all the time.
