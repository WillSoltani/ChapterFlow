After one team is truly committed, a new problem appears. Other teams still have to move in ways that connect. That is why the book treats alignment and connection as a separate superpower rather than as a minor extension of focus or commitment. A committed team can still work at cross-purposes with its neighbors if priorities stay private, dependencies stay hidden, or every group optimizes only for its own local win.

The chapter's central claim is straightforward. OKRs help teamwork when priorities become visible enough to connect across teams. The point is not that every group should chase identical work. The point is that teams should understand how their objectives relate, where their dependencies sit, and how one team's progress affects another team's ability to execute.

This matters because sincere effort inside silos is still not the same as alignment. A product group, an operations group, and a partner team can all work hard and still weaken one another if their priorities remain disconnected. The friction does not require bad intent. It only requires private priorities and unclear links between related work.

Alignment therefore depends on visibility with purpose. Teams need to see not only their own objectives but also the shared direction those objectives are supposed to serve. Once those links are visible, tradeoffs become easier to discuss, duplicate effort becomes easier to spot, and conflicting pulls become harder to ignore.

The chapter should not be read as an argument for bureaucratic sameness. Alignment is not forcing every team to own the same job or suppress its local expertise. It is a way of connecting differentiated work so that separate teams can move toward a shared result without pretending they are the same unit.

The durable lesson is that teamwork improves when priorities are connected clearly enough that dependencies and shared direction can be seen in advance rather than discovered through collision. Commitment gives one team force. Alignment lets multiple teams direct that force together. With that superpower established, the next chapter can test it inside a company story.
