After the tracking story shows how visible progress makes accountability concrete, the next question is whether teams should aim only for what already feels comfortably reachable. The stretch chapter says no. OKRs become a fourth superpower when goals are set boldly enough to enlarge what people attempt instead of letting performance settle into safe incrementalism.

The chapter's claim should stay narrow and useful. Stretch matters because ordinary targets can quietly shrink ambition to what already feels manageable. When a goal reaches beyond that comfort zone, it can enlarge effort, creativity, and seriousness. But the chapter does not defend fantasy. Stretch is stronger when ambition stays connected to visible effort, disciplined follow-through, and honest ownership.

That connection to reality matters because a bold target can fail in two different ways. One failure is timidity, where the goal never asks enough to expand performance. The other is theatrical ambition, where the target sounds impressive but floats free of evidence, effort, and correction. The chapter should preserve that stretch lives between those mistakes. It asks more than a safe goal, but it does not excuse wishful exaggeration.

This is why the chapter belongs after tracking and accountability. Stretch is easier to trust once progress is visible enough to own. A team can push beyond safe performance more seriously when it can still see what the work is doing, where effort is changing the result, and whether correction is needed. Stretch without visibility turns brittle. Visibility without stretch can stay too small.

The chapter also has to reject the lazy misreading that stretch means difficulty for its own sake. A goal is not stronger because it sounds punishing or because it ignores whether real effort can support it. The stronger claim is that ambitious reach changes what a team attempts, while visible accountability keeps that ambition honest.

The durable lesson is that stretch becomes a superpower when it raises the ceiling on performance without severing goals from disciplined effort and visible ownership. Once that idea is clear, the book can move into the next company story and test what stretch looks like in practice.
