Once teams are connected around real work, another question appears immediately. How does anyone know whether that work is actually moving? That is why the book treats tracking and accountability as a separate superpower instead of as a small afterthought after connection. Priorities can still weaken once execution begins if progress stays invisible, correction arrives too late, or ownership depends on guesswork.

The chapter's claim is narrow and useful. OKRs strengthen accountability when progress stays visible enough to be tracked, discussed, and adjusted while the work is still alive. Tracking is not passive scorekeeping. It is a live way of seeing whether priorities are moving, where slippage is appearing, and what needs correction before delay hardens into drift.

This matters because a chosen priority can remain official even while movement fades underneath it. Teams may still say the work matters, but without visible progress they lose the ability to tell whether effort is translating into motion. Accountability weakens when ownership becomes retrospective explanation instead of live responsibility.

Tracking therefore depends on visibility with purpose. Progress has to stay visible enough for teams to discuss it honestly, adjust it early, and own it while outcomes are still changing. When that happens, accountability becomes more serious because people cannot hide behind static intent alone. The work has to show movement.

The chapter should not be read as a defense of punitive surveillance. Tracking is not stronger because it feels harsher. Fear can imitate accountability on the surface while making learning, correction, and ownership thinner underneath. The stronger point is that live visibility makes responsibility more concrete without collapsing into monitoring theater.

The durable lesson is that accountability grows when progress is visible enough to be tracked and corrected before it disappears into excuses. Connection makes work move together. Tracking makes that movement visible enough to own. With that superpower established, the next chapter can test it inside a company story.
