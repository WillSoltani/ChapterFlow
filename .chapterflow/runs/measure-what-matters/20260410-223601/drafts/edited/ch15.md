After the stretch stories prove what ambitious goals can do, the book widens the frame and asks what kind of operating system can carry that work over time. Chapter 15 answers by moving into continuous performance management. OKRs matter, but they do not stand alone. The chapter argues that goals work best when they sit inside an ongoing system of conversation, feedback, and recognition.

The chapter's claim should stay narrow and practical. Performance weakens when management depends too heavily on static annual-review logic. A periodic judgment can capture a moment, but it is weaker than ongoing conversation that helps people adjust while the work is still alive. That is why CFRs matter here. Conversation, feedback, and recognition support performance in a more continuous way.

This does not mean the chapter is replacing discipline with soft encouragement. The stronger point is that performance is supported better when useful conversation happens more often, feedback arrives closer to the work, and recognition helps reinforce progress while people can still learn from it. A static review system is often too delayed and too narrow to carry the full burden.

That is why this chapter belongs after stretch. Bold goals and ambitious work need a broader system around them. Stretch can expand what a team attempts, but continuous performance management helps people sustain, discuss, and improve that work over time. OKRs and CFRs therefore belong together rather than being treated as separate worlds.

The chapter also has to preserve a limit. Continuous performance management is not constant surveillance or endless judgment theater. Ongoing conversation is stronger than static review when it helps performance move rather than becoming noise or pressure without usefulness.

The durable lesson is that OKRs become stronger inside a broader operating system of continuous performance management and CFRs. That is what opens Part 2. Once this system is clear, the book can move into the Adobe story and show what leaving annual reviews behind looks like in practice.
