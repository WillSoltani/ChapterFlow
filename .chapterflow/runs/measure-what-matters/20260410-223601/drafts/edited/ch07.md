Once one team is truly committed, another problem appears immediately. Other teams still have to move in ways that connect. That is why the book treats alignment and connection as a separate superpower instead of as a small afterthought after focus and commitment. One team can mean its priorities and still collide with neighboring teams if priorities stay private, dependencies stay hidden, or each group optimizes only for its own local win.

The chapter's claim is narrow and useful. OKRs improve teamwork when priorities become visible enough to connect across teams. The point is not identical work. The point is related work moving under visible shared direction. Teams should understand how their objectives link, where dependencies sit, and how one team's movement changes another team's ability to execute.

This matters because sincere effort inside silos is still not alignment. A product group, an operations group, and a partner team can all work hard and still weaken one another if their priorities remain disconnected. The friction does not require bad intent. It only requires private priorities and unclear links between work that should have been connected earlier.

Alignment therefore depends on visibility with purpose. Teams need to see more than their own objective lists. They need to see the shared direction those lists are supposed to serve. Once the links are visible, tradeoffs become easier to discuss, duplicate effort becomes easier to spot, and conflicting pulls become harder to ignore until they become costly.

The chapter should not be read as a defense of bureaucratic sameness. Alignment is not forcing every team into identical goals or flattening local expertise. It is a way of connecting differentiated work so that separate teams can move toward a shared result without pretending they are the same unit.

The durable lesson is that teamwork improves when priorities are connected clearly enough for dependencies and shared direction to be seen before collision. Commitment gives one team force. Alignment lets several teams direct that force together. With that superpower established, the next chapter can test it inside a company story.
