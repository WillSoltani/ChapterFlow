The MyFitnessPal story matters because it takes alignment out of principle and places it inside a company case. After the superpower chapter, the question is no longer whether connected priorities sound useful. The harder question is whether a company can make those connections visible enough that related teams move in shared direction instead of fragmenting into well-meant local agendas.

The first lesson in the case is that alignment is not identical work. It is not every team repeating the same objective or submitting to one centralized script. Alignment begins when connected priorities make dependencies visible across teams. A company can sound coordinated in a meeting and still behave like a set of private local plans if the links between teams remain unclear.

The MyFitnessPal case works because it shows alignment as linked execution rather than as control theater. Real connection changes what teams can see about one another's work, where handoffs matter, and how one group's movement affects another group's ability to execute. Without that visibility, teams can work hard and still collide.

This also explains why silo drift matters so much in the chapter. Teams do not need bad intent to fragment. They only need to keep optimizing locally while shared direction stays vague and dependencies stay hidden. That is how a company keeps the language of teamwork while letting private priorities weaken the whole effort.

The chapter should not be read as a sermon for identical goals. Forced sameness can imitate alignment on the surface while flattening the specialized work different teams actually need to do. The stronger point is that differentiated work still has to connect visibly if the company is going to move as one system.

The durable lesson is that alignment becomes real when a company connects priorities clearly enough for teams to see shared direction and dependency before collision. That is what keeps teamwork from turning into parallel motion with better branding. Once that is clear, the next question follows: how do teams track whether these aligned priorities are actually moving?
