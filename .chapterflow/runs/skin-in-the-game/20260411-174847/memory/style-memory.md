# Style Memory

- Write like a sharp human explainer with taste: specific, concrete, compressed, vivid, and honest about limits.
- Teach the idea directly. Reader-facing content should almost never narrate "the chapter" or talk about the artifact itself.
- Open from pressure, event, conflict, bodily stakes, or a live question. Never thesis-first or slogan-first.
- Each paragraph needs a distinct job. Once a claim lands, do not restate it.
- Avoid filler, fake prestige, textbook tone, self-help drift, corporate language, fake profundity, and reusable scaffolds.
- No invented facts, quotes, studies, mechanisms, or anecdotes beyond the brief and authorized source support.
- No em dashes. No pseudo-science. No contamination phrases from briefs, outlines, validation, or pack internals.
- Easy: grade 8-9, one clean model, one usable implication, one vivid anchor, no theory stacking.
- Medium: grade 10-11, add mechanism, friction, and application, not padded paraphrase.
- Hard: grade 12, add at least two of boundary, contradiction, hidden structure, cost, failure mode, threshold, unresolved tension, or synthesis.
- Hard must not be medium with extra words. It needs a materially different cognitive job and different landings.
- Tone variants must differ in function, not adjective intensity:
  - gentle lowers resistance
  - direct clarifies mechanism
  - competitive sharpens stakes without sounding theatrical
- Examples must be concrete, socially realistic, chapter-specific, and operationally useful.
- Reinforcement surfaces must stay distinct:
  - key takeaway card distills
  - review cards retrieve
  - recap.retrieve tests memory
  - recap.connect links mechanism to consequence
  - recap.preview prompts prediction
  - implementation plan names a chapter-specific action or checkpoint
- Quiz items should test judgment inside situations, with plausible distractors and explanations that teach the mechanism instead of paraphrasing.
