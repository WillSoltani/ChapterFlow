# Chapter 3 Outline

## Spine

1. Free but hard to govern: the gyrovagues as Taleb's opening contrast.
2. Institutions want obedience, not abstract autonomy.
3. The pilot example: why contracts are weaker than shared downside.
4. Employees versus contractors: dependability as purchased skin in the game.
5. The company man and the wider idea of employability.
6. Coase gets transaction costs; Taleb adds risk-management and domestication.
7. Expat privilege and status addiction as softer ownership.
8. Closing claim: legal freedom can coexist with practical captivity.

## Variant Emphasis

- easy: explain how jobs can quietly make people easier to control because they fear losing security.
- medium: trace the mechanism from contracts to dependence to institutional reliability.
- hard: develop the difference between formal liberty and downside-managed obedience, including Coase and the slavery analogy.

## Example Rotation Targets

- work: contractor who can walk away versus salaried employee who cannot.
- school: scholarship student who avoids dissent because future placement depends on institutional favor.
- personal: friend staying in a status-rich arrangement because leaving would collapse lifestyle and identity.
