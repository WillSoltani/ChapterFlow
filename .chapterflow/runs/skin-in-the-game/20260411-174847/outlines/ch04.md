# Chapter 4 Outline

## Spine

1. Whistleblower dilemma: the ethical choice that injures people around the actor.
2. Family, mortgage, and dependents as channels of control.
3. Why societies historically tried to detach heroes from private obligations.
4. Hidden vulnerabilities: institutions attack the softer targets around a dissenter.
5. Reputation-conscious organizations as vulnerable employees.
6. Freedom requires reducing the skin of others in one's game.
7. Extreme case: actors without downside and Taleb's deterrence logic.
8. Closing claim: indirect punishment explains much moral compromise.

## Variant Emphasis

- easy: make clear that people often stay quiet because others would suffer.
- medium: show how institutions weaponize surrounding vulnerability and reputational fear.
- hard: connect moral agency, indirect coercion, minority pressure, and deterrence asymmetry without losing precision.

## Example Rotation Targets

- work: employee who knows of harmful conduct but fears destroying family stability.
- school: student leader who stays silent because adviser and teammates would absorb retaliation.
- personal: family member who avoids public dissent because relatives would bear the social cost.
