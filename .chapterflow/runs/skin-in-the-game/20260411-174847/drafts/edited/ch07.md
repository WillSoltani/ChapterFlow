# Inequality and Skin in the Game

Taleb's concern here is not inequality in the abstract. It is whether differences in rank, reward, and status are joined to exposure. Two people may not end up equal, but Taleb does not treat that as the main problem. The real question is whether the people at the top also share the downside, or whether they keep the upside while offloading losses onto others.

That is why the chapter follows so directly from the attack on insulated prestige. Chapter 6 showed how status can outrun correction. Chapter 7 widens the test from expertise to hierarchy itself. An unequal arrangement may still be tolerable, even honorable, if the people with more power also carry more risk, burden, or consequence. But when hierarchy is protected from its own failures, inequality starts looking less like earned difference and more like organized asymmetry.

The chapter's logic is about legitimacy. Taleb does not want a world with no winners. He wants suspicion directed at winners who play without downside. A merchant, builder, owner, or leader who can lose with everyone else belongs to a different moral category from a figure who privatizes gain and socializes pain. In Taleb's frame, the first kind of inequality may be rough but coherent. The second is structurally dishonest.

That is why skin in the game functions here as a fairness test. It is not only an epistemic test for expertise or a political test for intervention. It is also a way to judge whether hierarchy has been earned under shared exposure. If the people making decisions are insulated from the harms those decisions create, then the distance between top and bottom is no longer just unequal. It becomes suspect.

So Chapter 7 keeps the book's symmetry rule intact while applying it to reward. Taleb is asking the reader to separate hierarchy tied to responsibility from hierarchy floating above it. The issue is not envy. The issue is whether the arrangement forces the powerful to live inside the same reality they are helping shape for everyone else.
