Chapter 4 source bundle

Primary chapter anchor
- Nassim Nicholas Taleb, "The Skin of Others in your Game" (INCERTO / Medium, May 6, 2017).
- Authorized excerpt tied to Skin in the Game chapter 4.
- Key supported moves from the excerpt:
  - Whistleblowers face moral conflict because exposing hidden harm can destroy their family's security and future.
  - Institutions and hostile actors attack vulnerable people around a dissenter because those associated people carry softer downside.
  - Family obligations, mortgages, status, and institutional reputations make people easier to own or silence.
  - Celibacy, financial independence, and lack of attachments are treated as partial shields that reduce the skin of others in one's game.
  - Taleb extends the argument from corporate retaliation to institutions, public-relations campaigns, and reputational coercion.
  - He argues that some actors are insulated while the people around them absorb the consequences of truth-telling or resistance.
  - The chapter closes by asking how to inject real downside into actors, such as suicide terrorists, who otherwise face no ordinary deterrent.
