Chapter 3 source bundle

Primary chapter anchor
- Nassim Nicholas Taleb, "How To Legally Own Another Person" (INCERTO / Medium, Aug. 21, 2016).
- Authorized excerpt tied to Skin in the Game chapter 3.
- Key supported moves from the excerpt:
  - Gyrovague monks illustrate freedom that institutions dislike because free people are hard to domesticate.
  - Firms and institutions seek dependable people by giving them something to lose, not merely by writing contracts.
  - Contractor versus employee contrast: employees are costlier but easier to rely on because they fear job loss more than contractors fear lawsuits.
  - Employment signals domestication, obedience, and willingness to surrender schedule and autonomy.
  - The company man evolved into the more general employable person, who is disciplined not only by one employer but by fear of losing future employability.
  - Coase explains firms through transaction costs; Taleb pushes further and interprets employment as a risk-management strategy.
  - Roman use of slaves as treasurers is presented as a stark historical case of maximizing downside on the steward to reduce owner's risk.
  - Expat packages and status perks operate like a softer form of ownership because people become addicted to the privileges and fear losing them.

Interpretive guardrails
- Keep the chapter focused on practical dependence, employability, and obedience under downside pressure.
- Do not overstate literal slavery claims; Taleb's point is about functional ownership through incentives, reputation, and dependence.
- Distinguish formal freedom from practical freedom.
