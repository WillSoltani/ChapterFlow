## Narrow factual notes

- Taleb's main claim is that a tiny intransigent minority can force the entire population to submit to its preference when the majority is flexible and the minority is not.
- The kosher lemonade example introduces the mechanism: almost all drinks can become kosher because kosher consumers require kosher while non-kosher consumers can still drink kosher products.
- Taleb repeats the same asymmetry with halal food, disability bathrooms, peanut allergies, and smoke-free spaces.
- He names the minority intransigent and the majority flexible. The power comes from an asymmetry in choices, not from formal majority support.
- The chapter uses renormalization and fractal-like scaling to show how a tiny local rule can spread outward through households, neighborhoods, stores, and supply chains.
- He extends the pattern to parties serving wine instead of beer, language choice in multinational meetings, and religious conversion under one-way rules.
- Decentralization appears as a limit case: if the minority threshold is local, some states may yield and others may not; once units merge, the minority rule can spread across the whole combined system.
- Taleb stresses that social change often does not come from consensus, polling, or committees. A small group plus the right asymmetry can move the whole system.
