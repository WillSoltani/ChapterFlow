# Style Memory

- Write like a sharp human explainer with taste: specific, concrete, readable, authored.
- Teach the idea directly, not "the chapter."
- Prefer vivid openings, distinct paragraph jobs, live tension, and memorable lines.
- Easy stays accessible and pictureable. Medium explains mechanism plus application. Hard adds boundary condition, failure mode, contradiction, or threshold question.
- Avoid generic self-help tone, corporate tone, textbook tone, fake profundity, filler mechanism language, and repetitive sentence skeletons.
- No em dashes. No invented authority signals, fake neuroscience, pseudo-precision, made-up quotes, or unsupported studies.
- Reader-facing content must not leak brief, outline, validator, or seed language.
- Tone differences must be functional:
  - gentle lowers resistance
  - direct clarifies the mechanism
  - competitive sharpens stakes and consequence
- Examples must be concrete, socially realistic, and chapter-specific, with distinct lessons and specific `whatToDo` moves.
- Quiz items should test judgment in situations, use 3 choices, and teach in the explanation.
