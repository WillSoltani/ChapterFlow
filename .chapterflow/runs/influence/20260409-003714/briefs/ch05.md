Core Claim:
This chapter argues that people often comply faster when a person or message appears to carry legitimate authority. The chapter's narrow point is that deference can begin before the underlying expertise has been checked, which makes apparent legitimacy powerful and risky.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about status-backed compliance rather than consensus-backed compliance. It is impossible to confuse with social proof because the force does not come from many others seeming aligned. It comes from one source seeming entitled to direct judgment.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows that authority cues can accelerate compliance before scrutiny catches up.
Then the author shows that people often confuse signs of authority with authority that is actually relevant to the decision.
Then the author shows why the defense must separate real expertise from appearances that borrow its force.

Specific Applications:

Common Misreadings:
- authority is always fake

Counterarguments or Limits:
Authority is often useful because real expertise and legitimate rank can save time and reduce error. The problem is not authority itself. The problem is letting the appearance of authority do more work than the evidence supports.
