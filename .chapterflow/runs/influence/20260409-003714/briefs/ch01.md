Core Claim:
This chapter argues that much persuasion works by triggering fast, automatic responses instead of careful thought. Cialdini sets up the whole book by showing that humans rely on shortcuts because modern life is too dense to analyze everything in full. Those shortcuts are often useful, but they also create predictable openings for influence. The chapter's job is not to teach one lever yet. It teaches the operating environment that makes all later levers powerful.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's systems-opening chapter. It is impossible to confuse with later chapters because it does not center one lever like reciprocation or scarcity. Instead it introduces automatic responding, fixed action patterns, trigger features, click-run behavior, and the ethical tension between being influenced, defending yourself, and using these patterns on purpose.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author establishes that seemingly baffling influence tactics often rest on hidden regularities.
Then the author shows that animals and humans both use shortcut response patterns triggered by narrow cues.
Then the author argues that these shortcuts are efficient but exploitable, which is why the rest of the book matters.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- fixed action patterns: fast preloaded response sequences triggered by narrow cues
- judgmental heuristics: simplified mental shortcuts used under complexity
- click-run responding: shorthand for automatic reaction
- controlled responding: slower fuller analysis
- trigger feature: the single cue that activates a larger response pattern
- profiteers / jujitsu: unethical exploitation versus skillful ethical use
- contrast principle: evaluation changes when one option is seen after another

Specific Applications:
- noticing when a small cue is steering a larger decision
- slowing down on decisions where trigger features may be doing too much work
- spotting how sellers structure comparisons to make one option look better

Common Misreadings:
- shortcuts are always irrational and should be eliminated
- influence only works on gullible people
- this chapter endorses manipulation rather than warning about it

Counterarguments or Limits:
Shortcuts are not just flaws; they are often necessary to function in a complex environment. The chapter's tension is that the same efficiency that helps judgment also makes judgment vulnerable.
