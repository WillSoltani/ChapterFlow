Core Claim:
After focusing on the mind as the instrument, the book needs to clarify what kinds of situations are truly matters of judgment. The chapter's job is to mark the zone where interpretation, assessment, and scale-setting are doing real work and where noise therefore becomes a live concern. It should help the reader distinguish judgment-heavy decisions from cases that are more mechanical or rule-bound.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It defines the scope of the book's problem by clarifying where judgment is actually present.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author asks what counts as a matter of judgment.
Then the author shows that interpretation and assessment make these cases vulnerable to unstable readings.
Then the author concludes that noise belongs wherever a human scale or assessment is doing meaningful work.

Required Anchors:
- situations where a person must assess rather than merely apply a fixed answer
- judgment as interpretation on a scale, not simple fact lookup
- noise becoming relevant when human assessment has room to vary

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

Judgment zone: the part of a process where a human scale or evaluation is still active.
