Core Claim:
After opening the broad improvement question, the book turns toward the judges themselves. The chapter's job is to suggest that judgment quality depends not only on process design but also on the qualities, conditions, or selection of the people doing the judging. It should make the reader feel that better judgments require attention to better judges, not just better complaints about bad outcomes.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It narrows the improvement discussion from general betterment toward the human side of judgment quality.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author moves from improving judgments broadly to the role of judges themselves.
Then the author links judgment quality to who is judging and under what conditions.
Then the author concludes that better judgments require better judging agents as well as better systems.
