Core Claim:
After showing how singular decisions hide spread, the book turns toward the judge rather than only the judged case. The chapter's job is to frame the mind as the instrument producing the assessment, which means instability can live in the instrument itself rather than only in the surrounding system or missing comparison set. It should make the reader feel that judgment is partly a measurement problem: if the instrument shifts, the output shifts.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It moves from visible decisions and hidden spread into the internal instrument that generates the judgment.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author uses the title-level metaphor that the mind functions like a measuring instrument.
Then the author connects unstable outcomes to instability in that instrument.
Then the author concludes that judgment quality depends partly on instrument reliability, not just case facts.

Required Anchors:
- mind as a measuring instrument rather than a pure source of insight
- judgments changing because the instrument can shift
- the need to think about reliability of the judge, not only properties of the case
