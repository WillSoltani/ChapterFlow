Core Claim:
After defining the judgment zone, the book turns to error itself. The chapter's job is to show that once readings genuinely count as judgments, error is not just a vague failure but something that can be inspected as a gap between what the judgment delivers and what steadier judgment should deliver. It should make the reader feel that measurement matters because noise becomes serious only when error can be named rather than merely complained about.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It shifts from defining where judgment lives to asking how misses or deviations inside judgment should be measured.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author narrows from judgment zones to the problem of error inside those zones.
Then the author treats error as something that can be inspected rather than only felt.
Then the author concludes that serious work on noise requires a clearer way of looking at error.

Required Anchors:
- error as a measurable miss rather than a vague bad outcome
- judgment outputs being compared against a steadier or more defensible reading
- the idea that analysis of noise depends on getting error into view first
