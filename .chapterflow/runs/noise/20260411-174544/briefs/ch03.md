Core Claim:
The book widens from vivid single domains to the idea that noise can be built into systems, not just isolated professions. A system becomes noisy when comparable cases are routed through many judgment points that each add spread while nobody owns the distribution as a whole. The chapter's job is to show that variability can become normal, invisible, and cumulative at system scale.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It shifts from the moral sharpness of punishment to the broader institutional architecture that lets spread persist across many decisions.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author broadens from noisy punishment to noisy systems.
Then the author shows that local explanations can hide system-level spread.
Then the author concludes that institutions need to inspect patterns, not just isolated decisions.

Required Anchors:
- many judgments inside one system adding spread cumulatively
- local defensibility versus global instability
- institutions normalizing variability because nobody sees the whole pattern

Counterarguments or Limits:
