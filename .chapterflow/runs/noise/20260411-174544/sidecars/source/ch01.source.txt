Chapter 1 title from authorized contents: Finding Noise.

Frozen support for this chapter:
- Official synopsis and official site define noise as variability in judgments that should be identical or at least much closer than they are.
- Official examples used across the frozen bundle: doctors diagnosing matched patients differently, judges sentencing similar crimes differently, inspectors or complaints handlers reaching inconsistent outcomes.
- Barron's interview adds a concrete opening case: an insurance company tested 48 underwriters on the same cases and found about 55% variability, not the roughly 10% they expected.
- Interview framing also distinguishes bias from noise: bias is average error; noise is variability in error.

Safe chapter-level implications:
- organizations often notice bias before they notice variability
- variability can be costly even when average judgment seems acceptable
- the opener should make disagreement visible and serious before later chapters move into taxonomy or remedy

Do not claim:
- detailed chapter substructure beyond the visible title
- unsupported studies, page numbers, or full-solution programs
- that all disagreement is bad rather than some disagreement being normal discretion
