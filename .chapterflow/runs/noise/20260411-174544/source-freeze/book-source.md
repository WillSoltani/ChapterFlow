## Authorized metadata and synopsis
- Google Books identifies the book as a 2021 Little, Brown release, 464 pages, ISBN `9780316451383`.
- The visible synopsis defines noise as variability in judgments that should be identical.
- The synopsis and official site both frame the core cost the same way: people and organizations see bias more easily than noise, so they often ignore costly variation in decisions that should be steadier.

## Chapter 1 support notes
- The chapter title shown in the authorized contents is `Finding Noise`.
- The Barron's interview adds a concrete opening case: an insurance company tested 48 underwriters on the same cases and saw about 55% variability, far above the 10% it expected.
- Kahneman's interview framing distinguishes bias from noise: bias is average error; noise is variability in error.
- Early practical remedy language in the interview emphasizes independent judgments and aggregation rather than group discussion first.
