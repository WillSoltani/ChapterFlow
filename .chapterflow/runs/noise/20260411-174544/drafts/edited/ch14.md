Once the book turns from combining judgments to guiding them, medicine becomes a vivid test case. The chapter title makes the issue plain. Guidelines in medicine are an attempt to reduce variation without pretending that judgment disappears.

That move matters because professional judgment can vary in high-stakes settings even when the cases look similar. A guideline offers structure that can narrow some of that spread. The chapter therefore treats guidance as a support for steadier judgment rather than as a replacement for judgment altogether.

This is where the chapter keeps its main tension alive. Guidelines can help reduce avoidable variation, but they do not make every case automatic. Professional discretion still matters, and the value of structure comes with tradeoffs about fit, flexibility, and rigidity.

The chapter keeps one useful limit in place. More guidance is not automatically better guidance, and medicine does not become frictionless because a protocol sheet exists. The point is narrower and more realistic. Structured guidance can improve consistency while still leaving room for difficult judgment.

That is what makes medicine such a strong case here. The stakes are high enough that the gains from reduced variation matter, but the domain is also complicated enough to keep the limits of structure visible.

Once that balance is clear, the next step can narrow further. The book can move toward how scales themselves shape judgment in performance ratings.
