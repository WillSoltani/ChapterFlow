Once the governance tradeoff is visible, the book can ask whether institutions are serious enough to act on what they know. The chapter turns to taking noise seriously.

That move matters because noticing variability is not the same thing as governing it deliberately. A problem can be named and still remain background.

This is where the book becomes more practical about institutional posture. Noise has to be treated as a real quality problem rather than as tolerated friction.

The chapter therefore does not call for instant perfection. It asks whether the institution is willing to make noise reduction a real priority.

That does not mean every tradeoff disappears. It means casual acknowledgment is no longer enough.

Once that seriousness is visible, the next move can show what such commitment might make possible. The book can turn to a less noisy world.
