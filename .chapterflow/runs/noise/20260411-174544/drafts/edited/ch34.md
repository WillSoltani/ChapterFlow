Once dignity is in view, the book can press the next design question. The chapter turns to rules or standards.

That move matters because institutions often have to choose between tighter rules that narrow discretion and looser standards that leave more room for judgment.

This is where the tradeoff becomes more explicit. Rules can reduce variation, but they can also harden into rigidity. Standards can preserve context, but they can also leave more room for noise.

The chapter therefore does not declare a universal winner. It asks what each form gains and loses when a system tries to judge well.

That does not mean the choice is arbitrary. It means governance has to weigh consistency, flexibility, and human treatment together.

Once that design tradeoff is visible, the next move can ask what it means to take noise seriously at the institutional level.
