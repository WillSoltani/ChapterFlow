Once guidance has done some work, the next question gets quieter but no less consequential. Performance ratings do not depend only on who is judging. They also depend on the scale that tells judges what distinctions they are expected to make.

That move matters because a rating scale is not just a neutral container waiting for judgment to be poured into it. If the levels are vague, the same performance can be split differently by different evaluators. The chapter therefore turns from guidance in general to the form that organizes evaluative judgment itself.

This is where the scale matters as structure. Defining the scale more clearly can help evaluators separate one level from another with less avoidable drift. The gain is not that judgment disappears. The gain is that the distinctions stop being quite so underdescribed.

The chapter keeps one useful limit in view. More detail is not automatically better, and a finer scale can create false precision if the distinctions are not genuinely meaningful. The claim is narrower than that. Better scale definition can improve the baseline while leaving judgment and ambiguity in place.

That is why performance ratings matter here. They show how variation can enter through the very format used to express judgment.

Once that becomes visible, the next move can widen again. The book can turn toward structure in hiring.
