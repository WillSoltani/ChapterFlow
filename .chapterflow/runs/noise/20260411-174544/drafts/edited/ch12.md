Decision hygiene becomes easier to grasp once one concrete lever comes into view: the order in which information arrives. The chapter's title makes that lever explicit and places it inside a high-stakes setting. Sequencing information in forensic science is not a cosmetic choice. It can shape judgment itself.

That move matters because what appears first can frame what gets noticed later. A judge may believe each piece of evidence is being weighed on its own merits, yet the order of exposure can still tilt the reading. The chapter uses forensic science to make that pressure vivid without needing to claim that the setting is unique.

This turns sequence into a practical design question. The issue is not only what information a person receives, but when it arrives and what it has already prepared the mind to expect. Once that becomes visible, order stops looking like presentation style and starts looking like part of judgment hygiene.

The chapter keeps one needed limit. Sequencing information is not a total remedy, and process order alone cannot erase every source of bias or noise. The point is narrower and more useful. A serious judgment process can improve by controlling one avoidable source of distortion.

That is what makes the forensic example travel beyond its own domain. It shows, in a vivid case, how subtle design choices can affect consequential judgments long before anyone notices the effect.
