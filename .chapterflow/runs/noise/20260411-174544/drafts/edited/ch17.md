Once structure has done some work, another question starts to matter. Judgments can still vary even when the process is better than it was before. The chapter title points to one reason: occasion noise.

That move matters because better design does not exhaust the problem of variation. A judgment can shift from one occasion to another even when the judge or system has not obviously changed. The chapter therefore deepens the analysis of noise rather than abandoning the gains from structure.

This is where occasion noise matters. Some spread belongs not only to different people or weak process design, but to the occasion of judgment itself. The chapter adds that layer to the picture so the remaining variation does not stay invisible.

The chapter keeps one useful limit in view. Occasion noise is not a reason to stop caring about process design, and it is not the whole story of judgment variation. The claim is narrower. Better process can help, and residual variation can still remain.

That is why the chapter matters after hiring. It explains why improved structure does not fully close the case against noise.

Once that becomes visible, the next move can widen again. The book can ask how groups amplify noise.
