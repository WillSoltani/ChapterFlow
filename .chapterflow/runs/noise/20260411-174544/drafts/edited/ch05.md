A judgment feels easiest to trust when the case takes up the whole frame. A hiring manager studies one résumé. An admissions reader studies one folder. A forecaster studies one number. The case looks central. The chapter's title interrupts that comfort by forcing a second question: what kind of instrument is the mind that is producing the reading?

That question matters because noise can live in the instrument as well as in the surrounding process. If the mind is functioning like a measuring device, then changes inside the device can alter the reading. The same file can look stronger on one pass than another. The same forecast can feel more convincing in one condition than another. The point is not that judgment becomes mechanical. The point is that judgment produces readings, and readings depend partly on how steady the instrument is.

This sharpens the previous chapter. Singular decisions were hard because the missing comparison set made the answer feel self-sealing. But if the mind is a measuring instrument, then some uncertainty belongs to the reader, not only to the case. The issue is no longer just what the file contains. It is also what kind of instrument is interpreting it.

That does not make human judgment useless. Instruments can be valuable without being perfect, and people do not stop needing judgment because the measuring device is less stable than they imagined. The pressure is narrower than that. Confidence should reflect not only trust in the evidence, but also trust in the instrument reading the evidence.

That is where humility becomes practical instead of decorative. A serious judge does not erase themselves from the process, but they stop pretending they are a transparent window onto the case. They admit that the answer may partly reflect the state of the instrument that produced it. The point is not to surrender judgment. It is to inspect it more honestly.

Once that move is made, the next question becomes unavoidable. In what situations is this instrument truly doing judgment rather than simply retrieving a fixed answer? The book has to define that zone before it can measure error inside it.
