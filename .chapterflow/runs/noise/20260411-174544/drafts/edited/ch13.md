Once process design enters forecasting, one individual estimate stops looking like the whole story. The chapter title points to a broader question. Forecast quality depends not only on one judgment, but on which judgments are selected and how they are aggregated.

That move matters because combining forecasts can reduce avoidable noise without pretending that any one forecaster sees clearly enough on every occasion. The chapter therefore shifts from the individual reading to the pool. Better judgment can come from using multiple judgments more intelligently.

This is where selection and aggregation have to stay separate while still working together. Selection asks which forecasts belong in the pool at all. Aggregation asks how those inputs are combined. The chapter needs both because a combined judgment depends on the quality and composition of what enters it, not only on the act of combining.

The chapter keeps one needed limit in view. Aggregation is not a promise of perfect foresight, and a larger pool does not make every estimate equally useful. The point is more practical than that. A better combination can improve the baseline while uncertainty remains real.

That is why forecasting matters here. It gives the book a wider case for how process design can strengthen judgment without requiring flawless individuals.

Once that lesson is visible, the next move can test structure in another high-stakes setting. The book can turn toward guidelines in medicine.
