Once scales come into view, the book can widen from rating design to a whole decision process. Hiring is an obvious test. It is a setting where impressions, comparisons, and confidence can drift unless the process gives them firmer support.

That move matters because similar candidates can be judged differently for reasons that are not only about merit. The chapter therefore treats structure in hiring as a way to narrow some avoidable variation without pretending candidate selection becomes automatic.

This is where process support matters most. Structure can help interviewers compare candidates on steadier terms and keep the evaluation from becoming a loose contest of impressions. The point is not to erase judgment. The point is to give judgment a stronger frame.

The chapter keeps one useful limit in place. More process is not automatically better process, and structure can harden into overformalization if it stops fitting the decision. The claim stays narrower than that. A better-designed hiring process can improve the baseline while complexity and discretion remain real.

That is why hiring belongs here. It shows how high-stakes selection can benefit from structure without becoming a machine.

Once that lesson is visible, the next step can move toward another source of variation. The book can turn to occasion noise.
