Once scales are in view, the book can shift from frame design to recurring variation. The chapter turns to patterns.

That move matters because some differences in judgment do not appear as isolated accidents. They recur in ways that start to look stable across cases.

This is where the chapter changes the reader's attention. Instead of asking only how a single judgment went wrong, it asks whether a repeated tendency is showing up again and again.

The chapter therefore treats stable personal variation as part of the noise picture. A repeated style of judging can matter even when it does not explain everything.

That does not mean every pattern is a total theory of the person or the process. The point is narrower than that.

Once recurring variation is visible, the book can widen the frame again. The next step can gather the sources of noise.
