Once the practical horizon is visible, the book can turn to the work of measurement. The chapter turns to how to conduct a noise audit.

That move matters because suspicion about inconsistency is not yet the same thing as evidence. Variability has to be measured through structured comparison.

This is where the book becomes more operational. A noise audit asks comparable judgments to be observed and compared instead of guessed at.

The chapter therefore does not promise a universal formula. It asks for disciplined assessment strong enough to make variability visible.

That does not mean every number settles every question. It means concern is weaker than organized evidence.

Once that method is visible, the next move can show what an observer should notice while judgments are happening. The book can turn to a checklist for a decision observer.
