Once noiseless rules are visible, another question follows. What if stronger structure still cannot settle everything? The chapter turns to objective ignorance.

That move matters because some limits do not come from sloppy process alone. A judgment can remain constrained by what cannot be cleanly known, even after the system becomes more disciplined.

This is where the chapter has to stay narrow. Objective ignorance does not mean improvement is useless, and it does not collapse into fatalism. The claim is simpler than that. Better structure can help while some knowledge limits still remain.

The chapter therefore sharpens the boundary of what consistency can achieve. Rules, models, and discipline matter, but they cannot abolish every limit that comes from incomplete knowledge.

That is why the chapter belongs here. It shows that stronger consistency has an edge beyond which the problem is not just noisy judgment but what the system cannot fully know.

Once that limit is visible, the next move can turn toward another difficult zone. The book can move into the valley of the normal.
