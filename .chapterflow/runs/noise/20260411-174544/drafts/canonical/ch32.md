Once the target question is visible, the book can turn to the costs of noise reduction. The chapter makes those costs explicit.

That move matters because tighter process can create burden, friction, or rigidity even when the goal of lower noise remains valuable.

This is where the chapter becomes more concrete. Reducing noise is not free. Stronger control can slow work, narrow discretion, or add human strain.

The chapter therefore does not argue that costs defeat reduction by themselves. It argues that the costs have to be seen and weighed.

That does not mean the value of lower noise disappears. It means the gains have to be judged beside the burdens.

Once those burdens are visible, the next move can turn to the human meaning of such tradeoffs. The book can move to dignity.
