The easiest way to trust a judgment is to focus on the case in front of you and forget about the instrument producing the reading. A hiring manager studies one résumé. An admissions reader studies one folder. A forecaster studies one number. The case feels central, but the chapter's title forces a different question. What kind of instrument is the mind that is doing the measuring?

That shift matters because noise can live in the instrument as well as in the setting around it. If the mind is acting like a measuring device, then changes inside the device can alter the reading. The same file can look stronger on one pass than another. The same forecast can feel more convincing in one condition than another. The chapter is not claiming that judgment is mechanical. It is claiming that judgment produces readings, and readings depend partly on the steadiness of the instrument.

This helps explain why singular decisions were so difficult in the previous chapter. When no comparison set is visible, it is tempting to trust the lonely answer as if the case itself had dictated it. But if the mind is a measuring instrument, then some uncertainty belongs to the reader, not only to the case. The issue is no longer just what the file contains. It is also what kind of instrument is interpreting it.

That does not make human judgment worthless. Instruments can be useful without being perfect, and people do not stop needing judgment just because the measuring device is not flawlessly stable. The chapter's pressure is narrower. Confidence should reflect not only how much trust we place in the evidence, but also how much trust we place in the instrument reading the evidence.

That is where humility becomes practical rather than decorative. A serious judge does not erase themselves from the process, but they stop pretending they are a transparent window onto the case. They acknowledge that the answer may partly reflect the state of the instrument that produced it. The point is not to surrender judgment. It is to inspect it more honestly.

Once that move is made, the next question becomes unavoidable. In what kinds of situations is this instrument truly doing judgment rather than simply retrieving a fixed answer? The book has to define that zone before it can measure error inside it.
