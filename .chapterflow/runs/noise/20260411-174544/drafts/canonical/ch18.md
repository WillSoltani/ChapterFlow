Once occasion noise is visible, the book can widen to a collective setting. Groups often look like a natural remedy for individual variation, but the chapter title points toward a harder possibility: groups can amplify noise.

That move matters because adding more people does not automatically smooth variation away. Collective judgment can intensify some drift instead of canceling it. The chapter therefore pushes the analysis of noise into committees, panels, and group deliberations.

This is where the warning matters. More voices can change a judgment process in ways that do not simply average the differences out. The point is not that all groups are bad. The point is that collective processes carry their own noise risks.

The chapter keeps one limit in place. Group amplification is not a blanket case against all collective judgment, and it does not replace every other source of variation. The claim is narrower than that. Groups can worsen noise under some conditions instead of automatically curing it.

That is why the chapter belongs here. It challenges the easy comfort that more judges must mean less noise.
