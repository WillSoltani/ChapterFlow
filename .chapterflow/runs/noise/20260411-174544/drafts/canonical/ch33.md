Once the costs of tighter process are visible, the book can turn to what those costs may mean for the people inside the system. The chapter turns to dignity.

That move matters because a cleaner process can still make someone feel flattened, scripted, or unseen. Lower noise does not answer every question about treatment.

This is where the argument becomes more human. Rules and standardization can improve consistency while still carrying a cost in respect.

The chapter therefore does not say that dignity defeats structure. It says that structure must answer for how it treats the person before it.

That does not mean the value of lower noise disappears. It means consistency has to be judged beside the quality of treatment it produces.

Once that human stake is visible, the next move can ask what kind of governance design best carries it. The book can turn to rules or standards.
