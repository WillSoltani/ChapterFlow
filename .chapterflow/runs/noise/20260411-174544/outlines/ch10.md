Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why better judgments depend partly on better judges and better judging conditions.

Opening Move:
Open from a process that looks improved on paper until the reader sees that the quality of the people judging still matters.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1 human-side lever
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2 better judges versus perfect judges
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3 bridge to hygiene

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: anchor
- P2: mechanism
- P3: tension
- P4: implication
- P5: limit
- P6: bridge

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Separate better judges from fantasies of flawless individuals and from the opposite mistake of ignoring the human side entirely.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve that judge quality matters alongside systems rather than instead of systems.

Threshold Question:
What changes when better judgment is treated as partly a question about better judges and judging conditions?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: judge quality as improvement lever
- Takeaway 2: human conditions matter
- Takeaway 3: better judges are not perfect judges
- Takeaway 4: systems still matter
- Takeaway 5: bridge into decision hygiene

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): one hiring assessor pool
- Scenario 2 (conflict, personal): one recurring judgment problem tied to who is deciding
- Scenario 3 (debrief, school): one admissions reader set
- Scenario 4 (comparison, work): stronger versus weaker judging conditions
- Scenario 5 (reversal, personal): ask whether the problem is the system, the judge, or both
- Scenario 6 (checklist, school): observer prompt for human-side quality

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, specify one sensory or situational detail to include.
- reader notes with different quality
- a panel room with uneven standards
- two judging conditions side by side
- a checklist about who is judging and how

Preview Constraint:
The preview must point into debiasing and decision hygiene.

Genericity Risks:
- hero worship of “good judges”
- vague claims about talent
- ignoring the interaction with systems

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: generic advice to hire better people
- filler move 2: vague praise of expertise
- invented authority move 1: unsupported training or selection framework
- brief-language leak 1: threshold question wording in reader-facing prose
