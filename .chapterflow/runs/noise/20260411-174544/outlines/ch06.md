Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand what kinds of situations are genuinely matters of judgment and why those situations invite noise.

Opening Move:
Open from a case that looks straightforward until the reader notices that a human scale or interpretation is still active inside it.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1 scope
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2 interpretation
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3 noise relevance

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: anchor
- P2: mechanism
- P3: distinction
- P4: implication
- P5: limit
- P6: bridge

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Separate rule execution from situations where human assessment is still setting the reading.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must preserve gray zones where rules and judgment mix rather than pretending the line is perfect.

Threshold Question:
When is a decision actually a matter of judgment rather than a matter of retrieval or fixed rule application?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: judgment involves interpretation
- Takeaway 2: scale-setting creates room for variation
- Takeaway 3: not every process is equally judgment-heavy
- Takeaway 4: gray zones between rules and assessment
- Takeaway 5: bridge into measuring error

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): one performance assessment
- Scenario 2 (conflict, personal): one contested family judgment
- Scenario 3 (debrief, school): one admissions read
- Scenario 4 (comparison, work): rule-following versus interpretive scoring
- Scenario 5 (reversal, personal): ask whether this is a fact question or judgment question
- Scenario 6 (checklist, school): observer prompt for judgment zones

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, specify one sensory or situational detail to include.
- a rating scale with empty labels
- a family text thread with one disputed phrase
- a folder margin note
- a rubric that still needs interpretation

Preview Constraint:
The preview must point into measuring error once the judgment zone has been defined.

Genericity Risks:
- flattening all decisions into one category
- generic talk about opinions
- forgetting the gray zone between rules and interpretation

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: everything is subjective
- filler move 2: rules solve all judgment problems
- invented authority move 1: unsupported taxonomy of decision types
- brief-language leak 1: threshold question wording in reader-facing prose
