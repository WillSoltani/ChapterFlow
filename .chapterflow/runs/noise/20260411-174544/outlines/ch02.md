Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should feel why sentencing variability is a justice problem rather than a mere technical annoyance.

Opening Move:
Open with two comparable defendants drawing meaningfully different punishment because different judges, or different judicial moments, tilt severity.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 comparable crimes / different judges
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3 same judge, different moments
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4 legitimacy and fairness consequences

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: anchor
- P2: mechanism
- P3: tension
- P4: implication
- P5: limit
- P6: bridge

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Separate stable harshness differences across judges from within-judge fluctuation across occasions.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must keep the justice boundary alive: how much discretion is necessary for humane sentencing, and how much variation turns that discretion into unequal treatment?

Threshold Question:
When courts call sentencing differences discretion, how much of that difference is really deserved by the case?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: sentencing makes noise morally visible
- Takeaway 2: between-judge severity
- Takeaway 3: within-judge fluctuation
- Takeaway 4: legitimacy cost
- Takeaway 5: discretion boundary

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): disciplinary panel sees same case differently
- Scenario 2 (conflict, personal): siblings notice unequal punishment
- Scenario 3 (debrief, school): conduct board variance
- Scenario 4 (comparison, work): two supervisors sanction same conduct differently
- Scenario 5 (reversal, personal): test whether a difference is earned
- Scenario 6 (checklist, school): observer questions for fairness review

Scenario Vividness Targets:
For at least 4 of 6 scenarios, specify one sensory or situational detail to include.
- courthouse clock or bench order
- fluorescent hearing room
- conduct-board folder packets
- supervisor notes marked in different colors

Preview Constraint:
The preview must widen from punishment to the idea of a noisy system.

Genericity Risks:
- flattening into generic fairness talk
- turning the chapter into a policy manifesto
- overstating unsupported data

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: anti-judge sermon
- filler move 2: abstract justice rhetoric with no mechanism
- invented authority move 1: unsupported statistics or studies
- brief-language leak 1: threshold question wording in reader-facing prose
