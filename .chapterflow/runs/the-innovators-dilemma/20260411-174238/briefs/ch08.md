Core Claim:
After structural fit is solved, managers still face a discovery problem. New and emerging markets cannot always be specified in advance with mature certainty. They must often be learned through contact with real users, experiments, and iterative discovery rather than by demanding complete forecasts before action.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about market discovery under uncertainty. Its identity is the claim that emerging markets are found and shaped through learning, not fully known before entry.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author extends Chapter 7 by noting that a right-sized unit still does not automatically know the market.
Then the author argues that emerging markets are often unclear, underdefined, and resistant to advance prediction.
Then the author reframes management as discovery-driven learning through real-market contact rather than forecast-first control.

Required Anchors:
- Emerging markets are not fully knowable in advance; the friction is uncertainty, the consequence is weak forecast quality.
- Real learning comes from contact with actual use and customers; the friction is ambiguity and iteration, the consequence is better market discovery.
- Managers should not demand complete certainty before entry into an emerging market; the friction is discomfort with incomplete knowledge, the consequence is discovery delay.
