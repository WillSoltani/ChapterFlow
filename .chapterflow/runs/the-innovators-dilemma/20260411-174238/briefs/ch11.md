Core Claim:
The book's argument resolves into recurring dilemmas rather than a single easy rule. Strong firms fail not because they are irrational, but because rational management practices, customer dependence, value-network fit, organizational structure, market size logic, capability limits, and premature strategic certainty can all work against disruptive response when combined.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is a synthesis. Its identity is not a new mechanism but the gathering of earlier mechanisms into one coherent pattern of innovation dilemmas.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author revisits the paradox that strong firms can fail while doing many sensible things.
Then the author ties that paradox to the book's recurring constraints: value networks, customer dependence, organizational fit, small markets, capabilities, and strategy development under uncertainty.
Then the author closes by showing that the management challenge is not solved by a slogan but by understanding the tensions together.

Required Anchors:
- Great firms can fail while acting rationally inside existing value networks; the friction is incumbent logic, the consequence is systematic blindness to disruption.
- Managerial responses must fit disruptive conditions through organizational design, market discovery, capability appraisal, and strategy development; the friction is applying old-business logic everywhere, the consequence is repeated mismatch.
- The book ends in dilemmas rather than a magic formula; the friction is desire for a single rule, the consequence is oversimplified reading.
