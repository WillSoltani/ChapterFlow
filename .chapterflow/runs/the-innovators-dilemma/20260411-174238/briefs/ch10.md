Core Claim:
Once managers understand market discovery and organizational fit, they still need a process for forming strategy under those realities. Strategy in disruptive settings cannot be treated as a finished top-down plan alone. It must develop through a process that works with learning, emergence, and the organization's actual capabilities and limits.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about strategy formation as a managed process. Its identity is the move from "what should fit" to "how strategy should actually develop under uncertainty and constraint."

Author Logic Chain:
First the author extends Chapter 9 by asking how strategy should be formed given the organization's real strengths and limits.
Then the author argues that strategy in disruptive settings cannot rely only on fixed advance design.
Then the author reframes management as shaping a strategy process that can absorb learning rather than pretending the final answer is known at the start.

Required Anchors:
- Strategy develops under uncertainty and organizational constraint; the friction is incomplete knowledge, the consequence is weak finished-plan certainty.
- Learning and emergence matter to strategy formation; the friction is managerial desire for control, the consequence is process mismatch if strategy is treated as fully fixed.
- Managers should shape the process by which strategy emerges, not only announce a final strategy; the friction is top-down overconfidence, the consequence is brittle response.
