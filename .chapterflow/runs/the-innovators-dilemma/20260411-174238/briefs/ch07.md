Core Claim:
Even if a disruptive effort sits in the right organizational home, it can still fail if the organization is too large for the market to matter. A small emerging market may be strategically important but economically trivial to a large unit, while a smaller organization can treat the same market as meaningful enough to prioritize.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about size fit, not customer-fit placement alone. Its identity is the argument that market significance is relative to organizational scale.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author extends Chapter 6 by asking whether the right home is also the right size.
Then the author shows that small markets can be invisible to large organizations even when they are real.
Then the author argues that a smaller unit can care about and grow inside a market too small to move a larger unit.

Required Anchors:
- Emerging markets may be too small to matter inside a large organization; the friction is growth threshold, the consequence is neglect despite real opportunity.
- Market importance is relative to organizational scale; the friction is mismatch between unit size and market size, the consequence is rational dismissal.
- Smaller organizations can live on opportunities that larger organizations treat as immaterial; the friction is scale fit, the consequence is a viable path for disruptive growth.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:

Specific Applications:

- deciding whether a smaller team should own an emerging opportunity
- separating strategic importance from immediate revenue impact on the incumbent unit
