Core Claim:
Once a market is being discovered, managers still need to judge whether their organization is actually built to serve it well. Organizational strengths are not universally portable. The same routines, habits, and priorities that make a company strong in one setting can become disabilities in another.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is about appraising organizational fit. Its identity is the reversal that capabilities in one context can become disabilities in a different market or operating challenge.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author extends Chapter 8 by asking what the organization itself brings into the emerging market.
Then the author argues that capabilities are context-dependent rather than universally good.
Then the author reframes appraisal as judging whether routines and strengths fit the new task rather than praising strengths in the abstract.

Required Anchors:
- Organizational strengths are context-bound; the friction is transfer across settings, the consequence is misfit.
- Routines and priorities can become disabilities in a new market; the friction is habit strength, the consequence is inability to respond well.
- Managers need self-appraisal, not generic confidence in existing excellence; the friction is overgeneralizing strength, the consequence is strategic error.
