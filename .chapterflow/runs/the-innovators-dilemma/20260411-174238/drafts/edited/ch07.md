A disruptive effort can still stall even after it reaches the right organizational home. Chapter 7 argues that the home must also be the right size for the market. An emerging market may be real, learnable, and strategically important while still being too small to matter inside a large organization with much bigger growth expectations.

That is the chapter's central correction. Market importance is not absolute. It is relative to the size of the organization judging it. A large unit can look at a small market and see something too minor to affect its revenue, prestige, or planning cycle. A smaller organization can look at the same market and see a meaningful chance to grow.

This is why rational dismissal can persist even after placement improves. The large unit is not necessarily wrong that the market is small. The mistake is treating "too small for us" as if it means "too small to matter at all." That collapses relative scale into universal irrelevance.

The scale mismatch affects behavior directly. If a market cannot move the numbers of a large organization, it struggles to win attention, talent, and sustained patience. The opportunity can remain real while the unit keeps pushing it aside for larger contests. In that setting, neglect does not need hostility. The market simply cannot clear the unit's growth threshold.

The chapter therefore favors aligning the size of the organization with the size of the market. A smaller unit can take a modest market seriously because success there matters to it. The point is not that small is always better. The point is that fit matters. The same market can be immaterial to one group and significant to another.

That limit is important. Chapter 7 is not saying that every small market deserves pursuit or that a right-sized unit solves all later uncertainty. A market can be small and still be weak. The narrower argument is that managers should not reject an opportunity only because it is too small for the current organization.

This naturally opens the next question. Once an organization is the right size for the market, managers still have to discover what the emerging market actually is and how it should be developed. Chapter 7 solves the scale problem. Chapter 8 turns to the discovery problem.
