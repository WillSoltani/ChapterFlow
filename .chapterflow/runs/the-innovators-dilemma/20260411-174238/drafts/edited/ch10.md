Strategy does not become simple once managers understand markets and organizational fit. Chapter 10 argues that they still need to manage how strategy develops. In disruptive settings, strategy cannot be treated as a fully finished answer handed down before learning begins. It has to form through a process that takes discovery, capability limits, and new evidence seriously.

That is the chapter's first correction. The issue is not strategy versus no strategy. It is whether leaders confuse strategic direction with a perfectly settled plan. In uncertain environments, a rigid top-down answer can freeze the organization before it has absorbed what the market is teaching.

This is why learning and emergence matter. New information arrives from market contact, from the organization's own limits, and from the way early choices perform under pressure. If strategy is locked too early, those signals arrive too late to shape anything important. The plan becomes a shield against reality instead of a guide for action.

The chapter therefore reframes responsibility. Managers still have to guide, choose, and set boundaries. But their task includes shaping a process by which strategy can develop under real conditions. Strategy remains managed, yet its final form may emerge through learning rather than from complete initial design.

That is what makes the chapter more demanding than a simple call for flexibility. It does not ask leaders to drift. It asks them to manage the path by which insight becomes strategy. A strategy development process has to be disciplined enough to guide action and open enough to absorb what reality reveals.

The limit matters here too. Chapter 10 is not saying planning disappears or that every strategic change is wise. The point is narrower. In disruptive settings, the process by which strategy is formed matters as much as the initial strategic intent, because critical knowledge develops during action.

This brings Part 2 to its final edge. Once managers understand how strategy should develop under uncertainty, the book can step back and summarize the broader dilemmas of innovation. Chapter 10 clarifies the process. Chapter 11 gathers the pattern.
