The book ends by gathering its argument into a pattern of dilemmas. Great firms do not fail only because leaders are foolish or lazy. They often fail while doing things that make sense inside the value networks, customer relationships, cost expectations, and organizational habits that made them successful in the first place.

That is the paradox the summary keeps intact. Listening to current customers can be rational. Investing where profits are visible can be rational. Relying on proven routines can be rational. Yet those same choices can push a firm away from disruptive opportunities that begin in small, uncertain, or unfamiliar markets.

This is why the conclusion does not point to one villain. The book has shown several interacting pressures: value networks shape incentives, small markets look unattractive, autonomous structures may be needed, emerging markets have to be discovered, capabilities are context-bound, and strategy itself must develop through learning. None of these tensions works alone for long.

The summary therefore changes the question managers should ask. Instead of searching for one error, they should ask which dilemma is operating and how multiple constraints may be reinforcing one another. A response that fixes only one problem can still fail if the rest of the pattern remains untouched.

That makes the book more demanding than a slogan about disruption. It is not anti-customer, anti-planning, or anti-success. It argues that good management can become misaligned when the environment changes and when the logic of the existing business is applied too broadly.

The limit matters in the ending. The book does not close with a magic formula that guarantees success. It closes with a clearer map of recurring tensions. Managers still have to judge fit, structure, market size, capabilities, and strategy process under real conditions.

That is why the final chapter works as a summary rather than a new departure. It names the pattern: innovation creates dilemmas in which sensible incumbent behavior can collide with disruptive adaptation. The lesson is harder than blame and thinner than certainty, but it is the book's real conclusion.
