Chapter Promise:
Show how mirroring turns listening into a practical negotiation tool by creating safety, buying time, and getting the other side to say more than they planned to say.

Opening Move:
Open on the awkward but powerful moment when someone repeats a few of your words back and you unexpectedly keep talking.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): 1-2 -> mirroring as an active listening tool, not passive politeness
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): 2-4 -> repeating the last one to three words or critical words
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): 4-6 -> pause, tone, and disclosure effect

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: picture why direct questions can trigger defense while a mirror invites expansion
- P2: define the mirror concretely
- P3: explain why silence after the mirror matters
- P4: show why tone matters and why aggression breaks the move
- P5: transfer the method to work, school, and personal negotiations
- P6: preserve the limit that mechanical mirroring can feel fake and bridge to labeling

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Mirroring is not repetition for its own sake. It is a low-pressure way to keep the other side talking until the hidden issue surfaces.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Mirroring works when it carries real curiosity. Used mechanically, it can make the other side feel managed rather than heard.

Threshold Question:
When does a mirror make someone feel invited to continue, and when does it make them feel like they are being worked on?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: mirroring is active listening, not passive politeness
- Takeaway 2: short repetition creates disclosure without overt pressure
- Takeaway 3: silence and tone are part of the skill, not extra garnish
- Takeaway 4: the move transfers across domains because it preserves autonomy
- Takeaway 5: overused or robotic mirroring loses trust

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): account manager deciding whether to mirror a vague objection
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): committee meeting failed because the speaker kept interrupting instead of reflecting
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): partner conflict where one mirrored phrase changes the whole tone
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): predicting what happens after a calm mirror and a pause
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): admissions interview where silence feels risky but useful
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): comparing direct rebuttal with a simple mirror

Preview Constraint:
The preview must make the reader expect a deeper move that reflects emotion, not just words.
