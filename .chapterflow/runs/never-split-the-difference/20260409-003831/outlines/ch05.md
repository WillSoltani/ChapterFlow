Chapter Promise:
Show why "that's right" matters more than "yes," and why deep summary can unlock movement only after the other side feels accurately represented.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between hearing agreement and hearing the other person say, with relief, that you finally captured what they meant.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show why "yes" can still be shallow at this stage
- P2: define the "that's right" moment
- P3: tie the moment to summary and recognition
- P4: explain why accurate perspective-taking unlocks movement
- P5: transfer the move across work, school, and personal negotiations
- P6: preserve the limit that scripted summary does not create real alignment

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
"That's right" signals felt understanding, not mere compliance. It comes after the other side hears their view represented accurately enough to stop correcting you.

Threshold Question:
When does "that's right" show real alignment, and when is it only polite approval of a summary that still missed the deeper concern?
