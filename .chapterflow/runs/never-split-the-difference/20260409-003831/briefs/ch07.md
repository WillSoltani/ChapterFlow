Core Claim:
Chapter 7 argues that calibrated questions let the negotiator guide the conversation while preserving the other side's feeling of agency, which lowers resistance and produces better problem-solving.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the calibrated-questions chapter. It must focus on how and what questions as steering tools, not generic open-ended questioning.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter shows why direct demands trigger resistance.
Then it introduces calibrated questions as a lower-pressure alternative.
Then it explains how the questions make the other side do the problem-solving work.
Then it warns that fake collaboration or hostile tone will break the effect.

Required Anchors:
- source-heading support that this chapter centers on calibrated questions, collaboration, how questions, and defusing conflict
- continuity from Chapter 6: perception can be guided without taking away agency
- continuity from earlier chapters: trust, recognition, and safety remain the base under any steering move

Counterarguments or Limits:
The illusion of control is not about deception for its own sake. It works only when the questions genuinely invite thought instead of functioning as disguised commands or traps.

Hard-Depth Minimum:
When do calibrated questions create useful agency and problem-solving, and when do they become manipulative theater that makes the other side feel steered anyway?
