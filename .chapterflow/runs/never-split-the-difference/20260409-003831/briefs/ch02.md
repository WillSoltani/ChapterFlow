Core Claim:
Chapter 2 turns the opening chapter's empathy principle into a concrete listening move: mirroring. By selectively repeating the other side's last words or critical words and then letting silence work, a negotiator can create rapport, lower pressure, and draw out information that direct argument would never surface.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the first operational technique chapter. It is not the general reset of Chapter 1 and not yet the deeper emotion-labeling chapter. It has to make mirroring feel precise, active, and strategically useful rather than passive or manipulative.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter reframes listening as an active leverage tool.
Then it introduces mirroring as repeating the last one to three words or critical words.
Then it shows why silence and tone matter after the mirror.
Then it argues that mirroring creates safety and disclosure rather than direct pressure.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- mirroring: repeating the last one to three words or a critical phrase
- effective pause: silence that invites the other side to fill the gap
- voice control: calm tone that reduces threat rather than increases it
- discovery through reflection: getting the other side to expand without interrogation

Specific Applications:
- client negotiation where a vague objection needs expansion
- salary conversation where a manager gives a short resistance phrase
- group-project or committee conflict where direct argument is escalating
- family disagreement where the other person feels interrupted or cornered

Supported Concrete Details:

Common Misreadings:
- treating mirroring as parroting with no curiosity
- using a mirror so often that it feels robotic
- thinking the point is to sound clever rather than to create disclosure

Counterarguments or Limits:
Mirroring is not mind control. If used mechanically or too often, it can feel artificial. The chapter should preserve the boundary that the skill works because it supports real listening and better timing, not because repetition alone forces trust.
