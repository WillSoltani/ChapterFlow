Core Claim:
Chapter 5 argues that the most valuable confirmation in negotiation is not "yes" but "that's right," because it signals the other side feels accurately understood and is therefore more ready to move.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the `that's right` chapter. It must separate true alignment from shallow agreement and show that accurate summary can unlock cooperation without pressure.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter distinguishes "that's right" from fake or polite agreement.
Then it shows that the phrase emerges after the other side feels fully understood.
Then it ties that moment to summary and perspective-taking.
Then it warns that forced summary or imitation alignment will fail.

Required Anchors:
- source-heading support that this chapter centers on "that's right," summarizing, alignment, and permission to persuade
- continuity from Chapter 4: truth and safety matter more than quick compliance
- continuity from Chapter 3: recognition must be accurate enough for the other side to hear themselves in your summary

Counterarguments or Limits:
"That's right" is not a magic phrase by itself. It matters only when it reflects genuine understanding rather than a scripted recap that pressures the other side to approve your framing.

Hard-Depth Minimum:
When does "that's right" signal real alignment, and when is it just verbal approval of a summary that never captured the person's actual perspective?
