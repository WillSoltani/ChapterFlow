Core Claim:
Chapter 3 sharpens tactical empathy by separating accurate emotional labeling from emotional absorption. The chapter argues that negotiators create trust when they name the other side's feelings clearly without getting pulled into those feelings themselves.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the labeling chapter, not the mirroring chapter and not the `no` chapter. It must show that naming emotion can lower defensiveness while preserving strategic distance.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter distinguishes feeling another person's pain from identifying it accurately.
Then it introduces labeling as a practical sentence-level move.
Then it argues that labels reduce fear and build trust.
Then it warns that empathy without distance can blur judgment.

Counterarguments or Limits:
Bad labels can feel fake, presumptuous, or manipulative. The chapter should preserve the limit that naming emotion is useful only when it is grounded in attentive listening and willing correction.
