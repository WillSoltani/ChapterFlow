Core Claim:
Chapter 4 argues that chasing yes too early creates false momentum, while a well-framed no can make the other side feel safe enough to speak honestly and retain agency.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the `no` chapter. It must separate meaningful movement from empty agreement and show why safety often arrives through preserved autonomy.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter challenges the usual belief that yes is the best immediate goal.
Then it argues that yes can be shallow, polite, or defensive.
Then it shows why no creates safety and honesty.
Then it reframes no-oriented questions as a way to move without trapping.

Required Anchors:
- official Black Swan support that mastering no matters because saying yes can be a trap and no can create safety
- official support that no-oriented questions give the counterpart a sense of control
- continuity from Chapter 3: safety and truth matter more than verbal compliance

Counterarguments or Limits:
No is not valuable because rejection is inherently good. It is valuable when it preserves autonomy and produces truth. The chapter should avoid romanticizing refusal for its own sake.

Hard-Depth Minimum:
When does a no open honest negotiation, and when does it become a dead stop because the relationship or framing never created enough trust to keep going?
