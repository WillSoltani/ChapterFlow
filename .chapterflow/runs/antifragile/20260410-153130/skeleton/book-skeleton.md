## Chapter order and one-line intents
1. **Between Damocles and Hydra** — defines the triad of fragile, robust, and antifragile, arguing that resilience is not the true opposite of fragility.
2. **Overcompensation and Overreaction Everywhere** — shows how redundancy, overreaction, and even suppression can create upside for antifragile systems.
3. **The Cat and the Washing Machine** — separates living complex systems from mechanical ones and argues that the right stressors are information.
4. **What Kills Me Makes Others Stronger** — introduces hormesis and the idea that small doses of stress can strengthen complex systems.
5. **The Souk and the Office Building** — contrasts decentralized, adaptive small-scale life with brittle top-down modern structures.
6. **Tell Them I Love (Some) Randomness** — argues that moderate volatility and variation help adaptation when downside is clipped.
7. **Naive Intervention** — attacks interventions that remove visible pain while compounding hidden fragility.
8. **Prediction as a Child of Modernity** — criticizes the prestige of prediction in domains that punish forecast arrogance.
9. **Fat Tony and the Fragilistas** — pits street-level payoff realism against polished but fragile intellectual abstraction.
10. **Seneca's Upside and Downside** — frames asymmetry as the key to handling uncertainty rather than average-case comfort.
11. **Never Marry the Rock Star** — argues for survival-first barbell logic that clips ruin while preserving upside.
12. **Thales' Sweet Grapes** — introduces optionality as freedom to benefit from favorable outcomes without needing precise prediction.
13. **Lecturing Birds on How to Fly** — claims practice and tinkering often produce theory, not the reverse.
14. **When Two Things Are Not the 'Same Thing'** — warns against confusing narrative polish with real capability or optionality.
15. **History Written by the Losers** — argues that formal retrospective storytelling often misses what practitioners actually built.
16. **A Lesson in Disorder** — treats trial, mess, and self-discovery as the right kind of rigor in uncertain life.
17. **Fat Tony Debates Socrates** — privileges exposure and payoff over elegant verbal certainty.
18. **On the Difference Between a Large Stone and a Thousand Pebbles** — explains fragility and antifragility through nonlinearity and uneven response to shocks.
19. **The Philosopher's Stone and Its Inverse** — pushes convexity and nonlinear payoff logic into more technical territory.
20. **Time and Fragility** — argues that time-tested things deserve respect because survival itself is evidence.
21. **Medicine, Convexity, and Opacity** — examines medicine as a high-opacity domain where intervention can create hidden harm.
22. **To Live Long, but Not Too Long** — applies subtraction and iatrogenics logic to longevity, health, and over-treatment.
23. **Skin in the Game: Antifragility and Optionality at the Expense of Others** — makes accountability and downside-bearing the ethical core of antifragility.
24. **Fitting Ethics to a Profession** — argues that professions need ethics shaped by exposure to real downside, not abstract talk alone.
25. **Conclusion** — closes by returning to volatility, convexity, and the question of what truly benefits from variation.
