# Chapter 20: Time and Fragility

Taleb opens Via Negativa by treating time as a discriminator rather than as a passive background. Time keeps applying exposure. It reveals hidden weakness, punishes what only looks stable in the short run, and gives informational weight to what continues to survive repeated contact with reality. In that sense, endurance is evidence.

That is why survival matters. Something that has lasted through repeated stress, use, or scrutiny has already faced many opportunities to fail. This does not make it sacred, but it does make its continued existence informative. By contrast, the new and impressive may simply be untested. Time has not had enough chances yet to reveal its brittleness.

The chapter therefore reframes durability. What survives is not automatically best because it is old. It matters because long exposure has kept pressuring it. If the thing remains standing after repeated contact, that survival tells us something about its resistance to hidden fragility. Time works here as a filter, separating temporary appearance from deeper robustness.

The practical application travels widely. A tool, institution, method, or habit should not be judged only by how appealing it looks now. Repetition, wear, stress, and continued use matter. A flashy option may fail when those forces accumulate. A quieter option may earn trust because it has already endured them. Taleb wants the reader to treat duration under pressure as information, not as decoration.

The chapter's limit is crucial. Taleb is not saying age by itself guarantees quality, and he is not turning waiting into a virtue on its own. Survival becomes evidence only when real exposure has kept testing the thing that survives. Mere oldness without pressure is weak evidence. Continued endurance under contact is stronger evidence.

That bridge matters because the next chapter turns toward medicine, convexity, and opacity. Once time is understood as a filter for hidden fragility, intervention must be judged more carefully in systems where what survives and what breaks are not easy to see in advance.
