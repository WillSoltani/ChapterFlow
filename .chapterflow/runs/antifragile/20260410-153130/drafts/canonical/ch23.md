# Canonical Draft — ch23

Ethics often gets discussed as if it were mainly about words, intentions, and stated principles. Taleb pushes in a harsher direction. Ethics becomes real when the person making a decision shares in the downside of that decision. If someone can keep the upside while exporting the cost to others, the moral language around the choice starts to look cosmetic.

That is the force of skin in the game. A system becomes ethically distorted when optionality is one-sided. The decision-maker enjoys flexibility, reputation, or gain, while someone else absorbs the losses if things go badly. Taleb's point is not simply that this is unfair. It is that insulation from downside corrupts judgment because it disconnects action from consequence.

This is why exposure matters so much. When a person bears the cost of their own decisions, their incentives, caution, and seriousness change. They are less free to gamble with hidden tails on behalf of others. Skin in the game is therefore not just a personal virtue. It is an alignment mechanism that keeps systems from becoming morally and practically reckless at the same time.

The chapter also clarifies what the real problem is. Optionality is powerful and often productive, but it becomes ethically troubling when one party keeps the upside while another party involuntarily carries the downside. The issue is asymmetry. A decision structure that privatizes gain and socializes harm invites fragility because the chooser is rewarded without being properly disciplined by loss.

The limit matters here too. Taleb is not arguing that everyone must bear identical risks in every situation. Nor is he turning ethics into revenge. He is insisting on a narrower and deeper standard: those who introduce downside should not be protected from it by the very system they are shaping.

That prepares the next chapter. Once skin in the game becomes the ethical core, the natural question is what a profession should look like if its norms are built around exposure, consequence, and lived accountability rather than abstract code alone.
