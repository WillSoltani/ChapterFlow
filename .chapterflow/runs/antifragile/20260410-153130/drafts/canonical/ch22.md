# Canonical Draft — ch22

Long life is easy to romanticize as a triumph of more. More tracking, more interventions, more corrections, more attempts to force the body into a cleaner and safer future. Taleb pushes against that instinct. In an opaque system, the danger is often not only what happens by itself, but what gets added in the name of improvement. Longevity can therefore depend less on constant enhancement than on subtraction.

That is the via negativa logic of the chapter. If a system is partly hidden, additions can create harms that are difficult to trace. A person may see one visible benefit and miss the broader cost of overmanagement, excess, and fragile-making intervention. The deeper lesson is not that all care is misguided. It is that not making things worse can be a more reliable principle than chasing every promising fix.

This changes how prudence looks. Prudence is no longer identical with doing more. It may instead mean removing obvious stressors, declining needless complexity, and resisting the urge to optimize every margin of life. When the body and its environment are only partly understood, subtraction can be wiser than accumulation because it avoids opening new channels of harm.

The chapter therefore treats harm avoidance as more than caution. It is a positive strategy. Eliminating what predictably adds fragility may matter more than layering on sophisticated interventions whose second-order effects remain obscure. Long life is framed less as engineering victory than as disciplined refusal to worsen the system.

The limit matters. Taleb is not offering a health-hack slogan, nor is he saying that all proactive care is pointless. The point is narrower and harder: in opaque domains, complicated additions deserve suspicion when simple removal of harm is available. The alternative to overengineering is not passivity. It is a better hierarchy of action.

That hierarchy also prepares the ethical move to come. Once subtraction matters because interventions can export hidden harm, the next question is who bears the downside of action. Longevity through restraint becomes a bridge into skin in the game.
