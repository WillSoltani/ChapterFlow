# Canonical Draft — ch24

A profession can surround itself with noble words and still remain ethically hollow. Taleb's point is that ethics becomes credible only when it is fitted to the real structure of a role: what the professional can decide, what risks they can impose, and whether they share the downside of being wrong. A code without exposure is too easy to recite and too easy to ignore.

This is why skin in the game has to move from general ethics into professional ethics. A profession earns trust not simply by declaring values but by binding practice to consequence. If an expert can advise, authorize, or intervene while remaining insulated from the losses created by that role, the profession's moral language becomes decorative. It may sound elevated while doing little to discipline conduct.

The chapter therefore treats fitted ethics as local rather than abstract. Different professions face different forms of risk, authority, and exposure, so the relevant ethical structure must match the actual downside channels of the role. What matters is not just whether a rule exists on paper. What matters is whether the professional world ties behavior to consequences that make the rule real.

That is where trust comes from. People do not rely deeply on professionals because a handbook uses respectable language. They trust when they sense that the role itself is constrained by consequence-bearing, reciprocity, and real accountability. Exposure makes promises expensive, and that expense is part of what gives ethics force.

The limit is important. Taleb is not saying that every written code is useless or that institutions cannot articulate norms. He is saying that written principles are weak when they float above a role with no mechanism of exposure. Ethics without consequence can become performance rather than discipline.

This prepares the conclusion. Once fragility, optionality, time, subtraction, and skin in the game have all been linked, the final task is to gather them back into one larger argument about what survives, what breaks, and what benefits from variation.
