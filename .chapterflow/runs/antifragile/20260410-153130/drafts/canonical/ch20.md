# Chapter 20: Time and Fragility

Taleb now turns to time as something more than a background condition. Time is a testing force. It keeps exposing hidden weakness, forcing repeated contact with reality, and revealing what only looked stable in a short window. Survival through time is therefore not empty duration. It is information.

This is why endurance matters. A thing that has survived long exposure has already passed through many chances to fail. That does not make it perfect, but it does make it less arbitrary than something new that has not yet been tested. Time keeps asking the same silent question: does this still hold up when reality keeps touching it?

The chapter therefore treats time as a filter rather than a neutral container. What is fragile may sparkle early, impress quickly, and still break once repeated exposure accumulates. What is durable may look less exciting at first and prove itself by continuing to survive. Time separates the temporarily flattering from the genuinely resistant.

This matters across tools, institutions, habits, and choices. A new method may look superior before stress, repetition, and wear reveal where it is brittle. An old practice may deserve attention not because age is magical but because prolonged survival suggests it has already faced many tests. Endurance is evidence when the environment has kept applying pressure.

Taleb is not saying old is always better or that waiting automatically produces wisdom. The limit is narrower. Time provides evidence when it repeatedly tests something under real exposure. Mere age without testing does not prove quality. But survival under ongoing pressure tells us more than short-run shine does.

This sets up the next chapter. If time reveals fragility by forcing exposure, then medicine, intervention, and opacity must be judged with that discipline in mind. The question becomes how to act when hidden fragility, convexity, and opaque systems interact.
