Core Claim:
This chapter should argue that in many complex domains, doing comes before explaining. Taleb uses the image of lecturing birds on flying to mock the idea that formal theory is always the origin of effective practice. The reader should leave seeing experimentation, craft, and iterative contact with reality as sources of knowledge rather than as lesser versions of clean abstraction.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the practice-before-theory chapter. It must stay on discovery through action and adaptation rather than drifting into anti-intellectual slogans.

Author Logic Chain:
First Taleb attacks the prestige assumption that theory always comes first. Then he shows how practice often generates workable knowledge before explanation catches up. Then he distinguishes reality-tested craft from empty hostility to thought.
