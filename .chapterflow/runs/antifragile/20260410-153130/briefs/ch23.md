Core Claim:
This chapter should argue that antifragility has an ethical demand: people who impose risk, intervention, or optionality on others should share in the downside they create. Taleb is moving from practical subtraction into responsibility, reciprocity, and accountability under asymmetry. The reader should leave seeing skin in the game as the moral answer to hidden downside exported onto others.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the accountability chapter. It must stay on downside-bearing, exposure, and moral asymmetry rather than drifting into generic fairness talk.

Author Logic Chain:
First Taleb extends the subtractive and anti-intervention logic into ethics. Then he argues that systems become more dangerous when decision-makers can offload downside while keeping upside. Then he frames skin in the game as the basic check that aligns action with consequence.
