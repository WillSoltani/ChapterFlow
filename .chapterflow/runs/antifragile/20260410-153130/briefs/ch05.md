Core Claim:
This chapter should argue that decentralized systems often adapt better because local trial-and-error distributes error, while centralized systems can hide fragility behind polished order. Taleb uses the souk and the office building as the memorable contrast. The reader should come away seeing that messiness can be a sign of adaptive learning and that tidiness can conceal concentration risk.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the decentralization chapter. It is the first major institutional application of antifragility and must stay concrete rather than collapsing into generic anti-bureaucracy rhetoric.

Author Logic Chain:
First Taleb contrasts souk-like decentralized adjustment with centralized design. Then he argues that local knowledge and distributed mistakes create adaptive strength. Then he shows how central neatness can hide larger shared fragility.
