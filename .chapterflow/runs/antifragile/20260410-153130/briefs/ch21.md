Core Claim:
This chapter should argue that medicine and other opaque intervention domains cannot be judged safely by intention or short-run benefit alone. Taleb is pushing the reader to see that convexity, hidden downside, and uncertainty make intervention risky when mechanisms are not clearly visible. The reader should leave understanding that doing less can sometimes dominate doing more when opacity hides the downside.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the medicine-and-opacity chapter. It must stay on opaque intervention, convexity, hidden harm, and asymmetry rather than drifting into generic anti-medicine rhetoric.

Author Logic Chain:
First Taleb applies time-and-fragility logic to intervention under opacity. Then he shows how convexity and hidden downside alter what counts as prudent treatment or action. Then he warns that acting in systems we do not clearly understand can create more harm than restraint would.
