Core Claim:
This chapter should argue that living systems and mechanical systems respond to stress differently because living systems can use stressors as information while machines mainly accumulate wear. Taleb uses the cat and the washing machine as the memorable contrast. The reader should come away seeing that the same treatment can strengthen one kind of system and degrade another because the underlying structure is different.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the chapter that prevents the earlier discussion of reserve and rebound from being applied too loosely. It introduces the difference between information-rich adaptation and mechanical breakdown.

Author Logic Chain:
First Taleb contrasts organism and machine. Then he shows why stressors can teach or regulate living systems. Then he concludes that interventions copied from mechanical thinking can damage living processes.
