Core Claim:
This chapter should argue that some systems respond to shocks not with simple recovery but with adaptive overcompensation. Taleb treats redundancy as a live source of resilience-plus rather than obvious waste, because spare capacity allows systems to recover and strengthen after stress. He also extends the idea to information: attempts to suppress, ban, or attack a message can increase its reach instead of weakening it. The chapter should leave the reader seeing that antifragility can show up in reaction patterns, not just in definitions.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 1 names the triad. Chapter 2 has to prove the category behaves in the world. The distinct move here is that what looks excessive, redundant, or even wasteful may be the very thing that allows overreaction to become adaptive rather than destructive.

Author Logic Chain:
First Taleb argues that strong responses to setbacks can create innovation or improvement. Then he reframes redundancy as hidden strength instead of obvious inefficiency. Then he shows that information itself can be antifragile because attacks on it may increase attention and spread.
