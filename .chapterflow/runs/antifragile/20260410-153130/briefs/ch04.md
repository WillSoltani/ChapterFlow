Core Claim:
This chapter should argue that small, bounded doses of harm can strengthen some systems, while large doses destroy them. The key is not suffering as a virtue but nonlinearity of response. Taleb uses this to show why dose matters more than abstract labels about good or bad stress. The reader should leave with a sharper sense that strengthening often happens within a narrow band that modern overprotection or overexposure can both miss.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 3 distinguishes organisms from machines. Chapter 4 sharpens that distinction into a dose argument. This is the chapter where small harm becomes strengthening only under limits.

Author Logic Chain:
First Taleb shows that low-dose stress can strengthen. Then he emphasizes nonlinearity and dose. Then he warns that readers misuse the idea when they generalize it into pain worship.
