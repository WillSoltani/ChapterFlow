Core Claim:
This chapter should argue that some randomness is beneficial when downside is limited and upside remains available. Taleb is not praising chaos in the abstract. He is defending variation, experimentation, and uneven exposure under bounded risk. The reader should leave with a sharper sense that what matters is not randomness alone but where it lands and what the payoff shape looks like.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the selective-randomness chapter. It must stay bounded and payoff-aware rather than becoming a slogan about spontaneity.

Author Logic Chain:
First Taleb separates some randomness from indiscriminate chaos. Then he shows why bounded downside plus open upside can be useful. Then he warns against environments that suppress all variation and therefore suppress discovery.
