Core Claim:
This chapter should argue that professions need ethics shaped by real exposure, reciprocity, and consequence-bearing rather than by detached verbal codes alone. Taleb is applying skin in the game to professional life and asking what kind of norms survive when practitioners must share the downside of their decisions. The reader should leave understanding that ethics fitted to a profession must be practical, embodied, and tied to exposure.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the profession-ethics chapter. It must stay on how ethics is fitted to actual roles, incentives, and downside-bearing rather than drifting into generic corporate-values language.

Author Logic Chain:
First Taleb takes skin in the game from general ethics into professions. Then he argues that professional norms become hollow when practitioners can talk ethically without sharing consequence. Then he frames durable ethics as local, role-fitted, and disciplined by exposure.
