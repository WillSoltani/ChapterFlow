Chapter Promise:
The reader will understand why Kahneman splits the mind into System 1 and System 2, what each one does, and why the rest of the book depends on keeping that interaction visible.

Opening Move:
Open with a contrast between something the mind does instantly and something that feels effortful enough to announce itself.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the difference in feel between automatic and effortful thought
- P2: introduce System 1 and System 2 as explanatory characters
- P3: explain how System 1 supplies impressions, impulses, and rapid answers
- P4: explain how System 2 checks, endorses, or misses those suggestions
- P5: show the practical risk of trusting the first answer too easily
- P6: bridge into attention as the resource that makes System 2 available at all

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
System 1 generates; System 2 monitors and can intervene.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The model is useful only if the reader keeps its limit visible: System 2 is real enough to matter but too limited to rescue every judgment.

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: what System 1 does
- Takeaway 2: what System 2 does
- Takeaway 3: why the two-system split feels invisible from the inside
- Takeaway 4: where first answers become dangerous
- Takeaway 5: why effort is a scarce resource
- Takeaway 6: why the model is explanatory, not anatomical

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): teaches the difference between an immediate impression and a checked judgment
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, school): teaches how a first answer looked obvious until slower review exposed the gap
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): teaches how the two systems appear in everyday conversation
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): teaches that effort changes what the mind notices
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): teaches when fast confidence outruns available evidence
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): teaches how naming the two systems changes self-monitoring

Genericity Risks:
- turning the chapter into generic dual-process psychology
- sounding as if System 2 is a heroic controller that can stay active indefinitely
- losing the felt difference between effortless and effortful thought

Forbidden Drift:
- lecture-tone abstractions
- unsupported neuroscience
- examples that could belong to any chapter in the book
