Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that wording and framing change decisions even when the underlying outcomes stay the same. Equivalent realities can feel different once they are described as gains or losses. The chapter matters because people often defend opposite choices even though the facts have not changed, only the presentation has. Its practical move is to restate the options in more than one frame before deciding that the preference is really about the outcomes.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Frames and Reality sits between Reversals and Two Selves. It is the chapter that makes description itself part of judgment rather than a neutral wrapper around it. Its distinctive value is that it shows how gain and loss language can shift preference without altering reality, revealing just how easily description steers choice.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that equivalent realities can produce different reactions when framed differently.
Then he shows that gain wording and loss wording change what feels acceptable, fair, or humane.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires rephrasing the same choice before trusting the first emotional verdict.
