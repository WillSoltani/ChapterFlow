Chapter Title: Bad Events

Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that bad events weigh more heavily than equally sized good events across attention, motivation, and choice. Losses command faster and stronger reactions than gains of similar size. The chapter matters because a negative turn can dominate memory, evaluation, and planning long after the raw magnitude should have been balanced against the positives around it. Its practical move is to compare magnitudes deliberately before allowing one negative event to define the whole situation.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Bad Events sits between The Endowment Effect and The Fourfold Pattern. It is the chapter that generalizes loss dominance beyond ownership and shows how negativity shapes attention itself. Its distinctive value is that it explains why one setback, criticism, or failure can crowd out a much larger field of ordinary success.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that losses and negative events seize attention faster than comparable gains.
Then he shows that this priority can distort memory, motivation, and judgment.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires restoring proportion before treating the bad event as the whole story.
