Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that when something violates expectation, the mind reacts fast. Surprise is not experienced as a bare interruption for very long. It quickly turns into a search for broken norms, hidden intention, and cause. The chapter matters because the first causal story that restores order can feel compelling before alternatives are tested. Its practical lesson is to distinguish the need for an explanation from evidence that the first explanation is right.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the chapter where smooth processing gives way to disruption. Chapter 5 explained why ease feels trustworthy. Chapter 6 explains what happens when that comfort breaks. Its distinctive value is that it shows how the mind rushes from violated expectation to cause, blame, and intention, often too quickly.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that surprise is tied to expectation and norm.
Then he explains that once a norm is broken, the mind races to restore order through a causal story.
Then he concludes that people need to inspect the first satisfying explanation before they let it harden into judgment.

Counterarguments or Limits:

- The problem is not the search for cause itself, but the speed with which a neat explanation acquires authority.
