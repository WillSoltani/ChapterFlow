Core Claim:
Kahneman argues that simple formulas often outperform expert judgment in stable prediction tasks. Consistent mechanical rules avoid noise and private mood swings. The chapter matters because human discretion adds unstable variation that feels insightful but often lowers accuracy. Its practical move is to use simple rules when the domain is regular and the predictive ingredients are known.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Intuitions vs. Formulas sits between The Illusion of Validity and Expert Intuition: When Can We Trust It?. It is the chapter that explains how simple formulas often outperform expert judgment in stable prediction tasks. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how consistent mechanical rules avoid the noise that enters when people improvise case by case.
Then he shows how that cue bends judgment before stronger checking arrives.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires a deliberate check on whether the confidence came from evidence or from the chapter mechanism.
