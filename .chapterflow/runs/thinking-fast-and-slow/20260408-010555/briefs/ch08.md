Chapter Title: How Judgments Happen

Core Claim:
Kahneman tracks how quick judgments borrow intensity, substitute dimensions, and convert vague questions into simpler impressions. The mind leans on accessible impressions when formal calculation is hard. The chapter matters because the answer often fits the easier impression rather than the original question. Its practical move is to state the exact question and ask whether the answer was built from a substitute dimension.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
How Judgments Happen sits between A Machine for Jumping to Conclusions and Answering an Easier Question. It is the chapter that explains how hard judgments are often converted into simpler impressions or intensity matches. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how the mind borrows accessible impressions when formal calculation is hard.
Then he shows how the judgment quietly shifts from the original question to a simpler surrogate.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires naming the real question before trusting the answer that came back quickly.
