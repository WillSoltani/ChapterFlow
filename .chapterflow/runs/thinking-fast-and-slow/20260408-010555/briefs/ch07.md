Chapter Title: A Machine for Jumping to Conclusions

Core Claim:
Kahneman describes the mind as a coherence engine that forms conclusions from thin slices of evidence. What is present gets treated as enough, even when important evidence is absent. The chapter matters because missing information stops exerting pressure once a coherent first answer has landed. Its practical move is to ask what relevant evidence is absent before trusting the quick conclusion.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
A Machine for Jumping to Conclusions sits between Norms, Surprises, and Causes and How Judgments Happen. It is the chapter that explains how thin evidence becomes a coherent conclusion before missing evidence gets a vote. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how the mind treats what is present as enough and builds a full story from a narrow slice.
Then he shows why the resulting confidence feels sensible even when key evidence never entered the scene.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires a deliberate check on what evidence is absent, not only on what evidence is vivid.
