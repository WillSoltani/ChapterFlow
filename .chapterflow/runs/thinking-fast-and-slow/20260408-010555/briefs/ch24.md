Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that optimism and overconfidence create energy for risk taking even while they also create waste and failure. The same bias that powers initiative can also distort realistic appraisal. The chapter matters because a socially productive bias gets mistaken for a private forecasting skill. Its practical move is to separate the motivational value of optimism from the accuracy demands of prediction.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The Engine of Capitalism sits between The Outside View and Bernoulli's Errors. It is the chapter that explains how optimism and overconfidence fuel initiative even while they also create waste and failure. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how the same bias that powers risk taking can distort how realistically people appraise the odds.
Then he shows how that cue bends judgment before stronger checking arrives.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires a deliberate check on whether the confidence came from evidence or from the chapter mechanism.
