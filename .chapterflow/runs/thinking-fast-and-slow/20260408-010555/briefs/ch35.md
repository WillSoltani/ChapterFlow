Chapter Title: Two Selves

Core Claim:
Kahneman introduces the experiencing self and the remembering self as partly different judges of life. Moment-by-moment experience and retrospective evaluation can pull in different directions. The chapter matters because people often speak as if one measure of happiness or suffering captures both selves at once. Its practical move is to ask which self is speaking before drawing a conclusion about a life, an episode, or a choice.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Two Selves sits between Frames and Reality and Life as a Story. It is the chapter that turns the book from errors in judgment about events into errors in judgment about experience itself. Its distinctive value is that it separates lived time from remembered evaluation and shows that both matter without collapsing into each other.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that experience in the moment and memory after the fact do not use the same criteria.
Then he shows that decisions about what makes life good are often made by the remembering self while the experiencing self bears the days.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires keeping the two selves distinct instead of pretending one measure speaks for both.
