Chapter Title: Experienced Well-Being

Core Claim:
Kahneman argues that well-being should be measured in lived time as well as in remembered judgment. Experience sampling exposes how different activities actually feel while they are happening. The chapter matters because retrospective judgment is often treated as the whole truth about well-being even though lived moments carry their own claim. Its practical move is to look at experienced time directly instead of relying only on summary memory.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Experienced Well-Being sits between Life as a Story and Thinking About Life. It is the chapter that pulls the argument back from remembered narrative to lived moments. Its distinctive value is that it treats well-being as something that can be measured across actual time rather than inferred entirely from memory or global satisfaction judgments.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that experience sampling can reveal what daily life actually feels like in the moment.
Then he shows that lived hours and remembered summaries do not always agree about what a good life contains.
Then he concludes that better judgment about well-being requires giving experienced time its own standing rather than letting memory speak for it entirely.
