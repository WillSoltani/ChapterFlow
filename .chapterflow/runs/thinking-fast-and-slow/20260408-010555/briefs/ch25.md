Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that classical utility theory misses a basic fact about judgment: people evaluate gains and losses relative to a reference point, not only by final wealth levels. Changes from the status quo often matter more in felt value than the absolute state that follows. The chapter matters because it explains why equal outcomes can feel radically different depending on what came before them. Its practical move is to ask what baseline is driving the reaction before trusting the reaction as a guide to value.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Bernoulli's Errors sits between The Engine of Capitalism and Prospect Theory. It is the transition chapter that breaks with the classical idea that final states alone explain value. Its distinctive value is that it introduces reference dependence as the missing piece, which then makes the later discussion of loss aversion and prospect theory legible instead of abrupt.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows why a theory centered on final wealth misses how people actually feel gains and losses.
Then he shows that the status quo or expected baseline quietly organizes the meaning of the outcome.
Then he concludes that better judgment starts by identifying the reference point before treating the reaction as evidence of objective value.
