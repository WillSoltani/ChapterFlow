Chapter Title: The Endowment Effect

Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that ownership changes value and makes people demand more to give something up than they would pay to acquire it. Ownership creates a reference point that makes loss aversion immediate and concrete. The chapter matters because it explains why possession itself can distort valuation, negotiation, and attachment. Its practical move is to ask what the item, feature, or resource would be worth if you did not already own it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The Endowment Effect sits between Prospect Theory and Bad Events. It is the chapter that brings loss aversion out of abstract choice and into ordinary possession. Its distinctive value is that it shows how simply having something can make surrender feel like a loss even when acquisition would never have justified the same price.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that ownership itself becomes a reference point.
Then he shows that giving something up feels like a loss while acquiring it feels like a smaller gain.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires separating genuine use value from the ownership premium.
