Core Claim:
Kahneman explains that associative memory links ideas rapidly and automatically. One cue can activate a larger network of related thoughts, feelings, and expectations before deliberate reflection catches up. Because of that, coherence produced by association can feel like evidence even when nothing has really been tested. The chapter matters because it shows where easy stories get their speed and emotional force.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the chapter that moves from lazy acceptance to fast generation. Chapter 3 showed why the reviewer signs off too early. Chapter 4 shows what kind of machine keeps handing that reviewer fluent material. Its distinctive value is that it turns association from a vague word into a mechanism for mood, salience, and seeming normality.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that a single cue can activate related ideas very quickly.
Then he explains how that spreading activation shapes what feels available, threatening, familiar, or true.
Then he concludes that associative coherence can mislead judgment because activated material feels informative even when it is only connected.
