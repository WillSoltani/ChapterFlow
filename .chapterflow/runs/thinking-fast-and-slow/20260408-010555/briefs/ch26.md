Core Claim:
Kahneman introduces prospect theory as a better account of how people value gains, losses, and certainty than classical expected utility can provide. Loss aversion and reference dependence reshape value more sharply than a smooth utility curve allows. The chapter matters because it explains why equal-sized gains and losses do not carry equal psychological weight. Its practical move is to map a choice as gains and losses around a live reference point before trusting the preference it produces.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Prospect Theory sits between Bernoulli's Errors and The Endowment Effect. It is the chapter that turns the reference-point insight into a broader theory of choice under risk. Its distinctive value is that it explains both why losses loom larger than gains and why certainty and framing can shift preference without changing the underlying stakes.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that people do not evaluate outcomes on one stable scale detached from context.
Then he shows that gains, losses, and certainty are filtered through a reference point and weighted asymmetrically.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires naming the frame of gain and loss before calling a preference rational or irrational.
