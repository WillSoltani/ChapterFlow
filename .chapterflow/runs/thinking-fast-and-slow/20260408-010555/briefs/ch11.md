Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that irrelevant starting points pull estimates and negotiations toward themselves. Initial numbers create a reference frame that keeps shaping later judgment. The chapter matters because arbitrary starting points distort estimates that feel independent. Its practical move is to generate an estimate from scratch or use a second reference point before accepting the first number's pull.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Anchors sits between The Law of Small Numbers and The Science of Availability. It is the chapter that explains how starting numbers keep pulling later estimates and negotiations toward themselves. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how an initial value creates a reference frame that keeps shaping later judgment.
Then he shows how that cue bends judgment before stronger checking arrives.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires a deliberate check on whether the estimate came from evidence or from the starting number's pull.
