Core Claim:
Kahneman argues that broad risk policies beat one-off choices because narrow framing invites inconsistency. Decisions improve when they are governed at the portfolio level instead of as isolated episodes. The chapter matters because single choices often look prudent in isolation while quietly undermining the larger strategy they belong to. Its practical move is to set a broad policy first and evaluate each local decision inside that policy.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Risk Policies sits between Rare Events and Keeping Score. It is the chapter that shifts from how individuals feel risk to how they should govern it across repeated decisions. Its distinctive value is that it shows why isolated caution can be globally reckless and why disciplined policy can outperform emotionally convincing case-by-case choices.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that narrow framing treats each risky choice as if it were a standalone event.
Then he shows that portfolio-level thinking often recommends a different action than one-shot emotional judgment.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires setting policies at the broader level and resisting improvised exceptions.
