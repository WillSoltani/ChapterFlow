Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that rare events are overweighted in description and often neglected in experience until they strike. The format of learning changes how the same probability feels. The chapter matters because the same low-probability outcome can trigger caution, neglect, panic, or indifference depending on whether people read about it, imagine it, or slowly sample it through routine experience. Its practical move is to ask whether the risk is being learned from description or from experience before trusting the felt probability.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Rare Events sits between The Fourfold Pattern and Risk Policies. It is the chapter that explains why the treatment of small probabilities changes when people learn through repeated experience instead of explicit description. Its distinctive value is that it links probability judgment to learning format rather than to probability alone.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that the same rare event feels different when it is described than when it is encountered through experience.
Then he shows that description tends to overweight rare risks while routine experience can underweight them until a vivid event breaks through.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires knowing how the probability entered the mind before treating the feeling as calibrated.
