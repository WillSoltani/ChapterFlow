Chapter Title: Keeping Score

Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that mental accounting tracks gains and losses in separate ledgers instead of one clean total. People bracket outcomes in ways that change emotion and choice. The chapter matters because equivalent totals can feel radically different depending on how the mind groups them. Its practical move is to ask whether gains and losses should be integrated with the broader picture before trusting the immediate reaction.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Keeping Score sits between Risk Policies and Reversals. It is the chapter that makes the bookkeeping structure of judgment explicit. Its distinctive value is that it shows how separate mental ledgers can help with discipline while also distorting valuation, fairness, and choice.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that people do not naturally keep one unified account of gains and losses.
Then he shows that separate brackets change how the same outcomes are felt and defended.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires asking whether outcomes belong in separate accounts or in a broader total.
