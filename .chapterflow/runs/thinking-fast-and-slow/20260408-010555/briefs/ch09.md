Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that hard questions are often answered by easier ones without the swap being noticed. Substitution lets the mind keep moving by replacing a demanding judgment with an easier proxy. The chapter matters because the mind can return a confident answer to the wrong question. Its practical move is to rewrite the target question in plain language and test whether the answer actually belongs to it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Answering an Easier Question sits between How Judgments Happen and The Law of Small Numbers. It is the chapter that explains how hard questions are replaced by easier proxy questions without the swap being noticed. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how substitution keeps judgment moving by answering a manageable question in place of a harder one.
Then he shows how the easier question can feel close enough to the real one that the swap stays hidden.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires recovering the original target before trusting the answer.
