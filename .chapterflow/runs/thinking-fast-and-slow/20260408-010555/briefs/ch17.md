Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that extreme outcomes are often followed by more ordinary ones without praise or blame causing the shift. Random variation naturally returns toward average outcomes over time. The chapter matters because natural reversion gets misread as proof that feedback, punishment, or praise caused the next outcome. Its practical move is to expect a move toward the average after extremes before assigning credit or blame.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Regression to the Mean sits between Causes Trump Statistics and Taming Intuitive Predictions. It is the chapter that explains how extreme outcomes often move back toward the average without praise or blame causing the shift. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how random variation naturally returns toward more ordinary results over time.
Then he shows how that cue bends judgment before stronger checking arrives.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires a deliberate check on whether the confidence came from evidence or from the chapter mechanism.
