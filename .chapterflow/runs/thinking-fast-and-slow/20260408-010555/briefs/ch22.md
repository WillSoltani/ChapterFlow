Core Claim:
Kahneman defines the conditions under which expert intuition deserves trust and when it does not. Valid intuition requires a regular environment plus repeated, high-quality feedback. The chapter matters because people trust intuition in chaotic domains where no such learning conditions exist. Its practical move is to ask whether the environment is regular and whether the expert has had enough clear feedback to learn it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Expert Intuition: When Can We Trust It? sits between Intuitions vs. Formulas and The Outside View. It is the chapter that explains how expert intuition deserves trust only in regular environments with repeated, high-quality feedback. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how valid intuition is learned through stable patterns and strong feedback loops.
Then he shows how that cue bends judgment before stronger checking arrives.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires a deliberate check on whether the confidence came from evidence or from the chapter mechanism.

- a coach or tutor’s feel for a recurring situation proves useful because the feedback is fast and reliable
