Chapter Title: Thinking About Life
Position In Book: package chapter 38 of 38

Core Claim:
Kahneman shows how life satisfaction is vulnerable to focusing illusions and to conflict between happiness, memory, and meaning. What people attend to in the moment of judgment can distort the verdict they give on life as a whole. The chapter matters because global life evaluations often overweight whichever factor is currently salient. Its practical move is to widen the frame, compare the voices of the different selves, and test whether the salient factor deserves its current weight.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Thinking About Life sits between Experienced Well-Being and the end of the numbered chapter sequence. It is the chapter that synthesizes the book's lessons about salience, framing, memory, and the two selves into a final warning about global life judgment. Its distinctive value is that it shows how easily the mind mistakes a narrow current concern for an overall truth about life.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that life satisfaction judgments are highly sensitive to what is salient at the moment of evaluation.
Then he shows that happiness, memory, and meaning do not automatically agree and can be flattened by the focusing illusion.
Then he concludes that better judgment about life requires a wider frame and a calmer comparison of the different measures being used.
