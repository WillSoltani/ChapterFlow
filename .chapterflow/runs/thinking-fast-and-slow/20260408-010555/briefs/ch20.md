Chapter Title: The Illusion of Validity
Position In Book: package chapter 20 of 38

Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that strong confidence in judgment can survive even when predictive validity is weak. Subjective certainty tracks coherence and confidence more than real accuracy. The chapter matters because subjective certainty gets mistaken for actual predictive power. Its practical move is to track outcomes and calibration instead of relying on how compelling the judgment feels.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The Illusion of Validity sits between The Illusion of Understanding and Intuitions vs. Formulas. It is the chapter that explains how strong confidence in judgment can survive even when predictive validity is weak. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how coherence and personal conviction create certainty more easily than real calibration does.
Then he shows how that cue bends judgment before stronger checking arrives.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires a deliberate check on whether the confidence came from evidence or from the chapter mechanism.
