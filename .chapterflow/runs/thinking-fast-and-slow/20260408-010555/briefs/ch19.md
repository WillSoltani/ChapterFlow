Core Claim:
Kahneman shows how coherent stories about the past create an exaggerated sense that the world was predictable all along. Narrative coherence hides luck, uncertainty, and missing alternatives. The chapter matters because hindsight turns messy history into a story that feels inevitable. Its practical move is to reconstruct what was actually uncertain then and what alternative futures were plausible.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The Illusion of Understanding sits between Taming Intuitive Predictions and The Illusion of Validity. It is the chapter that explains how coherent stories about the past exaggerate how predictable the world once was. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how narrative coherence hides luck, uncertainty, and the alternatives that were live at the time.
Then he shows how that cue bends judgment before stronger checking arrives.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires a deliberate check on whether the confidence came from evidence or from the chapter mechanism.
