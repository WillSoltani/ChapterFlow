Chapter Title: The Fourfold Pattern

Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that risk preferences flip depending on whether outcomes are framed as gains or losses and as likely or unlikely. Probability and valence interact to produce different appetites for risk. The chapter matters because a single rule about people being risk-averse or risk-seeking misses the actual pattern of judgment under uncertainty. Its practical move is to locate the choice in the right quadrant before making a confident claim about what preference should appear.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The Fourfold Pattern sits between Bad Events and Rare Events. It is the chapter that organizes earlier insights about gains, losses, and bad outcomes into a more systematic map of risk preference. Its distinctive value is that it shows why people can seem cautious in one case and reckless in another without abandoning the deeper logic of prospect theory.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that risk preference depends on both the sign of the outcome and its probability.
Then he shows that likely gains, likely losses, unlikely gains, and unlikely losses pull judgment in different directions.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires identifying the quadrant before treating a choice pattern as inconsistent or irrational.
