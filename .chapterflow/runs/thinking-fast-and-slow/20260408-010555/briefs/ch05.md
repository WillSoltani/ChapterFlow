Chapter Title: Cognitive Ease

Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that fluency, familiarity, and ease do not only make thinking feel pleasant. They also make claims feel truer, safer, and more trustworthy than they deserve. The mind uses felt ease as a quick signal for confidence, often before evidence has been checked carefully. The chapter matters because weak support can borrow authority from smooth processing. Its practical move is to separate the comfort of taking something in from the strength of the case itself.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the chapter that turns a feeling into a judgment mechanism. Chapter 4 explained how ideas become associated and ready. Chapter 5 explains how that readiness becomes comfortable, and how comfort quietly changes belief. Its distinctive value is that it shows why repetition, clarity, familiarity, and neatness can all make bad judgment feel reasonable.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that some thoughts, statements, and impressions are easier to process than others.
Then he explains that this ease gets read as a sign of truth, safety, and quality.
Then he concludes that judgment improves when the reader asks whether confidence came from evidence or merely from fluency.

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as an attack on clarity rather than on misplaced trust
- assuming every familiar or repeated idea is therefore suspicious

Counterarguments or Limits:
- Ease is often useful. People need fluent communication and quick recognition to function.
- The chapter is about selective suspicion when evidence is thin, not permanent distrust of anything clear or familiar.
