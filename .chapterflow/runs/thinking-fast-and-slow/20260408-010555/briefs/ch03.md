Chapter Title: The Lazy Controller

Core Claim:
Kahneman argues that System 2 often accepts System 1 suggestions with too little review. The supervising system conserves effort and lets a plausible first story stand when nothing forces deeper checking. This makes bias feel reasonable because the lowered inspection standard stays invisible. The chapter's practical point is that many errors come from premature mental closure rather than from dramatic irrationality.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the chapter that turns scarce attention into a behavioral habit. Chapter 2 established that deliberate thought is costly. Chapter 3 shows what the mind does with that cost structure: it often saves effort by accepting the first coherent answer. The chapter is distinctive because it explains how shallow review becomes normal, not exceptional.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman asks why System 2 so often does less checking than the reader expects.
Then he shows that the controller prefers to conserve effort and therefore accepts plausible System 1 outputs too quickly.
Then he concludes that bias often survives because easy answers get endorsed before a stronger search ever begins.
