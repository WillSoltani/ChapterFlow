Chapter Title: Attention and Effort

Core Claim:
This chapter turns the two-system model into a resource problem. System 2 is not just slower than System 1. It also depends on scarce attention. Tasks that require calculation, monitoring, memory, and inhibition draw from the same limited pool, which means effortful thinking competes with other effortful demands. The chapter's key point is that when attention is occupied, judgment quality changes, self-control weakens, and people often mistake overload for indifference, stupidity, or bad character.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 1 introduced the characters. This chapter shows what makes one of them hard to summon. Its distinctive contribution is that it gives System 2 a cost structure. The reader stops thinking of careful thought as a moral choice that should always be available and starts seeing it as a limited resource that can be taxed, split, and exhausted.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman shows that certain mental tasks clearly recruit effort while others run automatically.
Then he demonstrates that simultaneous effortful tasks interfere with each other because they draw on the same attentional budget.
Then he extends that insight into behavior, judgment, and self-control by showing that a loaded mind becomes less available for monitoring, restraint, and correction.

Counterarguments or Limits:

- Some skilled responses become automatic with practice, so the boundary between effortful and automatic work is functional rather than fixed forever.
