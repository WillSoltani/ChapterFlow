Chapter Title: The Law of Small Numbers
Position In Book: package chapter 10 of 38

Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that people expect stable patterns from samples that are far too small to justify that confidence. Sample-size neglect makes noise look meaningful and invites overreaction. The chapter matters because random variation is promoted into signal far too early. Its practical move is to ask how large the sample is and how much volatility should be expected before drawing a lesson.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The Law of Small Numbers sits between Answering an Easier Question and Anchors. It is the chapter that explains how small samples are treated as if they already reveal stable patterns. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how small samples get treated as if they should already look stable.
Then he shows how that expectation turns random swings into stories, lessons, and forecasts too quickly.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires checking sample size before promoting variation into signal.
