Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that planning improves when you use reference classes instead of relying on the inside view. Outside-view reasoning anchors forecasts in comparable cases rather than in hopeful detail. The chapter matters because the inside view turns vivid plans into unrealistically optimistic forecasts. Its practical move is to start with comparable cases and their outcomes before telling the detailed story of the current plan.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The Outside View sits between Expert Intuition: When Can We Trust It? and The Engine of Capitalism. It is the chapter that explains how planning improves when reference classes replace the inside view. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:

Then he shows how that cue bends judgment before stronger checking arrives.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires a deliberate check on whether the confidence came from evidence or from the chapter mechanism.
