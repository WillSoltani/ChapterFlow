Chapter Title: The Science of Availability

Core Claim:
Kahneman shows that ease of recall is often mistaken for frequency, importance, or probability. The mind samples memory fluency instead of counting the world directly. The chapter matters because what comes to mind most easily gets treated as what happens most often. Its practical move is to look for missing cases and ask whether recall is tracking frequency or only vividness and convenience.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The Science of Availability sits between Anchors and Availability, Emotion, and Risk. It is the chapter that explains how ease of recall is mistaken for real frequency, importance, or probability. Its distinctive value is that it turns a fast cue into a concrete audit point for later judgment errors.

Author Logic Chain:
First Kahneman identifies how memory fluency becomes a proxy for how common or likely something is.
Then he shows how that cue bends judgment before stronger checking arrives.
Then he concludes that better judgment requires a deliberate check on whether the confidence came from actual frequency or only from what memory supplied most easily.
