# Chapter Outline

Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that happiness in this chapter means a trainable peace baseline, not a reward granted by achievement.

Opening Move:
Open with the insult disguised as relief: you are just a monkey with a plan, and the plan keeps pretending the next win will finally settle you.

Anchor Allocation:
- Anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for Naval's baseline shift, learned happiness, default-state happiness, and the monkey-with-a-plan deflation.
- Anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for happiness as choice, presence, peace, nexting anxiety, desire contract, and success not earning peace.
- Anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for envy, internal scorecards, habits, positive reinterpretation, and change/accept/leave.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Hook with the monkey-with-a-plan line and Naval's own shift from low happiness to cultivated peace.
- P2: Define happiness as what remains when the sense of missingness quiets down; tie it to presence and internal silence.
- P3: Explain why Naval calls happiness a choice without making it sound magical; reality is mostly neutral, interpretation matters, and ego inflates expectations.
- P4: Turn to peace over joy and show how nexting, future-pulling, and externally imposed purpose keep the mind agitated.
- P5: Explain the desire contract, why success and status fail to settle the mind, and why too many desires multiply suffering.
- P6: Move into envy, internal scorecards, and happiness habits; show that peace is trained through interpretation, environment, and repeated behavior.
- P7: Close on change/accept/leave plus death as the perspective hack that shrinks ego battles, then bridge toward self-governance in the next chapter.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The core mechanism is that unhappiness here is usually not lack of stimulation but a self-renewing loop of desire, comparison, and mental motion. Happiness is not more winning. It is less inner argument with reality plus better habits of interpretation.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The hard version must preserve two limits at once: "happiness is a choice" is not a magic switch, and acceptance is not passive surrender. Meditation helps less than people think in the actual moment, and the chapter only stays honest if it keeps change/accept/leave together instead of preaching bland detachment.

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, work): teaches that external wins instantly create new craving unless the reader notices the contract.
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, work): teaches how success-chasing and constant nexting can quietly destroy peace even when outcomes look good.
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, school): teaches the envy full-swap test and internal scorecard logic.
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, school): teaches that happiness baseline rises more from habit and environment shifts than from one prestige win.
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): teaches change/accept/leave as the alternative to helpless resentment.
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): teaches the positive-interpretation and should-reduction habit as a practical mood reset.

Next-Chapter Bridge:
If peace depends on lowering ego, retraining habits, and refusing inner sabotage, what would it mean to save yourself from the self that keeps rebuilding suffering?

Genericity Risks:
- turning the chapter into airy "be present" language with no mechanism
- treating happiness as constant joy instead of peace plus lower mental motion
- making desire sound obviously bad rather than costly and selective
- collapsing habits into listicle wellness advice

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: gratitude wallpaper with no tension, tradeoff, or consequence
- filler move 2: "just choose happiness" language that ignores how stubborn the mind is in the moment
- invented authority move 1: unsupported neuroscience, therapy talk, or broad social claims beyond the source

Fresh Ticket Notes:
- Keep the chapter's voice sharp and unsentimental; it should sound practical, not soothing.
- Preserve the distinction between one meaningful desire and a life cluttered with many desires.
- Make the hard depth protect against both passive acceptance and fake spiritual superiority.
