# Chapter Outline

Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand that judgment is a trainable way of seeing long-term consequences more clearly, not a vague aura of intelligence.

Opening Move:
Open on force versus direction in a leveraged world: two people can work equally hard, yet one bad direction can waste everything while one correct decision can multiply.

Anchor Allocation:
- Anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for judgment, leverage, and direction-versus-force.
- Anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P4 for Feynman clarity, basics, suffering, ego, identity, and empty space.
- Anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for honesty, mental models, principal-agent, high-bar no's, run uphill, and reading.

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Hook with leverage making direction more important than force; define judgment as long-term consequence seeing.
- P2: Explain why clear thinkers understand basics and can re-derive instead of memorize jargon.
- P3: Show how ego, desire, and denial distort reality, with suffering as forced truth.
- P4: Explain identity shedding, unconditioning, and honest speech without labels.
- P5: Turn to decision heuristics and radical honesty as practical judgment protection.
- P6: Explain mental models plus concrete heuristics like principal-agent, inversion, if-you-cannot-decide-no, and run uphill.
- P7: Close on reading foundations, originals, and rereading as the long-term engine of judgment, then bridge to happiness.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's real distinction is between sounding smart and seeing reality clearly. Judgment is not information volume. It is clear contact with reality, built from fundamentals, protected from ego, and compressed into models that guide long-term choices.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The hard version must preserve the limit that heuristics and models can become new identity costumes. The chapter deepens only if it keeps showing that anti-ego thinking can itself become ego theater when slogans replace reality.
