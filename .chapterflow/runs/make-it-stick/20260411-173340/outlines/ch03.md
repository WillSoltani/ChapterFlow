# Chapter Outline

Chapter Promise:
Show that mixed practice feels messier because it forces choice, and that this messier structure builds stronger discrimination and transfer than blocked repetition alone.

Opening Move:
Open on the tempting comfort of doing the same kind of problem or drill repeatedly until it feels smooth, then show why that smoothness can misprice actual readiness.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1, P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2, P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3, P4

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: Contrast blocked practice with a mixed set that feels less smooth.
- P2: Explain the discrimination mechanism: mixed practice forces the learner to identify what kind of problem this is.
- P3: Show why interleaving helps transfer and flexible choice later.
- P4: Clarify that mixing must stay structured enough to reveal patterns.
- P5: Close by bridging from mixed practice into the broader category of desirable difficulties.

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter must distinguish variety-for-novelty from interleaving-for-discrimination. The mechanism is choosing the right move under changing conditions.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard mode must keep the line between productive mixing and chaotic overload.

Threshold Question:
When does mixed practice sharpen recognition of differences, and when does it become so scattered that the learner cannot see the underlying pattern?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: blocked practice can flatter performance in the moment
- Takeaway 2: interleaving strengthens discrimination among similar cases
- Takeaway 3: variation helps transfer because the learner practices selecting, not just repeating
- Takeaway 4: messy-feeling practice can be more honest than smooth practice
- Takeaway 5: mixing needs enough structure to stay legible

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, school): a student chooses between blocked algebra drills and a mixed packet
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, work): a trainer sees employees fail when scenarios stop repeating in one pattern
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): two musicians debate repeating one passage versus rotating similar passages
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, school): a student predicts which practice plan will hold up on a mixed exam
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, work): a coach decides how much variation to add without creating chaos
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): a learner shifts from blocked comfort to mixed practice and better choice

Scenario Vividness Targets:
- Scenario 1: algebra packet with grouped equations versus mixed pages
- Scenario 2: customer-support training dashboard and varied calls
- Scenario 3: music stand, metronome, and similar but distinct passages
- Scenario 4: exam page with shuffled problem types

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask what changes when hard-feeling practice is judged not only by mixing but by the broader category of desirable difficulties.

Genericity Risks:
- drifting into a generic praise of variety
- treating confusion as good by default
- underexplaining why blocked practice can feel stronger while teaching less

Forbidden Drift:
- no novelty-for-its-own-sake rhetoric
- no invented experimental specifics
- no anti-structure posture
- no brief-language leakage
