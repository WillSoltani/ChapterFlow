# Book Skeleton

## Metadata

- Book: *Make It Stick: The Science of Successful Learning*
- Authors: Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III, Mark A. McDaniel
- Edition: 2014 Belknap Press of Harvard University Press English edition
- Variant family: EMH
- Wave size: 2

## Chapter Order

1. Learning Is Misunderstood
   - Intent: break the reader's trust in easy-feeling study habits and establish durable learning as counterintuitive.
   - Source richness: medium-high
   - Concept density: medium
   - Moral complexity: low

2. To Learn, Retrieve
   - Intent: make retrieval practice the book's central learning mechanism.
   - Source richness: high
   - Concept density: medium-high
   - Moral complexity: low

3. Mix Up Your Practice
   - Intent: argue that interleaving and variation improve discrimination and transfer.
   - Source richness: medium
   - Concept density: medium
   - Moral complexity: low

4. Embrace Difficulties
   - Intent: distinguish desirable difficulties from pointless friction and show why effort can strengthen retention.
   - Source richness: medium
   - Concept density: high
   - Moral complexity: medium

5. Avoid Illusions of Knowing
   - Intent: expose familiarity, rereading, and subjective confidence as unreliable calibration tools.
   - Source richness: medium-high
   - Concept density: medium-high
   - Moral complexity: medium

6. Get Beyond Learning Styles
   - Intent: reject learning-style matching as the main answer and redirect the reader toward evidence-backed design.
   - Source richness: medium
   - Concept density: medium
   - Moral complexity: medium

7. Increase Your Abilities
   - Intent: challenge fixed-ability beliefs and connect improvement to strategy, practice, and response to setbacks.
   - Source richness: medium
   - Concept density: medium
   - Moral complexity: high

8. Make It Stick
   - Intent: synthesize retrieval, spacing, interleaving, calibration, and growth strategy into an applied system.
   - Source richness: medium-high
   - Concept density: high
   - Moral complexity: medium

## Thin-Chapter Risk Notes

- Chapter 3 can drift into generic "variety is good" language if interleaving is not made concrete.
- Chapter 6 can become a shallow anti-learning-styles rant unless it offers a better alternative.
- Chapter 7 can slide into generic growth-mindset rhetoric unless it stays tied to practice design and evidence.

## Premium-Routing Candidates

- Chapter 4 because desirable difficulties require careful boundary-setting.
- Chapter 5 because the chapter can easily collapse into obvious anti-rereading slogans.
- Chapter 7 because fixed-versus-expandable ability claims need nuance, not cheerleading.
