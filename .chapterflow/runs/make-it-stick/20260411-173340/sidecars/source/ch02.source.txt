Core claim:
- Retrieval practice strengthens learning better than repeated exposure alone.
- Self-quizzing and low-stakes testing are learning tools, not only judgment tools.
- Effortful recall can feel difficult because it reveals weakness, but that struggle can deepen retention when followed by correction.

Distinct mechanism:
- The chapter turns recall into the main mechanism rather than a final measurement.
- It reframes quizzes from scoreboard devices into memory-building routines.
- It treats missed recall as a repair signal instead of automatic failure.

Required anchors:
- Trying to retrieve information from memory is itself part of learning.
- Low-stakes testing and self-quizzing can improve retention.
- Failed recall should lead to feedback and another attempt, not resignation.

Common misreadings:
- Retrieval means pressure-heavy formal exams.
- A failed recall attempt proves the learner should stop quizzing.

Specific applications:
- Use blank-page recall, self-quizzes, or oral explanation during review.
- Add short low-stakes quizzes in classrooms or training routines.
- Treat a miss as information for repair and repeat.

Hard-edge requirement:
- Hard mode must keep the line between productive recall struggle and unsupported frustration visible.

Threshold question:
- When does a retrieval miss become useful evidence for repair, and when is the learner missing too much context for the attempt to help?
