Core claim:
- Some forms of difficulty strengthen learning because they force retrieval, generation, recovery, or correction after errors.
- Smooth, errorless practice can feel better while leaving weaker retention.
- Not every difficulty is desirable; support and mechanism matter.

Distinct mechanism:
- The chapter defines desirable difficulty as challenge with a learning mechanism.
- It treats errors as useful only when they are corrected and integrated.
- It keeps overload outside the desirable category.

Required anchors:
- Hard-feeling learning can help when it requires effortful recovery or generation.
- Errorless ease is not always the best route to durable learning.
- Productive challenge needs support and correction to avoid becoming waste.

Common misreadings:
- Difficulty itself is always a virtue.
- Errors are useful even when no repair happens.

Specific applications:
- Use low-stakes generation and retrieval even when they feel less smooth.
- Allow mistakes during practice when feedback and another attempt follow.
- Keep enough structure that challenge remains teachable.

Hard-edge requirement:
- Hard mode must preserve the line between productive challenge and unsupported overload.

Threshold question:
- When does a difficulty still preserve enough support to teach, and when does it collapse into discouragement?
