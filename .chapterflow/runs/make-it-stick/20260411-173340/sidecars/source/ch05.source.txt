Core claim:
- Familiarity and confidence can look like learning without proving durable recall or understanding.
- Rereading and highlighting are weak judges of mastery when they are not paired with honest checks.
- Testing, explanation, delayed recall, and feedback improve calibration.

Distinct mechanism:
- The chapter focuses on calibration: whether the learner can retrieve, explain, or apply without borrowing confidence from the page.
- It treats objective checks as a way to expose what feeling alone misses.
- It makes subjective ease answer to demonstrated performance.

Required anchors:
- Familiarity is not the same as knowing.
- Passive review can inflate confidence.
- Honest checks require the learner to retrieve, explain, or transfer.

Common misreadings:
- Confidence proves mastery.
- Highlighting and rereading are enough on their own.

Specific applications:
- Replace one reread with a closed-book explanation.
- Use delayed self-quizzes to judge retention.
- Ask for transfer or explanation before calling the material learned.

Hard-edge requirement:
- Hard mode must keep confidence and demonstrated knowledge visibly separate.

Threshold question:
- What kind of check forces knowledge to stand on its own instead of leaning on recognition?
