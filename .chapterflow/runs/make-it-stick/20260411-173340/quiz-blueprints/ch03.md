# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter: Mix Up Your Practice

Core concepts to test:
- blocked practice can flatter short-run performance
- interleaving strengthens discrimination among similar cases
- mixed practice supports transfer and flexible choice
- messy-feeling practice can be more informative than smooth repetition
- mixing needs structure rather than random chaos

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: analyze

Question plan:
- q01: core recall of what interleaving does
- q02: contrast between blocked and mixed practice
- q03: application to algebra or similar mixed problem sets
- q04: workplace transfer involving varied scenarios
- q05: why mixed practice can feel worse while teaching more
- q06: misreading about randomness and chaos
- q07: personal-skill scenario about music or drills
- q08: bridge question toward desirable difficulties
- q09: synthesis on discrimination and transfer
- q10: threshold question about too much variation

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: smoother session mistaken for stronger learning
- tempting wrong answer type 2: random variety mistaken for meaningful interleaving

Explanation diversity notes:
- avoid repeated openers
- one explanation should stress discrimination, another transfer, another structure
- at least one should protect against the misconception that blocked practice is always useless

CorrectIndex plan:
- keep distribution roughly balanced across 0 / 1 / 2
