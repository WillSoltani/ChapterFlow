# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter: Learning Is Misunderstood

Core concepts to test:
- fluency and familiarity are not reliable proof of learning
- rereading and cramming can feel strong while producing weak retention
- retrieval, spacing, and interleaving support durable learning
- effort helps when it creates reconstruction or discrimination, not when it is random
- the chapter is a reset of learning assumptions, not a generic work-harder sermon

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: analyze

Question plan:
- q01: core recall of what the chapter says people misjudge
- q02: concept contrast between familiarity and durable learning
- q03: application to rereading versus blank-page recall
- q04: workplace transfer about training that looked smooth but did not last
- q05: role of spacing or interleaving in making learning stick
- q06: misreading around difficulty automatically being good
- q07: school scenario about cramming versus spaced retrieval
- q08: chapter-bridge question leading toward retrieval practice
- q09: synthesis on why subjective ease is a weak signal
- q10: threshold question about productive difficulty versus wasted struggle

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: ease and confidence mistaken for mastery
- tempting wrong answer type 2: difficulty treated as valuable even without mechanism

Explanation diversity notes:
- avoid repeated openers
- one explanation should stress retention, another transfer, another calibration
- at least one explanation should distinguish productive difficulty from random struggle
