Position In Book: ability-beliefs chapter

Core Claim:
This chapter argues that ability is more expandable than fixed-ability beliefs suggest. Strategy, deliberate practice, feedback, persistence, and response to setbacks shape improvement more than static self-labels imply.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The chapter cannot dissolve into generic growth-mindset cheerleading. It has to keep improvement tied to disciplined practice, feedback, and error response rather than to slogans about believing in yourself.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors surface fixed-ability assumptions.
Then they show why strategy and practice can expand performance.
Then they connect setbacks and correction to growth.
Then they frame ability as shaped by design, effort, and feedback more than by static self-description.

Required Anchors:
- Ability is not as fixed as many learners assume.
- Strategy and practice design matter for improvement.
- Setbacks and correction can become part of growth when handled well.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- fixed-ability belief
- expandable ability
- strategy effect
- setback response

Specific Applications:
- Replace “I am bad at this” with a diagnostic question about strategy and practice.
- Use feedback and another attempt after failure.
- Judge improvement by response to practice, not by static label.

Common Misreadings:
- The chapter says anyone can do anything instantly.
- Effort alone is enough without better strategy.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter must keep limits and variation realistic. It should argue that ability is more expandable than fixed stories imply, not that constraints disappear.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
Chapter 6 rejected a weak theory about learning styles.
Next Chapter Bridge:
Chapter 8 should synthesize retrieval, spacing, interleaving, calibration, and growth-oriented practice into an applied system.
