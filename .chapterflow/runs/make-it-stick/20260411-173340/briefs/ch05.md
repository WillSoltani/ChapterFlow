Core Claim:
This chapter argues that familiarity, rereading, highlighting, and subjective confidence can mislead learners about what they actually know. Accurate calibration needs tests, generation, explanation, delayed recall, and objective checks that force knowledge to stand on its own.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
The chapter cannot stop at anti-rereading slogans. Its real job is metacognitive calibration. It has to show why confidence can rise faster than understanding and why honest checks feel rougher precisely because they expose what is missing.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors show that easy recognition and repeated exposure can impersonate knowing.
Then they explain why confidence built on familiarity is unstable.
Then they redirect the reader toward retrieval, explanation, delayed checks, and outside feedback.
Then they frame calibration as a discipline that protects the rest of the book's methods from self-deception.

Required Anchors:
- Familiarity is not the same as durable knowledge.
- Rereading and highlighting can inflate confidence without proving recall or transfer.
- Honest self-judgment requires tests, explanation, and objective checks.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- illusion of knowing: confidence that has not survived an honest check
- familiarity trap: recognition mistaken for recall or understanding
- calibration: judging what is actually retrievable or explainable
- objective check: quiz, explanation, delayed recall, or transfer prompt that reveals what the learner can do unaided

Specific Applications:
- Replace one reread with a closed-book recall or explanation round.
- Use delayed self-quizzes instead of relying on the feeling of recognition.
- Ask for explanation or transfer, not just recognition, before calling material learned.

Common Misreadings:
- Confidence is a reliable sign of mastery.
- Highlighting and rereading are enough if they feel fluent.

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should not imply that all rereading is useless. It has to argue that passive review is weak as a judge of mastery. It also cannot promise perfect self-knowledge; it should recommend better checks, not magical certainty.
