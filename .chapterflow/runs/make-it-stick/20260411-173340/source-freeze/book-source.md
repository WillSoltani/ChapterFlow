## Authorized Overview Notes

Frozen from Google Books and the De Gruyter/Harvard title page:

- The book argues that many common study habits create an illusion of mastery rather than durable learning.
- The book emphasizes self-testing, spacing, interleaving, and forms of desirable difficulty as stronger routes to long-term retention and transfer.
- The book pushes back on common assumptions about learning styles and on the idea that easy-feeling study is better learning.
- The book is written for students, teachers, parents, and lifelong learners rather than for a specialist-only audience.

## Frozen Chapter Support Notes

### Chapter 1. Learning Is Misunderstood
- Opening thesis chapter. Establishes that intuitive study habits feel productive while often producing weak retention.
- Core contrast: fluency and familiarity are poor proxies for durable learning.
- Sets up the book's willingness to defend counterintuitive strategies.

### Chapter 2. To Learn, Retrieve
- Retrieval practice is central: effortful recall strengthens learning better than repeated exposure alone.
- Self-quizzing and recall attempts are framed as learning tools, not only measurement tools.
- Productive struggle during recall is treated as part of the mechanism.

### Chapter 3. Mix Up Your Practice
- Interleaving and variation strengthen discrimination and flexible transfer.
- Blocked practice can make performance feel smoother while weakening later selection and adaptation.
- Mixing problems or skills helps learners recognize when to use which tool.

### Chapter 4. Embrace Difficulties
- Certain difficulties help learning when they are desirable rather than destructive.
- Spacing, generation, delayed restudy, and effortful retrieval can feel slower while improving long-run retention.
- The chapter distinguishes productive friction from pointless overload.

### Chapter 5. Avoid Illusions of Knowing
- Rereading, highlighting, and familiarity can mislead learners about what they actually know.
- Accurate self-calibration requires tests, generation, explanation, and objective checks.
- Metacognitive confidence needs external pressure, not just subjective ease.

### Chapter 6. Get Beyond Learning Styles
- The chapter rejects the idea that matching instruction to a preferred learning style is the core route to better learning.
- It shifts attention toward prior knowledge, practice design, representations that fit the material, and active engagement.
- It treats style beliefs as a distraction from stronger evidence-backed methods.

### Chapter 7. Increase Your Abilities
- Ability is treated as more expandable than fixed-ability beliefs suggest.
- Strategy, practice design, persistence, and feedback alter performance more than static self-labels imply.
- The chapter keeps growth grounded in disciplined practice rather than empty affirmation.

### Chapter 8. Make It Stick
- Closing synthesis chapter.
- Pulls retrieval, spacing, interleaving, generation, calibration, and growth strategy into an applied learning system.
- Emphasizes durable routines for students, teachers, and self-directed learners.
