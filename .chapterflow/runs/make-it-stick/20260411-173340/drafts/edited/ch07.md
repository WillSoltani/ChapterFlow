# Increase Your Abilities

Early struggle can look like proof of a fixed limit. This chapter argues that the story is often too final. Ability changes with strategy, feedback, repeated practice, and response to setbacks more than many learners assume.

The chapter keeps that claim disciplined. Effort alone is not enough. Improvement comes from better methods, correction, and another attempt. A setback can reveal what to change in the next round instead of serving only as a verdict on talent.

So the reader is asked to replace static self-labels with better diagnostic questions. What strategy failed? What background piece is missing? What kind of retrieval, feedback, or repetition would help? That growth-oriented stance prepares the book's closing synthesis, where all of the methods become a repeatable learning system.
