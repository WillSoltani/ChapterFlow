# Increase Your Abilities

Early struggle can feel like proof that ability has run out. This chapter pushes back on that conclusion. The book argues that performance changes with strategy, feedback, repeated practice, and response to setbacks. A learner is not infinitely plastic, but ability is more expandable than fixed stories usually allow.

That claim stays grounded. Improvement does not come from optimism alone. It comes from better methods, better diagnosis, and another attempt after correction. When a learner treats mistakes as information about strategy and preparation, the next round can be stronger than the first.

The chapter therefore treats setbacks as part of the learning process rather than as permanent verdicts. A poor result can show where the method is weak, where background knowledge is missing, or where more retrieval and feedback are needed. Growth is tied to disciplined practice, not to slogans.

So the book moves from myths about style to myths about capacity. Learners are asked to stop treating current performance as a fixed ceiling and to ask what stronger practice might change. That bridge matters because the final chapter gathers the book's methods into a complete routine for making learning stick.
