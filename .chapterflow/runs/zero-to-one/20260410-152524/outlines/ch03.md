Chapter Promise:
This chapter will show that strong companies become distinct enough to escape direct competition, while weak companies end up looking alike because rivalry strips away profits and room to plan.

Opening Move:
Open on the discomfort of the title claim and use it to separate uniqueness from competitive sameness.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P3
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: introduce the happy-companies contrast and frame the chapter's provocation
- P2: explain how competition drives prices, margins, and strategic freedom downward
- P3: show how rivalry makes firms converge on sameness
- P4: define monopoly as created distinctiveness rather than simple size
- P5: explain why monopoly gives room for planning and long-term building
- P6: preserve the moral limit, then bridge to the ideology of competition

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter's mechanism is that competition erodes profits and compresses behavior, while monopoly relaxes direct rivalry and creates room for differentiated strategy.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must defend monopoly as business strength without turning it into an unconditional social virtue.

Threshold Question:
If competition feels fair and healthy, why does it so often produce weak economics and copycat behavior instead of strong companies?
