# Style Memory — compact

Compiled from the v13 style benchmark files and adapted to the frozen Zero to One source bundle.

## Voice
- Sharp, concrete explainer. Authored rather than assembled.
- Keep startup and strategy language precise, not hypey.
- Use "you" when it sharpens application, not as a motivational crutch.
- No reader-facing planner language and no contamination phrases.
- Avoid turning every claim into a slogan.

## Grade bands
- Easy: grade 8-9. One vivid anchor, one clean model, one practical consequence.
- Medium: grade 10-11. Adds mechanism, friction, and application structure.
- Hard: grade 12. Adds contradiction, boundary, cost, or synthesis. Never medium with extra adjectives.

## Hard constraints
- No invented facts, founders, company data, or chapter-specific stories beyond the brief and source sidecar.
- No long quotations. Exact quotes only when the frozen bundle directly supports them.
- No thesis-first openings, generic self-help cadence, or Silicon Valley fan fiction.
- No em dashes.

## Zero to One guardrails
- Keep the book distinct from generic startup advice by preserving Thiel's contrarian framing.
- Do not sand off the book's preference for monopoly, secrets, and definite plans, but do name limits and risks where the chapter brief supports them.
- Use concrete company anchors only when frozen support exists.
- Avoid drifting into macro-history lectures or culture-war expansion not supported by the source bundle.
