Core Claim:
This chapter argues that great products do not automatically reach customers. Distribution is a core competence, and companies that ignore it misunderstand how businesses actually work.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the distribution chapter. Its job is to reject the product-sells-itself fantasy and show that delivery to the market is part of company design rather than an awkward afterthought.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter challenges the belief that product quality alone creates reach.
Then it argues that distribution deserves the same seriousness as product creation.
Then it shows why channels, sales, and market access are strategic rather than secondary.
Then it preserves the limit that distribution is not generic hype or empty promotion.

Required Anchors:
- the chapter's rejection of the build-it-and-they-will-come myth
- distribution as a core competence
- product quality does not remove the need for reach
- selling and channel design matter to company success
