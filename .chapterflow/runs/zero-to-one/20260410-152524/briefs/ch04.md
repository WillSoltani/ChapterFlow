Core Claim:
This chapter argues that competition is not just an economic condition but an ideology that captures attention, drives imitation, and keeps people fighting inside narrow frames instead of building stronger positions.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the rivalry-trap chapter. Its job is to explain why competition keeps its cultural prestige even when it weakens judgment, distracts teams, and turns strategy into reaction.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter treats competition as a mindset, not just a market structure.
Then it shows how rivalry absorbs focus and pushes imitation.
Then it argues that people mistake constant contest for seriousness or virtue.
Then it preserves the limit that not all comparison is avoidable, but obsessive rivalry is strategically deforming.

Required Anchors:
- competition operates as an ideology or mindset, not only an external condition
- rivalry narrows attention and encourages copycat behavior
- people often confuse competitive intensity with importance or excellence
- the chapter wants stronger positioning, not blind refusal to notice rivals
