Core Claim:
This chapter argues that the dot-com crash taught builders several lessons that sounded prudent but became overcorrections. The safe rules people learned after 2000 often discouraged definite plans, durable differentiation, and bold concentration, even though real startup success still requires them.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the backlash chapter. Its job is to show how one era of excess produced a culture of excessive caution, and to prepare the book's later defense of monopoly, planning, and conviction.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter revisits the late-1990s bubble and crash.
Then it extracts the four common post-crash rules in their cautious form.
Then it argues those rules were overlearned and became anti-creation dogma.
Then it positions better startup judgment as disciplined boldness rather than reckless hype.

Required Anchors:
- the dot-com bubble and crash as the historical trigger
- the post-bubble startup rules: make incremental advances, stay lean and flexible, improve on the competition, focus on product not sales
- the claim that each rule contains partial truth but becomes misleading when treated as universal law
- the handoff back to definite thinking after a fearful era
