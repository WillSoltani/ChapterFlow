Core Claim:
This chapter argues that returns are governed by asymmetry. A small number of outliers matter far more than average outcomes, so builders and investors have to think in power-law rather than evenly distributed terms.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the asymmetry chapter. Its job is to show that once choices still matter under uncertainty, the next question is which choices can dominate the economics so heavily that ordinary averaging becomes misleading.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter rejects average-case thinking.
Then it explains that returns are concentrated rather than evenly spread.
Then it shows why one big winner can matter more than many modest outcomes.
Then it preserves the limit that this is not an excuse for random gambling or sloppy selection.
