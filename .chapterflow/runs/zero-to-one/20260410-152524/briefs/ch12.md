Core Claim:
This chapter argues that the strongest technological systems often come from complementarity between computers and people rather than from simple human replacement.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the complementarity chapter. Its job is to resist simplistic automation narratives and show how human and machine strengths can combine into more powerful business systems.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter rejects the idea that technology's main role is to eliminate people outright.
Then it argues that computers and humans often do different kinds of work well.
Then it shows why complementary systems can be more powerful than replacement thinking alone.
Then it preserves the limit that the chapter is not utopian futurism or automation panic.

Required Anchors:
- the chapter's human-machine complementarity logic
- computers and people have different strengths
- strong systems combine those strengths rather than flattening them
- technology design should improve work rather than just fantasize about replacement
