Core Claim:
This chapter argues that the founding moment matters disproportionately because early decisions about structure, relationships, incentives, and ownership get baked into the company and are expensive to fix later.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the early-structure chapter. Its job is to move from hidden truth to company design and show that good startups are not only about insight but also about how the initial organization is put together.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter argues that beginnings have outsized force.
Then it shows that early structural choices persist longer than founders expect.
Then it treats role clarity, relationship quality, and incentive alignment as foundational rather than administrative.
Then it preserves the limit that foundations are not bureaucratic ceremony but durable design.

Required Anchors:
- the chapter's claim that early choices get locked in
- foundations are unusually hard to repair later
- roles, ownership, and relationship structure matter at the start
- company design should fit the truth the founders want to build around
