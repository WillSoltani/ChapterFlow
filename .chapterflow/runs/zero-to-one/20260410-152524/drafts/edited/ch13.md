Many people talk as if a technology bet becomes solid the moment the technology itself sounds ambitious enough.

Chapter 13 rejects that comfort. It argues that large technology bets can still fail when timing, scale, and business design are wrong. The point is not that technical ambition is fake. The point is that ambition alone does not decide whether the business works.

That is why the chapter keeps timing and scale in view. A technology can be meaningful in principle and still be pursued at the wrong moment or at the wrong operating scale. The chapter refuses to treat those errors as minor details around the real story. They are part of the story.

Business design matters for the same reason. A compelling technological narrative can still hide weak sequencing, weak economics, or a poor fit between the market and the plan. Failure can therefore come from how the bet is structured rather than from the technology being worthless in principle.

The limit matters. This chapter is not anti-technology rhetoric, and it is not hindsight mockery. It is trying to diagnose failure with more discipline than generic sneering usually allows. The lesson is not that important technology should be abandoned. The lesson is that disciplined judgment matters as much as excitement.

That leads into the next chapter. Once the book explains why large technology bets can fail despite ambition, the next question becomes why the unusual founder energy behind such bets can be both powerful and unstable.
