# Book Skeleton — Zero to One

## Metadata
- title: `Zero to One: Notes on Startups, or How to Build the Future`
- author: `Peter Thiel`
- co-author note: `Blake Masters credited on the locked edition`
- canonical edition: `2014 English first-edition family`
- package chapter count: `14`
- wave size: `2`
- source posture: `official metadata + preview chapter map + secondary chapter summaries`

## Governing voice guardrails for the whole book
- Keep the book contrarian, strategic, and specific rather than motivational.
- Preserve the distinction between invention and copying.
- Keep monopoly, secrets, timing, culture, and distribution as separate lenses rather than one generic startup blob.
- Name tradeoffs where the chapter supports them so the book does not read like pure ideology.

## Motif / callback opportunities
- `0 to 1` versus `1 to n` should recur as a diagnostic, not a catchphrase.
- Definite optimism, planning, and secrets connect chapters 1, 6, and 8.
- Monopoly logic should echo across chapters 3, 4, 5, and 11.
- Foundations and culture should reinforce each other across chapters 9 and 10.
- Distribution, technology, and timing should meet again in chapters 11 through 13.

## School-setting plan
- ch01: startup seminar on whether progress still exists
- ch02: business-school class reviewing the dot-com bubble
- ch03: student team choosing between a crowded app idea and a narrow monopoly wedge
- ch04: campus incubator competition where teams copy each other
- ch05: finance lab evaluating a company with small profits but large future upside
- ch06: founder fellowship deciding between plan and drift
- ch07: venture practicum discussing power-law portfolios
- ch08: research group hunting for a contrarian thesis
- ch09: capstone team defining roles before launch
- ch10: club founders choosing for culture instead of perks
- ch11: demo-day team learning that distribution work matters
- ch12: AI and labor seminar debating replacement versus complementarity
- ch13: sustainability startup case review
- ch14: founder-led student venture under strain

## Vocabulary watchlist
- keep precise: monopoly, competition, distribution, secrets, planning, leverage, cash flow, culture, timing, complementarity
- use carefully: disruption, innovation, founder, startup, power law
- avoid overusing: visionary, hustle, grind, moonshot, destiny

## Thin-chapter risk notes
- `ch01`: can drift into generic future talk if the definite/indefinite split is not named clearly.
- `ch04`: easy to sound like bumper-sticker anti-competition rhetoric; keep the mechanism visible.
- `ch06`: chance-versus-planning chapter can become abstract without concrete decisions.
- `ch10`: culture chapter can become startup-romance fluff if role clarity and mission fit disappear.
- `ch13`: clean-tech chapter can turn into hindsight sneering unless the seven-question logic stays specific.
- `ch14`: founder paradox must keep both upside and instability in frame.

## Premium-routing candidates
- `ch01` because it sets the book's whole definition of progress
- `ch03` because monopoly logic can easily flatten into corporate apology
- `ch08` because the secrets chapter carries the core contrarian method
- `ch11` because distribution is easy to underwrite and easy to make generic
- `ch13` because the clean-tech failure diagnosis needs disciplined specificity

## Cross-chapter tensions to preserve
- invention versus copying
- planning versus drift
- monopoly value versus competitive erosion
- founder intensity versus organizational stability
- technology as complement versus technology as replacement

## Quality decay watch
- If any later chapter falls more than `1.5` critic points below the Chapter 1-2 baseline, stop and reroute.
- Watch especially for generic startup-blog drift in chapters 4, 6, 10, and 14.
