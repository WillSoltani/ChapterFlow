## Chapter order and one-line intent
1. `Leadership: The Single Most Important Factor`
   Establish the book's opening claim that leadership quality is the deciding factor under pressure and frame the Ramadi combat dilemma that makes the rest of the principles matter.
2. `Extreme Ownership`
   Show that the leader must own failure fully, fix the system, and remove excuse-making.
3. `No Bad Teams, Only Bad Leaders`
   Argue that leadership standards and behavior change team performance more than excuses about team quality.
4. `Believe`
   Show that leaders must understand and explain why the mission matters before their people can execute with conviction.
5. `Check the Ego`
   Warn that arrogance, internal rivalry, and status games destroy coordination and mission focus.
6. `Cover and Move`
   Teach interdependence: teams win only when units support one another instead of operating as silos.
7. `Simple`
   Show that plans and communication must stay simple enough to survive chaos and be understood at the front.
8. `Prioritize and Execute`
   Teach leaders to detach, identify the most urgent problem, and restore order one priority at a time.
9. `Decentralized Command`
   Show why junior leaders need clear intent, authority, and trust to decide at the point of action.
10. `Plan`
   Argue that careful planning, rehearsal, and commander's intent create adaptability rather than rigidity.
11. `Leading Up and Down the Chain of Command`
   Show that leaders must communicate mission purpose downward and give superiors the context they need upward.
12. `Decisiveness amid Uncertainty`
   Explore the tension between hesitation and rashness when information is incomplete and stakes are high.
13. `Discipline Equals Freedom: The Dichotomy of Leadership`
   Close on disciplined systems and balanced leadership traits that free teams to move fast without losing control.

## Moral complexity flags
- `ch01-ch04`: strong. The book treats battlefield leadership as a source of leadership lessons without inviting moral simplification of war.
- `ch05`: medium. Humility is strategic and cooperative, not submissive.
- `ch06-ch11`: medium. The advice must stay team-centered and not drift into manipulative office tactics.
- `ch12-ch13`: strong. Decisiveness and discipline must be framed as balanced judgment, not domination.

## Rotation plan for examples

## Thin-chapter risk notes

- `ch05`: ego can drift generic fast; keep the cross-unit respect angle visible.
- `ch12`: uncertainty chapter can collapse into generic decisiveness talk; keep the wrongful-friendly-fire risk and the cost of bad calls visible.
