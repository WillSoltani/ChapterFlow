Core Claim:
This chapter argues that leadership quality often explains team performance more than the supposedly fixed quality of the team itself. The Hell Week boat-crew story gives the argument its force because the same group performs very differently once leadership changes. The chapter is not claiming talent differences never exist. It is claiming that leaders use team quality as an excuse too quickly when standards, energy, trust, and clarity may be more decisive. The real lesson is that leaders should inspect their own effect on the team before concluding the team is the problem.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It takes the ownership principle and widens it from a single failure into a broader claim about team performance. Its distinct move is to challenge the story that weak teams are simply stuck being weak.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter uses the Hell Week boat-crew swap to show the same team performing differently under different leadership.
Then it argues that leaders often blame the team before examining their own standards, example, and energy.
Then it reframes weak-team complaints as leadership questions rather than as final diagnoses.
