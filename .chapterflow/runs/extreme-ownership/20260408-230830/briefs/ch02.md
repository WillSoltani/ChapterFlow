Core Claim:
The chapter argues that leaders must own failures fully instead of blaming subordinates, circumstances, or bad luck. The Ramadi blue-on-blue incident gives the claim its force because Jocko Willink treats a catastrophic friendly-fire mistake as a leadership failure he must take responsibility for, even though many tactical details were messy and many people were involved. Ownership is not framed as self-punishment. It is framed as the only stance that creates learning, trust, and system repair. Once a leader owns the result, excuses lose their power and improvement can begin.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the book's namesake principle and the first concrete answer to the intro's leadership frame. It is distinct because it turns leadership from general pressure management into radical responsibility for outcomes.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter re-enters the blue-on-blue Ramadi failure and refuses the instinct to spread blame outward.
Then it shows Willink treating the event as a leadership failure requiring system correction.
Then it argues that the same ownership posture applies to teams, companies, and everyday leadership contexts because responsibility is the precondition for improvement.
