Core Claim:
This chapter argues that ego, arrogance, and status-protection break coordination even inside elite teams. The chapter's force comes from the reminder that high performers can still damage the mission when pride makes them dismiss input, compete internally, or protect status instead of working together. The chapter says humility is not softness. It is strategic discipline that keeps people teachable, cooperative, and focused on the mission rather than on themselves.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It shifts from mission belief to anti-ego discipline. Its mechanism is not purpose or buy-in. Its mechanism is humility and cross-unit respect preserving coordination.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter shows that elite status or confidence can turn corrosive when ego outruns mission focus.
Then it shows internal rivalry, arrogance, or dismissiveness weakening coordination.
Then it argues that humility, respect, and teachability improve execution because they keep the mission above personal status.
