Core Claim:
This chapter argues that leaders must fully understand and believe in the mission before they can expect real buy-in from the people below them. The Iraqi partner-force mission gives the chapter its force because the SEALs initially resist the work until leaders explain why building Iraqi capability matters strategically. The chapter says compliance is weaker than belief. When leaders cannot answer why a mission matters, confusion and resentment spread downward. When leaders believe and can explain the purpose clearly, execution strengthens because people understand what they are actually helping accomplish.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It shifts from leadership effect on team standards to leadership effect on understanding and commitment. Its distinct mechanism is mission belief, not ownership or raw standards.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter shows resistance to a mission that feels unattractive or unclear.
Then it shows leaders needing to understand the strategic why before they can persuade others.
Then it argues that belief travels down the chain and improves execution because people understand purpose, not just orders.
