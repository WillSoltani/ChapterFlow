Core Claim:
This chapter argues that leaders cannot centralize every decision if they want teams to move effectively under pressure. The chapter's force comes from the idea that front-line action depends on junior leaders having enough intent, authority, and trust to decide where the work is actually unfolding. Decentralized command is treated as controlled distribution, not as loss of discipline.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It shifts from priority sequence to distributed decision-making. Its mechanism is trusted authority at the front, not priority selection alone.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter shows why centralized decision bottlenecks slow execution.
Then it argues that leaders must train and trust junior leaders with clear intent.
Then it shows that decisions improve when authority and understanding move closer to the action.

Required Anchors:
- leaders training and trusting junior leaders
- decisions happening at the front instead of bottlenecking at the top
- mission success depending on intent and distributed authority rather than central control alone
