# Style Memory — Extreme Ownership

Operating cheat sheet for writer, editor, and converter. Distilled from the v13 pack style files plus this run's frozen-source limits.

## Voice
Concrete, forceful, and disciplined without sounding like boot-camp cosplay. Keep the prose readable, specific, and grounded in recognizable stakes rather than macho slogans.

## Book-specific posture
- This book teaches leadership through battlefield stories, but the lesson is responsibility, clarity, trust, and execution under pressure.
- Keep the chain visible: own the problem, align the team, communicate clearly, act decisively, and improve the system.
- Use Ramadi anchors and business parallels only when the frozen bundle supports them.
- The book is not a general self-esteem manual and not a celebration of aggression for its own sake.

## Non-negotiables
- Use only claims supported by the brief and chapter source sidecar.
- No em dashes.
- No invented battlefield details, body counts, page claims, or business case specifics.
- No raw source splice into reader-facing prose.
- No contamination phrases, prompt leakage, or validator language.

## Banned drift
- Do not turn the book into generic hustle or accountability talk.
- Do not glorify war or treat strategic violence as a personality trait to copy.
- Do not flatten every chapter into "take responsibility and work harder."
- Do not make the examples sound like military fan fiction.

## Depth contract
- Easy: one live frame, one concrete contrast, one practical implication.
- Medium: mechanism, friction, and application.
- Hard: preserve a real limit, tension, or leadership tradeoff.

## Tone differentiation
- Gentle: lowers defensiveness and clarifies responsibility.
- Direct: names the operating principle plainly.
- Competitive: sharpens cost, consequence, and urgency without turning cartoonish.

## Book-specific craft checks
- Intro chapter must explain why leadership is the organizing factor under chaos.
- Part I chapters must stay on internal leadership posture, not reuse Part II mechanics.
- Part II chapters must stay operational and team-oriented rather than personal-motivation heavy.
- Part III chapters must keep command, planning, and leadership balance distinct from the earlier material.
