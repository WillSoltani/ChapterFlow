Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that Meditations begins by naming the people, habits, and restraints that formed Marcus Aurelius before the book asks him to command himself under pressure.

Opening Move:
Open on the surprise that a ruler begins with debts rather than declarations, then move into the moral logic of learning virtue by observing people closely.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for the opening lineage of grandfather, mother, and early teachers
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for Rusticus, Epictetus, and disciplined simplicity
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for Antoninus Pius and unshowy public duty
- anchor 4 used in paragraph(s): P6-P7 for the closing thanks to the gods and the limit between received standards and lived standards

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: open on the oddity and significance of starting with debts
- P2: show how the early list turns virtue into observed conduct rather than theory
- P3: deepen discipline through Rusticus and the anti-display standard
- P4: explain why this chapter is about formation, not flattery or biography
- P5: use Antoninus Pius as the largest political model of measured power
- P6: widen to gratitude toward the gods and conditions that prevented corruption
- P7 if needed: bridge into the harder test of Book II

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes inherited standards from embodied standards. Gratitude functions as moral accounting, not as mood.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Seeing virtue in others can either sharpen your standards or become a way of hiding behind admiration. The hard layer must keep that risk visible.

Threshold Question:
What does a person do when he can name the virtues he owes to others but still has to prove them under pressure himself?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- character as inheritance before assertion
- gratitude as moral accounting
- Rusticus and anti-display discipline
- Antoninus Pius as model of power without vanity
- the gap between admired virtue and enacted virtue

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, school): credit a demanding teacher's standard instead of treating discipline as self-invented
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, personal): trace a good habit back to a family example and see the obligation it creates
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, work): distinguish steady leadership from loud leadership
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, school): a student stops performing seriousness and starts copying a mentor's actual method
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): gratitude as honest accounting versus sentimental thanks
- Scenario 6 (before_after, work): moving from status display to quiet reliability

Preview Constraint:
The preview must point to the first real pressure test in Book II: difficult people, mortality, and the demand to act now.

Genericity Risks:

- flattening the chapter into role-model cliches

Forbidden Drift:
