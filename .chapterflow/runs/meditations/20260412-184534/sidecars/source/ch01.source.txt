Book: Meditations
Chapter: 1
Title: Book I
Locked edition: George Long translation (Project Gutenberg ebook 15877)

Frozen support:
- The opening book consists of a ledger of lessons learned from relatives, teachers, governors, and exemplary rulers.
- Rusticus is explicitly tied to discipline, simplicity, reconciliation, careful reading, and access to Epictetus.
- Sextus is tied to benevolence, living according to nature, and gravity without affectation.
- Antoninus Pius is described as mild, steady, anti-flattery, economical, careful in deliberation, and free from display.
- The closing section thanks the gods for family, teachers, correction, and the chance to live near private simplicity even within imperial power.

Supported chapter-local notes:
- Book I is not a command manual yet. It is a moral inheritance ledger.
- Gratitude here is practical and diagnostic. It shows which traits Marcus thinks are worth carrying forward.
- The chapter repeatedly values restraint, simplicity, anti-display, fairness, endurance, and reconciliation.
- Rusticus matters because he turns philosophy away from showmanship and toward discipline.
- Antoninus Pius matters because he shows what power looks like when it is steady, economical, and not hungry for display.
- The closing thanks to the gods widens the ledger from people to conditions, luck, correction, and protection from corruption.
- The chapter supports a real limit: receiving good examples does not itself prove that one can live up to them.

Allowed claims:
- Marcus opens with debts rather than self-congratulation
- the chapter treats virtue as learned through concrete examples
- gratitude functions as a moral inventory
- the bridge to Book II can frame the next test as action under pressure rather than gratitude alone

Do not claim:
- invented scenes with named teachers beyond what the text supports
- exact biographical timelines not contained in Book I
- modern psychology of gratitude
- certainty about disputed note-level identities where the text itself is cautious
