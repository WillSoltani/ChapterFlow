Chapter 3 source sidecar
Book: Meditations
Chapter: 3
Title: Book III
Locked edition: George Long translation (Project Gutenberg ebook 15877)

Frozen support:
- Marcus warns that understanding may fail before life ends and says "we must make haste."
- The chapter trains perception through ripe figs, bent grain, old age, and other natural consequences that still belong to beauty.
- It attacks wasted life spent in thoughts about others and calls for simple, benevolent, purposeful inner content.
- It praises standing erect, preferring justice, truth, temperance, fortitude, and the deity planted within over praise or pleasure.
- It ends by urging principles kept ready like a physician's tools and by throwing away idle hopes while there is still time.

Supported chapter-local notes:
- Book III is urgency without panic.
- The beauty passage belongs to perceptual discipline: seeing nature truly, even when consequence includes decay.
- Idle curiosity about others is treated as theft from one's own ruling power.
- The chapter repeatedly narrows life toward a few worthy goods and ready principles.
- Praise, fame, and pleasure are demoted because they distract from rational and social good.

Allowed claims:
- understanding can fail before death, so self-command cannot be postponed forever
- beauty can be perceived in natural consequence rather than only polished form
- a few ready principles protect action under pressure
- a good life stands erect rather than needing external support

Do not claim:
- that Marcus advocates panic or speed for its own sake
- that the chapter rejects all social concern
- that natural decay is good because suffering is unreal
- modern productivity frameworks not in the source
