Chapter 2 source sidecar
Book: Meditations
Chapter: 2
Title: Book II
Locked edition: George Long translation (Project Gutenberg ebook 15877)

Frozen support:
- The book opens with a morning instruction to expect difficult people while remembering shared nature and cooperation.
- Marcus reduces the self to flesh, breath, and the ruling part, then insists the ruling part must not become enslaved.
- The chapter ties personal action to providence, the whole universe, and a fixed limit of time.
- It instructs the reader to do each act as if it were the last and not to lose life by prying into other minds.
- It states "all is opinion," says the present is all anyone loses, and defines philosophy as keeping the inner daemon free from violence and aimlessness.

Supported chapter-local notes:
- Book II is the first active self-command chapter after the inheritance map of Book I.
- The opening command is realistic, not sentimental. Difficult people remain difficult, but their vice does not license hatred.
- The ruling faculty is the chapter's center of gravity. Marcus keeps separating inward rule from body, reputation, pain, and other externals.
- Mortality is used as a deadline for purpose rather than as a reason to collapse.
- The chapter repeatedly treats distraction by externals and by other minds as self-harm.

Allowed claims:
- difficult people can be anticipated without surrendering cooperation
- the ruling part is the chapter's protected inner jurisdiction
- every act should be done with purpose under limited time
- philosophy is defined as protection of inward government

Do not claim:
- that Marcus denies pain or political duty
- that "all is opinion" means facts do not matter
- specific war context for every passage
- modern self-help language about boundaries or mindset hacks
