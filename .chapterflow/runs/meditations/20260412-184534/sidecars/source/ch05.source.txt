Book: Meditations
Chapter: 5
Title: Book V
Locked edition: George Long translation (Project Gutenberg ebook 15877)

Frozen support:
- Book V opens by attacking morning reluctance: a human being rises for human work, not for warm bedclothes.
- Marcus compares human duty to plants, birds, ants, spiders, and bees all doing their part in the order of the world.
- The chapter keeps returning to quick inner reset, right action without concern for blame, and the ruling part's power to wipe away troublesome impressions.
- It widens from work discipline to social action done without scorekeeping, acceptance of what common nature prescribes, and repeated return to philosophy without melodrama.
- Later sections restate key Stoic boundaries: things do not touch the soul directly, obstacles can become aids, the common good is the real scale of harm, and good fortune is the disposition of the soul.

Supported chapter-local notes:
- Book V is the anti-reluctance book.
- The morning passage is not mere productivity talk; it is about function, nature, and social labor.
- Return matters almost as much as steadiness: Marcus explicitly allows failure and insists on returning again to right principles.
- Social action must be done without theatrical self-congratulation.
- The chapter keeps human work tied to common nature, not private achievement.

Allowed claims:
- reluctance in the morning is answered by remembering human function
- work is dignified because it belongs to rational and social nature
- troublesome impressions can be wiped away quickly
- right action should not be stalled by praise, blame, or self-display
- philosophy is a repeated return to proportion, not a dramatic escape from life

Do not claim:
- that Marcus is endorsing hustle for status or output metrics
- that rest is condemned absolutely rather than bounded
- that social duty means becoming a martyr for every demand
- that external success is the chapter's reward for disciplined work
