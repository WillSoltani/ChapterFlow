Chapter 4 source sidecar
Book: Meditations
Chapter: 4
Title: Book IV
Locked edition: George Long translation (Project Gutenberg ebook 15877)

Frozen support:
- The ruling part adapts to what is possible and uses opposition like fire using what falls into it.
- Marcus says the fastest retreat is into one's own soul and gives ready principles about externals not touching the soul and all things changing quickly.
- The chapter argues from common reason to common law, fellow citizenship, and the world as a political community.
- It says complaint depends on the opinion of harm and that taking away the opinion removes the complaint.
- It returns repeatedly to fame's emptiness, change, mortality, and the short way of natural reason.

Supported chapter-local notes:
- Book IV is not withdrawal from duty but renewal for cleaner action.
- The obstacle image belongs to adaptation under purpose, not to forced optimism.
- Common citizenship keeps the chapter from collapsing into private serenity only.
- The chapter demotes praise and complaint by shrinking them inside change and common law.
- Justice, reason, and simplicity remain active demands, not passive moods.

Allowed claims:
- opposition can become material for stronger inward action
- retreat inward can restore justice and perspective
- complaint often depends on an opinion that can be examined
- fame and praise are too unstable to rank against reason

Do not claim:
- that Marcus says nothing can hurt the body
- that retreat means disappearing from community
- that world citizenship erases local duties
- modern self-care or optimization language
