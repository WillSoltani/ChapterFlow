Book: Meditations
Chapter: 6
Title: Book VI
Locked edition: George Long translation (Project Gutenberg ebook 15877)

Frozen support:
- Book VI opens by grounding everything in the universe's obedient substance and the governing reason that does no evil.
- Marcus then insists that duty stays duty whether one is cold or warm, drowsy or rested, praised or blamed, living or dying.
- The chapter repeatedly says to look within, strip appearances bare, and judge fame, luxury, and outward admiration by their actual worth.
- It pushes rational and political life as the true scale: the valuable soul is one fitted for social life, and the useful city is ultimately the world.
- Later sections gather practical social severity: do not become like wrongdoers, teach without anger, imitate Antoninus rather than Caesar, adapt to one's lot sincerely, and remember that what is not good for the swarm is not good for the bee.

Supported chapter-local notes:
- Book VI is a widening book: from self-command to public reason and cosmic scale.
- Duty is detached from weather, mood, praise, and bodily convenience.
- The chapter treats analysis of appearances as moral protection, not cynical debunking.
- Political or civic language is central here; the rational soul is social by nature.
- Simplicity, justice, and cooperation remain stronger themes than private serenity.

Allowed claims:
- duty does not wait on favorable conditions
- public life must stay aligned with reason instead of image or power
- appearances should be stripped to their real substance
- the rational creature belongs to a larger civic and cosmic order
- correction of others should happen without anger and without imitation of their vice

Do not claim:
- that Marcus endorses political domination or imperial self-importance
- that cosmic order removes the need for judgment in practical life
- that stripping appearances means despising material reality itself
- that social duty requires pleasing everyone
