High-confidence chapter notes:
- Gallo uses Bryan Stevenson's TED talk on injustice as the primary anchor for the chapter.
- Stevenson mixes heavy prison-system data with stories from his life and family, and Gallo treats that mix as a model of persuasive storytelling.
- Gallo explains the talk through Aristotle's rhetoric triad: ethos, logos, and pathos.
- In Gallo's breakdown of Stevenson's talk, pathos occupies the largest share, with logos and ethos supporting it.
- Data without narrative can distance the audience; story lowers emotional barriers and makes agreement more likely.
- Gallo groups presentation stories into three usable types: personal stories, stories about other people, and brand-success stories.
- The chapter also stresses that audiences want a hero and some movement through struggle toward resolution.
- The closing claim is that if ideas are valuable, story is one of the main ways they get exchanged.
