Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader understands why strong TED-style delivery feels conversational rather than recited, and how practice plus congruent vocal and physical signals make that feeling believable.

Opening Move:
Open on a speaker who knows the words cold but still sounds trapped inside them until rehearsal turns the script back into live speech.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with the paradox that natural-sounding delivery is usually heavily rehearsed
- P2: explain conversation-at-scale and why underpreparation increases self-consciousness instead of authenticity
- P3: introduce passion, practice, and presence as the chapter's operating frame
- P4: break verbal delivery into rate, volume, pitch, and pauses
- P5: turn to body-language congruence and why mismatch reads false quickly
- P6: practical paragraph on gestures, movement, anti-fidgeting review, and the failure mode of performance tricks
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 4 by asking how even a well-delivered talk earns novelty and surprise

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Conversational delivery is not the opposite of rehearsal. Rehearsal is what frees the speaker to sound present instead of mechanically self-aware.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The same preparation that creates presence can also create stiffness if it becomes line-protection instead of idea ownership.

Threshold Question:
When does practice disappear into presence, and when does it remain visible as control?
