Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader understands how one well-designed wow moment can make a talk more memorable without turning it into a stunt show.

Opening Move:
Open on an audience settling into a serious talk, then interrupt that expectation with one vivid, unexpected action that permanently changes what they remember.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with the claim that memorable talks often contain one peak moment that breaks routine
- P2: explain why surprise and emotionally heightened moments stick more than steady explanation
- P3: use Bill Gates to show the unforgettable power of a brief unexpected demonstration
- P4: use Jill Bolte Taylor and Steve Jobs to show vivid props and staged reveal as wow structures
- P5: practical paragraph on wow tools such as props, stats, visuals, headlines, and personal stories
- P6: hard-edge paragraph on creativity over showmanship and why the peak must serve the point
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 6 by asking how warmth and humor keep the room open after the peak

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The wow moment is not the message. It is a designed peak that makes the message harder to ignore and easier to remember.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The same surprise that makes a talk memorable can also hijack attention if it becomes disconnected from the idea it was supposed to support.

Threshold Question:
What makes a moment unforgettable: how extreme it is, or how cleanly it dramatizes the point?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

If the chapter makes one argument from multiple practical angles, use the lower end.
If it builds a multi-part mechanism plus a real warning, use the higher end.

- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): turning a predictable appeal into a memorable peak that still serves the point
