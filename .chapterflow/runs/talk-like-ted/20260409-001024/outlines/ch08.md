Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader understands why multisensory and concrete imagery can make an idea easier to picture, easier to grasp, and easier to remember without turning the talk into decoration.

Opening Move:
Open on an abstract idea that feels hard to hold, then show how one clear sensory picture makes the audience see it instead of merely hearing about it.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: introduce the value of vivid mental picture-making
- P2: explain why sensory concreteness helps the audience imagine the point
- P3: show how imagery supports memory and recall
- P4: clarify that vividness should serve the idea rather than decorate it
- P5: practical paragraph on making abstractions more pictureable through concrete detail
- P6: hard-edge paragraph on the risk of ornamental description and drift
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 9 by asking how originality keeps vividness authentic

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Imagery matters because it helps the audience picture and hold the idea, not because description is automatically impressive.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The same sensory detail that makes a point memorable can also distract if it becomes ornamental or excessive.

Threshold Question:
What makes vivid language useful in a talk: the beauty of the description, or the clarity of the mental picture it creates?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6
