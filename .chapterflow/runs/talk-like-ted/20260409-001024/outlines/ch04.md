Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader understands why useful novelty wakes an audience up, and how to make content feel fresh without turning the talk into a gimmick.

Opening Move:
Open on a room that thinks it already knows the topic until the speaker reframes it in a way that makes the familiar feel newly visible.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with novelty as an attention shift rather than a trivia dump
- P2: explain that newness can be a fresh frame on known material, not only unseen facts
- P3: use Ballard and Cameron to show curiosity and discovery at work
- P4: use Hans Rosling to show data becoming newly legible through presentation
- P5: hard-edge paragraph on clichés, exhausted solutions, and defeatist nothing-new-to-add thinking
- P6: practical paragraph on teaching one useful new thing and stating it clearly
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 5 by asking how novelty becomes unforgettable in a single moment

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Novelty is not spectacle by itself. It is the experience of seeing, learning, or understanding something in a way that feels newly useful.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Freshness can sharpen attention or collapse into shallow cleverness depending on whether the surprise changes the audience's understanding.

Threshold Question:
What actually makes an idea feel new: the fact itself, or the frame that lets the audience see it differently?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6
