Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader understands why stories make ideas more persuasive and memorable, and how to choose narrative material that serves the message rather than distracting from it.

Opening Move:
Open on Bryan Stevenson holding a room with stories inside a talk that could have drowned in prison-system statistics.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P2-P4
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P4-P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: hook with Stevenson and the surprise that a serious talk wins by leaning heavily on narrative
- P2: introduce ethos, logos, and pathos as the explanatory frame
- P3: show why data without story creates distance and why story lowers emotional barriers
- P4: break down the three story types and what each does
- P5: explain hero, tension, and movement toward resolution
- P6: hard-edge paragraph on the limit: story must carry an idea rather than becoming anecdotal clutter
- P7 if needed: bridge to Chapter 3 by asking how a speaker delivers story conversationally

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
Story is not decoration added after the idea is built. It is one of the main ways the idea becomes human enough to travel.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The same narrative power that creates connection can collapse into sentiment, self-display, or entertainment if the story is not tied to a clear governing point.

Threshold Question:
When does a story deepen an idea, and when does it simply make the speaker more interesting than the message?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 6

If the chapter makes one argument from multiple angles, use the lower end.
If it builds a multi-part framework, use the higher end.
Do not leave this decision to the converter.

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: story lowers emotional barriers
- Takeaway 2: ethos / logos / pathos need balance
- Takeaway 3: pathos often does more work than standard business advice admits
- Takeaway 4: three story types and their uses
- Takeaway 5: hero and tension make story memorable
- Takeaway 6: anecdote without idea is a trap
