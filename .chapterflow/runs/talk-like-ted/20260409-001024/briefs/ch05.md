Core Claim:
Gallo argues that memorable talks contain peak moments that surprise, delight, shock, or vividly demonstrate the point. These wow moments make the audience more likely to remember both the moment and the surrounding message. Bill Gates, Jill Bolte Taylor, and Steve Jobs show that the strongest surprises are not random stunts. They are staged reveals, props, or demonstrations that make the idea impossible to ignore.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is not just about fresh content. It is about engineered peaks inside the delivery of that content. Its mechanism is surprise in service of retention. Chapter 4 asked whether the idea felt new. Chapter 5 asks how to make one part of the talk hit hard enough to stick.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author argues that wow moments create emotional peaks that help an audience remember.
Then the author uses Bill Gates to show how a brief unexpected demonstration can dominate memory.
Then the author uses Jill Bolte Taylor to show how a vivid prop can create visceral attention.
Then the author uses Steve Jobs to show staged reveal and delight through product demonstration.
Then the author lists practical tools for creating wow moments without depending on extravagance.
Then the author stresses that creativity matters more than showmanship and that the presentation should end on a high note.

Required Anchors:
- Bill Gates: malaria mosquito release as an unexpected and highly memorable demonstration.
- Jill Bolte Taylor: real human brain prop as a vivid physical wow moment.
- Steve Jobs: staged reveal and demonstration, including the three-devices-to-one reveal.
- wow methods list: props and demos, shocking statistics, pictures or videos, memorable headlines, personal stories.
- end on a high note rule
