Chapter 6 argues that scheduling deep work is not enough if the mind itself has been trained to flee boredom. A person can reserve two quiet hours and still find that their attention keeps reaching for novelty every few minutes. Newport treats that pattern as a training problem. The issue is not simply that distractions exist. The issue is that repeated checking teaches the mind to expect quick stimulation on demand.

That is why the chapter asks the reader to embrace boredom rather than eliminate it immediately. Mild boredom is the moment when attention learns whether it will remain under command or automatically chase relief. If every urge to check is rewarded, then the next deep-work session begins with a weaker mind than the one before it. The shallow habit is being rehearsed long before the serious work starts.

The chapter's practical answer is to stop negotiating with every impulse in real time. Pre-decided rules around when distraction is allowed are stronger than constantly asking whether this one glance would be acceptable. That rule-based approach creates distance between urge and action. It keeps the mind from interpreting every flicker of restlessness as an instruction.

A second important move is treating recovery as part of the skill. Attention will wander. The point is not to achieve impossible purity. The point is to notice the drift and return deliberately. Chapter 6 therefore turns concentration into training. The mind becomes stronger when boredom stops functioning like an emergency and starts functioning like practice.

The chapter is therefore less moralistic than it first sounds. It is not condemning pleasure or light stimulation. It is asking whether the reader wants to keep outsourcing command of attention to whatever feels easiest in the moment. Once that question becomes active, boredom stops looking like wasted time and starts looking like one of the places where freedom is rebuilt. The chapter wants attention to become more directed, more trainable, and less cheaply pulled by habit. That is the real training edge.
