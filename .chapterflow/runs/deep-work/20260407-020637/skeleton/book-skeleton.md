# Book Skeleton — Deep Work

## Metadata
- Title: *Deep Work*
- Author: Cal Newport
- Locked edition: Original English edition, Grand Central Publishing, 2016
- Package map: 9 validated ChapterFlow chapters

## Chapter order and intent
1. `ch01` Introduction
   Intent: define deep work, shallow work, and the book's central hypothesis.
   Source richness: medium.
   Concept density: medium-high.
2. `ch02` Deep Work Is Valuable
   Intent: argue that deep work helps people learn hard things quickly and produce high-value output.
   Source richness: high.
   Concept density: high.
3. `ch03` Deep Work Is Rare
   Intent: explain why modern organizations reward shallow work and visible busyness.
   Source richness: high.
   Concept density: high.
4. `ch04` Deep Work Is Meaningful
   Intent: connect depth to satisfaction, craftsmanship, and meaning.
   Source richness: medium-high.
   Concept density: medium-high.
5. `ch05` Work Deeply
   Intent: turn the thesis into scheduling, ritual, and shutdown practices.
   Source richness: medium-high.
   Concept density: high.
6. `ch06` Embrace Boredom
   Intent: explain why concentration must be trained and distraction constrained.
   Source richness: medium.
   Concept density: medium-high.
7. `ch07` Quit Social Media
   Intent: apply a craftsman-style filter to digital tools and distraction.
   Source richness: medium-high.
   Concept density: medium-high.
8. `ch08` Drain the Shallows
   Intent: reduce shallow obligations and defend time for meaningful work.
   Source richness: medium-high.
   Concept density: high.
9. `ch09` Conclusion
   Intent: synthesize the case for a deep life and close on durable practice.
   Source richness: low.
   Concept density: medium.

## Moral complexity flags
- Do not romanticize overwork as depth.
- Distinguish necessary shallow work from shallow default living.
- Treat tool reduction as selective design, not purity theater.

## Rotation plan for examples
- Every chapter uses 2 work, 2 school, and 2 personal examples.
- Work examples rotate among office knowledge work, creative production, teaching, and management.
- School examples rotate among undergrad, graduate, and high-school settings.
- Personal examples rotate among home routines, devices, family logistics, and solitary creative practice.

## School-setting plan
- Keep school settings contemporary and concrete: library, lab, dorm, lecture hall, office hours, shared apartment desk.
