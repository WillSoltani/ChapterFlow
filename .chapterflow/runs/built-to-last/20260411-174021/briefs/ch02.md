Core Claim:
The chapter argues that durable greatness comes from building an institution that can keep producing value over time, not from relying on one gifted leader or one signature program. Clock building shifts attention from brilliance in a moment to design that survives the moment. It reframes leadership as building a system others can inherit and operate. The chapter's force comes from turning a vivid metaphor into an organizational principle.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the first real answer to Chapter 1's opening question. If Chapter 1 says study institutions, Chapter 2 says what to look for first inside them.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author rejects the instinct to equate greatness with a brilliant time teller.
Then the author shows that lasting impact comes from building an organization that keeps working.
Then the author concludes that the institution, not the momentary star, is the true strategic asset.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- Clock building: building an institution that can keep working over time.
- Time telling: producing a strong answer or move that still depends on a singular figure.
- Institutional inheritance: designing something others can keep using after the original builder is gone.

Specific Applications:
- evaluating founders and managers by what survives them
- designing teams that do not depend on one hero
- distinguishing system quality from one-off brilliance
