An institution can become weaker while still looking successful. The systems exist, the leaders know their roles, and the organization begins accepting work that would once have felt too loose or merely adequate. Chapter 9 goes after that drift. Durable companies do not let success redefine the minimum downward. They build standards that keep the institution from settling into comfort.

That is the force of Good Enough Never Is. The chapter is not endorsing private perfectionism, cruelty, or permanent strain for its own sake. It is arguing that excellence has to be built into the system. Standards belong to the institution, not to one intense leader who temporarily raises the temperature.

The connection to Chapter 8 matters. Internal continuity can preserve the core, but continuity alone can still become soft. A company needs standards strong enough to stop inherited stability from turning into complacency. Otherwise the system slowly normalizes work that falls short of its own stated ambitions.

This is why the chapter treats standards as institutional discipline. They are not side comments about quality. They are a way of keeping the company honest when failure is not dramatic enough to force urgency from the outside. The best standards prevent decline from becoming respectable.

The warning has to stay visible. Pressure can go bad when leaders turn standards into vanity, fear, or punishment theater. High expectations are not automatically healthy. They serve the institution only when they protect mission, quality, and integrity rather than the self-image of whoever is enforcing them.

So Chapter 9 is about resisting adequacy without collapsing into personal obsession. Durable institutions need standards that keep progress alive even after success arrives. With that final pressure in place, the next chapter can gather the whole system and return the burden of building it to the reader.
