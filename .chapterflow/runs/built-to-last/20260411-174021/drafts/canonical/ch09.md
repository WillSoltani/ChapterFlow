Success can make an institution feel safer than it really is. Systems are in place, leadership continuity looks stronger, and people begin to assume that what already works will keep working at roughly the same level. Chapter 9 turns against that comfort. Enduring companies do not treat adequacy as a stable resting place. They build standards that keep the institution from settling there.

That is what Good Enough Never Is means in this book's logic. The chapter is not praising private perfectionism or emotional severity. It is saying that durable institutions embed pressure against complacency into the system itself. The standards belong to the organization, not to one obsessive personality. Excellence becomes part of the operating environment rather than a temporary burst of managerial intensity.

The bridge from Chapter 8 makes the need clear. Once a company has culture, experimentation, and internal leadership continuity, it still faces a quieter risk: success can soften the edge of all those strengths. Leaders can inherit a functioning system and gradually start accepting work that would once have looked too loose, too average, or too comfortable. The chapter exists to keep that erosion visible.

That is why standards are not a side theme here. They are a structural safeguard. A strong institution sets expectations that resist drift toward adequacy even when nobody is panicking and nobody is failing dramatically. The point is not to create fear. The point is to prevent the organization from normalizing decline in small, respectable increments.

Still, the danger is real. Standards can become vanity, punishment theater, or burnout with noble language attached. Leaders can confuse institutional excellence with making people feel perpetually pressed. The chapter only works if the pressure is serving the mission and the quality of the institution rather than the ego of the people enforcing it.

So the chapter's force is sharper than a slogan about excellence. Good enough never is because durable institutions cannot rely on yesterday's success to protect tomorrow's quality. They need standards that keep the system honest and alive. Once that piece is in place, the book can finally ask what the whole framework now demands from the reader as a builder.
