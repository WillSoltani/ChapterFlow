Some large goals change nothing except the leader's posture. They sound exciting in the room, land well on slides, and then sit there like decoration. Other goals do something more serious. They pull attention into one direction, force tradeoffs, and make people decide what they will actually risk together. Chapter 5 is about that second kind of goal. Collins and Porras give it a famous name: the Big Hairy Audacious Goal, or BHAG.

The term matters because the chapter is not praising size for its own sake. A BHAG is a stretch target that organizes movement. It is large enough to demand commitment, coordination, and courage, yet it is supposed to serve the institution rather than flatter the people at the top. The chapter lands only if that distinction stays clear. Audacity can be useful. Audacity can also turn theatrical very fast.

That pressure comes directly out of Chapter 4. Once a company knows it should preserve its core and stimulate progress, the next problem is direction. Progress without direction becomes scattered motion. Teams stay busy, projects multiply, and energy spreads across too many priorities. A bold goal can solve that by giving progress a destination large enough to matter. The aim is not constant novelty. The aim is concentrated movement.

This is why the chapter treats ambition as a coordinating device. A strong big goal makes people line up effort that would otherwise stay fragmented. It creates a common test for decisions: does this move us toward the target or not? In that sense, the goal does more than inspire. It disciplines. It narrows. It forces the company to choose what deserves resources, patience, and sacrifice.

Still, the danger sits close by. A large target can become an ego monument if leaders treat scale itself as proof of seriousness. Then the goal stops serving the core and starts serving image. The institution performs ambition instead of using ambition to strengthen itself. That is the chapter's hard edge. A BHAG works only when it is tethered to values and purpose strong enough to tell the company why this stretch matters.

So the chapter is not a pep talk about dreaming bigger. It is an argument about directed institutional audacity. The right bold goal concentrates progress, but it does so under discipline. It points beyond the current state without replacing the core with spectacle. If Chapter 4 separated the center from the changing edge, Chapter 5 asks what kind of far target can make that moving edge count.

Once that question is in place, another one follows. If people are going to live under pressure from a large shared target, what kind of culture keeps that pressure energizing instead of corrosive? That bridge leads into the next chapter.
