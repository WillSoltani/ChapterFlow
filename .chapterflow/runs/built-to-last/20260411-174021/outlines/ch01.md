Chapter Promise:
By the end, the reader should understand why the book starts with a comparative study of enduring companies instead of a string of founder legends.

Opening Move:
Open on the familiar temptation to explain greatness by pointing at one brilliant person, then break that instinct by shifting the frame toward institutions that last longer than their heroes.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P2-P3 Stanford six-year study and eighteen-company frame
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P4 roll call of companies across industries
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5 paired comparison companies

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: tension between heroic storytelling and institution-building
- P2: anchor the six-year research project
- P3: explain why comparison companies matter
- P4: widen the frame with the cross-industry company set
- P5: make the comparison logic concrete with company pairs
- P6: define the chapter's distinct claim and its limit
- P7: bridge into what a durable institution is actually built from

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes admiration from evidence. A visionary company is not just famous or successful; it is a durable institution whose pattern becomes clearer when set against a matched rival.

Hard-Depth Tension:
The chapter must admit the risk of survivor myth and hindsight bias while still defending the value of comparative pattern-finding.

Threshold Question:
What kind of evidence makes a famous success story worth treating as a repeatable institutional lesson rather than a flattering legend?

- Takeaway 4: cross-industry durability matters
- Takeaway 5: fame still needs a limit check
