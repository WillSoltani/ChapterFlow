## Rotation Plan For Examples

- Wave 1 focus: work hierarchy, school organizations, family traditions, student clubs, early-career management, founder dynamics.
- Reserve stronger founder-board and institution-design examples for chapters 4 through 8 where identity and continuity pressures are sharper.
- Keep chapter 1 examples diagnostic and comparative; keep chapter 2 examples about institution-vs-hero tradeoffs.
- Do not let more than one scenario per chapter reduce to "be humble with your boss." Each chapter needs six distinct lesson targets.

## School-Setting Plan

- Rotate among seminar, student newspaper, robotics team, debate club, department lab, and student government.
- Use school settings where hierarchy, standards, tradition, and institutional memory can be felt concretely.
- Avoid reusing the same authority figure dynamic across adjacent chapters.

## Vocabulary Watchlist

- Avoid generic business-speak such as synergy, culture fit, best practice, transformational, alignment workshop.
- Prefer concrete institutional vocabulary: operating system, founder imprint, standards, selection pressure, continuity, succession, discipline.
- Keep BHAG language precise and avoid empty ambition rhetoric.

## Motif And Callback Opportunities

- Built institution vs brilliant moment
- Identity vs adaptation
- Standard-setting vs comfort
- Internal pipeline vs imported savior
- Experimentation vs prediction

## Thin-Chapter Risk Notes

- Chapter 3 risks turning into generic purpose-talk if not anchored to ideology and institutional consequences.
- Chapter 5 risks collapsing into motivational goal-setting language.
- Chapter 8 risks sounding like generic promotion-from-within advice.
- Chapter 9 risks flattening into perfectionism slogans.
- Chapter 10 risks recap drift unless it preserves a live application question.

## Premium-Routing Candidates

- `ch06` because moral complexity around exclusion pressure needs disciplined framing.
- `ch07` because experimentation language can drift generic fast.
