# Style Memory

- Write like a sharp human explainer with taste: specific, concrete, compressed, and authored.
- Start from pressure, event, conflict, or a live question. Never open with thesis summary or chapter narration.
- Keep paragraph jobs distinct. Use the ledger: anchor, mechanism, tension, limit, implication, bridge.
- Easy must be readable and concrete. Medium must add mechanism and application. Hard must add boundary, contradiction, cost, failure mode, or synthesis.
- Tone variants must differ by function, not volume. Competitive must stay severe but credible, never sloganized.
- Avoid generic self-help, corporate polish, fake-deep abstraction, textbook tone, and writer-note leakage.
- No em dashes. No made-up quotes. No unsupported mechanisms or prestige language.
- Every reader-facing surface must teach directly rather than narrate "the chapter."
- Reinforcement surfaces must do distinct jobs and may not echo each other with recycled stems or endings.
