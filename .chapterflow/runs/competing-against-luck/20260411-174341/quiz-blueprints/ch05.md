# Chapter Quiz Blueprint

Chapter: How to Hear What Your Customers Don't Say
Core concepts to test:
- literal customer wording is incomplete evidence by itself
- circumstances and forces explain what customer statements mean
- push, pull, habit, and anxiety shape switching and hesitation
- interpretation must stay tied to observable tradeoffs and behavior
- the chapter prepares the reader for Chapter 6 resumes of repeated hiring

Bloom ceiling:
- highest justified level for this chapter: evaluate

Question plan:
- q01: orienting distinction between literal wording and deeper listening
- q02: why circumstances matter when interpreting a customer statement
- q03: apply force-based listening to a switch decision
- q04: named work scenario about a buyer asking for a feature while revealing a different job
- q05: trap of overreading one vague comment
- q06: limit case where anxiety blocks a switch even when pull sounds strong
- q07: school scenario about repeated complaints that point to one hidden pressure
- q08: personal-choice transfer from stated preference to inferred progress
- q09: deeper synthesis on why observable compromise matters in listening
- q10: bridge toward Chapter 6 and the resume of repeated hires

Distractor notes:
- tempting wrong answer type 1: take the feature request as the whole job
- tempting wrong answer type 2: invent motives without situation evidence

Explanation diversity notes:
- avoid repeated openers
- each direct explanation must begin differently

CorrectIndex plan:
