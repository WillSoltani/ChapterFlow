# Book Skeleton

## Metadata

- bookId: competing-against-luck
- title: Competing Against Luck: The Story of Innovation and Customer Choice
- author: Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon, David S. Duncan
- locked edition: 2016 English HarperBusiness / HarperCollins trade family
- chapter count: 10

## Chapter Order

1. The Milk Shake Dilemma
   - intent: show that customer choice becomes legible when you study the progress a product is hired to deliver in a real circumstance
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: low
2. Progress, Not Products
   - intent: shift segmentation away from product features and toward the progress people are trying to make
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: low
3. Jobs in the Wild
   - intent: prove that jobs logic travels across categories and contexts, not only the milk-shake case
   - source richness: medium-low
   - concept density: medium
   - moral complexity: low
4. Job Hunting
   - intent: explain how to discover hidden jobs through observation, struggle, and circumstance rather than surveys alone
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: medium
5. How to Hear What Your Customers Don't Say
   - intent: show how unstated anxieties, tradeoffs, and switching friction surface only through deeper listening
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: medium
6. Building Your Resume
   - intent: explain how a product earns repeat hiring through a trustworthy job-specific record, not a feature pile
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: medium
7. Integrating Around a Job
   - intent: show that organizations must coordinate operations around the customer's full job rather than around internal silos
   - source richness: medium-low
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: medium
8. Keeping Your Eye on the Job
   - intent: preserve focus on the customer's progress while push, pull, anxiety, and habit try to distort decisions
   - source richness: medium
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: medium
9. The Jobs-Focused Organization
   - intent: turn Jobs Theory into an operating model for structure, incentives, and decision-making
   - source richness: medium-low
   - concept density: high
   - moral complexity: medium
10. Final Observations About the Theory of Jobs
   - intent: close by clarifying what Jobs Theory can and cannot promise, and why disciplined application still matters
   - source richness: medium-low
   - concept density: medium-high
   - moral complexity: medium

## Rotation Plan For Examples

- keep two work, two school, and two personal examples per chapter
- rotate formats across decision_point, dialogue, dilemma, postmortem, predict_reveal, and before_after
- avoid reusing the same named character in adjacent chapters

## School-Setting Plan

- rotate among student startup lab, case-method classroom, debate team, campus club budget meeting, student paper, and capstone project settings

## Vocabulary Watchlist

- avoid empty innovation jargon: disrupt, ecosystem, synergy, optimization, innovation theater, customer-centricity
- prefer concrete verbs: hire, switch, notice, compare, struggle, trust, coordinate, listen

## Motif / Callback Opportunities

- progress over profile
- causality over correlation
- switching friction and hidden anxiety
- product promises becoming organizational discipline

## Thin-Chapter Risk Notes

- Chapters 3 and 7-10 have thinner primary source support and must stay narrow
- later organizational chapters can easily drift into generic management advice if the briefs are not tied to jobs language and chapter headings
- Chapter 6 needs care not to over-literalize the resume metaphor beyond the frozen bundle

## Premium-Routing Candidates

- Chapter 4 because discovery methods can drift into vague research advice
- Chapter 5 because hearing unsaid needs can invite unsupported psychology language
- Chapter 8 because forces-of-progress framing can sprawl if the chapter loses the job focus
