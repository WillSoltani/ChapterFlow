Core Claim:
The fifth chapter has to show that once teams go to the right struggling situations, they still cannot rely on literal stated preferences alone. The frozen bundle safely supports a narrower claim that customer behavior is driven by circumstances and forces, so the listener has to interpret tradeoffs, anxieties, habits, and desired progress without inventing mind-reading or unsupported psychology. The chapter therefore moves from where to look toward how to hear evidence that buyers reveal indirectly.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 4 teaches teams where to search. Chapter 5 teaches them how to listen once they reach the field, especially when the most important evidence is embedded in behavior, hesitation, substitutions, and compromise rather than in explicit feature requests.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors reject the idea that customers can simply state the job in clean language on demand.
Then they show that listening has to stay tied to circumstances, switching pressure, and the forces shaping movement.
Then they prepare the reader for Chapter 6, where repeated patterns become a usable resume of what a solution gets hired to do.
