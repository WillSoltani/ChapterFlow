Core Claim:
The second chapter deepens the opening case by arguing that a job is really a form of progress people are trying to make in a particular circumstance. The frozen bundle supports Jobs Theory as a lens that goes beyond demographics and product attributes toward functional, social, and emotional dimensions of choice. The chapter therefore needs to explain progress as a richer causal target than the product category without drifting into vague empathy language.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
Chapter 1 teaches that the moment matters more than the category. Chapter 2 teaches what to look for inside that moment: progress, struggle, and the forces that make one answer feel worth hiring. This is the chapter that turns the opening anecdote into a clearer demand model.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors reject product-centered and demographic-centered explanations as too shallow.
Then they define a job as the progress a person is trying to make in specific circumstances.
Then they show that the progress includes functional, emotional, and social dimensions, which is why simple product attributes miss causality.

Required Anchors:
- Jobs Theory defines a job as progress in a particular circumstance; the friction is products and personas are too static, the consequence is progress becomes the better target.
- Functional, social, and emotional forces all shape hiring; the friction is teams often privilege only the practical feature layer, the consequence is partial explanations fail.
- Circumstances change which job is active; the friction is one person can hire different products for different forms of progress, the consequence is progress is situational rather than identity-based.
