# Chapter 6 Canonical Draft

One well-understood hire is useful, but it is not yet a resume. That is the distinction this chapter needs to make. Chapter 5 showed how to hear the forces shaping one hiring decision more clearly. The next step is to ask what becomes visible when similar hires keep happening. If the same kind of progress keeps being trusted to the same solution, then the solution begins to build a record of what buyers believe it can reliably do.

The title *Building Your Resume* names that move. The chapter is not talking about a literal career document. It is using a metaphor for accumulated hiring history. A solution builds a resume through the jobs it keeps getting hired to do, not through the story its maker wants to tell about it. That is why repeated hiring matters more here than one persuasive anecdote or one ambitious positioning statement.

This chapter works only if the metaphor stays disciplined. A resume is narrower and more earned than branding language. It is closer to demonstrated trust. If buyers repeatedly turn to one offering for the same kind of progress under similar pressures, that pattern starts to define what the offering is really known for in practice. The important evidence is not what the company claims the product can do. The important evidence is what buyers keep trusting it to do again.

That also explains the chapter's limit. One vivid success does not automatically create a durable resume. Repetition matters because it shows that the hiring logic is not just a one-time accident. But even repeated use has to be read carefully. The team still has to ask whether the same progress is being hired across those situations or whether several different jobs are being lumped together too quickly. A resume should emerge from consistent hiring evidence, not from hurried pattern hunger.

Once a team sees the resume more clearly, it gains a better basis for judgment. It can compare what the offering is actually trusted for with what the team wishes it stood for. It can see whether expansion ideas build on the earned hiring record or fight against it. It can also see why some moves feel natural to buyers while others demand more re-education than expected. The resume becomes a way to read the product's real standing in buyers' lives.

That is why the chapter matters before the book turns toward organization design. If a solution's real resume is blurry, later decisions about integration, priorities, and consistency will drift. But if the repeated hiring pattern is clearer, the organization has a stronger foundation for deciding what it should align around. That is the bridge forward. Before a company can integrate around a job, it has to know what record of jobs its offering has actually earned.
