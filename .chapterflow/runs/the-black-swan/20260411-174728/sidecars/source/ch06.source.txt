Chapter 6 expands the book's attack on how humans manage uncertainty by turning from filtered evidence to story-shaped understanding. Frozen sources support a narrow paraphrase: the mind prefers smooth causal narrative to messy reality, so randomness and omitted facts get compressed away. This is not just literary style. It changes what people think they know about success, failure, and history. The chapter becomes a bridge from confirmation habits toward Taleb's wider criticism of prediction and explanation.
