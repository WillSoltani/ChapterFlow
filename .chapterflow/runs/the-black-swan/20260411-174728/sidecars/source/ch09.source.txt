Chapter 9 turns from broken samples to broken formalism. Frozen sources support a narrow paraphrase: the ludic fallacy is the error of treating reality as if it behaved like a bounded game with known rules and manageable uncertainty. Taleb's point is not that games are useless. It is that game-like clarity is a poor template for domains where rules shift, hidden variables matter, and tail events dominate. This chapter naturally prepares the next attack on prediction culture.
