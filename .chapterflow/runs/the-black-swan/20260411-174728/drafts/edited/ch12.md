# Edited Draft: ch12

Chapter 12 asks who deserves authority once prediction has lost its glamour. Taleb's answer is narrow: deference should shrink when opacity is high and when status outruns accountability.

The dream of epistemocracy is that influence would track real knowledge. Instead institutions often reward fluent opinion, credentials, and distance from consequences. That is how prestige can outrun evidence.

The chapter is not anti-knowledge. It is anti-unearned authority. Trust should lean toward people with contact, constraint, and exposure to outcomes. That sets up Chapter 13's turn toward robust action.
