# Canonical Draft: ch17

Chapter 17 sharpens the complaint against neat statistics by asking a simpler question: where are they being used? Taleb's target is not arithmetic in the abstract. It is the placement of orderly tools inside domains whose structure does not justify that order.

A bell curve in the wrong place does more than miss a detail. It creates a category mistake. It pretends the world is smoother, more bounded, and more measurable than it is. Once that happens, institutions can start speaking with the confidence of a method that never belonged to the terrain.

The chapter matters because misuse often survives by looking technical. Taleb wants the reader to ask not only whether a calculation is elegant, but whether the domain ever authorized it. That points directly to Chapter 18, where experts keep selling precision long after their tools have outrun their jurisdiction.
