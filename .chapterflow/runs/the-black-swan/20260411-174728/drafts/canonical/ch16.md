# Canonical Draft: ch16

Taleb now turns to a softer but powerful accomplice in error: the beauty of order. Human beings do not merely reason badly about randomness. They are drawn to patterns, smooth lines, coherent stories, and elegant structures because these feel more satisfying than noise.

That aesthetic pull matters because it helps weak models survive. A clean chart, a tidy narrative, or a graceful pattern can look true partly because it looks beautiful. Randomness, by contrast, feels insulting, shapeless, and cognitively expensive.

Chapter 16 explains why false order keeps winning social and mental prestige. The problem is not only statistical ignorance. It is also taste. The next chapter will show what happens when that taste installs neat curves where the world never granted them permission.
