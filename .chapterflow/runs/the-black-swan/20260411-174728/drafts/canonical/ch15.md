# Canonical Draft: ch15

Once Extremistan is visible, the bell curve stops looking universal and starts looking provincial. Taleb's charge is not that mathematics is evil. It is that Gaussian assumptions become dangerously flattering when they are carried into domains where rare events dominate the totals.

In bounded worlds, normal variation can behave decently. In fat-tail worlds, the outlier is not a small nuisance at the edge. It is often the main actor. A framework that treats those extremes as negligible can create false calm, false precision, and false estimates of safety.

That is why Taleb calls the bell curve fraudulent in these settings. The problem is jurisdiction. A tame-world tool is being made to speak in a wild kingdom. The next chapter will show why the mind loves that kind of elegance even when it misreads randomness.
