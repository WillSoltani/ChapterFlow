# Canonical Draft: ch12

Once prediction has been cut down to size, a political question appears: who should be trusted under uncertainty? Taleb's answer is not that nobody knows anything. It is that prestige should contract when opacity is high and accountability is weak.

Epistemocracy is the dream that authority would track actual knowledge. The problem is that institutions often reward something easier to display: fluent opinion, formal status, and the appearance of command. In opaque domains that can produce a class of impressive talkers whose authority outruns their evidence.

The correction is not rage at expertise. It is harsher filtering. Ask who bears consequences, who stays in contact with reality, and who is merely insulated while advising others. The chapter pushes the reader toward a tougher standard of legitimacy before moving to robust action in the next chapter.
