# Canonical Draft: ch18

By Chapter 18, Taleb's target has a face. The phony is the expert who speaks with more precision than the world granted, borrows prestige from method and status, and turns weak visibility into performed authority.

The offense is not error alone. It is overclaimed knowledge. A phony can be socially powerful precisely because technical language, institutional reputation, and polished confidence create the impression that uncertainty has been domesticated when it has not.

This chapter ties together the earlier attacks on prediction, Gaussian misuse, and misplaced curves. The result is a figure who sells certainty beyond jurisdiction. That prepares the final move: if phonies dominate the stage, the reader needs a practical posture for living and acting without kneeling to them. Chapter 19 provides it.
