# Canonical Draft: ch14

Taleb now draws a sharp line between two kinds of worlds. In Mediocristan, variation is bounded. One person can be taller than another, but no single height can dominate the total height of a stadium. Aggregates stay relatively well-behaved.

Extremistan is different. One observation can rewrite the whole picture. One bestseller can dominate publishing revenue. One billionaire can distort the wealth total. One market event can outweigh many ordinary days. In such domains the average becomes fragile because the outlier owns too much of the sum.

The chapter matters because many modern systems look more like Extremistan than people admit. Taleb is preparing the reader to stop carrying tame-world intuitions into wild domains. That leads directly to the coming critique of Gaussian reasoning.
