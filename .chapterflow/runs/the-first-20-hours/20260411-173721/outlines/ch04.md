Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that a body skill often improves faster when false difficulty is stripped away and live correction simplifies the first reps.

Opening Move:
Open on the tendency to make yoga more intimidating and complicated than the beginner actually needs.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for not making it harder than needed
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for working with an instructor
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P6 for misconception clearing and simplification

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the intimidation trap
- P2: explain false difficulty
- P3: bring in instruction and correction
- P4: show how misconception clearing changes the first reps
- P5: explain simplification as a practice advantage
- P6: keep the limit visible so simplification does not become softness
- P7: bridge toward programming as a different complexity problem

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes useful simplification from removing necessary discipline.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve the risk that simplification can become self-protective under-challenge if it stops serving actual correction.

Threshold Question:
When are you removing false difficulty from a skill, and when are you removing the very pressure that would teach you?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- false difficulty
- instructor correction
- misconceptions as blockers
- simplification as acceleration
- limit between clarity and under-challenge

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (before_after, personal): a beginner stops treating yoga like a flexibility exam and improves once the practice simplifies
- Scenario 2 (dialogue, school): two students compare self-guided confusion with instructor feedback
- Scenario 3 (decision_point, work): a worker chooses between overcomplicating a mobility practice or starting a simpler corrective routine
- Scenario 4 (dilemma, personal): a learner has to decide whether simplification is helping or becoming avoidance
- Scenario 5 (predict_reveal, school): a student expects harder routines to work better, then sees basic correction drive progress
- Scenario 6 (postmortem, work): someone reflects on how misconceptions made the practice harder than necessary

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include mats, mirrors, a studio clock, the feel of balance instability, or a teacher's quick correction.

Preview Constraint:
The preview must ask how the method behaves when the skill is cognitively dense rather than bodily, without summarizing Chapter 5.

Genericity Risks:
- wellness fluff
- generic self-care language
- oversimplified "listen to your body" prose detached from correction

Forbidden Drift:
- therapeutic inflation
- unsupported physiology
- guru cadence
- brief-language leakage
