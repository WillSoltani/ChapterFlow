Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that ukulele becomes learnable faster when the learner narrows the musical target, drills a few essential moves repeatedly, and aims at a real public-ready subset instead of broad instrumental mastery.

Opening Move:
Open on the way an instrument can feel too large because every chord, song, and performance possibility seems to demand attention at once.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3 for deliberate narrowing and entry control
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for isolated repetition and reliable physical patterns
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for public performance readiness and its limits

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the intimidation of instrumental breadth
- P2: explain why deliberate narrowing changes the entry cost
- P3: connect narrowing to isolated repetition of specific moves
- P4: show how repeated small drills create reliable performance pieces
- P5: bring in public readiness as a real but narrow target
- P6: keep the limit visible so narrowing does not become tiny private avoidance
- P7: bridge toward windsurfing and environment-shaped practice

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes useful narrowing for performance readiness from under-ambitious loops that never meet an honest public standard.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve the risk that a learner can confuse a tiny comfortable subset with a real playable standard.

Threshold Question:
When are you narrowing the skill so a public-ready subset can emerge, and when are you narrowing it only to avoid performance pressure?

- Hard: 5

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dialogue, work): coworkers debate whether a few repeatable moves can count as real musical progress
- Scenario 2 (decision_point, school): a student chooses between broad instrument browsing and drilling one narrow performance subset
- Scenario 3 (postmortem, personal): a learner realizes repetition worked only after the target got smaller
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): someone predicts more songs will help more than repeating one transition, then the repeated pattern proves more useful
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): a learner wonders whether the narrowed set is performance-ready or just comfortable
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): a beginner becomes publicly playable after narrowing to a reliable small repertoire

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include a ukulele neck, a repeated chord change, a strumming hand, a music stand, or the pause before playing for other people.
