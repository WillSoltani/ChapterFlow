Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand how to learn enough to make practice smarter without turning learning itself into an avoidance loop.

Opening Move:
Open on the temptation to keep studying because studying feels safer than messy practice.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for research, but not too much
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for models, hooks, practitioners, and predictions/tests
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for distraction control, spaced repetition, checklists, and biology-aware learning

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the attraction of endless preparation
- P2: set the bounded-research rule
- P3: explain mental models and hooks
- P4: show active learning through practitioners, predictions, and tests
- P5: connect distraction and biology to learning quality
- P6: show checklists and scaffolds as practice supports
- P7: bridge toward applying the system in a real domain

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes learning that sharpens reps from learning that delays reps.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve the boundary problem: when is more research prudent, and when is it fear wearing the clothes of diligence?

Threshold Question:
How do you tell whether your preparation is protecting the next rep or protecting you from the next rep?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 6
- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- bounded research
- jump in as anti-delay
- mental models and hooks
- prediction and testing
- distraction and biology constraints
- checklists as supports rather than substitutes

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dialogue, work): two coworkers debate whether more reading will help before a new software task
- Scenario 2 (decision_point, school): a student decides whether to keep researching or start a live project
- Scenario 3 (postmortem, personal): a hobbyist realizes passive study replaced reps
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, school): predictions and tests reveal understanding gaps faster than rereading
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, work): a professional needs enough preparation to avoid waste without slipping into research mode
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): a learner improves once checklists and memory hooks support live practice

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include tabs open on a laptop, index cards, a whiteboard, sticky notes, a checklist, or a tired late-evening desk.
