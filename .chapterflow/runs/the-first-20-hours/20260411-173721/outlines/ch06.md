Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that repairing a familiar skill often requires slowing down, tolerating frustration, and repeating a corrected pattern long enough for consolidation to help.

Opening Move:
Open on the strange humiliation of becoming worse on purpose because the old typing habit is faster than the corrected one.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for habit repair and performance dip
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for frustration tolerance and corrected repetition
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for sleep-linked consolidation and the limit of passive waiting

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the frustration of overwriting a familiar habit
- P2: explain why slowing down is necessary during correction
- P3: connect frustration tolerance to habit repair
- P4: show corrected repetition as the real mechanism
- P5: bring in consolidation and spaced return
- P6: keep the limit visible so "slow down" does not become passive drift
- P7: bridge toward strategic complexity in Go

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes useful slowdown for habit repair from passive under-effort that never reinforces the corrected pattern.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve the risk that a learner can interpret temporary regression as failure and flee back to the old habit too early.

Threshold Question:
When are you slowing down to install a cleaner pattern, and when are you merely moving slowly without reinforcing anything new?

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (before_after, work): a worker stops pecking at the keyboard and accepts slower corrected reps
- Scenario 2 (dialogue, personal): two friends argue about whether the temporary slowdown means the new pattern is failing
- Scenario 3 (decision_point, school): a student chooses between rushing a familiar bad habit or drilling the corrected one
- Scenario 4 (dilemma, work): a learner wonders whether slower practice is helping or has turned into avoidance
- Scenario 5 (predict_reveal, school): a student predicts more forceful repetition will help more than spacing, then sleep-linked improvement appears
