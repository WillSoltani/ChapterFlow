Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that windsurfing becomes learnable faster when the learner isolates smaller subskills, chooses conditions that match current control, and respects risk instead of treating the whole environment as one immediate test.

Opening Move:
Open on the way windsurfing feels unmanageably large when board, sail, wind, water, balance, and recovery all arrive as one demand.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3 for environment-shaped practice and why the environment changes the learning problem
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for staged isolation of subskills and trainable components
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for risk-aware progression and the limit between useful staging and fake shelter

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the intimidation of environment-driven complexity
- P2: explain why the live skill must be broken into smaller controllable parts
- P3: connect subskill isolation to balance, sail handling, launch, or recovery
- P4: show how condition-fit practice creates real learning rather than false bravery
- P5: bring in staged exposure as the chapter's honest progression logic
- P6: keep the limit visible so staging does not become permanent avoidance of real conditions
- P7: bridge toward the book's closing reflection rather than another case chapter

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes useful staged isolation that prepares the learner for the live environment from overprotected practice that never reconnects with wind, water, and recovery pressure.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve the risk that a learner can confuse safe fragments with real windsurfing readiness if conditions never become meaningfully live.

Threshold Question:
When are you isolating subskills so the live environment becomes more learnable, and when are you isolating them only to avoid the uncontrollable parts of the skill?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- environment-shaped practice
- staged isolation of subskills
- condition-aware practice windows
- risk-aware progression
- limit between honest staging and permanent shelter

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dialogue, work): coworkers debate whether a calmer training window counts as real windsurfing practice
- Scenario 2 (decision_point, school): a student chooses between live chaotic conditions and one narrower dockside subskill session
- Scenario 3 (postmortem, personal): a learner realizes setup and recovery were separate skills, not side details
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): someone predicts broad exposure will teach faster, then staged subskill work proves more durable
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): a learner wonders whether careful conditions are still preparing them for the real environment
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): a beginner becomes more competent after sequencing balance, sail handling, and recovery instead of chasing the full experience at once

Scenario Vividness Targets:
At least four scenarios should include a board, sail, shoreline, dock, launch attempt, fallen rig, calm window, or changing wind.

Preview Constraint:
