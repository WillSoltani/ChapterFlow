Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that rapid skill acquisition works best when practice is deliberately designed around one target, low friction, fast feedback, and dense repetition.

Opening Move:
Open on the difference between wanting to practice and having a practice design that actually compounds.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P2 for lovable project and one skill at a time
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P4 for target performance level, deconstruction, and critical tools
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for barriers, time, feedback loops, short bursts, and quantity plus speed

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: shift from permission to practice architecture
- P2: explain why narrowing to one skill matters
- P3: show target performance level and deconstruction
- P4: explain critical tools and barrier removal
- P5: connect time-making and short bursts to repetition
- P6: show why fast feedback loops matter
- P7: bridge to Chapter 3 by asking how much learning is enough before practice turns into delay

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes practicing more from practicing in a way that produces dense, feedback-rich reps around the critical few parts of the skill.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve the risk that these principles become planning theater if the learner keeps optimizing the setup instead of entering real reps.

Threshold Question:
When do skill-acquisition principles accelerate practice, and when do they become one more sophisticated way to avoid the discomfort of practice itself?

Takeaway Count Lock:

- Hard: 6

Hard Takeaway Topics:

- barrier removal and time protection

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (decision_point, school): a robotics student narrows one skill instead of treating the whole competition as one giant practice target
- Scenario 2 (postmortem, work): a manager studies presentation theory but never builds feedback-rich reps
- Scenario 3 (dialogue, personal): two friends debate whether enthusiasm or practice structure matters more
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): a learner thinks long sessions will win, then discovers short bursts with fast feedback work better
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, personal): a hobbyist keeps buying tools instead of removing barriers to real reps
- Scenario 6 (before_after, school): a student shifts from broad practice to deconstructed drills and suddenly improves

Scenario Vividness Targets:
