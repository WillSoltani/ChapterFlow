Chapter Promise:
Readers will understand that a strategically complex skill like Go becomes learnable when the learner starts seeing patterns, survives local attack/defense pressure, and stops waiting for total mastery.

Opening Move:
Open on the way a Go board can feel infinite because every move seems to imply more consequences than the learner can hold at once.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3 for pattern recognition and strategic scale
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P3-P5 for attack/defense pressure and local reading
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P5-P7 for not needing total mastery and selective competence

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: show the intimidation of the board's apparent vastness
- P2: explain why pattern recognition changes the entry cost
- P3: connect patterns to local attack/defense choices
- P4: show strategic pressure without pretending the whole game is solved
- P5: argue for selective competence over total mastery
- P6: keep the limit visible so partial understanding does not become shallow confidence
- P7: bridge toward deliberate narrowing and performance readiness in ukulele

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The chapter distinguishes useful selective competence from fake confidence that mistakes a few patterns for understanding the whole game.

Hard-Depth Tension:
Hard depth must preserve the risk that "you do not need total mastery" can decay into careless intuition or under-reading if the learner stops respecting real board pressure.

Threshold Question:
When are patterns helping you enter the game honestly, and when are they becoming an excuse to stop reading the position carefully?

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dialogue, work): coworkers debate whether learning Go requires understanding the whole board before playing
- Scenario 2 (decision_point, school): a student chooses between freezing on full-board complexity or reading one local fight
- Scenario 3 (postmortem, personal): a learner realizes the board became manageable once recurring shapes started standing out
- Scenario 4 (predict_reveal, work): someone predicts an aggressive move solves the position, then local defense changes the reading
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): a learner wonders whether confidence in one pattern has become careless under-reading
- Scenario 6 (before_after, personal): a beginner starts progressing after accepting partial competence and repeated local reading

Scenario Vividness Targets:
