Core Claim:
Chapter 7 applies the rapid skill acquisition method to Go. The frozen support frames the chapter through pattern recognition, attack/defense, and not needing total mastery. The chapter's job is to show that progress in a strategically complex skill comes faster when the learner recognizes useful patterns, survives the pressure between attack and defense, and accepts partial competence instead of waiting for total understanding.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the first case chapter where the main difficulty is strategic complexity rather than repetition or simple execution. Its distinct pressure is the feeling that the game is too vast to enter unless the learner understands everything at once.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows how Go looks impossibly large because each move seems connected to too many unseen consequences.
Then he uses pattern recognition and attack/defense pressure to reduce that largeness into recognizable strategic situations.
Then he concludes that selective competence is enough to begin learning because the learner does not need total mastery to play meaningfully.
