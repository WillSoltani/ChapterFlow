Core Claim:
Chapter 3 argues that learning supports skill acquisition only when it stays subordinated to practice. The official chapter blurb frames ten principles of effective learning: research, but not too much; jump in; mental models and hooks; imagine the opposite; talk to practitioners; eliminate distractions; spaced repetition; scaffolds and checklists; predictions and tests; honor your biology. The chapter's job is to make these principles feel like anti-drift controls for Chapter 2's practice loop rather than as a separate study philosophy.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the only chapter that explicitly manages the line between useful preparation and endless study. It turns "learn before doing" into a bounded support system instead of an excuse to avoid reps.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author limits research so it supports action instead of replacing it.
Then he shows how mental models, practitioner contact, prediction, tests, and checklists make early practice smarter.
Then he concludes that learning accelerates skill acquisition only when it sharpens attention, memory, and correction without swallowing the reps.

- honor your biology plus eliminating distractions as practical constraints on learning quality
