Core Claim:
Chapter 4 applies the rapid skill acquisition method to yoga. The official chapter blurb emphasizes not making the skill harder than needed, working with an instructor, getting rid of misconceptions, and simplifying the practice. The chapter's job is to show that learning accelerates when the learner strips away avoidable complication rather than performing seriousness through unnecessary difficulty.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the first embodied case chapter and the first place where the method meets posture, correction, and bodily misunderstanding. Its distinct pressure is not abstract theory but the difference between useful simplification and self-defeating complication.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows how yoga is often made more intimidating than it needs to be.
Then he uses instruction and misconception-clearing to simplify the entry path.
Then he concludes that a body skill can improve faster when the learner reduces unnecessary complexity and practices the critical basics directly.
