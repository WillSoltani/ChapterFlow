Core Claim:
Chapter 6 applies the rapid skill acquisition method to touch typing. The frozen support frames the chapter through habit repair, frustration tolerance, and sleep-linked motor consolidation. The chapter's job is to show that relearning a familiar but flawed skill improves when the learner accepts a temporary drop in fluency, repeats the corrected pattern, and lets consolidation do work that brute forcing cannot.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the first case chapter where the learner must overwrite an old habit rather than start from obvious zero. Its distinct pressure is the frustration of becoming worse before becoming cleaner.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows how an existing bad habit resists replacement because the old pattern still feels easier.
Then he uses corrected repetition, frustration tolerance, and sleep-linked consolidation to explain how the new pattern stabilizes.
Then he concludes that habit repair requires temporary humility, not just more effort.
