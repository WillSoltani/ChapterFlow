Core Claim:
Chapter 5 applies the rapid skill acquisition method to programming. The frozen support frames the chapter through breakdown, real-problem iteration, and avoiding endless research. The chapter's job is to show that progress in a mental skill comes faster when the learner builds and tests small working pieces instead of hiding inside tutorials, abstractions, or prestige complexity.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the first case chapter where complexity is mostly cognitive rather than bodily. Its distinct pressure is tutorial paralysis, debugging confusion, and the temptation to study the whole field instead of solving one real problem directly.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author shows how programming looks larger and more abstract than the learner really needs at the start.
Then he uses deconstruction, small working loops, and real-problem iteration to reduce that complexity.
Then he concludes that programming improves faster when the learner keeps building, testing, and fixing instead of collecting more theory than the next working attempt can use.
