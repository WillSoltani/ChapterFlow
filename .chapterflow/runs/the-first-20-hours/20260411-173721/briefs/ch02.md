Core Claim:
Chapter 2 argues that competence does not arrive fastest through vague enthusiasm or brute repetition. It arrives through a deliberate practice architecture. The official chapter blurb frames that architecture as a set of ten principles: lovable project, one skill at a time, target performance level, deconstruction, critical tools, barriers to practice, make time, fast feedback loops, short bursts, and quantity plus speed. The chapter's job is to make those principles feel like a sequence for designing practice rather than like a pile of tips.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
It is the chapter that converts Chapter 1's permission to start into a method for starting well. It is the only place where the rapid skill acquisition system is named as a full operating stack rather than as a general promise.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author narrows the project so practice has a defined target.
Then he strips friction out of the practice environment and emphasizes fast feedback.
Then he concludes that competence grows faster when practice cycles are short, repeated, and aimed at the critical few elements of the skill.
