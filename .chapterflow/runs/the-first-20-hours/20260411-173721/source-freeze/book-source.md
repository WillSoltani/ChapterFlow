# Frozen Book Source Bundle

## Core book identity
- Title: `The First 20 Hours: How to Learn Anything... Fast!`
- Author: `Josh Kaufman`
- Primary edition family used: `Portfolio English edition family (2013 original / 2014 paperback-compatible)`

## Official positioning
The official site frames the book as a practitioner's guide to rapid skill acquisition built around deconstructing complex skills, practicing the most important elements first, and removing barriers to deliberate practice.

Google Books describes the book as a method for going from knowing almost nothing to performing well in roughly twenty hours by deconstructing the skill, using time deliberately, and clearing obstacles.

## Official chapter blurbs
- Chapter 1: modern life feels overloaded; the book questions whether 10,000 hours is really required and introduces rapid skill acquisition.
- Chapter 2: lovable project, one skill at a time, target performance level, deconstruction, critical tools, barriers to practice, make time, fast feedback loops, short bursts, quantity and speed.
- Chapter 3: research but not too much, jump in, mental models and hooks, imagine the opposite, talk to practitioners, eliminate distractions, spaced repetition, scaffolds and checklists, predictions and tests, honor your biology.
- Chapter 4: yoga as simplification and misconception-clearing.
- Chapter 5: programming as breakdown, real-problem iteration, and avoiding endless research.
- Chapter 6: touch typing as habit repair, frustration tolerance, and sleep-linked motor consolidation.
- Chapter 7: Go as pattern recognition, attack/defense, and not needing total mastery.
- Chapter 8: ukulele as isolated repetition, deliberate narrowing, and public performance readiness.
- Chapter 9: windsurfing as environment-shaped practice and staged isolation of subskills.
