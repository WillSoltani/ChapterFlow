# Book Skeleton

## Metadata
- bookId: `the-first-20-hours`
- title: `The First 20 Hours: How to Learn Anything... Fast!`
- author: `Josh Kaufman`
- edition lock: `Portfolio English edition family (2013 original / 2014 paperback-compatible)`
- source basis: official site TOC and blurbs, Google Books bibliographic record, publisher metadata, narrow secondary support
- chapter count used for run: 9 core chapters, plus note/introduction/afterword

## Chapter Order And Intent
1. `A Portrait of the Author as a Learning Junkie`
Intent: break the myth that worthwhile skill development always requires mastery-scale time and frame rapid skill acquisition as a practical answer to overloaded adult life.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity flags: avoid anti-expertise oversimplification

2. `10 Principles of Rapid Skill Acquisition`
Intent: formalize the operational method for getting from zero to usable performance.
Source richness: high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity flags: avoid productivity-maximalist flattening

3. `10 Principles of Effective Learning`
Intent: show how early-stage learning supports the acquisition loop without becoming endless research or perfectionist delay.
Source richness: high
Concept density: high
Moral complexity flags: avoid false certainty or pseudo-science

4. `Yoga`
Intent: test the method in a body-based practice where simplification and misconception cleanup matter.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity flags: avoid overclaiming physical outcomes

5. `Programming`
Intent: test the method in a cognitively dense domain where deconstruction and iteration beat research hoarding.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity flags: avoid glamorizing speed over correctness

6. `Touch Typing`
Intent: show how relearning a familiar but flawed skill involves frustration, habit repair, and consolidation.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity flags: avoid generic habit-talk

7. `Go`
Intent: show how strategic exploration, pattern recognition, and selective competence work in a high-complexity game.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: high
Moral complexity flags: avoid implying total mastery is the only worthy aim

8. `Ukulele`
Intent: show how narrow deliberate practice can turn a daunting skill into a public-performance-capable one quickly.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium
Moral complexity flags: avoid talent-myth rhetoric

9. `Windsurfing`
Intent: stress-test the method in an environment-dependent skill where practice conditions and risk management shape progress.
Source richness: medium
Concept density: medium-high
Moral complexity flags: keep safety and environmental limits visible

## Rotation Plan For Examples
- Ch01: overloaded adults choosing whether to start; work, school, and personal identity friction
- Ch02: method-design situations, project setup, barrier removal, feedback, time boxing
- Ch03: research restraint, model building, testing, distraction control, memory supports
- Ch04: body-awareness, instructor correction, misconceptions, simplification
- Ch05: debugging, build-test-fix loops, real projects, avoiding tutorial paralysis
- Ch06: habit overwrite, frustration tolerance, sleep-linked practice rhythm
- Ch07: strategic tradeoffs, attack/defense, pattern reading, selective scope
- Ch08: isolated subskills, performance rehearsal, public deadline tension
- Ch09: equipment, conditions, staged risk, seasonal timing, environmental respect

## School-Setting Plan
- Rotate among seminar room, library study table, lab workstation, gym / activity class, club room, exam-prep desk, campus lawn, and skills workshop to avoid setting reuse.

## Vocabulary Watchlist
- Avoid generic productivity language: consistency, hustle, grind, mindset, accountability.
- Avoid pseudo-neuroscience and mastery hype.
- Keep skill-acquisition language concrete: deconstruct, practice, barrier, feedback, burst, target level, misconception, transfer.

## Motif / Callback Opportunities
- frustration barrier from Ch01 can echo in later practice chapters
- deconstruction / barrier removal from Ch02 should recur in example chapters
- learning-versus-doing tension from Ch03 can echo in programming, Go, and windsurfing
- public proof moments can link ukulele and other practice cases

- Ch06 risk: becoming generic habit advice
- Ch08 risk: collapsing into motivational performance narrative
