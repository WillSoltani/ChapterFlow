Heading:
A Portrait of the Author as a Learning Junkie

Frozen support summary:
- Official site chapter blurb frames the chapter around overloaded modern life: "So much to do... and so little time."
- The same official blurb explicitly asks whether it really takes 10,000 hours to become good at something and says the chapter explains what rapid skill acquisition is and how it works.
- Official book positioning frames the whole book as a guide to deconstructing complex skills, removing barriers to practice, and getting from knowing almost nothing to performing noticeably well.
- Secondary public talk summary supports the narrower distinction that the 10,000-hour idea concerns expert performance rather than basic practical competence.
- The same secondary summary supports the idea that early practice is emotionally frustrating and that a focused twenty-hour commitment can get a learner to reasonable competence.

Safe chapter-local facts:
- The chapter opens the book's main tension: many adults want to learn more than their time budget seems to permit.
- The chapter challenges a mastery-scale benchmark as the wrong default for ordinary learning goals.
- The chapter's practical target is usable competence, not elite expertise.
- Early frustration is part of the opening problem and helps explain why learners quit too soon.
