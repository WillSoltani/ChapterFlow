Core Claim:
Once safety exists, the reader still needs a disciplined way to place a hard message into the pool. Chapter 7 teaches STATE: Share your facts, Tell your story, Ask for others' paths, Talk tentatively, and Encourage testing. The sequence matters because people usually either jump straight to accusation or hide inside sterile facts.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is the book's clearest speaking method. It is not a formula for sounding polite. It is a structure for saying something difficult without turning the story into a verdict.

Author Logic Chain:
First the author says the reader is now ready to actually say the hard thing.
Then the author teaches Share your facts as the least inflammatory starting point.
Then the author teaches Tell your story while owning it as a story rather than a fact.
Then the author teaches Ask for others' paths so the other person's meaning enters the pool too.
Then the author teaches Talk tentatively and Encourage testing so the conversation stays in dialogue under pushback.

Required Anchors:
- Team lead and slipping project: facts first, then a tentative story, then a real invitation for the peer's view.
- School supervision conversation: a student needs to raise a hard concern without turning interpretation into accusation.
- Personal relationship talk: one partner has to say what they are starting to worry about without speaking as if the worry is already proven.

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- STATE: Share your facts, Tell your story, Ask for others' paths, Talk tentatively, Encourage testing.
- Share your facts: start with observable, verifiable information.
- Tell your story: name the interpretation you are starting to make from those facts.
- Ask for others' paths: invite the other person's view for real.
- Talk tentatively: soften certainty without hiding the message.
- Encourage testing: explicitly welcome disagreement or correction.

Specific Applications:
- raising a milestone concern to a peer without accusing them of carelessness
- naming a supervision concern in school without acting as if the motive is already proven
- sharing a worry in a relationship without collapsing into either silence or blame

Common Misreadings:
- Facts first is not a data dump that avoids the real message.
- Tentative language is not weakness or vagueness.
- Asking for others' paths is not a rhetorical trap.
- Encouraging testing does not mean abandoning the concern the moment pushback arrives.

Counterarguments or Limits:
Some facts are disputed, some records are incomplete, and some rooms use the demand for more facts as a delay tactic. The chapter should preserve that the reader still has to make a judgment call about how much evidence is enough to speak. It should also admit that STATE becomes manipulative if the speaker invites testing but is not actually open to being changed.

Previous Chapter Bridge:
Chapter 6 rebuilt safety. Chapter 7 teaches how to use that repaired room well by placing a hard message into it.

Next Chapter Bridge:
Once the reader can state their own path well, the next skill is drawing out the other person's path when they hold back or escalate. Chapter 8 teaches AMPP and ABC.

Cross-Chapter Tensions:
- facts versus interpretation

Moral Complexity:
The chapter should keep clear that owning a story as a story is an ethical move, not only a tactical one. It signals that the speaker might be wrong and is willing to be corrected.

Concept Budget:
- target concept count: 5
- rationale: the five STATE moves are the whole chapter, so the prose should keep them distinct without turning the chapter into an acronym lecture.

Hard-Depth Minimum:
Hard depth must preserve that the sequence matters, that tentative language protects dialogue without erasing conviction, and that Encourage testing only works when the speaker is genuinely open to disconfirmation.

Unsupported Zones:
Do not reduce the chapter to corporate script talk. Do not present tentativeness as chronic hedging. Do not imply that facts speak for themselves without interpretation.
