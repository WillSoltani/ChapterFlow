Chapter Promise:
By the end of this chapter, the reader can slow a strong reaction down into fact, story, feeling, and action, and can name whether a victim, villain, or helpless story is running.

Opening Move:
Open with a designer already angry before the conversation starts and make the invisible interpretation step visible before any theory terms appear.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P4-P5
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: designer sees a shipped feature and feels instant anger that seems like direct reality
- P2: introduce Path to Action and name the hidden story step
- P3: reverse the path and show how one question interrupts the automatic chain
- P4: explain victim story and why relief can erase agency
- P5: explain villain story and why righteousness flattens the other person
- P6: explain helpless story and reopen choice under pressure
- P7: preserve the limit around real constraints and bridge to watching the room turn

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The non-generic mechanism is that feelings are real and consequential, but they follow interpretations of facts rather than facts alone.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How does the reader revise a damaging story without pretending the other person's behavior or the external power structure is harmless?

Threshold Question:
If emotion feels immediate, what lets a person find the story step in time to change the next move?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: the Path to Action reveals the hidden interpretation step
- Takeaway 2: reverse-path diagnosis begins with the feeling already present
- Takeaway 3: victim stories erase role and therefore action
- Takeaway 4: villain stories satisfy emotion while making dialogue harder
- Takeaway 5: helpless stories hide choice but do not erase constraint

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (before_after, work): product designer shifts from villain story to process diagnosis
- Scenario 2 (dilemma, school): parent or student advocate notices a victim story in a school meeting
- Scenario 3 (postmortem, personal): couple sees how a story shaped a weekend fight
- Scenario 4 (decision_point, work): team lead decides whether a stakeholder problem is helplessness or fear
- Scenario 5 (dialogue, school): professor-student exchange separates facts from interpretation
- Scenario 6 (predict_reveal, personal): partner predicts what happens if the current story goes unchallenged

- ex01: launch notes, design file, red Slack dot

Preview Constraint:
The preview should point to the visible signs that dialogue is slipping, not explain safety repair yet.

- flattening the three story types into one vague "mindset" lesson
