Chapter Promise:
By the end of this chapter, the reader can use AMPP to draw out another person's path and ABC to work with it without collapsing into agreement theater or blunt contradiction.

Opening Move:
Open with a disengaged 1:1 where a manager keeps getting one-word answers and has to draw out what is actually going on.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P4-P5
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P6

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: disengaged 1:1 or lab check-in where the visible problem is missing meaning
- P2: explain Ask and Mirror as the first two moves that create space for truth
- P3: explain Paraphrase and Prime, including why Prime is the riskiest move
- P4: explain the shift from uncovering meaning to working with it through ABC
- P5: clarify Agree, Build, and Compare with a disagreement that shrinks once agreement is named
- P6: personal or school anchor showing the difference between listening and leading
- P7: preserve the limit around misreading emotion and bridge to closing the conversation with action

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The non-generic mechanism is the progression: AMPP brings hidden meaning into the room, and ABC lets the speaker work with that meaning without erasing disagreement.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How does the reader use Prime and Compare without either putting words in the other person's mouth or flattening the disagreement into false harmony?

Threshold Question:
What do you do when the other person's path is the missing part of the conversation?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: AMPP escalates in intervention and risk
- Takeaway 2: Mirror works because it gives emotion permission without certainty theater
- Takeaway 3: Prime must stay humble and rejectable
- Takeaway 4: ABC keeps agreement and disagreement in proportion
- Takeaway 5: listening is not the same as surrender or control

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dialogue, work): manager uses Ask and Mirror in a disengaged 1:1
- Scenario 2 (before_after, school): quiet teammate shares more once mirrored and paraphrased
- Scenario 3 (decision_point, personal): partner decides whether to paraphrase before responding
- Scenario 4 (postmortem, work): a failed conversation shows what happened when Prime became imposed meaning
- Scenario 5 (dilemma, school): student weighs whether to Compare after first naming genuine agreement
- Scenario 6 (predict_reveal, personal): predict what happens when someone hears "yes, but" instead of Build or Compare

Scenario Vividness Targets:
- ex01: check-in room, crossed arms, calendar history
- ex02: lab bench or project doc, quiet teammate, visible frustration
- ex03: couch conversation, incomplete sentence, rising tone

Preview Constraint:
The preview should point toward decision clarity and follow-through, not back toward safety repair.

Genericity Risks:
- turning Ask into vague empathy
- making Prime sound like helpful mind-reading
- making Agree, Build, and Compare feel interchangeable

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: generic advice to listen better
- filler move 2: agreement without conflict specificity
- invented authority move 1: implying Prime is safe if phrased softly
- brief-language leak 1: threshold question, hard-depth minimum, concept budget
