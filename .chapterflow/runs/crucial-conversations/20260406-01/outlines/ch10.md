Chapter Promise:
By the end of this chapter, the reader can hold onto the same framework in hard rooms by changing the order, scope, and emphasis rather than inventing a new philosophy.

Opening Move:
Open with a reader objection inside a real scene: a hard conversation under a power gap, over text, or with someone evasive enough to make the framework feel unrealistic.

Anchor Allocation:
- anchor 1 used in paragraph(s): P1-P3
- anchor 2 used in paragraph(s): P4-P5
- anchor 3 used in paragraph(s): P6-P7

Paragraph Job Map:
- P1: open with the "yeah, but" objection in a concrete hard case
- P2: state the chapter's meta-point that the framework stays the same while order and emphasis shift
- P3: handle power asymmetry through narrowed ask, safety, and honest scope
- P4: handle text or async conflict by naming what the medium removes
- P5: handle non-engagement, subject-shifting, or chronic complaint without abandoning the framework
- P6: bring in identity or cultural difference and preserve humility around assumptions
- P7: end with the anti-manipulation warning and the demand to pick one real avoided conversation

Required Distinction or Mechanism:
The non-generic mechanism is sameness of principle with difference of sequencing: restore safety, then speak honestly, but adapt the order and the size of the ask to the room.

Hard-Depth Tension:
How do you stay honest without turning honesty into domination, and how do you preserve safety without using safety as another name for avoidance?

Threshold Question:
What changes in a hard case, and what must not change?

Takeaway Count Lock:
- Easy: 3 (always)
- Medium: 5
- Hard: 5

Hard Takeaway Topics:
- Takeaway 1: the framework does not change just because the room is hard
- Takeaway 2: power gaps change scope and timing, not the need for safety and honesty
- Takeaway 3: text strips cues and often requires re-entry into a richer medium
- Takeaway 4: chronic complaint or evasion requires returning to the actual issue without losing safety
- Takeaway 5: the framework fails if used to manipulate instead of staying open to change

Scenario Lesson Map:
- Scenario 1 (dialogue, personal): a strained relationship conversation gets a re-entry offer instead of another spiral
- Scenario 2 (before_after, work): an employee narrows a power-gap ask and gets farther than blunt accusation would
- Scenario 3 (dilemma, school): a seminar conflict across identity difference slows assumptions and restores safety first
- Scenario 4 (postmortem, personal): a text fight worsens because tone and repair signals never made it through
- Scenario 5 (decision_point, work): a manager chooses whether to confront a chronic complainer or reframe the issue
- Scenario 6 (predict_reveal, school): predict what changes when a student finally plans the avoided conversation instead of rehearsing arguments alone

Scenario Vividness Targets:
- ex01: paused conversation, half-finished sentence, re-entry request
- ex02: office-hours or review room, status difference, narrowed ask
- ex03: seminar table, visible flinch, corrected assumption
- ex04: message thread, escalating punctuation, no repair channel
- ex05: recurring complaint in a team room, subject shift, named pattern
- ex06: office-hours calendar slot, written notes, rising stomach-drop

Preview Constraint:
The final chapter cannot preview a next chapter. It should instead leave the reader with a live commitment question about which avoided conversation they will actually have.

Genericity Risks:
- flattening all hard cases into courage slogans
- treating power and identity as decorative rather than structural
- turning openness into sentimental softness instead of a real discipline

Forbidden Drift:
- filler move 1: generic advice to be brave
- filler move 2: claiming all hard rooms just need confidence
- invented authority move 1: promising the framework guarantees success
- brief-language leak 1: threshold question, concept budget, hard-depth minimum
