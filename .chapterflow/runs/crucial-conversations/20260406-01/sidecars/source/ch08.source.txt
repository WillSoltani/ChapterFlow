Chapter 8 — Explore Others' Paths: How to Listen When Others Blow Up or Clam Up
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The reverse skill is to draw the other person out when they are the one
holding back or escalating. The book teaches this as AMPP:

  A — ASK. Start with a genuine, open question. Not a leading question, not
      a question that reveals you already have the answer you want — a real
      request for their view.

  M — MIRROR. If they are displaying an emotion that doesn't match what
      they are saying, name it out loud, gently. "You're saying it's fine,
      but you seem frustrated — am I reading that right?" Mirroring creates
      safety for them to admit what they were about to withhold.

  P — PARAPHRASE. When they do share, play it back in your own words to
      show you received it. Not a parrot repetition — a paraphrase that
      demonstrates understanding.

  P — PRIME. If they are still reluctant, and you think you know what they
      might be afraid to say, say it for them as a guess. "I wonder if part
      of the concern is that the deadline feels impossible." Priming is a
      risky move — done wrong, it sounds like putting words in their mouth
      — but done with genuine humility, it gives the other person
      permission to confirm a fear they were holding back.

Once the other person's path is on the table, the next move is to work with
what's there. The book teaches ABC:

  A — AGREE where you actually agree, and say so explicitly. Most
      disagreements overstate their size. Most people in heated arguments
      agree on 90% and are fighting about 10%. Naming the agreement first
      shrinks the fight to its actual size.

  B — BUILD on what the other person has said, adding what they missed.
      Not "yes, but" — "yes, and."

  C — COMPARE when you genuinely disagree: "I see it differently — here's
      what I'm seeing." Not "you're wrong."

Modern example to generate: A manager is getting one-word answers in a
1:1 from a direct report who has been increasingly disengaged. Asking
"how are things?" produces "fine." Mirroring: "You're saying fine, but
the last three check-ins have felt heavier than this. What's actually
going on?" Now the conversation can start.
