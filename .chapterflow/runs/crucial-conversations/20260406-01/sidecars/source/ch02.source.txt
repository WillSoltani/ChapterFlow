Chapter 2 — Mastering Crucial Conversations: The Power of Dialogue
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The book's central metaphor is the Pool of Shared Meaning. When two people
are in real dialogue, they each pour their facts, stories, feelings, and
opinions into a shared pool. The pool is what both people draw on to reach a
decision. When the pool is large — when both people have contributed what
they really think — the resulting decision tends to be better, and both
people feel committed to it because they helped shape it. When the pool is
small — when one or both people are holding back — the decision suffers, and
the person who held back usually ends up sabotaging it after the fact,
consciously or not.

The enemy of the pool is anything that causes information to be withheld.
Most often that "anything" is a loss of safety: one or both people start to
feel that it is unsafe to say what they actually think, and so they stop
contributing.

The skill of crucial conversations is, at its heart, the skill of keeping
the pool filling while emotions are running high. Everything else in the
book is in service of that.

Teaching implication: The pool metaphor is the book's most portable idea.
Readers who internalize it can diagnose their own failed conversations in
retrospect — "I held back the thing I was actually thinking because I felt
unsafe" — and they can watch for the same dynamic in real time.

Modern example to generate: A hybrid-team planning meeting where the two
remote team members say almost nothing, and the team lead mistakes their
silence for agreement. Three weeks later, the remote members are the ones
quietly dragging on the project. The pool was never filled.
