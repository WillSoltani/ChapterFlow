Chapter 4 — Master My Stories: Staying in Dialogue When You're Upset
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The second internal skill is about the stories people tell themselves
between seeing a fact and feeling an emotion. The book's model, called the
Path to Action, walks the sequence in slow motion:

  SEE/HEAR (the fact)  ->  TELL A STORY (the interpretation)  ->  FEEL
  (the emotion)  ->  ACT (the behavior)

Most people experience this sequence as compressed: they see something, they
feel an emotion, they act. The story step is invisible to them, so their
emotional reaction feels like a direct consequence of the facts. It isn't.
The emotion is a consequence of the story they told themselves about the
facts.

The diagnostic move is to reverse the path: notice the emotion, notice the
behavior it is producing, and then work backward to ask, "What story am I
telling myself?" and then, "What facts do I actually have?"

The book names three common unhelpful stories people tell under stress:
- Victim stories: "This is not my fault."
- Villain stories: "It's all their fault."
- Helpless stories: "There's nothing else I could do."

Counter-questions:
- Am I pretending not to notice my own role in this problem?
- Why would a reasonable, rational, decent person do what this person did?
- What do I actually want, and what would I need to do to get it?

Teaching implication: Do not frame emotion as irrational or wrong. The gain
is not numbness. The gain is seeing the interpretation step clearly enough
to revise the next move.
