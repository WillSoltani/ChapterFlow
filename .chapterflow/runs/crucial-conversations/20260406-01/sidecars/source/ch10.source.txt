Chapter 10 — Yeah, But: Tough Cases and Putting It All Together
----------------------------------------------------------------
The closing chapter collects the "tough cases" — the situations where
readers say "the framework sounds nice, but my situation is special."
The book works through variants: the person who won't engage, the
person who engages but keeps changing the subject, the chronic
complainer, the person with significantly more power, the person with
significantly less power, the conversation that has to happen over
text, the conversation across identity or cultural difference.

The book's meta-point in this chapter is that the framework does not
change in the tough cases; only the order and the emphasis change. The
same underlying move — restore safety, then speak honestly — still
applies, even when one person has more power, even when the medium is
async, even when one person is actively hostile.

The closing teaching move is to encourage the reader to pick one or two
specific conversations in their own life, plan them using the
framework, and have them. Not to "get good at crucial conversations" in
the abstract, but to have the specific conversation that they've been
avoiding.

The closing warning: the framework is not a technique for winning
arguments. Readers who use it that way — to manipulate, to extract
compliance, to sound reasonable while steamrolling — will find that it
fails. The framework only works when the user is genuinely open to being
changed by what the other person says. That openness is the actual
skill. The words are just the scaffolding.
