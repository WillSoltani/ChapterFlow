Chapter 5 — Learn to Look: Watch for When the Conversation Turns Crucial
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Most people miss the moment when a conversation turns crucial. By the time
they notice, they are already flooded and reacting. The skill is to watch
for the signs that safety has slipped and catch them early.

The chapter distinguishes two layers:
- Content: the topic being discussed.
- Conditions: the emotional and relational state of the people discussing it.

Skilled operators track both.

When dialogue breaks, the warning signs usually fall into two patterns:

Silence — withdrawing contribution from the pool.
- Masking: understating or sugarcoating.
- Avoiding: steering around the real issue.
- Withdrawing: physically or emotionally leaving the conversation.

Violence — forcing meaning into the pool.
- Controlling: cutting others off or speaking in absolutes.
- Labeling: reducing the other person to a type or stereotype.
- Attacking: insults, threats, or belittling.

The practical gain is that the reader can catch themselves mid-move:
"I am about to mask." "I am about to label." Naming the move removes much
of its power.

Teaching implication: The cues are harder to read on video and easier to
freeze on the screen in text. The chapter should keep that medium effect
visible.
