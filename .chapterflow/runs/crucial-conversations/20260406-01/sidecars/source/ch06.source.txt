Chapter 6 — Make It Safe: How to Make It Safe to Talk About Almost Anything
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Once the reader has noticed that safety is gone, the next skill is to put it
back. Safety requires two things at once:

- Mutual Purpose: both people believe the other is working toward a goal
  they both care about.
- Mutual Respect: both people believe the other sees them as an equal worth
  hearing.

If either one is missing, the other person will leave dialogue. The repair
starts with diagnosing which one is missing.

Repair moves:
- Apologize when you have genuinely done something that contributed to the
  loss of safety.
- Contrast when the other person has misunderstood your intent.
  Format: "I do NOT mean X. I DO mean Y."
  The order matters, and the "do not" half must be true.
- CRIB when mutual purpose itself has broken:
  Commit to seek mutual purpose.
  Recognize the purpose behind the strategy.
  Invent a mutual purpose.
  Brainstorm new strategies.

Teaching implication: Contrasting is practical but only works when sincere.
Power asymmetry can also change what "making it safe" can realistically mean.
