# Style Memory

- Write like a sharp human explainer with taste: specific, concrete, readable, and authored.
- Open with tension or a scene, not a thesis announcement.
- Keep paragraph jobs distinct. If two paragraphs do the same work, merge or cut.
- Reader-facing prose should teach the idea directly and rarely mention "the chapter."
- Easy must feel accessible and pictureable, medium must explain mechanism, hard must add a real limit or unresolved tension.
- Prefer vivid nouns, familiar verbs, and a small concept budget over abstract inflation.
- Use memorable lines sparingly, but make them earn their keep.
- Avoid corporate tone, textbook tone, fake profundity, repeated sentence skeletons, and note-to-writer residue.
- Tone differences must be functional: gentle lowers resistance, direct names the mechanism, competitive sharpens stakes.
