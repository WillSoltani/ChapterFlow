Balance can sound responsible even when it weakens execution. That is the pressure Chapter 11 takes up. Moran and Lennington move from greatness in the moment into intentional imbalance because some actions carry more consequence than others, and treating every demand as equal can drain force from the one move that matters most.

The chapter title matters because it refuses the fantasy that fairness always means evenness. Intentional imbalance does not mean chaos or neglect. It means giving disproportionate weight to what carries disproportionate leverage. The chapter argues that equal distribution can become a disguise for avoiding hard priority decisions.

That is where the chapter sharpens. A person may stay busy, attentive, and even sincere while still spreading attention so evenly that the most consequential work never gets enough force. The problem is not lack of effort. The problem is failure to recognize that some moments and actions deserve more protection than others.

Execution depends on leverage, not on symmetrical effort. If one conversation decides a project, if one study block determines most of the exam performance, or if one habit stabilizes the rest of a routine, then equal treatment becomes a mistake. Chapter 11 says priorities have to be honored unevenly when reality is already uneven.

The limit has to stay visible. The chapter is not authorizing selfishness, permanent neglect, or indifference to obligations. Intentional imbalance is not recklessness. It is a deliberate adjustment in weight so the highest-value demand is not diluted by a shallow idea of balance.

That is why the chapter follows greatness in the moment so naturally. Chapter 10 argues that execution happens in the current moment. Chapter 11 asks which moments deserve more force, time, and protection than the rest. It turns present-tense performance into a question of leverage.

The harder warning sits underneath the chapter. People can hide inside balance language because equal treatment sounds mature and defensible. Meanwhile the most important work keeps losing the exact protection it needs. The language sounds fair while the execution stays weak.

That pressure leads directly into the next chapter. If execution requires deliberate imbalance around what matters most, what full system keeps those choices active week after week instead of leaving them to intention alone?
