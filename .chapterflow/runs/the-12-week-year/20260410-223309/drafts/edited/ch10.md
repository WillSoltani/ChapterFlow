Commitment can still stay abstract if it never becomes performance in the current moment. That is the pressure Chapter 10 takes up. Moran and Lennington move from commitment into greatness in the moment because execution only ever happens in the next action directly in front of the reader, not in a distant picture of who the reader hopes to become.

The chapter title matters because it reframes greatness. Greatness is not a future identity to admire. It is a present-tense performance choice. The chapter says the reader builds a larger result by handling the moment in front of them well instead of worshiping a bigger image from a distance.

That is where the subhead, Performing in the Moment, matters. The chapter turns performance away from grand talk and toward the immediate task. A person may love the goal, care deeply about the outcome, and still avoid the exact action that the current moment is asking for. Chapter 10 closes that gap by forcing greatness into the present.

This matters because commitment finally becomes visible there. The reader does not prove resolve by describing the future self vividly. The reader proves it by doing the next needed thing with attention and discipline. The chapter treats the moment in front of the reader as the real location of execution.

This does not mean turning every moment into self-important drama. The chapter is not romanticizing ordinary action. It is not asking the reader to act grand. It is asking the reader to perform the current demand instead of hiding inside abstract ambition.

That is why Chapter 10 follows commitment so closely. Chapter 9 asked whether commitment survives the bargaining moment. Chapter 10 asks where that commitment actually appears once the task is present. It shifts execution from durable resolve into present-tense performance.

The chapter leaves one more pressure alive. If greatness is built in the moment, what deserves disproportionate weight and protection instead of equal treatment everywhere? That is the question Chapter 11 raises next.
