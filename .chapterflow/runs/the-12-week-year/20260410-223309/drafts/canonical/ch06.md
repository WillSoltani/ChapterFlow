A focused week can still lie. That is the problem Chapter 6 takes on. Moran and Lennington move from weekly planning into measurement because execution gets distorted when people rely on intention, busyness, or feeling instead of evidence. A week can feel serious and still fail to produce the result it was supposed to move.

Confronting the Truth is the chapter title because the authors want the reader to stop guessing about execution quality. Measuring results changes the question from "Did I work hard?" to "Did the week actually produce what it was meant to produce?" That shift matters because effort is easy to overestimate when there is no clear feedback loop.

The chapter's next move is the Weekly Scorecard. The scorecard gives execution a visible signal each week instead of leaving progress at the level of mood or memory. A weekly plan can put the right work into the week, but the scorecard shows whether that work was actually carried through. It turns vague self-assessment into something harder to manipulate.

That is why the chapter is not anti-effort. It is anti-illusion. Activity can be real and still not be the right activity. Motion can be high while results remain flat. The scorecard matters because it makes that gap visible before the cycle is over. It keeps the reader from mistaking seriousness for follow-through.

The chapter also keeps one limit clear. Scorekeeping is feedback, not the whole system. Measurement does not replace planning, commitment, or disciplined action. It tells the truth about whether those things are working. If the scorecard becomes empty tracking, vanity, or punishment, it stops serving the chapter's purpose.

This truth-telling function makes the chapter a natural follow-on to weekly planning. Chapter 5 asked whether the right priorities entered the week. Chapter 6 asks whether the week delivered on them. Together they close the gap between planning and evidence.

The chapter leaves one more practical question alive. Once the truth is visible, how does the reader protect the right actions more intentionally so the score actually improves? That is the pressure Chapter 7 takes up.
