# The 12 Week Year Frozen Source Notes

## Locked Edition
- Title: *The 12 Week Year: Get More Done in 12 Weeks than Others Do in 12 Months*
- Authors: Brian P. Moran, Michael Lennington
- Publisher: Wiley
- Edition family: 2013 unabridged English trade edition
- Page count: 208

## Authorized Preview Summary

### Book-Level Overview
The official preview frames the book as a response to the low-productivity effects of annualized thinking. The system argues that compressing the execution horizon to 12 weeks increases urgency, focus, and clarity.

### Chapter 1: The Challenge
- Opens with a performance question: why do some people accomplish far more than most others seem capable of?
- Positions the authors' long-running work as helping people and organizations execute more effectively.
- Emphasizes the gap between the life people live and the life they are capable of living.
- Sets up execution, not lack of ideas, as the central challenge.

### Chapter 2: Redefining the Year
- States that most people and organizations do not lack ideas; the breakdown is in applying them.
- Argues that annual goals and annual plans often become a barrier to high performance.
- Names the trap as annualized thinking.
- Moves toward a shorter execution cycle in which 12 weeks functions like a year.

### Durable Method Support From Official Site
- The official 12 Week Year site summarizes the system in three phases: craft a vision, set 12 week goals, and execute weekly.
- The site also highlights weekly execution and scorekeeping as part of the broader system.

## Use Limits
- No exact long quotations in reader-facing prose.
- Do not invent client stories, corporate case studies, or measurement claims absent from the frozen bundle.
- Do not overstate the Chapter 1 or Chapter 2 mechanism beyond execution gap, annualized thinking, urgency, focus, vision, and weekly execution.
