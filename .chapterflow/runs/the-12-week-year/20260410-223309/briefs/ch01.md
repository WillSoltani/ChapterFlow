Core Claim:
Chapter 1 establishes that the main gap is not between people and good ideas but between people and consistent execution. It asks why some people seem to accomplish so much more than others, then frames the real challenge as performing closer to your actual capacity day after day. The chapter's job is to make execution feel like the bottleneck before the system begins solving it.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the opening challenge chapter, not yet the annualized-thinking chapter and not yet the planning chapter. It has to center the tension between potential and actual output, show why execution is the real problem, and open the door to a system without pretending the whole system has already been explained.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors open with the question of why some people accomplish so much more than others.
Then they connect that question to the gap between the life people live and the life they are capable of living.
Then they position their work as helping people and organizations execute more effectively.
Then they set execution, not knowledge, as the challenge that the rest of the book will address.

Required Anchors:
- The opening question about why some people accomplish much more and what would change if you performed up to your potential each day.
- The authors' statement that they have spent years helping individuals, teams, and organizations execute more effectively.
- The contrast between the life people live and the life they are capable of living.

Specific Applications:

Common Misreadings:
- turning the chapter into generic motivation about being your best self
- treating the execution gap as a personality flaw instead of a practical systems problem
- importing planning mechanics that the chapter has not introduced yet

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter can diagnose the problem but it cannot solve it fully on its own. It should preserve the limit that knowing your potential is not the same as having a working execution system, and it should not promise change without structure.
