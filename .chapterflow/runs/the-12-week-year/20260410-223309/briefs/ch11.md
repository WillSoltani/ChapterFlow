Core Claim:
Chapter 11 argues that effective execution requires deliberate imbalance around what matters most instead of equalized attention everywhere. The chapter says some responsibilities, moments, and actions deserve disproportionate weight because they move outcomes more than the rest.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is not permission for neglect, selfishness, or chaos. Its job is to explain why balance can become a false ideal when the real need is protected priority and disproportionate focus on the few moves that matter most.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter questions the ideal of equal balance.
Then it argues that results require intentional imbalance around priorities.
Then it shows why some moments deserve more protection and force than others.
Then it preserves the limit that imbalance must stay deliberate rather than reckless.
Then it prepares the reader for the full execution system in Chapter 12.

Required Anchors:
- The chapter title itself: Intentional Imbalance.
- The skeleton intent that deliberate imbalance around priorities is better than equalized attention everywhere.
- The Chapter 10 bridge question about which moments deserve disproportionate weight and protection.
- The moral-complexity note that imbalance can be misread as permission to neglect obligations permanently.

Allowed Quotes or Near-Quotes:

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- intentional imbalance: deliberate unequal allocation toward what matters most
- false balance trap: treating all demands as equal when they are not
- disproportionate protection: guarding high-leverage moments and actions

Specific Applications:
- a reader protects the one meeting that decides a project instead of treating every task equally
- a student gives extra weight to the study block most tied to the exam result
- a personal goal improves once the most consequential habit gets priority over lower-value activity

Common Misreadings:
- using imbalance to justify neglecting core duties forever
- treating every preference as a priority claim
- sliding into later system-summary language from Chapter 12

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should keep the limit visible that imbalance is intentional and contextual. It is not random obsession or an excuse to abandon responsibilities.
