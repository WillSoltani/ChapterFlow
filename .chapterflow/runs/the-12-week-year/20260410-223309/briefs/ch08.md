Core Claim:
Chapter 8 argues that accountability works best when the reader treats it as ownership instead of as supervision, blame, or outside pressure. The chapter says execution strengthens when the reader owns choices and follow-through personally, especially after priorities have already been protected. Its job is to recast accountability as chosen responsibility rather than punishment.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the ownership chapter, not the intentionality chapter and not yet the commitment chapter. It has to show why follow-through improves when responsibility is owned from within without drifting into later commitment mechanics, accountability partnerships, or moralizing lectures.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter rejects accountability as external pressure.
Then it reframes accountability as ownership of actions and outcomes.
Then it shows why ownership matters when the week gets difficult anyway.
Then it prepares the reader for the sharper distinction between interest and commitment in the next chapter.

Required Anchors:
- The chapter title itself: Accountability as Ownership.
- The skeleton intent that accountability is self-owned responsibility rather than external punishment.
- The Chapter 7 bridge question about who owns follow-through once work is protected.
- The broader execution logic that ownership has to live inside the system, not outside it as blame.

Counterarguments or Limits:
