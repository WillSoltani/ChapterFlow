Core Claim:
Chapter 5 argues that a 12 week plan only becomes operational when it is translated into a weekly plan. The chapter says execution improves when the reader stops relating to goals only at the cycle level and starts governing the current week directly. Its job is to make weekly planning the bridge between strategy and action.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the weekly-bridge chapter, not the 12 week planning chapter and not yet the scorekeeping chapter. It has to explain why the week is the live execution unit without drifting into later routine mechanics or generic time-management advice.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter says a 12 week plan is still too distant to govern daily choices by itself.
Then it introduces the weekly plan as the execution bridge.
Then it argues that the weekly plan keeps priorities current when real demands arrive.
Then it prepares the reader for measurement and truth-telling in the next chapter.
