Core Claim:
Chapter 2 argues that annual goals and annual plans often limit performance because they create too much psychological distance. The chapter names annualized thinking as the trap, then reframes the working year as 12 weeks so urgency, focus, and execution pressure become real. Its job is to show that the calendar itself is part of the execution problem.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the annualized-thinking reset chapter. It is not the opening challenge chapter and not yet the emotional-connection chapter. It has to make the reader feel how yearly planning encourages drift, then establish why 12 weeks creates sharper action without pretending every downstream discipline has already been explained.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors say people and organizations usually have more ideas than they can implement.
Then they argue that the breakdown is not knowing but applying.
Then they identify annual goals and annual plans as a hidden barrier to high performance.
Then they name annualized thinking and replace it with a shorter 12 week execution horizon.

Required Anchors:

Frameworks or Terms Introduced:
- annualized thinking: treating a full year as the true unit of execution and evaluation
- 12 week year: compressing the execution horizon to create urgency and focus
- year-end effect: the tendency for intensity to rise when time feels short
- periodization: shorter performance windows improve concentration and effort

Specific Applications:
- a sales team that waits too long because year-end still feels far away
- a student who defers work because the semester feels endless until the last month
- a personal savings or fitness goal that only becomes urgent when the deadline is near

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as anti-planning
- confusing urgency with panic or overwork
- assuming a shorter cycle means smaller ambition rather than tighter execution

Counterarguments or Limits:
A shorter cycle does not solve weak priorities by itself. The chapter should preserve the limit that 12 weeks sharpens execution pressure, but it still depends on real priorities and follow-through rather than calendar magic.
