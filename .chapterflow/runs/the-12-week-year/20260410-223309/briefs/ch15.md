Core Claim:
Chapter 15 argues that a 12 week plan only survives if the reader installs process control through weekly plans, a weekly routine, and some form of accountability instead of trying to rely on intention alone.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This chapter is the execution-control bridge. It must convert the written 12 week plan into repeatable weekly behavior without drifting into vague motivation talk or making accountability sound optional.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter explains that process control is how things actually get done.
Then it narrows that control into weekly plans and a weekly routine.
Then it adds the social pressure of not going it alone.
Then it sets up scorekeeping by showing that process needs a visible feedback loop.

Common Misreadings:
- treating the plan itself as sufficient without routines
- seeing accountability as dependence instead of pressure toward execution
- assuming process control means rigidity instead of regular contact with commitments

Counterarguments or Limits:
