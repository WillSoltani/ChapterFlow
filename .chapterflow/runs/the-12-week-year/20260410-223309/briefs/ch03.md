Core Claim:
Chapter 3 argues that execution usually fails when short-term comfort keeps beating long-term importance. The chapter says a compelling vision gives the reader an emotional reason strong enough to choose difficult action over immediate ease. Its job is to connect the tighter 12 week frame from Chapter 2 to a motive strong enough to survive discomfort.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the vision-and-comfort chapter, not the annualized-thinking chapter and not yet the planning chapter. It has to show why urgency alone is not enough and why execution needs an emotional stake in the future, without drifting into vague inspiration or generic purpose talk.

Author Logic Chain:
First the authors say execution is not complicated, but it is not easy because new actions are uncomfortable.
Then they argue that people often abandon strategy because short-term discomfort outweighs long-term benefit in the moment.
Then they say a compelling vision creates an emotional stake strong enough to beat comfort.
Then they connect that vision to shorter-term goals and plans.

Required Anchors:
- The preview statement that execution requires new actions and new actions are often uncomfortable.
- The claim that people choose comfortable actions over uncomfortable important actions unless they have a compelling reason not to.
- The need for a compelling vision of the future aligned to shorter-term goals and plans.
- The chapter map subhead `Your Brain and Vision`.

Counterarguments or Limits:
