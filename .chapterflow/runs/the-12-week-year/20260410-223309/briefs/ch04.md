Core Claim:
Chapter 4 argues that once the year is redefined and the vision is emotionally alive, the annual plan should no longer govern execution. The chapter replaces annual planning with 12 week planning and focused goal selection. Its job is to show that planning should become shorter, tighter, and more execution-linked.

What Makes This Chapter Distinct:
This is the 12 week planning chapter, not the emotional-connection chapter and not yet the weekly planning chapter. It has to convert the earlier vision and urgency logic into a shorter planning frame without collapsing into generic productivity advice.

Author Logic Chain:
First the chapter rejects the annual plan as the dominant execution document.
Then it introduces 12 week planning as the proper frame.
Then it links planning quality to goal choice and structure.
Then it prepares the reader for weekly planning in the next chapter.

Specific Applications:
- a team plan shrinking from annual wish list to one 12 week focus
- a student replacing a semester-wide vague intention with a shorter execution map
- a personal goal moving from yearly aspiration to short-cycle commitments

Common Misreadings:
- treating the chapter as anti-planning
- making a 12 week plan as long and overloaded as an annual plan
- confusing many tasks with a strong plan

Counterarguments or Limits:
The chapter should preserve the limit that a 12 week plan still depends on clear priorities and later weekly execution. A shorter plan is not enough if it remains bloated or vague.
