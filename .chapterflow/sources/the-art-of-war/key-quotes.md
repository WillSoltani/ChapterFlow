# The Art of War — Key Quotes

All quotes below are direct Lionel Giles 1910 wording from the public-domain text preserved in Project Gutenberg #17405. Each note explains why the line is load-bearing for its chapter rather than merely famous.

## Chapter 1: Laying Plans
- **Quote**: “The art of war is of vital importance to the State.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This line gives the whole book its scale. Strategy is framed as a state-survival problem, not a personality contest.
- **Quote**: “All warfare is based on deception.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This is the chapter's core mechanism for execution after assessment has been completed.
- **Quote**: “Thus do many calculations lead to victory, and few calculations to defeat.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: It states the chapter's planning thesis in one sentence: outcomes are shaped before battle by disciplined comparison.

## Chapter 2: Waging War
- **Quote**: “There is no instance of a country having benefited from prolonged warfare.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The chapter's anti-attrition argument depends on this claim about time, cost, and national exhaustion.
- **Quote**: “Bring war material with you from home, but forage on the enemy.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This line converts logistics into strategic leverage rather than a back-office detail.
- **Quote**: “In war, then, let your great object be victory, not lengthy campaigns.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: It condenses the chapter's tempo doctrine and explains why speed is a moral and political necessity.

## Chapter 3: Attack by Stratagem
- **Quote**: “Supreme excellence consists in breaking the enemy's resistance without fighting.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This is the chapter's thesis and the book's most famous statement of strategic indirection.
- **Quote**: “The worst policy of all is to besiege walled cities.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The line establishes the hierarchy of methods by naming the most destructive and least intelligent option.
- **Quote**: “If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the result of a hundred battles.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: It turns victory from a matter of courage into a matter of relational knowledge.

## Chapter 4: Tactical Dispositions
- **Quote**: “The good fighters of old first put themselves beyond the possibility of defeat.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This is the chapter's opening principle: security comes first, aggression second.
- **Quote**: “One may know how to conquer without being able to do it.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The line separates abstract understanding from conditions that actually permit victory.
- **Quote**: “He wins his battles by making no mistakes.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: It rejects heroic romanticism and explains why structural advantage matters more than dramatic daring.

## Chapter 5: Energy
- **Quote**: “Indirect tactics, efficiently applied, are inexhaustible as Heaven and Earth.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This line explains why the chapter cares so much about combinations rather than single moves.
- **Quote**: “Simulated disorder postulates perfect discipline.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: It shows that deception works only when the underlying system is stronger than it looks.
- **Quote**: “The clever combatant looks to the effect of combined energy.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The chapter's real subject is force multiplication, not brute force.

## Chapter 6: Weak Points and Strong
- **Quote**: “The clever combatant imposes his will on the enemy, but does not allow the enemy's will to be imposed on him.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This is the chapter's control principle in its cleanest form.
- **Quote**: “Appear at points which the enemy must hasten to defend.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: It captures how to create movement and divide the opponent.
- **Quote**: “Just as water retains no constant shape, so in warfare there are no constant conditions.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The water analogy is the chapter's final argument against rigid methods.

## Chapter 7: Maneuvering
- **Quote**: “In war, practise dissimulation, and you will succeed.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: Maneuver is not just movement; it is movement masked and timed for advantage.
- **Quote**: “Let your rapidity be that of the wind, your compactness that of the forest.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This line turns operational qualities into memorable command standards.
- **Quote**: “A whole army may be robbed of its spirit; a commander-in-chief may be robbed of his presence of mind.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The chapter depends on psychological timing as much as on route and supply.

## Chapter 8: Variation in Tactics
- **Quote**: “There are roads which must not be followed.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The line announces the chapter's anti-routine stance from the start.
- **Quote**: “Rely not on the likelihood of the enemy's not coming, but on our own readiness to receive him.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This is the chapter's clearest statement of disciplined preparedness.
- **Quote**: “These are the five besetting sins of a general.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The chapter closes by relocating failure from maps to character.

## Chapter 9: The Army on the March
- **Quote**: “Movement amongst the trees of a forest shows that the enemy is advancing.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The chapter teaches commanders to read the environment as live intelligence.
- **Quote**: “When the soldiers stand leaning on their spears, they are faint from want of food.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: It shows how operational truth leaks through bodily signals before formal reports do.
- **Quote**: “Soldiers must be treated in the first instance with humanity, but kept under control by means of iron discipline.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This line ties reconnaissance and camp management back to command authority.

## Chapter 10: Terrain
- **Quote**: “The natural formation of the country is the soldier's best ally.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The chapter begins with geography but treats it as usable intelligence, not scenery.
- **Quote**: “If fighting is sure to result in victory, then you must fight, even though the ruler forbid it.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This is the chapter's sharpest statement of operational judgment over political vanity.
- **Quote**: “If you know Heaven and know Earth, you may make your victory complete.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: It expands the book's epistemic formula from self and enemy to full situational knowledge.

## Chapter 11: The Nine Situations
- **Quote**: “Rapidity is the essence of war.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The chapter's whole campaign logic depends on speed interacting with surprise and depth.
- **Quote**: “Throw your soldiers into positions whence there is no escape, and they will prefer death to flight.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: It states the chapter's harsh psychological theory of cohesion under desperate conditions.
- **Quote**: “At first, then, exhibit the coyness of a maiden; afterwards emulate the rapidity of a running hare.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This closing image compresses concealment and sudden action into one formula.

## Chapter 12: The Attack by Fire
- **Quote**: “Those who use fire as an aid to the attack show intelligence.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The chapter is about using instruments intelligently, not worshipping force multipliers.
- **Quote**: “Move not unless you see an advantage.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This is the chapter's general rule, broader than fire and central to its restraint ethic.
- **Quote**: “A kingdom that has once been destroyed can never come again into being.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The line justifies the chapter's warnings against angry or vain escalation.

## Chapter 13: The Use of Spies
- **Quote**: “What enables the wise sovereign and the good general to strike and conquer, and achieve things beyond the reach of ordinary men, is foreknowledge.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: This is the chapter's thesis and the book's final answer to uncertainty.
- **Quote**: “Knowledge of the enemy's dispositions can only be obtained from other men.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: It rejects mystical, purely deductive, or purely experiential shortcuts.
- **Quote**: “Spies are a most important element in war, because on them depends an army's ability to move.”
  **Why it's load-bearing**: The final line makes intelligence the book's closing operational foundation.
