- **Memorable lines are prose splices.** All 12 memorable lines are verbatim sentences already in a tier ("Lord Loudoun holds the ship in New York for weeks." is a plot sentence, not a line).
- **One example mold.** All four chapters deal the six examples in the same tag order; ch2 ties back 6 of 6 whyItMatters with "the identical premise/gap/terms/principle"; ch1 ex03 and ex05 are the same beat (a drafted public post deleted); ch3 opens five of six scenarios with a character who "read how the old Library Company got its start".
- **Cards and quizzes test recognition.** 15 of 28 card backs open on a scaffold ("The contrast is", "The boundary is", "The trigger was"); 10 of 36 keys carry an "only ... , not ..." shape the distractors lack; distractor-tell is 22% against a 20% target; ch2 q06 stem echoes its own key ("Where is the boundary..." / "The boundary is that...").
- **One if-then formula.** ch3's three if-thens all say "run the two-option comparison"; the 24-hour challenge opens "Within the next twenty-four hours" in every chapter.
- **No protagonist in chapter 3.** "Franklin" appears 0 times in the whole chapter (23 / 39 / 48 in the others). The chapter reads "So people built these things a different way" and "credit stayed with unnamed friends and subscribers, never with one founder."
- **No voice.** The writers received no voice card (see §4); the prose is a house third-person summary, and the readers name the missing register: "flat third-person summary voice with none of Franklin's wry first-person irony."
- **Limits by omission.** ch2 never says Franklin let the courses lapse ("one course only in a year ... omitted them entirely"); ch1's closing "Judging a sponsor by delivery, not by praise" contradicts its own Keith story two paragraphs up.

## 5. Panel versus rubric (§5 calibration question)

Facts. The seats and the catalog rubric use the same ten factor names and identical weights (`src/review/readerReview.ts:93-104` equals `compose.py`). Seats see only the chapter title, never the book title or author, never the answer key or explanations, never the counterintuition field, never other chapters (`src/review/renderReaderDoc.ts:47-79`). Seats are forbidden factual authority (`readerExperienceReview.ts:78`) and escalate what they cannot check: the r7 review carries 36 escalation WARNs on a primary-source memoir with no consumer. The chapter bar is 70, borrowed from a descriptive band of a different instrument; across 32 scored Franklin reviews the per-review mean chapter composite stayed within 75.4 to 79.3 while blockers swung 0 to 7, and BELOW_FLOOR never fired on the r7 lineage. The panel is functioning as a defect detector; the score axis decides nothing.

Recommendation (calibration agent, which I endorse): do not move the catalog factor glosses into the seats. Keep the panel unchanged as the floor. Add the book-score instrument as a separate, additive, book-level promotion gate bound to the candidate's manifestDigest, with two hardenings: a split gate vote fails closed until the orchestrator adjudicates it against a cited source (compose.py currently PASSes 2-1), and a majority-of-seats blind key derivation that disagrees with correctIndex becomes a BLOCKER. Reserve ≥85 as the badge, not the promotion gate, unless the owner accepts about 4% catalog throughput. Must not change: the 70 floor, union-any-seat blockers, ERROR on a failed seat, median not mean, weights, key isolation, the source-truth authority limit.

The fresh six-reader result adds a second point the calibration agent did not have: the same rubric, read by a different reader class, moves 12 points. Whatever promotion gate is chosen must pin the reader model and route and never re-roll unchanged bytes.
